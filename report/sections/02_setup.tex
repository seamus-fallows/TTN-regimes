\section{Setup: model, data, teacher, training and diagnostics}\label{sec:setup}

This section fixes the model and the measurements used to classify training regimes. Every
experiment uses these definitions; deviations are stated where they occur.

% ===========================================================================================
\subsection{Tree tensor network regression}\label{sec:setup-model}

\paragraph{Inputs.}
An input $x$ has $\Ns$ sites; site $i$ carries a local feature vector $\feat{i}(x)\in\R^{\pd[i]}$
(default $\pd[i]=d$). Unless stated otherwise $\feat{i}(x)=\xs{i}\sim\Normal(0,\Sigi{i})$,
independent across sites, and the inputs are \emph{whitened}, $\Sigi{i}=\Id$. We write
\begin{equation*}
  \Feat(x)=\feat{1}(x)\otimes\dots\otimes\feat{\Ns}(x)\in\R^{\pd[1]\times\dots\times\pd[\Ns]},
  \qquad
  \Sig=\Sigi{1}\otimes\dots\otimes\Sigi{\Ns}.
\end{equation*}

\paragraph{Trees and cores.}
A tree tensor network (TTN) is specified by a rooted tree $\tree$ with node set $\nodes$,
$\abs{\nodes}=\Kc$, and an assignment of each site to one node. For a node $v$, $\sites{v}$ is its
(possibly empty) set of sites, $\ch{v}$ its children and $\pa{v}$ its parent. Every non-root node $v$
is joined to its parent by the edge (bond) $\edge{v}$ of \emph{bond dimension} $\bd{v}$;
$\bd{\vroot}=1$. Node $v$ carries a \emph{core} $\G{v}$ with one \emph{physical leg} of dimension
$\pd[i]$ per $i\in\sites{v}$, one leg of dimension $\bd{c}$ per child $c$, and a parent leg of
dimension $\bd{v}$ (at the root, the scalar output). The dimensions of the other (\emph{child}) legs
multiply to the \emph{fan-in}
\begin{equation*}
  \fanin{v}=\prod_{i\in\sites{v}}\pd[i]\prod_{c\in\ch{v}}\bd{c},
\end{equation*}
so $\G{v}$ is an $\fanin{v}\times\bd{v}$ matrix; the parameters are $\params=(\G{v})_{v\in\nodes}$.

\paragraph{Model.}
The tensor $\W(\params)\in\R^{\pd[1]\times\dots\times\pd[\Ns]}$ is the contraction of all cores
over all bonds, and the model is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model}
  f_\params(x)=\ip{\W(\params)}{\Feat(x)}.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, $f_\params$ is computed by passing messages $m_v(x)\in\R^{\bd{v}}$ from the leaves to
the root,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:messages}
  m_v(x)_a=\sum_{(j_i),(b_c)}\G{v}\big[(j_i)_{i\in\sites{v}},(b_c)_{c\in\ch{v}},a\big]
  \prod_{i\in\sites{v}}\feat{i}(x)_{j_i}\prod_{c\in\ch{v}}m_c(x)_{b_c},
  \qquad
  f_\params(x)=m_{\vroot}(x).
\end{equation}
The model is multilinear in the site features, and $\W$ is multilinear in the cores (each appears
once), so $\params\mapsto\W(\params)$ is homogeneous of degree $\Kc$: $\G{v}\mapsto c_v\G{v}$
rescales $\W$ by $\prod_v c_v$.

\paragraph{Bipartitions and bond spectra.}
Let $\below{v}$ be the sites in the subtree rooted at $v$; the edge $\edge{v}$ separates them from the
rest, and $\mat{\W}{\edge{v}}$ is the matricization of $\W$ with the sites of $\below{v}$ as rows.
Contracting the subtree below $v$ and the rest separately gives
$\mat{\W}{\edge{v}}=\Sub{v}C_v\T$ with $\Sub{v}\in\R^{(\prod_{i\in\below{v}}\pd[i])\times\bd{v}}$, so
$\rank\mat{\W}{\edge{v}}\le\bd{v}$ \citep{hackbusch2009new,grasedyck2010hierarchical}. The
\emph{bond spectrum} (Schmidt values) of $e=\edge{v}$ is the list
$\bs{e}{1}\ge\dots\ge\bs{e}{\bd{v}}\ge0$ of the $\bd{v}$ largest singular values of $\mat{\W}{e}$;
they include all nonzero ones, so $\sum_k(\bs{e}{k})^2=\normF{\W}^2$ on every edge. With anisotropic
inputs we use the function-space spectra of $\mat{(\Sig^{1/2}\W)}{e}$,
$\Sig^{1/2}=\bigotimes_i\Sigi{i}^{1/2}$. QR sweeps over the cores give them to about $10^{-15}$
relative accuracy without forming $\W$.

\paragraph{Why this model.}
It is the simplest genuine TTN. (i)~With at least three sites and an internal bond, $\W$ is a
genuine tensor; a deep linear network is a chain with physical legs only at its two ends.
(ii)~Whitened inputs reduce the population loss to a Frobenius distance (\cref{sec:setup-loss}), the
setting in which \citet{saxe2014exact} solved deep linear networks. (iii)~The matrix product state
(MPS) models of \citet{stoudenmire2016supervised} have the same form with non-Gaussian features.
(iv)~Training is then a fully observed tensor factorization. On HT trees it has the mode tree of the
hierarchical tensor factorization of \citet{razin2022implicit} (one leaf matrix per site), but there
each internal node is a local CP factorization,
$\G{v}[b_1,\dots,b_c,a]=\sum_r\delta_{b_1r}\cdots\delta_{b_cr}M^{(v)}_{ra}$, trained on general losses
such as tensor completion; our dense cores carry the matrix gauge symmetries of
\cref{sec:setup-conservation} instead of one scalar invariant per local component.

\subsection{Tree topologies}\label{sec:setup-trees}

\Cref{tab:trees} lists the built-in topologies and \cref{fig:trees} draws them. Binary trees and
MPS with the same $\Ns$ are the extremes of tree shape at nearly equal $\Kc$, the default pair for
saddle-to-saddle studies. The hierarchical Tucker (HT) format adds one leaf core per site; with leaf
bond dimension $d$ this adds no expressivity but changes $\Kc$ and the number of cores that must
move to learn a component. Unless stated otherwise every edge has the same nominal bond dimension
$\chi$, capped at the largest rank its bipartition allows,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bond-cap}
  \bd{v}=\min\Big(\chi,\ \prod_{i\in\below{v}}\pd[i],\ \prod_{i\notin\below{v}}\pd[i]\Big).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Teacher, data and loss}\label{sec:setup-loss}

\paragraph{Teachers.}
The target is realizable, $f^*(x)=\ip{\Wt}{\Feat(x)}$. A \emph{CP teacher} has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cp-teacher}
  \Wt=\sum_{r=1}^{\ncomp}\tw{r}\U{r},\qquad
  \U{r}=\fac{r}{1}\otimes\dots\otimes\fac{r}{\Ns},\qquad
  \norm{\fac{r}{i}}=1,\qquad \tw{1}\ge\dots\ge\tw{\ncomp}>0.
\end{equation}
Three kinds are used:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Odeco} (orthogonally decomposable): $\ip{\fac{r}{i}}{\fac{r'}{i}}=\delta_{rr'}$ at every
    site (so $\ncomp\le\min_i\pd[i]$); by default the factors at each site are columns of an
    independent Haar-random orthogonal matrix.
  \item \emph{Pattern teachers}: site $i$ has an orthonormal basis $(\beta^{(i)}_j)_j$ and component
    $r$ uses $\fac{r}{i}=\beta^{(i)}_{\pi_r(i)}$ for a pattern $\pi_r\colon\{1,\dots,\Ns\}\to\{1,\dots,d\}$.
    With $\pi_1=(1,1,1,1)$ and $\pi_2=(2,2,1,1)$ the second component differs from the first only on
    $D=\{1,2\}$ (a \emph{local} component).
  \item \emph{Random CP}: $\fac{r}{i}=g/\norm{g}$ with independent $g\sim\Normal(0,\Id)$; the
    components are generically non-orthogonal.
\end{itemize}
A \emph{TTN teacher} gives $\Wt$ as a TTN on the student's tree or on another tree; a random TTN
teacher is a fan-in initialization (\cref{sec:setup-init}) with $\isc=1$, rescaled to
$\normF{\Wt}=1$. The overlap of the student with component $r$ of a CP teacher,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:overlap}
  \ov{r}=\ipS{\W}{\U{r}}/\ipS{\Wt}{\U{r}},
\end{equation}
is $0$ at $\W=0$ and $1$ at $\W=\Wt$; for an odeco teacher with whitened inputs,
$\ov{r}=\ip{\W}{\U{r}}/\tw{r}$.

\begin{proposition}[Teacher bond spectra]\label{prop:teacher-spectra}
  \proven{} Let $e$ be an edge whose two sides both carry sites (every edge of the trees of
  \cref{tab:trees}). (a)~For an odeco teacher the bond spectrum of $\Wt$ on $e$ is
  $(\tw{1},\dots,\tw{\ncomp})$, for every tree. (b)~For a two-component pattern teacher whose
  components differ exactly on the site set $D$, the bond rank of $\Wt$ on $e$ is $2$ if $e$ lies in
  the smallest subtree of $\tree$ containing the nodes that carry the sites of $D$, and $1$ otherwise.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  With $A$ the sites on one side of $e$, $x_r=\bigotimes_{i\in A}\fac{r}{i}$ and
  $y_r=\bigotimes_{i\notin A}\fac{r}{i}$, $\mat{(\Wt)}{e}=\sum_r\tw{r}x_ry_r\T$. (a)~Both families are
  orthonormal, so this is a singular value decomposition. (b)~If $D\subseteq A$ or $D\cap A=\emptyset$,
  then $y_1=y_2$ or $x_1=x_2$: rank one. Otherwise the components differ (by orthogonal basis vectors)
  on both sides, so $x_1\perp x_2$, $y_1\perp y_2$: rank two. And $e$ separates two sites of $D$
  exactly when it lies on the tree path between their nodes.
\end{proof}
The number of cores in that subtree is the quantity $k$ of the locality hypothesis
(\cref{sec:s2s-locality}).

\paragraph{Population loss.}
Training minimizes the population square loss
\begin{equation}\label{eq:loss}
  \Loss(\params)=\tfrac12\,\E_x\big[(f_\params(x)-f^*(x))^2\big]
  =\tfrac12\normS{\W(\params)-\Wt}^2,
  \qquad \normS{A}^2=\ip{A}{\Sig A}.
\end{equation}
The second equality holds because, by independence across sites,
$\E[\Feat\otimes\Feat]=\bigotimes_i\E[\feat{i}\feat{i}\T]=\Sig$. With whitened inputs $\Loss$ is half
the squared Frobenius distance. Only second moments enter: every local feature map with
$\E[\feat{i}\feat{i}\T]=\Id$ gives the same dynamics, and a non-orthonormal one is equivalent to
$\Sigi{i}=\E[\feat{i}\feat{i}\T]\ne\Id$.

\paragraph{Closed form.}
Expanding \eqref{eq:loss},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:loss-expanded}
  \Loss(\params)=\tfrac12\ipS{\W}{\W}-\ipS{\W}{\Wt}+\tfrac12\ipS{\Wt}{\Wt},
\end{equation}
and every term is an exact contraction:
\begin{itemize}
  \item For two TTNs $\W,\W'$ on the same tree (bond dimensions may differ),
    $\ipS{\W}{\W'}=E_{\vroot}$, with environments $E_v\in\R^{\bd{v}\times\bd{v}'}$ computed from the
    leaves to the root at a cost linear in $\Kc$,
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:environment}
      (E_v)_{aa'}=\sum\G{v}\big[(j_i),(b_c),a\big]\,\G{v}'\big[(j'_i),(b'_c),a'\big]
      \prod_{i\in\sites{v}}(\Sigi{i})_{j_ij'_i}\prod_{c\in\ch{v}}(E_c)_{b_cb'_c}.
    \end{equation}
  \item For a CP teacher, $\ipS{\W}{\U{r}}$ is the student evaluated at the site vectors
    $\Sigi{i}\fac{r}{i}$, so for any tree
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:cp-inner}
      \ipS{\W}{\Wt}=\sum_r\tw{r}\,f_\params\big(\Sigi{1}\fac{r}{1},\dots,\Sigi{\Ns}\fac{r}{\Ns}\big),
      \qquad
      \ipS{\Wt}{\Wt}=\sum_{r,r'}\tw{r}\tw{r'}\prod_i\fac{r}{i}{}\T\Sigi{i}\fac{r'}{i},
    \end{equation}
    where $f_\params(z_1,\dots,z_\Ns)$ is \eqref{eq:messages} with $\feat{i}(x)$ replaced by $z_i$.
  \item A TTN teacher on another tree is glued to the student along the physical legs, and the
    loopy network is contracted exactly along an optimized path.
\end{itemize}
For an odeco teacher with whitened inputs, \eqref{eq:loss-expanded} reads
$\Loss=\tfrac12\normF{\W}^2-\sum_r\tw{r}^2\ov{r}+\tfrac12\sum_r\tw{r}^2$. A \emph{partial fit}
$\W_S=\sum_{r\in S}\tw{r}\U{r}$ has loss $\tfrac12\sum_{r\notin S}\tw{r}^2$, so learning the components
in decreasing order of $\tw{r}$ passes through the candidate plateau levels
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partial-fit}
  \Lpart{k}=\tfrac12\sum_{r>k}\tw{r}^2,\qquad k=0,\dots,\ncomp.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Anisotropic and correlated inputs.}
Anisotropic product covariances are handled exactly by \eqref{eq:loss}--\eqref{eq:cp-inner}; the
default family has power-law site spectra,
$\Sigi{i}=c_\beta\diag(1^{-\beta},2^{-\beta},\dots,d^{-\beta})$ with $c_\beta$ fixed by
$\tr\Sigi{i}=d$, aligned with the teacher's factors or rotated away from them. Cross-site
correlations break the product structure of $\Sig$; for small $\Ns$ the loss is then computed with
the dense second-moment matrix (\cref{sec:covariance-teacher}).

\paragraph{Finite samples.}
A training set $(x_j,y_j)_{j=1}^{\nsamp}$ has
$x_j\sim\Normal(0,\Sig)$ \iid and $y_j=f^*(x_j)+\varepsilon_j$ with optional label noise
$\varepsilon_j\sim\Normal(0,\varsigma^2)$. The empirical loss is
$\Lemp(\params)=\frac{1}{2\nsamp}\sum_{j}(f_\params(x_j)-y_j)^2$, and the test error is the
population loss \eqref{eq:loss}.

\subsection{Initialization and its variants}\label{sec:setup-init}

\paragraph{Fan-in initialization.}
The default initialization is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:init}
  \G{v}=\frac{\iscv{v}}{\sqrt{\fanin{v}}}\,Z_v,\qquad Z_v \text{ with \iid } \Normal(0,1)
  \text{ entries},
\end{equation}
with a single per-core scale $\iscv{v}=\isc$ unless stated otherwise. Each core is then
approximately $\iscv{v}$ times an isometry (when $\fanin{v}\gg\bd{v}$), so with $\iscv{v}=1$ the
messages \eqref{eq:messages} keep their size up the tree.

\begin{proposition}[Initial scales]\label{prop:init-norm}
  \proven{} Under \eqref{eq:init}, $\E\normF{\W(\params_0)}^2=\prod_{v}\iscv{v}^2$ exactly,
  $\E\normF{\G{v}}^2=\iscv{v}^2\bd{v}$, and hence $\E\norm{\params_0}^2=\sum_v\iscv{v}^2\bd{v}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  Let $\Sub{v}$ be the contraction of the subtree below $v$ with its parent leg open ($\bd{v}$
  columns). Conditionally on the children's subtrees, the independent entries of $\G{v}$, of variance
  $\iscv{v}^2/\fanin{v}$, give $\E[\Sub{v}\T\Sub{v}]=\frac{\iscv{v}^2}{\fanin{v}}
  \prod_{i\in\sites{v}}\pd[i]\prod_{c\in\ch{v}}\tr(\Sub{c}\T\Sub{c})\,\Id$. The children's subtrees are
  independent and $\fanin{v}=\prod_{i\in\sites{v}}\pd[i]\prod_{c}\bd{c}$, so by induction from the
  leaves $\E[\Sub{v}\T\Sub{v}]=\big(\prod_{u}\iscv{u}^2\big)\Id$ over the subtree of $v$; at the root
  $\Sub{\vroot}$ is $\W$ flattened. Finally, $\E\normF{\G{v}}^2=\fanin{v}\bd{v}\,\iscv{v}^2/\fanin{v}$.
\end{proof}

We report both $\isc$ and the function-space scale $\fsc=\isc^{\Kc}$, the root-mean-square
norm of $\W(\params_0)$: small $\isc$ makes $\W(\params_0)$ exponentially small in $\Kc$, while
$\norm{\params_0}^2\approx\isc^2\sum_v\bd{v}$ grows with the bond dimensions.

\paragraph{Variants.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
  \item \emph{Imbalance}: per-core scales $\iscv{v}=\isc c_v$ with a fixed profile $(c_v)$ (for
    example root versus rest), compared at fixed $\prod_v\iscv{v}$.
  \item \emph{Centred model}: the target is $\Wt+\W(\params_0)$, that is, $f_\params-f_{\params_0}$ is
    trained on $f^*$ \citep{chizat2019lazy}. The initial function is removed at any $\isc$ without
    changing the parameterization, so $\isc$ alone moves the model from small to large
    initialization; overlaps and bond spectra refer to $\W-\W(\params_0)$. This is the main
    construction of the regime map (\cref{sec:regime-map}).
  \item \emph{Two-copy model} \citep{woodworth2020kernel}: $\W(\params^+)-\W(\params^-)$ with
    $\params^+(0)=\params^-(0)=\params_0$, zero at initialization for every $\isc$; a cross-check of
    the centred model (\path{notes/model.md}, \path{notes/decisions.md}). It is stored as one TTN with
    doubled bonds $2\bd{v}$ and block-diagonal cores ($\G{v}^\pm$ on the block where all bond indices
    of $v$ are in the ``$\pm$'' half; the root holds $\G{\vroot}^+$ and $-\G{\vroot}^-$) and needs a
    gradient mask (\cref{rem:two-copy-mask}). Both differences are contracted without cancellation,
    as one TTN on doubled bonds in half-sums and half-differences of the two copies.
  \item \emph{Aligned (decoupled) initialization} for CP teachers (\cref{prop:aligned}): cores
    diagonal in the teacher's basis, giving exactly solvable Saxe-type dynamics.
  \item \emph{Per-core learning-rate multipliers} $\lrm{v}$ \eqref{eq:gf}. A fixed
    forward multiplier $c_v$ ($\G{v}=c_vH_v$, $H_v$ trained) is equivalent to the initial scale
    $c_v\iscv{v}$ with the multiplier $c_v^2\lrm{v}$; this expresses NTK- and $\mu$P-type
    parameterizations for large bond dimension \citep{yang2021tensor} (\cref{sec:other-regimes}).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{remark}[The two-copy model needs a gradient mask]\label{rem:two-copy-mask}
  \proven{} Gradient flow on the doubled TTN does not keep its block-diagonal form: for a root with
  children $c_1,c_2$ and no sites, the root entry that pairs index $b_1$ of the $+$ half of
  $\edge{c_1}$ with index $b_2$ of the $-$ half of $\edge{c_2}$ has gradient
  $\ipS{\W-\Wt}{\Sub{c_1}^+[:,b_1]\otimes\Sub{c_2}^-[:,b_2]}$ (up to the order of sites), at
  initialization $-\ipS{\Wt}{\Sub{c_1}^+[:,b_1]\otimes\Sub{c_2}^-[:,b_2]}$, generically nonzero; mixed
  blocks of other cores also receive gradient. The two-copy dynamics is therefore the masked flow
  $\dot\params=-\mask\odot\nabla\Loss(\params)$, $\mask$ the $0/1$ indicator of the diagonal blocks,
  which keeps the blocks and equals gradient flow of
  $\tfrac12\normS{\W(\params^+)-\W(\params^-)-\Wt}^2$ in $(\params^+,\params^-)$.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Aligned initialization]\label{prop:aligned}
  \proven{} \knownresult[deep linear networks: \citealp{saxe2014exact}] Let $\Wt$ be odeco, inputs
  whitened, $\bd{v}\ge\ncomp$ on every edge, and let every subtree and its complement carry a site
  (true for the trees of \cref{tab:trees}). Initialize
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:aligned-init}
    \G{v}=\sum_{r=1}^{\ncomp}\amp{v}{r}\Big(\bigotimes_{i\in\sites{v}}\fac{r}{i}\Big)\otimes
    \Big(\bigotimes_{c\in\ch{v}}\varepsilon_r\Big)\otimes\varepsilon_{r(v)},
  \end{equation}
  with $\varepsilon_r$ the $r$-th standard basis vector of the bond in question, $r(v)=r$ for
  $v\ne\vroot$ and $r(\vroot)=1$. Under gradient flow with multipliers $\lrm{v}$ the cores keep this
  form, $\W=\sum_r\prodamp{r}\U{r}$ with $\prodamp{r}=\prod_v\amp{v}{r}$, $\ov{r}=\prodamp{r}/\tw{r}$,
  $\Loss=\tfrac12\sum_r(\tw{r}-\prodamp{r})^2$, and
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:aligned-ode}
    \dot q_{v,r}=\lrm{v}\,(\tw{r}-\prodamp{r})\prod_{u\ne v}\amp{u}{r}.
  \end{equation}
  With $\lrm{v}=1$ the differences $\amp{v}{r}^2-\amp{u}{r}^2$ are conserved; a balanced start
  $\amp{v}{r}(0)=q_r(0)$ stays balanced, and $\dot q_r=q_r^{\Kc-1}(\tw{r}-q_r^{\Kc})$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  On the family \eqref{eq:aligned-init}, contracting the bond basis vectors gives
  $\W=\sum_r\prodamp{r}\U{r}$ with orthonormal $\U{r}$. The derivative of $\W$ with respect to an
  entry of $\G{v}$ is a product of basis vectors on the physical legs of $v$, the child-subtree
  columns $\Sub{c}[:,b_c]$ and the environment column above $v$ indexed by $a$; on the family these
  are multiples of $\bigotimes_{i\in\below{c}}\fac{b_c}{i}$ (and of the analogous product over the
  sites outside $\below{v}$), so their inner product with $\W-\Wt=\sum_r(\prodamp{r}-\tw{r})\U{r}$
  vanishes unless all bond indices equal one $r$ ($a=1$ at the root). The gradient thus stays in the
  family, with component $(\prodamp{r}-\tw{r})\prod_{u\ne v}\amp{u}{r}$ along $\amp{v}{r}$; with
  $\lrm{v}=1$, $\ddt\amp{v}{r}^2=2(\tw{r}-\prodamp{r})\prodamp{r}$ for every $v$.
\end{proof}

For $\Kc\ge3$ a balanced mode started at $q_r(0)\ll\tw{r}^{1/\Kc}$ reaches $\ov{r}=\frac12$ at
\begin{equation}\label{eq:aligned-escape}
  t_r=\frac{q_r(0)^{-(\Kc-2)}}{(\Kc-2)\,\tw{r}}+O\big(\tw{r}^{-(2-2/\Kc)}\big),
\end{equation}
and for $\Kc=2$ at $t_r=\log(\tw{r}/q_r(0)^2)/(2\tw{r})+O(q_r(0)^2/\tw{r}^2)$, by direct integration
of the balanced ODE \proven. With $q_r(0)\propto\isc$ the first plateau grows like
$\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}$, hence adaptive integration in double precision.

\subsection{Training dynamics}\label{sec:setup-dynamics}

\paragraph{Gradient flow} is the reference dynamics,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gf}
  \dot\params=-\Pre\,\nabla\Loss(\params),
\end{equation}
where the diagonal $\Pre$ holds, for each parameter $j$ of core $v$, the learning-rate multiplier
$\lrm{v}$ (default $1$) times an optional $0/1$ mask $\mask_j$ (\cref{rem:two-copy-mask}; default
$1$). Here $t$ denotes gradient-flow time. The default integrator is the adaptive
Dormand--Prince 5(4) pair \citep{dormand1980family} with relative and absolute tolerances $10^{-8}$
and $10^{-12}$ and standard step-size control \citep[Sec.~II.4]{hairer1993solving}; steps follow the
local time scale $\norm{\params}/\norm{\dot\params}$, so long plateaus are cheap. Past the first
escape the flow is stiff, and many experiments use SciPy's LSODA with the exact Hessian as Jacobian
(\cref{app:experiments}). Diagnostics are recorded at $t=0$ and at $200$ log-spaced times from
$10^{-3}\min(\tau_0,\tau_\Loss,\tmax)$ to $\tmax$, where $\tau_0=\norm{\params}/\norm{\dot\params}$
and $\tau_\Loss=\Loss/\abs{\dot\Loss}$ at $t=0$. This grid cannot resolve escapes, so the recorder
also keeps a \emph{fine row} (loss, overlaps, bond spectra) after each integrator step at which
$\params$ has moved by $1\%$ of its norm, or $\ln\Loss$ by $0.01$, since the last kept row; each rise
is sampled about $20$ times or more (\path{Recorder} and \path{merged_rows} in \path{ttn/dynamics.py}).
A run stops at $\tmax$, below a prescribed loss floor, or at step or wall-time limits. Accuracy is
checked with the conservation laws (\cref{sec:setup-conservation}) and the aligned solutions
(\cref{prop:aligned}).

\paragraph{Gradient descent} with learning rate $\lr$,
$\params_{k+1}=\params_k-\lr\Pre\nabla\Loss(\params_k)$, is recorded at $t=\lr k$. A run has diverged when $\Loss$ becomes non-finite or exceeds
$10^{6}\Loss(\params_0)$.

\paragraph{Stochastic gradient descent} uses the minibatch loss
$\hat\Loss_{\batch}(\params)=\frac{1}{2\batch}\sum_{b=1}^{\batch}(f_\params(x_b)-y_b)^2$ in the
same update. Online SGD draws fresh inputs $x_b\sim\Normal(0,\Sig)$ with
$y_b=f^*(x_b)$ at every step; with a fixed training set, each epoch uses a new random permutation.
Optional label noise is drawn afresh each step.

\paragraph{Precision.}
All computations are in double precision: with $\isc=10^{-2}$ and $\Kc=7$,
$\normF{\W(\params_0)}\approx10^{-14}$ and gradients are of order $10^{-12}$. A dormant component
whose escape signal falls below about $10^{-15}\normF{\W}$ is seeded by roundoff, not by the
initialization; such runs are flagged.

\subsection{Symmetries and conservation laws}\label{sec:setup-conservation}

For a bond $e=\edge{v}$ joining core $v$ to its parent $p=\pa{v}$, let $\Gm{u}{e}$ denote core
$u\in\{v,p\}$ matricized with its $e$-leg as rows (a $\bd{v}\times\fanin{v}$ matrix for $u=v$). For
every invertible $B\in\GL(\bd{v})$ the \emph{gauge transformation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gauge}
  \Gm{v}{e}\mapsto B\,\Gm{v}{e},\qquad \Gm{p}{e}\mapsto B^{-\top}\Gm{p}{e}
\end{equation}
leaves $\W$, and hence any loss that depends on $\params$ only through $\W$, unchanged.

\begin{proposition}[Conservation laws]\label{prop:conservation}
  \proven{} \knownresult[deep linear networks: \citealp{arora2018optimization,du2018algorithmic}]
  Under gradient flow $\dot{\G{u}}=-\lrm{u}\nabla_{\G{u}}\Loss$ of any loss that depends on $\params$
  only through $\W$ (population or empirical), the $\bd{v}\times\bd{v}$ matrix
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:conserved}
    \Q{e}=\frac{1}{\lrm{v}}\Gm{v}{e}\Gm{v}{e}\T-\frac{1}{\lrm{p}}\Gm{p}{e}\Gm{p}{e}\T
  \end{equation}
  is constant in time, for every bond $e=\edge{v}$ with $p=\pa{v}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  With $X=\Gm{v}{e}$, $Y=\Gm{p}{e}$ and $B=\Id+\epsilon M$ in \eqref{eq:gauge}, $X\mapsto X+\epsilon MX$
  and $Y\mapsto Y-\epsilon M\T Y+O(\epsilon^2)$; first-order invariance of $\Loss$ for every $M$ gives
  $C:=\nabla_X\Loss\,X\T=Y(\nabla_Y\Loss)\T$, so $\ddt(XX\T)/\lrm{v}=-(C+C\T)=\ddt(YY\T)/\lrm{p}$.
\end{proof}

This is the tree version of the balancedness of deep linear networks, an instance of
symmetry-induced conservation laws \citep{kunin2021neural}. Consequences:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Norm balance}: $\tr\Q{e}=\normF{\G{v}}^2/\lrm{v}-\normF{\G{p}}^2/\lrm{p}$; with equal
    multipliers all differences $\normF{\G{u}}^2-\normF{\G{u'}}^2$ are conserved, so all cores grow
    together.
  \item \emph{Balancedness at small initialization}: under \eqref{eq:init},
    $\E\,\Q{e}(0)=\big(\iscv{v}^2/\lrm{v}-\iscv{p}^2\bd{p}/(\lrm{p}\bd{v})\big)\Id$, so at small $\isc$
    the cores stay balanced up to $O(\isc^2)$ while they grow to $O(1)$. Imbalanced initializations
    or multipliers set $\Q{e}$ to other values, a second knob for the regime map
    \citep{kunin2024get}.
  \item The masked two-copy flow conserves the $\Q{e}$ of each copy; gradient descent and SGD conserve
    $\Q{e}$ only up to $O(\lr^2)$ per step.
  \item \emph{Integrator check}: the conservation drift
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:drift}
      \mathrm{drift}(t)=\frac{\max_e\normmax{\Q{e}(t)-\Q{e}(0)}}
      {\max_e\normmax{\Q{e}(0)}+\max_u\normF{\G{u}(t)}^2/\lrm{u}},
    \end{equation}
    with $\normmax{\cdot}$ the largest absolute entry, measures the integration error relative to the
    terms that cancel in $\Q{e}$; it must stay below $10^{-6}$ under gradient flow.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Learning events.}
For CP teachers with orthogonal components (in the metric $\Sig$), event $r$ is dated at the last
upward crossing $\tev{r}$ of $\ov{r}=\frac12$, the first crossing unless a transient (while another
component is learned) crossed and fell back. Its width $\wev{r}$ is the final rise, from the last
upward crossing of $0.1$ before $\ov{r}$ first reaches $0.9$ to that time. For
other teachers one escape can raise several overlaps, so events $(e,k)$ come from the bond spectra,
by one of three rules (\cref{app:experiments} gives each experiment's): (a)~the onset, the first time
$\bs{e}{k}$ reaches $f\bst{e}{k}$ with $f=0.05$ (sensitivity $f\in[0.03,0.1]$), without widths
(locality bond events, data-teacher v2 analyses); (b)~half the teacher's value (first crossing for
the optimizers, last upward crossing under correlated inputs), with final-rise widths from $0.1$ to
$0.9$ of it; (c)~the library default (\path{spectral_events}, used by \path{runner.summary}), the last
upward crossing of half the run's final value, with final-rise widths from $0.1$ to $0.9$ of it;
consecutive events whose gap is at most the larger width are then merged into one, with their mean
time and largest width. Rule (a) is recommended: a half-value rule can skip intermediate saddles whose
spectra stay below the final ones (\cref{cor:A-second-escape}). Crossings are interpolated on the
merged log and fine rows (or found by root finding on dense output); an event narrower than three
sample intervals is unresolved, and separation ratios involving it are lower bounds. The
\emph{effective bond rank} is $\rk{e}(t)=\#\{k:\bs{e}{k}(t)>0.1\,\bst{e}{\min}\}$, with
$\bst{e}{\min}$ the smallest nonzero teacher Schmidt value on $e$; the threshold-free alternative is
the entropy rank $\rkH{e}=\exp(-\sum_kp_k\log p_k)$, $p_k=(\bs{e}{k})^2/\sum_j(\bs{e}{j})^2$.

\paragraph{Plateaus and the saddle-to-saddle signature.}
Number the events in time order, $\tev{1}<\tev{2}<\cdots$, with widths $\wev{i}$; the
\emph{separation ratios} are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:separation}
  \sep{i}=\frac{\tev{i+1}-\tev{i}}{\max(\wev{i},\wev{i+1})}.
\end{equation}
Between two events with $\sep{i}>3$ the loss sits on a plateau; its midpoint $\tmid{i}$ is the log
time of minimal speed $\norm{\dot\params}$ between them. A run shows the \emph{saddle-to-saddle (S2S)
signature} when (i)~there are at least two events, (ii)~every $\sep{i}>3$, and (iii)~across a sweep,
plateau durations grow without bound as $\isc\to0$ (like $\log(1/\isc)$ or a power of $1/\isc$).
Plateaus are judged in linear time: an escape that needs two cores takes a time of order
$\log(1/\isc)$, invisible on a log-time axis after a long first plateau.

\begin{remark}[Reading of the rules]\label{rem:regime-rules}
  ``Converges'' means $\Loss(T)<10^{-3}\Loss(0)$; labels are checked in the order diverged,
  stalled, then the converged regimes. Condition (iii) belongs to a sweep: a run is
  labelled S2S on (i)--(ii), and a region of a phase diagram is S2S only if (iii) holds there. A
  converged run with $\dTK(T)\ge0.1$ and a single (merged) event is rich, non-incremental. The code
  (\path{classify_regime}) adds two labels: \emph{lazy (projected fit)}, checked before stalled, for
  $\dTK(T)<0.1$ and a final loss within $10\%$ of that of the linearized fit (positive when a bond is
  deficient), and \emph{undetermined} when every $\sep{i}\le3$ involves an unresolved event. The
  regime map measures convergence against $\frac12\normS{\Wt}^2$, which equals $\Loss(0)$ when the
  fitted tensor vanishes at $t=0$ (centred and two-copy models) and differs only for the plain model
  at large $\isc$.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Saddle type at a plateau.}
At the plateau midpoint we compute $\Hess$ and its restriction $\Hperp$ to the orthogonal complement
of the well-conditioned gauge directions: the left singular vectors of the unnormalized generator
matrix with singular values above $10^{-3}$ times the largest (\path{project_out_gauge}).
Normalizing the tiny generators of nearly unused bond indices, as an earlier cutoff of $10^{-10}$
did, removes part of the escape direction and biases $\lambda_{\min}$ near rank-deficient saddles
(\cref{app:exp-locality}); the raw $\lambda_{\min}(\Hess)$ is always reported too. The type is read
across a sweep from how $\lambda_{\min}(\Hperp)$ at the midpoint scales with $\isc$
(\cref{prop:A-signatures}): it tends to a negative constant at a \emph{strict saddle} and vanishes
like $\isc^{k-2}$ at a \emph{higher-order saddle} of order $k\ge3$ (a route through $k$ cores, or the
origin with $k=\Kc$). A fixed threshold confuses the two: the single-$\isc$ fallback, strict if
$\lambda_{\min}(\Hperp)<-10^{-3}\max_j\abs{\lambda_j(\Hperp)}$, calls $k=3$ plateaus strict for
$\isc\gtrsim0.01$--$0.04$.

\paragraph{Reporting rules.}
Every scaling claim uses at least five initialization scales over at least one decade and five seeds
per point, and reports mean $\pm$ standard error, a fitted exponent with its standard error, and the
theoretical prediction. Every regime boundary comes with the threshold sensitivity check of
\cref{tab:regimes}, and every number states the dynamics that produced it (integrator tolerances,
step size, batch size).

\subsection{What the results may depend on}\label{sec:setup-scope}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Population loss.} Plateaus and saddle sequences are properties of population gradient
    flow; implicit bias and generalization need finite samples.
  \item \emph{Multilinear features, product input measure.} There is no pointwise nonlinearity, and
    the population loss is a tree contraction, which cross-site correlations break.
  \item \emph{Placement of physical legs.} Binary trees and HT differ in $\Kc$ and in how many cores
    must change to learn a component, which sets plateau-time exponents (\cref{sec:s2s-locality}).
  \item \emph{Fan-in initialization.} It fixes the relative scales of the cores; exponents should not
    depend on it, but prefactors and the learning order might, so imbalanced, balanced ($\Q{e}=0$)
    and aligned initializations are also studied.
\end{itemize}
