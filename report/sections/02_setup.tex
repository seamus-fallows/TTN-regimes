\section{Setup: model, data, teacher, training and diagnostics}\label{sec:setup}

This section fixes the model, the measurements used to classify training regimes, and the terms
the later sections use: routes, saddle order and stalls (\cref{sec:setup-routes}), events and regime
labels (\cref{sec:setup-diagnostics}). Every experiment uses these definitions; deviations are stated
where they occur. \Cref{tab:notation} collects the notation.

% ===========================================================================================
\subsection{Tree tensor network regression}\label{sec:setup-model}

\paragraph{Inputs.}
An input $x$ has $\Ns$ \emph{sites} (input positions), numbered $i=0,\dots,\Ns-1$ throughout; site $i$
carries a local feature vector $\feat{i}(x)\in\R^{\pd[i]}$ (default $\pd[i]=d$). Unless stated
otherwise $\feat{i}(x)=\xs{i}\sim\Normal(0,\Sigi{i})$, independent across sites, and the inputs are
\emph{whitened}, $\Sigi{i}=\Id$. We write
\begin{equation*}
  \Feat(x)=\feat{0}(x)\otimes\dots\otimes\feat{\Ns-1}(x)\in\R^{\pd[0]\times\dots\times\pd[\Ns-1]},
  \qquad
  \Sig=\Sigi{0}\otimes\dots\otimes\Sigi{\Ns-1}.
\end{equation*}

\paragraph{Trees and cores.}
A tree tensor network (TTN) is specified by a rooted tree $\tree$ with node set $\nodes$,
$\abs{\nodes}=\Kc$, and an assignment of each site to one node. For a node $v$, $\sites{v}$ is its
(possibly empty) set of sites, $\ch{v}$ its children and $\pa{v}$ its parent. Every non-root node $v$
is joined to its parent by the edge (bond) $\edge{v}$ of \emph{bond dimension} $\bd{v}$;
$\bd{\vroot}=1$. Node $v$ carries a \emph{core} $\G{v}$ with one \emph{physical leg} of dimension
$\pd[i]$ per $i\in\sites{v}$, one leg of dimension $\bd{u}$ per child $u$, and a parent leg of
dimension $\bd{v}$ (at the root, the scalar output). The dimensions of the other (\emph{child}) legs
multiply to the \emph{fan-in}
\begin{equation*}
  \fanin{v}=\prod_{i\in\sites{v}}\pd[i]\prod_{u\in\ch{v}}\bd{u},
\end{equation*}
so $\G{v}$ is an $\fanin{v}\times\bd{v}$ matrix; the parameters are $\params=(\G{v})_{v\in\nodes}$.

\paragraph{Model.}
The tensor $\W(\params)\in\R^{\pd[0]\times\dots\times\pd[\Ns-1]}$ is the contraction of all cores
over all bonds, and the model is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model}
  f_\params(x)=\ip{\W(\params)}{\Feat(x)}.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, $f_\params$ is computed by passing messages $h_v(x)\in\R^{\bd{v}}$ from the leaves to
the root,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:messages}
  h_v(x)_a=\sum_{(j_i),(b_u)}\G{v}\big[(j_i)_{i\in\sites{v}},(b_u)_{u\in\ch{v}},a\big]
  \prod_{i\in\sites{v}}\feat{i}(x)_{j_i}\prod_{u\in\ch{v}}h_u(x)_{b_u},
  \qquad
  f_\params(x)=h_{\vroot}(x).
\end{equation}
The model is multilinear in the site features, and $\W$ is multilinear in the cores (each appears
once), so $\params\mapsto\W(\params)$ is homogeneous of degree $\Kc$: $\G{v}\mapsto c_v\G{v}$
rescales $\W$ by $\prod_v c_v$.

\paragraph{Bipartitions and bond spectra.}
Let $\below{v}$ be the sites in the subtree rooted at $v$; the edge $\edge{v}$ separates them from the
rest, and $\mat{\W}{\edge{v}}$ is the matricization of $\W$ with the sites of $\below{v}$ as rows.
Contracting the subtree below $v$ and the rest separately gives
$\mat{\W}{\edge{v}}=\Sub{v}C_v\T$ with $\Sub{v}\in\R^{(\prod_{i\in\below{v}}\pd[i])\times\bd{v}}$, so
the \emph{bond rank} $\rank\mat{\W}{\edge{v}}$ is at most $\bd{v}$
\citep{hackbusch2009new,grasedyck2010hierarchical}. A bipartition is written by its two site sets
as digit strings, e.g.\ $01|23$ for $\{0,1\}$ against $\{2,3\}$ (ranges: $0$--$3|4$--$7$). The
\emph{bond spectrum} of $e=\edge{v}$ is the list $\bs{e}{1}\ge\dots\ge\bs{e}{\bd{v}}\ge0$ of the
$\bd{v}$ largest singular values of $\mat{\W}{e}$ (the Schmidt values of tensor-network practice; we
say singular values). They include all nonzero ones, so the bond rank is the number of nonzero
$\bs{e}{j}$, and $\sum_j(\bs{e}{j})^2=\normF{\W}^2$ on every edge. With anisotropic inputs we use the
function-space spectra of $\mat{(\Sig^{1/2}\W)}{e}$, $\Sig^{1/2}=\bigotimes_i\Sigi{i}^{1/2}$. QR sweeps
over the cores give them to about $10^{-15}$ relative accuracy without forming $\W$.

\paragraph{Why this model.}
It is the simplest genuine TTN. (i)~With at least three sites and an internal bond, $\W$ is a
genuine tensor; a deep linear network is a chain with physical legs only at its two ends.
(ii)~Whitened inputs reduce the population loss to a Frobenius distance (\cref{sec:setup-loss}), the
setting in which \citet{saxe2014exact} solved deep linear networks. (iii)~The matrix product state
(MPS) classifiers of \citet{stoudenmire2016supervised} compute, for each class $y$,
$f^y(x)=\ip{\W^y}{\bigotimes_i\feat{i}(x)}$ (the class index sits on one core), with non-Gaussian
local features $(\cos(\pi\xs{i}/2),\sin(\pi\xs{i}/2))$ that are correlated across sites.
(iv)~Training is then a fully observed tensor factorization. On HT trees it has the mode tree of the
hierarchical tensor factorization of \citet{razin2022implicit}, whose internal nodes are local CP
factorizations (their Eq.~3) with one scalar invariant per local component; our dense cores carry the
matrix gauge symmetries of \cref{sec:setup-conservation} instead.

\subsection{Tree topologies}\label{sec:setup-trees}

\Cref{tab:trees} lists the tree families and \cref{fig:trees} draws them; binary$N$, MPS$N$ and
HT$N$ denote the member with $N$ sites (binary4, MPS8, \dots). Binary trees and MPS with the same
$\Ns$ are the extremes of tree shape at nearly equal $\Kc$, the default pair for saddle-to-saddle
studies. The hierarchical Tucker (HT) format adds one leaf core per site; with leaf bond dimension
$d$ this adds no expressivity but changes $\Kc$ and the number of cores that must move to learn a
component. Tree depth is not network depth: binary4 (tree depth one) computes the three-factor
product $\mat{\W}{01|23}=ABC\T$ of its bottom cores $A$, $C$ (as $d^2\times\chi$ matrices) and its
root $B$, a three-layer deep linear network on a $d^2\times d^2$ matrix. Results on binary4 can
therefore be compared with deep-linear-network theory; tensor-specific behaviour starts with MPS4,
binary8 and HT4. Unless stated otherwise every edge has the same nominal bond dimension $\chi$,
capped at the largest rank its bipartition allows,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bond-cap}
  \bd{v}=\min\Big(\chi,\ \prod_{i\in\below{v}}\pd[i],\ \prod_{i\notin\below{v}}\pd[i]\Big).
\end{equation}
The cap $\min(\prod_{i\in\below{v}}\pd[i],\prod_{i\notin\below{v}}\pd[i])$, the largest rank
$\mat{\W}{\edge{v}}$ can have, is the \emph{cut dimension} of $\edge{v}$, and a bond is \emph{full} when
$\bd{v}$ reaches it (binary4 with $d=3$: both bonds have cut dimension $9$). A bond below its cut
dimension limits the tensors the network can represent; a lazy regime needs every bond full
(\cref{sec:regime-map}).

\subsection{Teacher, data and loss}\label{sec:setup-loss}

\paragraph{Teachers.}
The target is realizable, $f^*(x)=\ip{\Wt}{\Feat(x)}$. A \emph{CP teacher} has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cp-teacher}
  \Wt=\sum_{r=1}^{\ncomp}\tw{r}\U{r},\qquad
  \U{r}=\fac{r}{0}\otimes\dots\otimes\fac{r}{\Ns-1},\qquad
  \norm{\fac{r}{i}}=1,\qquad \tw{1}\ge\dots\ge\tw{\ncomp}>0.
\end{equation}
Three kinds are used:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Odeco} (orthogonally decomposable): $\ip{\fac{r}{i}}{\fac{r'}{i}}=\delta_{rr'}$ at every
    site (so $\ncomp\le\min_i\pd[i]$); by default the factors at each site are columns of an
    independent Haar-random orthogonal matrix.
  \item \emph{Pattern teachers}: each site $i$ has a fixed orthonormal basis of $\R^{\pd[i]}$, with
    vectors numbered $0,\dots,d-1$, and component $r$ uses as $\fac{r}{i}$ the basis vector numbered
    $\pi_r(i)$; the \emph{pattern} $\pi_r$ maps each site to a basis index, and we write it as the digit string
    $\pi_r(0)\pi_r(1)\cdots\pi_r(\Ns-1)$; powers mark repeats ($0^31^20^3=00011000$). Components with
    distinct patterns are orthonormal. Two components \emph{differ} on the site set
    $D=\{i:\pi_r(i)\ne\pi_{r'}(i)\}$: $0000$ and $1100$ differ on $D=\{0,1\}$, so the second is a
    \emph{local} modification of the first. We write $V_D$ for the set of cores that carry a site of
    $D$, $m=\abs{V_D}$, and $\mathrm{St}(V_D)$ for their \emph{Steiner subtree}, the smallest subtree
    of $\tree$ that contains them ($V_D$ and every core on a path between two of its cores).
  \item \emph{Random CP}: $\fac{r}{i}=g/\norm{g}$ with independent $g\sim\Normal(0,\Id)$; the
    components are generically non-orthogonal.
\end{itemize}
A \emph{TTN teacher} gives $\Wt$ as a TTN on the student's tree or on another tree; a random TTN
teacher is a fan-in initialization (\cref{sec:setup-init}) with $\isc=1$, rescaled to
$\normF{\Wt}=1$. The overlap of the student with component $r$ of a CP teacher,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:overlap}
  \ov{r}=\ipS{\W}{\U{r}}/\ipS{\Wt}{\U{r}},
\end{equation}
is $0$ at $\W=0$ and $1$ at $\W=\Wt$; for an odeco teacher with whitened inputs,
$\ov{r}=\ip{\W}{\U{r}}/\tw{r}$.

\begin{proposition}[Teacher bond spectra]\label{prop:teacher-spectra}
  \proven{} Let $e$ be an edge whose two sides both carry sites (every edge of the trees of
  \cref{tab:trees}). (a)~For an odeco teacher the bond spectrum of $\Wt$ on $e$ is
  $(\tw{1},\dots,\tw{\ncomp})$, for every tree. (b)~For a two-component pattern teacher whose
  components differ exactly on the site set $D$, the bond rank of $\Wt$ on $e$ is $2$ if $e$ is an
  edge of the Steiner subtree $\mathrm{St}(V_D)$, and $1$ otherwise.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  With $A$ the sites on one side of $e$, $x_r=\bigotimes_{i\in A}\fac{r}{i}$ and
  $y_r=\bigotimes_{i\notin A}\fac{r}{i}$, $\mat{(\Wt)}{e}=\sum_r\tw{r}x_ry_r\T$. (a)~Both families are
  orthonormal, so this is a singular value decomposition. (b)~If $D\subseteq A$ or $D\cap A=\emptyset$,
  then $y_1=y_2$ or $x_1=x_2$: rank one. Otherwise the components differ (by orthogonal basis vectors)
  on both sides, so $x_1\perp x_2$, $y_1\perp y_2$: rank two. And $e$ separates two sites of $D$
  exactly when it lies on the tree path between their nodes.
\end{proof}
Its number of cores, $k=\abs{\mathrm{St}(V_D)}$, is the size of the route along which the second
component is learned (\cref{sec:setup-routes}).

\paragraph{Population loss.}
Training minimizes the population square loss
\begin{equation}\label{eq:loss}
  \Loss(\params)=\tfrac12\,\E_x\big[(f_\params(x)-f^*(x))^2\big]
  =\tfrac12\normS{\W(\params)-\Wt}^2,
  \qquad \normS{A}^2=\ip{A}{\Sig A}.
\end{equation}
The second equality holds because, by independence across sites,
$\E[\Feat\otimes\Feat]=\bigotimes_i\E[\feat{i}\feat{i}\T]=\Sig$. With whitened inputs $\Loss$ is half
the squared Frobenius distance. Only second moments enter: every local feature map with
$\E[\feat{i}\feat{i}\T]=\Id$ gives the same dynamics, and a non-orthonormal one is equivalent to
$\Sigi{i}=\E[\feat{i}\feat{i}\T]\ne\Id$.

\paragraph{Closed form.}
Expanding \eqref{eq:loss},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:loss-expanded}
  \Loss(\params)=\tfrac12\ipS{\W}{\W}-\ipS{\W}{\Wt}+\tfrac12\ipS{\Wt}{\Wt},
\end{equation}
and every term is an exact contraction. Two TTNs on the same tree (bond dimensions may differ) are
contracted from the leaves to the root at a cost linear in $\Kc$; for a CP teacher,
$\ipS{\W}{\U{r}}$ is the student \eqref{eq:messages} evaluated at the site vectors
$\Sigi{i}\fac{r}{i}$; a TTN teacher on another tree is glued to the student along the physical legs
and the loopy network is contracted exactly along an optimized path. For an odeco teacher with
whitened inputs, \eqref{eq:loss-expanded} reads
$\Loss=\tfrac12\normF{\W}^2-\sum_r\tw{r}^2\ov{r}+\tfrac12\sum_r\tw{r}^2$. For orthonormal components
(odeco or pattern teachers) and whitened inputs, the \emph{partial fit} $\W_S=\sum_{r\in S}\tw{r}\U{r}$
of a set $S$ of components has loss
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partial-fit}
  \Loss_S=\tfrac12\sum_{r\notin S}\tw{r}^2,
\end{equation}
the candidate plateau levels; learning in decreasing order of $\tw{r}$ passes through
$S=\emptyset,\{1\},\{1,2\},\dots$.

\paragraph{Anisotropic and correlated inputs.}
Anisotropic product covariances are handled exactly by the same contractions; the default family has
power-law site spectra,
$\Sigi{i}=c_\beta\diag(1^{-\beta},2^{-\beta},\dots,d^{-\beta})$ with $c_\beta$ fixed by
$\tr\Sigi{i}=d$, aligned with the teacher's factors or rotated away from them. Cross-site
correlations break the product structure of $\Sig$; for small $\Ns$ the loss is then computed with
the dense second-moment matrix (\cref{sec:covariance-teacher}).

\paragraph{Finite samples.}
A training set $(x_j,y_j)_{j=1}^{\nsamp}$ has
$x_j\sim\Normal(0,\Sig)$ \iid and $y_j=f^*(x_j)$, plus optional Gaussian label noise of variance
$\varsigma^2$. The empirical loss is
$\Lemp(\params)=\frac{1}{2\nsamp}\sum_{j}(f_\params(x_j)-y_j)^2$, and the test error is the
population loss \eqref{eq:loss}.

\subsection{Initialization and its variants}\label{sec:setup-init}

\paragraph{Fan-in initialization.}
The default initialization is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:init}
  \G{v}=\frac{\iscv{v}}{\sqrt{\fanin{v}}}\,Z_v,\qquad Z_v \text{ with \iid } \Normal(0,1)
  \text{ entries},
\end{equation}
with a single per-core scale $\iscv{v}=\isc$ unless stated otherwise. Each core is then
approximately $\iscv{v}$ times an isometry (when $\fanin{v}\gg\bd{v}$), so with $\iscv{v}=1$ the
messages \eqref{eq:messages} keep their size up the tree.

\begin{proposition}[Initial scales]\label{prop:init-norm}
  \proven{} Under \eqref{eq:init}, $\E\normF{\W(\params_0)}^2=\prod_{v}\iscv{v}^2$ exactly,
  $\E\normF{\G{v}}^2=\iscv{v}^2\bd{v}$, and hence $\E\norm{\params_0}^2=\sum_v\iscv{v}^2\bd{v}$
  (proof in \cref{app:conservation}).
\end{proposition}

We report both $\isc$ and the function-space scale $\fsc=\prod_v\iscv{v}$ ($=\isc^{\Kc}$ with one
scale), the root-mean-square norm of $\W(\params_0)$: small $\isc$ makes $\W(\params_0)$
exponentially small in $\Kc$, while $\norm{\params_0}^2\approx\isc^2\sum_v\bd{v}$ grows with the
bond dimensions.

\paragraph{Variants.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
  \item \emph{Imbalance}: per-core scales $\iscv{v}=\isc c_v$ with a fixed profile $(c_v)$ (for
    example root versus rest), compared at fixed $\prod_v\iscv{v}$.
  \item \emph{Centred model}. The \emph{plain model} \eqref{eq:model} trained on $\Wt$ starts, at
    large $\isc$, from a large random function $f_{\params_0}$. The centred model removes it: the
    target is $\Wt+\W(\params_0)$, that is, $f_\params-f_{\params_0}$ is trained on $f^*$
    \citep[Sec.~3.2]{chizat2019lazy}, so that $\isc$ changes the dynamics but not the target, and
    $\isc$ alone moves the model from small to large initialization. Overlaps and bond spectra refer
    to $\W-\W(\params_0)$. This is the main construction of the regime map (\cref{sec:regime-map}).
  \item \emph{Two-copy model} (\citealp[Sec.~3.1]{chizat2019lazy};
    \citealp[Sec.~4]{woodworth2020kernel}): $\W(\params^+)-\W(\params^-)$ with
    $\params^+(0)=\params^-(0)=\params_0$, zero at initialization for every $\isc$; a cross-check of
    the centred model, trained as one TTN with a gradient mask (\cref{rem:two-copy-mask}).
  \item \emph{Aligned (decoupled) initialization} for CP teachers (\cref{prop:aligned}): cores
    diagonal in the teacher's basis, giving exactly solvable Saxe-type dynamics.
  \item \emph{Per-core learning-rate multipliers} $\lrm{v}$ \eqref{eq:gf}. A fixed
    forward multiplier $c_v$ ($\G{v}=c_v\tilde G_v$, $\tilde G_v$ trained) is equivalent to the
    initial scale $c_v\iscv{v}$ with the multiplier $c_v^2\lrm{v}$; this expresses the NTK and $\mu$P
    parameterizations of wide networks \citep{yang2021tensor}, transposed to large bond dimension
    (\cref{sec:other-regimes}).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{proposition}[Aligned initialization]\label{prop:aligned}
  \proven{} \knownresult[deep linear networks: \citealp{saxe2014exact}] Let $\Wt$ be odeco, inputs
  whitened, $\bd{v}\ge\ncomp$ on every edge, and let every subtree and its complement carry a site
  (true for the trees of \cref{tab:trees}). Initialize
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:aligned-init}
    \G{v}=\sum_{r=1}^{\ncomp}\amp{v}{r}\Big(\bigotimes_{i\in\sites{v}}\fac{r}{i}\Big)\otimes
    \Big(\bigotimes_{u\in\ch{v}}\varepsilon_r\Big)\otimes\varepsilon_{r(v)},
  \end{equation}
  with $\varepsilon_r$ the $r$-th standard basis vector of the bond in question, $r(v)=r$ for
  $v\ne\vroot$ and $r(\vroot)=1$. Under gradient flow with multipliers $\lrm{v}$ the cores keep this
  form, $\W=\sum_r\prodamp{r}\U{r}$ with $\prodamp{r}=\prod_v\amp{v}{r}$, $\ov{r}=\prodamp{r}/\tw{r}$,
  $\Loss=\tfrac12\sum_r(\tw{r}-\prodamp{r})^2$, and
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:aligned-ode}
    \dot q_{v,r}=\lrm{v}\,(\tw{r}-\prodamp{r})\prod_{u\ne v}\amp{u}{r}.
  \end{equation}
  With $\lrm{v}=1$ the differences $\amp{v}{r}^2-\amp{u}{r}^2$ are conserved; a balanced start
  $\amp{v}{r}(0)=q_r(0)$ stays balanced, and $\dot q_r=q_r^{\Kc-1}(\tw{r}-q_r^{\Kc})$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  On the family \eqref{eq:aligned-init}, $\W=\sum_r\prodamp{r}\U{r}$ with orthonormal $\U{r}$. The
  derivative of $\W$ with respect to an entry of $\G{v}$ is a product of physical basis vectors,
  child-subtree columns and the environment column above $v$; on the family these are multiples of
  products of teacher factors, so their inner product with $\W-\Wt=\sum_r(\prodamp{r}-\tw{r})\U{r}$
  vanishes unless all bond indices equal one $r$ ($a=1$ at the root). The gradient thus stays in the
  family, with component $(\prodamp{r}-\tw{r})\prod_{u\ne v}\amp{u}{r}$ along $\amp{v}{r}$; with
  $\lrm{v}=1$, $\ddt\amp{v}{r}^2=2(\tw{r}-\prodamp{r})\prodamp{r}$ for every $v$.
\end{proof}

For $\Kc\ge3$ a balanced mode started at $q_r(0)\ll\tw{r}^{1/\Kc}$ reaches $\ov{r}=\frac12$ at
\begin{equation}\label{eq:aligned-escape}
  t_r=\frac{q_r(0)^{-(\Kc-2)}}{(\Kc-2)\,\tw{r}}+O\big(\tw{r}^{-(2-2/\Kc)}\big),
\end{equation}
and for $\Kc=2$ at $t_r=\log(\tw{r}/q_r(0)^2)/(2\tw{r})+O(q_r(0)^2/\tw{r}^2)$, by direct integration
of the balanced ODE \proven. With $q_r(0)\propto\isc$ the first plateau grows like
$\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}$.

\subsection{Training dynamics}\label{sec:setup-dynamics}

\paragraph{Gradient flow} is the reference dynamics,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gf}
  \dot\params=-\boldsymbol{\lr}\odot\nabla\Loss(\params),
\end{equation}
where the diagonal $\boldsymbol{\lr}$ holds, for each parameter of core $v$, the learning-rate
multiplier $\lrm{v}$ (default $1$), set to zero where a gradient mask applies
(\cref{rem:two-copy-mask}). Here $t$ denotes gradient-flow time. It is integrated by the adaptive
Dormand--Prince 5(4) pair \citep{dormand1980family} with standard step-size control
\citep[Sec.~II.4]{hairer1993solving} (relative and absolute tolerances $10^{-8}$ and $10^{-12}$) and,
past the first escape, where the flow is stiff, mostly by LSODA with the exact
Hessian; steps follow the local time scale, so long plateaus are cheap (choices and tolerances per
experiment: \cref{app:exp-common}). Diagnostics are recorded on a log-spaced time grid plus
\emph{fine rows}, kept whenever $\params$ has moved by $1\%$ of its norm, or $\ln\Loss$ by $0.01$,
since the last kept row, so that every escape is sampled about $20$ times or more. A run stops at
$\tmax$, below a prescribed loss, or at a step or wall-clock limit; together these are its
\emph{budget}. Accuracy is checked with the conservation laws (\cref{sec:setup-conservation}) and the
aligned solutions (\cref{prop:aligned}). All computations are in double precision: with
$\isc=10^{-2}$ and $\Kc=7$, $\normF{\W(\params_0)}\approx10^{-14}$. A dormant component whose escape
signal falls below about $10^{-15}\normF{\W}$ is seeded by roundoff, not by the initialization; such
runs are flagged.

\paragraph{Gradient descent} with learning rate $\lr$ iterates
$\params\leftarrow\params-\lr\,\boldsymbol{\lr}\odot\nabla\Loss(\params)$, and each step advances $t$
by $\lr$. A run has \emph{diverged} when $\Loss$ becomes non-finite or exceeds $10^{6}\Loss(\params_0)$.

\paragraph{Stochastic gradient descent} uses the minibatch loss
$\hat\Loss_{\batch}(\params)=\frac{1}{2\batch}\sum_{b=1}^{\batch}(f_\params(x_b)-y_b)^2$ in the
same update. Online SGD draws fresh inputs $x_b\sim\Normal(0,\Sig)$ with
$y_b=f^*(x_b)$ at every step; with a fixed training set, each epoch uses a new random permutation.
Optional label noise is drawn afresh each step.

\subsection{Symmetries and conservation laws}\label{sec:setup-conservation}

For a bond $e=\edge{v}$ joining core $v$ to its parent $p=\pa{v}$, let $\Gm{u}{e}$ denote core
$u\in\{v,p\}$ matricized with its $e$-leg as rows (a $\bd{v}\times\fanin{v}$ matrix for $u=v$). For
every invertible $\Omega\in\GL(\bd{v})$ the \emph{gauge transformation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gauge}
  \Gm{v}{e}\mapsto \Omega\,\Gm{v}{e},\qquad \Gm{p}{e}\mapsto \Omega^{-\top}\Gm{p}{e}
\end{equation}
leaves $\W$, and hence any loss that depends on $\params$ only through $\W$, unchanged.

\begin{proposition}[Conservation laws]\label{prop:conservation}
  \proven{} \knownresult[deep linear networks: \citealp{arora2018optimization,du2018algorithmic}]
  Under gradient flow $\dot{\G{u}}=-\lrm{u}\nabla_{\G{u}}\Loss$ of any loss that depends on $\params$
  only through $\W$ (population or empirical), the $\bd{v}\times\bd{v}$ matrix
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:conserved}
    \Q{e}=\frac{1}{\lrm{v}}\Gm{v}{e}\Gm{v}{e}\T-\frac{1}{\lrm{p}}\Gm{p}{e}\Gm{p}{e}\T
  \end{equation}
  is constant in time, for every bond $e=\edge{v}$ with $p=\pa{v}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  With $X=\Gm{v}{e}$, $Y=\Gm{p}{e}$ and $\Omega=\Id+\epsilon M$ in \eqref{eq:gauge}, $X\mapsto X+\epsilon MX$
  and $Y\mapsto Y-\epsilon M\T Y+O(\epsilon^2)$; first-order invariance of $\Loss$ for every $M$ gives
  $C:=\nabla_X\Loss\,X\T=Y(\nabla_Y\Loss)\T$, so $\ddt(XX\T)/\lrm{v}=-(C+C\T)=\ddt(YY\T)/\lrm{p}$.
\end{proof}

This is the tree version of the balancedness of deep linear networks, an instance of
symmetry-induced conservation laws \citep{kunin2021neural}. Consequences:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Norm balance}: $\tr\Q{e}=\normF{\G{v}}^2/\lrm{v}-\normF{\G{p}}^2/\lrm{p}$; with equal
    multipliers all differences $\normF{\G{u}}^2-\normF{\G{u'}}^2$ are conserved, so all cores grow
    together.
  \item \emph{Balancedness at small initialization}: under \eqref{eq:init},
    $\E\,\Q{e}(0)=\big(\iscv{v}^2/\lrm{v}-\iscv{p}^2\bd{p}/(\lrm{p}\bd{v})\big)\Id$, so at small $\isc$
    the cores stay balanced up to $O(\isc^2)$ while they grow to $O(1)$. Imbalanced initializations
    or multipliers set $\Q{e}$ to other values, a second knob for the regime map
    \citep{kunin2024get}.
  \item The masked two-copy flow conserves the $\Q{e}$ of each copy; gradient descent and SGD conserve
    $\Q{e}$ only up to $O(\lr^2)$ per step.
  \item \emph{Integrator check}: under gradient flow the drift \eqref{eq:drift} of $\Q{e}$
    (\cref{app:exp-common}) measures the integration error.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Saddles, routes and saddle order}\label{sec:setup-routes}

The terms below are used from \cref{sec:intro} on; \cref{sec:s2s} states the results and
\cref{app:saddles,app:saddle-order} derive them.

\paragraph{Learned saddles and routes.}
From small initialization gradient flow rests, between escapes, near a \emph{learned saddle}
$\params_S$: a critical point that represents a partial fit $\W_S$ (for orthonormal teachers the sum
of the learned components, \eqref{eq:partial-fit}; in general a best approximation of $\Wt$ with the
current bond ranks). Each bond uses only its first $\rank\mat{(\W_S)}{e}$ (\emph{learned}) channels;
the others are zero. A new channel opened in one core meets zeros in its neighbours and leaves $\W$
unchanged: new channels move $\W$ only when every core of a connected set $\mathcal C$ opens one together, and
then by a product of $\abs{\mathcal C}$ small entries, a small TTN on $\mathcal C$ attached to the learned network
(\cref{lem:A-routes}). Such a set is a \emph{route}. It lowers the loss at order $\abs{\mathcal C}$ in the new
entries, while the quadratic (Gauss--Newton) term $\frac12\normS{\Delta\W}^2$ is of order $2\abs{\mathcal C}$,
so near the saddle it behaves like a fresh $\abs{\mathcal C}$-core TTN started near zero
(\cref{prop:A-escape-degree}). The \emph{ends} of a route are its leaves as a subtree; the
\emph{slice} of an end is that core's part on the route's new channel (for a leaf core, one column).
The learned channels can also rotate; when such moves descend faster than the cheapest route is
decided in \cref{app:saddle-order}.

\paragraph{Route size $k$ and $k^*$.}
For an orthonormal pattern teacher with one learned component, the route that adds component $r$ is
the Steiner subtree $\mathrm{St}(V_D)$ of the cores that carry the sites $D$ where $r$ differs from the
learned component, and the bonds whose rank must grow are exactly its edges
(\cref{prop:teacher-spectra,prop:A-steiner}) \proven. Its size $k=\abs{\mathrm{St}(V_D)}$ is the number of
cores that must move together, and $k^*$ is the smallest $k$ among the components not yet learned (in
general, the smallest route with a nonzero local residual, \cref{prop:A-escape-degree}). If $D$ lies on
one core ($k=1$), that core fits the component through its learned channel and the flow does not stop.
Otherwise, when the saddle order is $k^*$ (below), the next plateau follows the first-escape law
with $\Kc$ replaced by $k^*$: it lasts $\propto\ln(1/\isc)$ for $k^*=2$ and $\propto\isc^{-(k^*-2)}$ for
$k^*\ge3$ \derived{} (\cref{sec:s2s-locality}). Example: $0000$ and $0110$ differ on sites $1$ and $2$; on MPS4 these sit
on adjacent cores ($k=2$), on binary4 on the two bottom cores, joined through the root ($k=3$).

\paragraph{Saddle order.}
The \emph{order} of a saddle is the power of the distance at which the loss first decreases: the
largest exponent $\operatorname{ord}$ with
$\Loss(\params)-\Loss_S\ge-C\norm{\params-\params_S}^{\operatorname{ord}}$ near $\params_S$
(\cref{app:saddle-order}). A \emph{strict} saddle has order $2$ (a negative Hessian eigenvalue); at a
\emph{higher-order} saddle (order at least $3$) every descent direction is flat to second order. A
route of $k^*$ cores descends at order $k^*$, so the order is at most $k^*$ \proven{}
(\cref{prop:A-steiner}); when it equals $k^*$ is stated in \cref{sec:s2s} and proven in
\cref{app:saddle-order}.

\subsection{Regime diagnostics}\label{sec:setup-diagnostics}

Regimes are assigned from measured quantities, never by eye; \cref{app:experiments} gives the
settings of each experiment.

\paragraph{Recorded quantities.}
Along every run we record the loss, the bond spectra of $\W$, the overlaps, the relative parameter
change $\dth=\norm{\params(t)-\params(0)}/\norm{\params(0)}$ (also per core), the relative kernel change
$\dTK$ and the conservation drift \eqref{eq:drift}, and at plateaus the Hessian $\Hess$ and its gauge
projection $\Hperp$ (\cref{tab:notation}). The \emph{effective bond rank} $\rk{e}$ counts
the $\bs{e}{j}$ above $0.1$ times the smallest nonzero teacher singular value on $e$. The tangent kernel
$\TK=\Jac\,\diag(\boldsymbol{\lr})\,\Jac\T$, $\Jac=\partial\W/\partial\params$, is the operator on
$\R^{d^\Ns}$ that drives the function-space dynamics of \eqref{eq:gf}, and
$\dTK=\normF{\TK_t-\TK_0}/\normF{\TK_0}$; the \emph{probe kernel} restricts $\TK$ to 128 fixed inputs, and
its change is written $\dTK^{\rm probe}$ (where each kernel is used: \cref{app:exp-common}).

\paragraph{Learning events.}
For CP teachers with orthogonal components (in the metric $\Sig$), \emph{event} $r$ is the time
$\tev{r}$ at which the overlap $\ov{r}$ rises through $\frac12$, and its \emph{width} $\wev{r}$ is the
duration of that final rise, from $\ov{r}=0.1$ to $0.9$. Experiments date the crossing by slightly
different rules: the last upward crossing before $\ov{r}$ first reaches $0.9$ (recommended), the last
upward crossing after which $\ov{r}$ stays above $\frac12$, or the first crossing. The rules agree
unless a learned component later dips below $\frac12$ (star teachers, gradient noise, rotations to a
better component; the second rule then dates the relearning, or gives none) or a transient crosses $\frac12$ while
another component is learned (the third rule then dates the transient). For other teachers one escape
can raise several overlaps, and events are read from the bond spectra instead (for example the onset,
when $\bs{e}{j}$ first reaches $0.05$ times the teacher's value); on real data from the loss rate.
\Cref{tab:exp-common} gives each experiment's rule. Crossings are interpolated on the recorded rows or
found by root finding on the integrator's dense output; an event narrower than three sample intervals
is \emph{unresolved}, and separation ratios involving it are lower bounds.

\paragraph{Plateaus and the saddle-to-saddle signature.}
Number the events in time order, $\tev{1}<\tev{2}<\cdots$, with widths $\wev{i}$; the
\emph{separation ratios} are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:separation}
  \sep{i}=\frac{\tev{i+1}-\tev{i}}{\max(\wev{i},\wev{i+1})}.
\end{equation}
Between two events with $\sep{i}>3$ the loss sits on a plateau; its midpoint $\tmid{i}$ is the log
time of minimal speed $\norm{\dot\params}$ between them. A run shows the \emph{saddle-to-saddle (S2S)
signature} when (i)~there are at least two events, (ii)~every $\sep{i}>3$, and (iii)~across a sweep,
plateau durations grow without bound as $\isc\to0$ (like $\log(1/\isc)$ or a power of $1/\isc$).
Plateaus are judged in linear time: an escape that needs two cores takes a time of order
$\log(1/\isc)$, invisible on a log-time axis after a long first plateau.

\paragraph{Regime assignment.}
Each run, trained up to time $T$, receives one label (\cref{tab:regimes}).

\begin{remark}[Reading of the rules]\label{rem:regime-rules}
  ``Converges'' means $\Loss(T)<10^{-3}\Loss(0)$; labels are checked in the order diverged,
  stalled, then the converged regimes. Condition (iii) belongs to a sweep: a run is labelled S2S on
  (i)--(ii), a region of a phase diagram only if (iii) holds there. A converged run with
  $\dTK(T)\ge0.1$ and a single (merged) event is rich, non-incremental. The regime map measures
  convergence against $\frac12\normS{\Wt}^2$, which equals $\Loss(0)$ when the fitted tensor vanishes
  at $t=0$ (centred and two-copy models).
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Stalled runs.}
\emph{Stalled} is an umbrella label (\cref{tab:regimes}); its four causes fall into three
subclasses. \emph{Dead route} (trap): an end of the route that the next component needs has its slice
at roundoff. The route's drive is a product over its cores, so it cannot escape, and the run stays at
a partial fit. The mechanism is the conservation-law trap (\cref{prop:A-trap}; \derived{} in a reduced
model): on a long plateau a conserved quantity of each route end decides whether its slice decays, at a
rate $\propto\isc^2$ ($\propto\isc^4$ at HT and binary leaves; tests and known gaps: \cref{sec:s2s-failure}). \emph{Slow}: every route is live,
but the budget ends first, on a plateau longer than the time budget (a route of $k$ cores needs a time
$\propto\isc^{-(k-2)}$) or at the step limit (unfinished lazy fits at full bonds, some deep-tree runs).
\emph{Floor}: in the centred model with a bond below its cut dimension, the target $\Wt+\W(\params_0)$
is not representable and the loss stops at the best fit with the available bond ranks, above the
threshold at large $\isc$ (\cref{sec:other-lazy}); only this cause is specific to the centred model.
Experiments test the subclass in their own ways (a route-end slice or a bond singular value at
roundoff, a gradient below a threshold, a continued run; \cref{app:experiments}); runs whose subclass
is not determined are reported as stalled.

\paragraph{Saddle type at a plateau.}
At the plateau midpoint we compute the Hessian $\Hess$ and its restriction $\Hperp$ to the
orthogonal complement of the well-conditioned gauge directions, which are the tangent vectors
$(M\Gm{v}{e},-M\T\Gm{p}{e})$ of the orbits \eqref{eq:gauge} (cutoff: \cref{app:exp-common}); the raw
$\lambda_{\min}(\Hess)$ is reported too. The type is read across a sweep from how $\lambda_{\min}(\Hperp)$ at the midpoint scales with
$\isc$ (\cref{prop:A-signatures}): it tends to a negative constant at a strict saddle and vanishes like
$\isc^{k-2}$ at a higher-order saddle reached through a route of $k\ge3$ cores (the origin: $k=\Kc$).
A fixed threshold at one $\isc$, strict if $\lambda_{\min}(\Hperp)<-10^{-3}\max_j\abs{\lambda_j(\Hperp)}$,
cannot separate the two: it calls $k=3$ plateaus strict for $\isc\gtrsim0.01$--$0.04$.

\paragraph{Reporting rules.}
Scaling fits use at least five initialization scales over at least one decade and at least five
seeds per point, and report mean $\pm$ standard error, a fitted exponent with its standard error,
and the theoretical prediction. Fits that fall short of this rule are reported but marked
\emph{limited range}, with their range of $\isc$ and their seed count. Every regime boundary comes
with the threshold sensitivity check of \cref{tab:regimes}, and every number states the dynamics that
produced it (integrator tolerances, step size, batch size).

\subsection{What the results may depend on}\label{sec:setup-scope}

Plateaus and saddle sequences are properties of population gradient flow; implicit bias and
generalization need finite samples. The features are multilinear, with no pointwise nonlinearity, and
the population loss is a tree contraction only for a product input measure, which cross-site
correlations break. Binary trees and HT differ in $\Kc$ and in how many cores must change to learn a
component, which sets plateau-time exponents (\cref{sec:s2s-locality}). Fan-in initialization fixes
the relative scales of the cores; exponents should not depend on it, but prefactors and the learning
order might, so imbalanced, balanced ($\Q{e}=0$) and aligned initializations are also studied.
