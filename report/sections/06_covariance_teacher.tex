\section{Input covariance and teacher structure}\label{sec:covariance-teacher}

\Cref{sec:s2s,sec:other-regimes} use whitened inputs and orthogonally decomposable or pattern teachers.
Here one complication is added at a time (covariance, correlations between sites, overlapping
components, random TTN teachers, components that share a core, and finally real images), and we ask
whether the saddle-to-saddle picture changes, and whether the baseline theory predicts the change after
a substitution. An aligned covariance only reweights the components (\cref{sec:cov-aligned}). Each of
the four ways we tried of making the components non-orthogonal in the metric of the loss (overlapping CP
components, a misaligned covariance, random TTN teachers, correlated sites) changes the law of the escape
from the rank-one saddle ($\Ns=4$, $8$; correlated sites only at $\Ns=4$ and not on HT), and the tree decides
how: strict and logarithmic on an MPS, of higher order on binary and HT trees, with escapes
$\propto\varepsilon^{-1}$ from the saddle and, on binary4, $\propto\isc^{-1}$ from random initialization. This is the central result
(\cref{sec:teacher-generic,sec:cov-misaligned}). Rank-one plateaus sit at critical points of the
bond-rank-constrained loss, blends rather than partial sums of components (on binary4 and MPS4; under
correlated inputs only on binary4, under misalignment up to $30^\circ$; \cref{sec:cov-misaligned,sec:teacher-ttn});
binary students learn bottom-up
(\cref{sec:teacher-mismatch}); two
smaller components that share a core can destabilize a learned larger one (\cref{sec:teacher-star}); and on
MNIST the same tree contrast appears with the data in place of the teacher (\cref{sec:real-data}).
Per-teacher tables and secondary checks are collected in \cref{app:supp-cov}.

\paragraph{Setup} (details in \cref{app:exp-data-teacher}).
Gradient flow on the exact population loss, fan-in initialization, $\chi=4$ unless stated. The
\emph{generic teachers} have $d=2$: (a)~CP teachers with $\fac1i=e_1$ and $\fac2i=\cos\phi\,e_1+\sin\phi\,e_2$
at every site (overlap $\cos\phi$, weights $(1,0.7)$); (b)~an odeco teacher (weights $(1,0.8)$ on $e_1$, $e_2$)
under a misaligned covariance, the power-law spectrum of \cref{sec:setup-loss} rotated by an angle in the
$(e_1,e_2)$ plane; (c)~random binary-tree and MPS teachers with bond ranks 2. Students are MPS, binary and
HT trees with $\Ns=4$ and $8$. Two protocols: random initialization at scale $\isc$, or the \emph{saddle
start} of \cref{sec:s2s-locality}, the exact balanced bond-rank-one critical point of lowest loss,
zero-padded, plus fan-in noise of scale $\varepsilon$. An escape is dated by an \emph{onset event}, the first
time the second singular value of some edge reaches $5\%$ of the teacher's; that edge is the
\emph{realized edge}. Laws are fitted per seed; \emph{asymptotic} fits use the three smallest $\isc$ or the
five smallest $\varepsilon$; $\pm$ is the standard error over 4--10 seeds, statistical only, so agreements are
cited at the $1$--$2\%$ level.

\subsection{Covariance aligned with the teacher: a reweighting}\label{sec:cov-aligned}

\paragraph{Prediction.}
Let the teacher be odeco with factors that are covariance eigenvectors, and let
$\Lambda_r=\prod_i{\fac ri}\T\Sigi i\fac ri$ be the product of the covariance eigenvalues of component $r$. Near
the origin the flow sees only the escape tensor $\Sig\Wt=\sum_r\Lambda_r\tw r\U r$, so the \emph{race} (which
component escapes first, and when) is that of the whitened problem with weights $\Lambda_r\tw r$; the
\emph{landscape} (critical points, plateau levels $\frac12\sum_{r\notin S}\Lambda_r\tw r^2$, saddle types) is that
of $\Sig^{1/2}\Wt$, with weights $\Lambda_r^{1/2}\tw r$ \derived{} (\cref{prop:A-escape-cov,prop:A-precond}). From an
aligned initialization each component follows its whitened trajectory on the clock $\Lambda_rt$ \proven{}
\knownresult[Saxe-type modes: \citealp{saxe2014exact}; depth two: \citealp{gidel2019implicit}; new: every
tree] (\cref{prop:A-aligned-cov}). When the two orderings disagree, a learned component $j$ of binary4 is
unstable toward $r$ (a rotation through a strict saddle) iff $\Lambda_r\tw r^2>\Lambda_j\tw j^2$; on MPS4 the
rotation needs all four cores \proven{} \newresult[for TTNs; on binary4, a deep linear network, it follows
from \citealp{achour2024loss}, Thm~7, with \cref{prop:A-precond}] (\cref{prop:A-piggyback-cov}).

\paragraph{Experiments.}
\empirical{} On binary4 and MPS4 ($d=\chi=4$, two teachers with the smaller component on the high-variance
direction, 40 seeds; \cref{app:supp-cov}) the aligned clocks hold to $10^{-9}$; from random initialization the
first component and first-escape time are those of the whitened race with weights $\Lambda\tw{}$ ($95$--$100\%$ of
paired runs, times within $1.3\%$), so the covariance can reverse the order, while a control with the same
landscape but the whitened flow differs in $10$--$53\%$ of the seeds: the race is set by the flow metric.
Exponents are unchanged; a learned component is replaced only on binary4 and only if the other has the
larger $\Lambda\tw{}^2$; plateau levels follow $\frac12\sum_{r\notin S}\Lambda_r\tw r^2$. The change is quantitative only
and is predicted after two different substitutions \newresult[for TTNs; in
two-layer linear networks the split is implicit in \citealp{gidel2019implicit}, Thm~2, whose Corollary~2
states no ordering condition; related: escape along $\Sig\Wt$ with a limited loss decrease,
\citealp{rawal2026theory}, Thm~4.2 and Prop.~4.5].

\subsection{Generic teachers: the tree decides the saddle type}\label{sec:teacher-generic}
% Sources: verification "safe to cite" rows 1-6, errata E1, E2, E12-E17; notes/data_teacher.md R2.1;
% r2_saddle_fits.csv, r2_saddle_*hessian.csv, r2_random_v2_summary.csv, report_first_escape.csv.

\paragraph{Prediction.}
\derived{} \newresult{} (\cref{app:teachers}, ``Generic teachers''; \cref{prop:A-mps-rates,cor:A-second-escape})
After the first escape the flow sits near a balanced bond-rank-one critical point $\W_1$, a local best
rank-one approximation of $\Wt$ with value $\bar s$. A bond can gain rank through a two-core route if both of
its cores have other legs of dimension above its rank. For odeco and pattern teachers the route's
residual vanishes by orthogonality, which gives the $\isc^{-(k-2)}$ laws of \cref{sec:s2s-locality}; for a
generic teacher it does not. On an MPS every core carries a site, so $\W_1$ is a strict saddle. Let $M_e$
be the $d\times d$ matrix that $\Wt$ leaves on the two sites of the cores of edge $e$ when the other sites
are contracted with the factors of $\W_1$, with singular values $s_n(M_e)$ (indexed by $n$, as in \cref{app:derivations}). Edge $e$ contributes the Hessian
eigenvalues $-\bar s^{(\Ns-2)/\Ns}s_n(M_e)$, $n\ge2$, each $\bd e-1$ times, in all $\sum_e(d-1)(\bd e-1)$ negative
ones, and it escapes at the rate $\lambda_e=\bar s^{(\Ns-2)/\Ns}s_2(M_e)$, the analogue of $\lambda_2$ in
\eqref{eq:locality-rule}: from the saddle start in a time $\lambda_e^{-1}\ln(1/\varepsilon)+O(1)$, and from random
initialization with the slope $h_e=(1-s_2(M_e)/\bar s)/\lambda_e$ in $\ln(1/\isc)$, where $s_2(M_e)/\bar s$ is the head
start. On binary and HT trees the internal cores carry no site, so the cheapest route has $k=3$: no
negative eigenvalue and an escape $\propto\varepsilon^{-1}$ (or $\isc^{-1}$). The landscape bound of
\citet[Lemmas~D.7--D.9]{furman2026benign} allows descent of order $\Kc$ at a rank-one point of an MPS; for
generic teachers it is $2$.

\paragraph{Experiments.}
Thirteen generic teachers (\cref{tab:dt-saddle}), each on the MPS, binary and HT student with the same
$\Ns$; five seeds; saddle start with $\varepsilon$ from $10^{-8}$ (MPS) or $10^{-5}$ (binary, HT) to $5\cdot10^{-2}$, and
random initialization for the six teachers with $\Ns=4$ on MPS4 ($\isc=3\cdot10^{-4}$--$0.3$) and binary4
($10^{-4}$--$0.1$).
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Saddle type.} \derived{} and computed, \newresult: all 13 MPS students are strict, with 5
    (MPS4) and 17 (MPS8) negative eigenvalues, the count above; none of the 26 binary and HT students has
    an eigenvalue below $-10^{-8}$.
  \item \emph{Escape from the saddle.} \empirical{} \newresult{} (\cref{fig:dt-contrast}a,b) On MPS the log
    law wins in 65 of 65 seeds, and the slope equals $1/\lambda_e$ of the realized edge within $0.4\%$ for all
    13 teachers. On binary and HT students $t_{\rm esc}\propto\varepsilon^{-1}$, with fitted
    exponents $0.9998$--$1.0005$ in 23 of the 24 cases ($1.0036\pm0.0006$ in the last, over a wider window),
    and the log law wins in no seed.
  \item \emph{Random initialization, $\Ns=4$.} \empirical{} \newresult{} (\cref{fig:dt-contrast}c,d) On MPS4
    the log law holds, with slopes within $1.3\%$ of $h_e$ for the five teachers with a prediction; the head
    start matters (for one teacher $1.038\pm0.005$ against $h_e=1.051$, and $2.78$ without the head start).
    Binary4 gives $\isc^{-1}$ (fitted exponents $1.001$--$1.005$). The curvature at the plateau does not depend
    on $\isc$ on MPS4 and falls $\propto\isc$ on binary4, and the first escape keeps its $\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}$ law
    (per-teacher values in \cref{app:supp-cov}).
\end{itemize}
One teacher (misaligned covariance, $\Ns=8$, $20^\circ$) first escapes to an intermediate saddle whose second
singular values stay below the onset threshold; timed at that level, its MPS slope matches $1/\lambda_e$ to
$0.24\%$ (\cref{app:supp-cov}). \emph{Scope:} the escape from the rank-one saddle (the first rank increase),
$\Ns=4$ and $8$, $d=2$; random initialization for $\Ns=4$ only. Later saddles on binary and HT students were
not timed; there two-core routes can open on bottom edges at higher ranks, never for the root bipartition
(\cref{app:teachers}). Correlated inputs were never run on HT (\cref{sec:cov-correlated}).

\paragraph{Overlapping CP components.}\label{sec:teacher-cp}
The rank-one critical points are those of $g(x)=\ip{\bigotimes_ix_i}{\Wt}$ on unit rank-one tensors \proven. With
two components of equal weight and overlap $\cos\phi$ at every site, an MPS has two rank-one maxima (one near
each component) iff $\cos\phi<(\Ns-2)/\Ns$ and one blended maximum above \proven{} (among symmetric points);
unequal weights move the merger to a fold \derived{} \newresult[elementary] (\cref{prop:A-two-components}); on
binary4 the rank-one point is the top singular pair of the $01|23$ matricization (\cref{rem:A-binary4-dln}), a
blend for every $\cos\phi>0$. The plateau is never a component. \empirical{} With $d=\chi=4$ and overlaps up
to $0.8$, the measured rank-one minima are as predicted (two on MPS4 below the merger, one on binary4), and
the first plateaus sit at these blends (\cref{app:supp-cov}).

\paragraph{Misaligned covariance.}
Under a product covariance the critical points, critical values and saddle types are those of the
whitened problem with teacher $\Sig^{1/2}\Wt$; escape rates are not, because the flow is preconditioned
\proven{} \newresult[for TTNs] (\cref{prop:A-precond}). With misaligned factors $\Sig^{1/2}\Wt$ is a
non-orthogonal CP teacher, with factors $\propto\Sigi i^{1/2}\fac ri$, while the escape is driven by $\Sig\Wt$,
with factors $\propto\Sigi i\fac ri$: the state must rotate after the escape, and misalignment alone can
reverse the order \derived{} \newresult{} (\cref{app:covariance}). \empirical{} With $d=\chi=4$ and weights $(1,0.8)$,
the first plateau is the predicted rank-one critical point of the $\Sig$-loss for angles up to $30^\circ$ (median
relative level errors $10^{-6}$--$10^{-3}$; at $45^\circ$ most MPS4 runs have no resolved plateau). Binary4 runs
first align with the best rank-one approximation of $\Sig\Wt$ and then turn to the saddle ($75$--$100\%$ of the
runs from $10^\circ$ on) \knownresult[in linear networks, binary4 being one: \citealp{atanasov2022neural}]; MPS4
runs do so only at $45^\circ$.

\paragraph{Correlated sites.}\label{sec:cov-correlated}
In the one-factor model $\xs i=(1-c)^{1/2}z_i+c^{1/2}z$ (independent standard Gaussians $z$ and $z_i$, as in
\cref{app:covariance}) every site is whitened and two sites have covariance $c\Id$. The second-moment matrix
is not a product, so no whitening substitution exists; the loss is computed with the dense matrix. The
residual acquires \emph{pairing} tensors such as $(\xs0\cdot\xs1)\,\xs2_r\xs3_r$, of bond rank one on binary4 but
$d$ on MPS4 \derived. On MPS4 the escape from the product saddle passes through binary4's first plateau
point, which is strict on MPS4 but of higher order on binary4, so the second plateau grows by a fixed
amount per halving of $\isc$, set by the rates at the two saddles and the head start (the two-stage law of
\cref{app:covariance}) \derived{} \newresult; binary4 stays at $\isc^{-1}$. \empirical{} On binary4 ($d=\chi=4$,
weights $(1,0.7)$, $\isc=0.05$) the first plateau falls from $0.33\,\Loss(0)$ at $c=0$ to $0.10\,\Loss(0)$ at $c=0.5$,
always at the predicted critical value: a blend with pairing terms, no longer a partial fit (the MPS4
rank-one point keeps the pure component). The MPS4 plateaus separate only logarithmically: at $c=0.4$ the
separation ratio grows from $2.6$ to $11$ between $\isc=0.05$ and $0.0016$, against $19$ to $185$ on binary4.
HT students and $\Ns>4$ were not run with correlated inputs.

\paragraph{The second escape on MPS4.}
\empirical{} \newresult[overlap and misalignment; the correlated-site law and its first check are in
\cref{app:covariance}] With any of the three, the second escape of
MPS4 from random initialization follows the log law in every seed, with the predicted slope where there is a
prediction (at overlap $0.05$ $4\%$ above it, not yet asymptotic; \cref{tab:cov-slopes,fig:dt-mps-laws}), and
the plateau curvature tends to its exact-saddle value as $\isc$ decreases; the orthogonal baselines give
$\isc^{-2.00}$. Under misalignment the slope is only bounded, by $1/\abs{\lambda_{\min}}$ at the plateau, since
the head start under the preconditioned flow was not derived. Binary4 keeps $\isc^{-1}$: with
correlated inputs (fitted exponents $1.005$--$1.011$) and, in \cref{sec:teacher-generic}, for overlaps $0.2$ and
$0.45$ and a $20^\circ$ misalignment.

\subsection{Random TTN teachers: plateaus are rank-constrained critical points}\label{sec:teacher-ttn}

With bond ranks fixed, the critical points are the fixed points of one-site alternating least squares
\proven{} \knownresult[in substance: \citealp{elden2011perturbation,holtz2012alternating}]
(\cref{prop:A-critical-points}); the hierarchical SVD is generically not one \proven{} (\cref{prop:A-hsvd}). The
rank-one plateau should approach a local best rank-one approximation as a power of $\isc$ (\conjecture: a
heuristic crossing argument gives the exponent), and an MPS then gains rank bond by bond, first on the edge
$e^*$ with the largest $s_2(M_e)$, with slope $h_{e^*}$ \derived. Experiments \empirical{} \newresult{} (MPS4
student, $d=3$, $\chi=4$; five random MPS4 teachers with bond ranks $(2,3,2)$; eight $\isc$ from $10^{-3}$ to
$0.2$; six seeds): the rank-one plateau converges to the bond-rank-one critical point as a power of $\isc$
(exponents $0.07$--$0.15$ above the heuristic; \cref{app:supp-cov}); for three teachers that point is separated
from every SVD-type truncation, while for the other two, and at the higher-rank plateaus, it lies within
$0.0032$ of one, so the plateaus do not discriminate. Ranks then grow one bond at a time, e.g.\
$(1,1,1)\to(2,1,1)\to(2,2,1)\to(2,3,1)\to(2,3,2)$; the first increase follows the log law in 26 of 26 seeds, with
single-route slopes within $0.3$--$1.6\%$ of $h_{e^*}$, always on $e^*$ at $\isc=10^{-3}$ (later increases were not
timed). In CP factorization small initialization follows a rank-one trajectory
\citep{razin2021implicit}, and gradient descent on tensor-train completion shows a plateau followed by
abrupt rank growth \citep{milanesi2021implicit}.

\subsection{Teacher and student on different trees}\label{sec:teacher-mismatch}

A student bond must reach the rank of the teacher's matricization across it, bounded by products of
bond dimensions over cuts of the teacher's tree \proven{} \knownresult[standard min-cut bound; cf.\
\citealp{grasedyck2011introduction}, Lemmas~7--8] (\cref{app:teachers}): a binary8 student of an MPS8 teacher
with bond ranks 2 and $d=2$ needs rank 4 on the bonds above the bottom cores of sites 2, 3 and of sites
4, 5, whose cuts cross two teacher bonds. The routes give \emph{bottom-up order} \derived{} \newresult: the root
bipartition $0$--$3|4$--$7$ (the teacher's middle bond) has a three-core route only after both halves have
bond rank above one below it, so it grows only after a bottom bond on each half, whatever its teacher
singular value. Experiments \empirical{} \newresult{} (binary8 student, $\chi=4$, twelve random MPS8 teachers
with bond ranks 2, $\isc=0.3$, $0.4$, $0.5$ (two teachers at $0.4$ only), eight seeds): in the 7 teachers whose
root cut has a larger teacher
singular value than both bottom bonds of a half, so that a weight order disagrees, bottom-up order holds
in 163 of 168 runs (56 of 56 at $\isc=0.3$), including 117 of 120 for five teachers whose root cut is the
strongest of all by $5.5$--$13\%$; the 5 exceptions grow the root after a bottom bond on one half only. Over
all 12 teachers: 249 of 256. ``Largest teacher singular value first'' fails, and ``a sibling pair first''
is no rule (8--100\% of the runs by teacher). Scope: $\Ns=8$, $\isc=0.3$--$0.5$; the reverse mismatch, an MPS8
student of binary8 teachers, was run only at a $\isc$ too large to test the asymptotic race
(\cref{app:supp-cov}).

\subsection{Star teachers: forgetting a learned component}\label{sec:teacher-star}

A learned component $j$ without a two-core route to the rest is a strict saddle iff $\lambda_{\max}(\Gamma)>\tw j$,
$\Gamma$ the weighted adjacency matrix of the piggyback graph, and then
$\lambda_{\min}=-\tw j^{(\Kc-2)/\Kc}(\lambda_{\max}(\Gamma)-\tw j)$ \proven{} \newresult[for general trees; deep linear
networks: \citealp{achour2024loss}, Thm~7; related: active units can become dormant,
\citealp{pesme2023saddletosaddle}, and \citealp{kunin2025alternating}, Lemma~B.1]
(\cref{prop:A-piggyback-graph}). The \emph{star} has $q=0000$ ($\tw q=2$)
and $r=1010$, $r'=1001$ (weights $\tw r=\tw{r'}$, $d=2$), which share the piggyback node: $\lambda_{\max}(\Gamma)=\sqrt2\,\tw r$,
so ``$q$ learned'' ($\params_q$) is strict iff $\tw r>\sqrt2$, although both residual components are smaller and
each needs three or more cores. Experiments ($\chi=3$; MPS4, binary4, HT4; saddle start at $\params_q$ with
$\varepsilon=10^{-6}$--$10^{-2}$, five seeds; random initialization at $\tw r=1.2$, $1.6$, ten seeds):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Hessian} \proven{} and computed: the formula holds to $10^{-15}$ on MPS4, binary4, HT4 and a
    binary8 analogue; there is no negative eigenvalue for $\tw r\le1.40$.
  \item \emph{Threshold} \empirical{} \newresult[dynamics]: started at $\params_q$ with $\varepsilon=10^{-6}$, no seed leaves
    it at $\tw r=1.4138$ ($\lambda_{\max}(\Gamma)/\tw q=0.9997$) and all do at $1.4146$ ($1.0003$; three seeds each) on
    MPS4 and binary4; on HT4 the step lies between $1.40$ and $1.42$ for $\varepsilon\le10^{-3}$. At $\varepsilon=10^{-2}$ it
    moves to $1.43$--$1.44$ on MPS4 and binary4, where the polynomial route that adds $r$ beats the slow strict
    escape.
  \item \emph{One or two strict stages} \derived{} (from the computed Hessians) and \empirical{} \newresult:
    where one strict stage is timed ($\tw r\ge1.48$; every $\tw r$ on binary4) the slope of the escape time
    against $\ln(1/\varepsilon)$ is $1/\lambda_q$ within $0.21\%$, with $\lambda_q=-\lambda_{\min}$ at $\params_q$. Near the threshold
    on MPS4 the flow stops at a blended rank-one point that is itself strict, and the two times add (within
    $0.2\%$). On HT4 that point has no negative eigenvalue and is left in a time $\propto\varepsilon^{-1}$: a star
    forgets $q$ in logarithmic time on MPS4 and binary4 but polynomially on HT4, the tree contrast of
    \cref{sec:teacher-generic} again.
  \item \emph{Random initialization} \empirical{} \newresult{} ($\tw r=1.6$): the flow never sits at $\params_q$
    (at most $0.13$ time units at its loss level, one seed $1.7$). The initialization direction sets the
    class, but not the outcome: $q$ learned last; $\ov q$ rising to $0.78$--$1.00$ and then, in most runs,
    decaying (on MPS4 one of these seeds keeps $q$ for $\isc\ge0.025$, and three more do at $\isc=0.1$); or a
    blended start.
    Where $q$ decays, the decay deepens steeply as $\isc$ decreases, not as a power law, and $q$ is relearned
    last. Sub-critical stars ($\tw r=1.2$) keep $q$ ($\min\ov q\ge0.991$). Per-class counts and the near-threshold
    values are in \cref{app:supp-cov}.
\end{itemize}

Do the teacher--student results survive real inputs, with a non-product input law and no teacher? The
empirical loss is $\Loss=\frac12\ip{\W}{\Sig\W}-\ip{Y}{\W}+\mathrm{const}$, with the second-moment operator
$\Sig=\E[\Feat\otimes\Feat]$ of the features, which is not a product, and the label tensor $Y=\E[y\Feat]$ (averages over the training set). Two
predictions need no product law \derived. Near the origin $\Loss\approx\Loss(0)-\ip{Y}{\W}$, so the first
escape is that of \cref{sec:s2s-escape} with $Y$ in place of $\Wt$ (\cref{prop:A-escape-cov}). At a bond-rank-one
critical point the routes that can open depend only on bond ranks and site dimensions (\cref{app:teachers},
``Generic teachers''): on a binary tree, whose internal cores carry no pixel, the cheapest routes join
two sibling bottom cores ($k=3$: a higher-order saddle, a plateau $\propto\isc^{-1}$); on an MPS any adjacent
pair of cores can open one ($k=2$: strict, $\propto\ln(1/\isc)$). These are the generic-teacher predictions
of \cref{sec:teacher-generic}, with $Y$ for $\Wt$ in the first escape; the route counting at the rank-one
point does not depend on the target. The data decide which of the cheapest routes goes first,
and the assignment of pixels to sites decides which pixel interactions lie on short routes.

\paragraph{Setup} (details in \cref{app:exp-real-data}).
MNIST 3 ($y=+1$) against 5 ($y=-1$): 4000 training and 1600 test images, cropped to $24\times24$ and
block-averaged to $4\times4$ pixels ($\Ns=16$), with the features $\feat{}(x)=(\cos\frac{\pi x}2,\sin\frac{\pi x}2)$
of \citet{stoudenmire2016supervised}; square loss ($\Loss(0)\approx\frac12$), fan-in initialization, $\chi=4$.
Gradient flow runs through the first plateau to the time $t_a$ at which the loss has fallen by $1\%$,
then on the clock $\tau=t-t_a$ up to $\tau=10^4$. Trees: binary16 ($\Kc=15$; two pixels per bottom core,
none on the internal cores) and MPS16 ($\Kc=16$). Pixel orders: \emph{quadtree} (Morton order; bottom cores
carry horizontally adjacent pixel pairs, level-2 subtrees $2\times2$ blocks), \emph{snake} (boustrophedon, for
the MPS) and random permutations (\emph{scrambled}). Main sweep: binary quadtree, snake MPS and three
scrambled binary orders, seven $\isc$ from $0.05$ to $0.5$ (four for one scrambled order), seeds 0--4, plus
whitened site features for three of the orders. Events are loss drops (peaks of $-\dd\Loss/\dd t$; gaps on
the $\tau$ clock) and rank events (a ratio $\bs ej/\bs e1$ of data-weighted singular values crossing $0.05$). Runs
of one (order, seed) at different $\isc$ mostly reach the same rank-one critical point, so independent
counts are per (order, seed) or per \emph{unit} (order, seed, critical point); per-state counts (one state
per run, repeating a critical point over $\isc$) are given only next to them. $\pm$ is the standard error over
seeds (over series for the free gap exponent below).

\paragraph{What survives.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{First plateau: an identity, not evidence.} $t_a\isc^{\Kc-2}$ is constant per seed to four
    digits or better (exponents $13.000\pm0.000$ on binary16, $14.000\pm0.000$ on MPS16). As $\isc\to0$ it tends to a
    per-seed constant for any input law: with $\params=\isc\tilde\params$ and $s=\isc^{\Kc-2}t$, $\tilde\params$ follows the
    flow of $\ip{Y}{\W}$ from $Z$ up to terms of relative order $\isc^{\Kc}$ \proven{} \knownresult[the exponent:
    \citealp{jacot2022saddletosaddle,kumar2025early}]. It checks the integrator and the clock, not the theory.
  \item \emph{The first binary route is a sibling pair} \empirical{} \newresult. After the escape, 96 of
    the 124 binary states lie within $0.05$ (relative) of a rank-one product, the others mostly at
    $\isc\ge0.3$. In all 20 (order, seed) pairs the first two edges to gain a singular value belong to two
    sibling bottom cores, the predicted $k=3$ route, and in all 96 rank-one states they cross the threshold
    together; 11 independent runs (nine orders, $\isc=0.1$) agree.
  \item \emph{Route couplings pick the route} \empirical{} \newresult. At the rank-one state the
    \emph{route coupling} $\mu_{uw}$ of two bottom cores is the norm of the residual's correlation between
    their unused feature directions, weighted by the other cores \eqref{eq:real-route-amplitude}: the coupling
    of \cref{rem:A-sequence}, not the size of the dormant route entries. It sets the rate of the route through
    them up to factors common to all routes with the same number $k_{uw}\in\{3,5,7\}$ of cores
    (\cref{def:A-local-residual}). The sibling pair with the largest $\mu_{uw}$ rises first in 91 of the 96
    rank-one states, in about 80--85\% of the critical-point units (chance $\frac14$; \cref{app:supp-cov}) and in
    11 of 11 independent runs. Unlike in the teacher runs, the couplings themselves decide these ties: the
    largest sibling coupling exceeds the second by a median factor $60$ (quadtree) and $5$ (scrambled
    orders). Route size comes before coupling: in 95 of the 96 states (25 of the 26 units) the largest of the 28
    couplings lies on a route with $k=5$ or $7$, yet a sibling pair rises first (\cref{rem:A-sequence}).
  \item \emph{The $k=3$ gap is $A/\isc+t_0$, not logarithmic} \empirical{} \newresult{} (\cref{fig:real-gaps}).
    Over the 11 series (one per order and seed) whose first route, one sibling pair, rises inside the
    second loss drop at every $\isc$ (at least four values over at least half a decade), the gap between the
    first two drops fits $A/\isc+t_0$, the $k=3$ law \eqref{eq:locality-rule} with its $O(1)$ correction
    (\cref{prop:A-head-start}), with relative rms residual at most $0.060$ and $t_0<0$ in all 11; the log law of a
    two-core route leaves $0.11$--$0.30$. A free exponent ($1.22\pm0.03$, range $1.06$--$1.36$) mostly measures
    the offset and depends on the $\isc$ range. All 11 series use raw features; the verdicts also hold over
    all 124 binary runs (\cref{app:supp-cov}).
  \item \emph{Saddle types} \empirical{} \newresult, from the $\isc$-scaling of $\lambda_{\min}(\Hperp)$ at plateau
    midpoints (\cref{sec:setup-diagnostics}). Binary16 (quadtree) at the rank-one plateau ($\Loss\approx0.27$):
    $\abs{\lambda_{\min}}\propto\isc^{0.87}$ to $\isc^{0.89}$ on the three seeds with five or six $\isc$ (and $\isc^{1.15}$ on a
    seed with three): higher order, though below the exponent $1$ of $k=3$ (\cref{prop:A-signatures}) at these $\isc$.
    MPS16 at $\Loss\approx0.23$ (five seeds): $\lambda_{\min}=-0.054$ to $-0.079$, exponent $0.047\pm0.016$, a strict
    saddle; it comes after the first route has risen, with two or three eigenvalues below $-10^{-3}\lambda_{\max}$
    at $\isc\le0.1$: the saddle of later two-core routes.
  \item \emph{Logarithmic later routes on the MPS} \empirical{} \newresult. The first rank increase is on
    one edge in all 25 runs where it is resolved (in 10 of 35 the threshold is met at $\tau=0$), within
    two time units of the first drop's peak: it merges with the escape and has no measurable gap. Of the
    later edges, timed by their first rise after the first drop, edges 3, 4 and 6--9 prefer the log law
    (28 of 29 seed fits; relative rms $0.03$--$0.11$ against $0.17$--$0.36$), edge 4 with slope $14.7\pm1.5$
    per unit of $\ln(1/\isc)$, comparable to $1/\abs{\lambda_{\min}}=13$--$19$. Edges 1 and 2 prefer a power
    law and edge 0 is mixed, which rank-one counting ($k=2$ on every MPS edge) does not explain.
  \item \emph{Plateaus creep} \empirical: between drops the loss falls by a median $0.76$ of the next
    drop (56 intervals in 20 binary runs, $\isc=0.1$). With drops dated by rate peaks the separation ratios on
    binary trees grow as $\isc\to0$ (quadtree: median smallest ratio $2.8$ at $\isc=0.5$, $24$ at $0.05$; all quadtree
    runs saddle-to-saddle at $\isc\le0.07$), not on the MPS.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Pixel order: a pre-registered test.}
Among the binary orders of the main sweep the quadtree ends lowest at small $\isc$: at $\isc\le0.14$ a
scrambled order ends below the quadtree of the same seed in 1 of 50 runs (at $\isc=0.1$,
$\Loss(10^4)=0.098\pm0.001$ against $0.19$--$0.23$ for the three scrambled orders), while at $\isc=0.5$ scrambled
orders end below it in 14 of 15 runs (the snake MPS ends lower still at every seed for $\isc\le0.3$). The
quadtree puts most of $\sum\mu_{uw}^2$ on routes with $k\le5$ ($0.89\pm0.01$ at $\isc=0.05$; scrambled orders
$0.17\pm0.07$) \empirical. In independent runs, though, the equally local transposed quadtree (vertical
pairs) ended above six random orders at seed 0. Before running any new order we registered a test of
this share; the registration and the predictor values were committed before the runs (\cref{app:supp-cov}),
and the test is reported here as registered.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Design.} Hypothesis: across pixel orders of binary16 at $\isc=0.1$, the share $\mathcal S$ of
    $\sum_{uw}\mu_{uw}^2$ on routes with $k_{uw}\le5$, evaluated at the best rank-one fit of the labels (no
    training), predicts (O1) the training loss at $\tau=10^4$ and (O2) $\log_{10}$ of the time to
    $\Loss\le0.25$: Spearman correlation $r_S<0$ with a 95\% bootstrap interval that excludes 0, for each
    outcome, over all orders and within the new random orders. Orders: 20 new random ones and 7 structured
    ones (quadtree; quadtreeT, the Morton order of the transposed image; row-major; column-major; snake; a
    Hilbert curve; mortonrev, bit-reversed Morton keys, which put every sibling pair two rows apart).
    Seeds 0--2: 81 runs, 74 new (the outcomes of quadtree, quadtreeT and row-major were seen before
    registration); outcomes averaged over seeds; O2 censored at $\tau=10^4$ (4 of 81 seeds).
  \item \emph{Result} \empirical{} (pre-registered) \newresult{} (\cref{fig:real-order}). O1:
    $r_S=-0.60$ $[-0.82,-0.22]$ over the 27 orders (permutation p-value $0.001$) and $-0.55$ $[-0.85,-0.09]$
    within the 20 random ones (p-value $0.015$): \emph{supported}. O2: $-0.30$ $[-0.59,+0.06]$ and $-0.26$
    $[-0.63,+0.23]$: \emph{not supported}. The registered secondary analyses agree (O1 without the three orders
    seen before registration: $-0.51$ $[-0.80,-0.08]$; with the three scrambled orders of the main sweep
    added: $-0.60$). Orders with the same pixel pairs share $\mathcal S$ (24 distinct values over 27 orders), so the
    within-random result is the cleaner test. Also registered: the rank-one search on the data does not
    always find the rank-one state that gradient flow reaches (quadtree: a fit at loss $0.310$, while four of
    five runs plateau at $0.27$), so $\mathcal S$ is a noisy proxy.
  \item \emph{Deviations.} None in the design or the analysis; the compute budget of 8 processes was exceeded
    for a few minutes, and two procedural points (the timing of one commit and an interim summary) are
    listed in \cref{app:supp-cov}.
\end{itemize}
The pixel order thus sets how low a binary TTN ends at fixed training time, and a route-coupling share
computed from the data before training predicts the ranking of the orders by that loss (Spearman
correlation $-0.60$; $-0.55$ within random orders), but not by their early speed. \emph{Descriptive, not
registered} \empirical: locality in the image is neither necessary nor sufficient. The three orders with horizontal pixel pairs (quadtree, snake, row-major;
$\mathcal S=0.989$) rank 1, 2 and 4 of 27 by O1, but the equally local orders with vertical or mixed pairs (quadtreeT,
column-major, Hilbert) rank 13--21; the transposed quadtree ends at twice the loss of the quadtree ($0.189$
against $0.097$; the quadtree's outcome, the lowest, and two of the three transposed-quadtree seeds were
seen before registration); and among random orders one with $\mathcal S=0.994$ ranks third (4 of its 8 bottom pairs
happen to be adjacent in the image, against $1.6$ expected by chance) and one with $\mathcal S=0.990$ and no adjacent
pair ranks sixth. Tree tensor networks for images are
often built to follow the pixel geometry (e.g.\ \citealp{cheng2019tree}). Our explanation is a
\conjecture: an order helps by placing label-relevant pixel interactions on routes that escape within the
horizon ($k=5$, plateaus $\propto\isc^{-3}$) rather than on routes with $k=7$ ($\propto\isc^{-5}$).
\emph{Exploratory, not registered:} the same share at the state each run actually reaches ranks O1 more
strongly ($r_S=-0.83$ $[-0.91,-0.66]$; $-0.85$ $[-0.95,-0.60]$ within random orders) and O2 no better
\empirical.

\paragraph{Scope.}
The scaling claims rest on one task at $4\times4$ pixels, gradient flow and 4000 fixed images; $8\times8$
images (12 runs) show the same first routes but allow no fits, and whitened site features do not make
the dynamics cleaner. The horizon $\tau=10^4$ cuts the escapes along routes with $k\ge5$ at $\isc<0.1$, so
final losses are losses at fixed training time. Events are read from loss rates and spectral
thresholds, so late events are only partly identified by route. Results on Fashion-MNIST, at $\chi=8$, on
the clock $\tau$ against the absolute clock, and at large $\isc$ are in \cref{app:supp-cov}.

\subsection{Summary}\label{sec:cov-summary}

Only an aligned covariance leaves the qualitative picture unchanged (\cref{tab:cov-summary}). When a
complication tested breaks the orthogonality of the teacher's components in the metric of the loss
(overlap, misalignment, correlated sites, random TTN teachers), the student's tree decides whether the
escape from the rank-one saddle is logarithmic (MPS) or polynomial (binary; HT from the saddle start,
never with correlated inputs). Stars, whose components are orthogonal, change which saddle is strict. On
