\section{Derivations}\label{app:derivations}

This appendix gives the derivations behind the theoretical statements of the main text, organized
by topic: conservation laws (\cref{app:conservation}), the escape from the origin
(\cref{app:escape}), the saddles visited after it (\cref{app:saddles}), teachers that are not
orthogonally decomposable (\cref{app:teachers}), input covariance (\cref{app:covariance}), discrete
steps and gradient noise (\cref{app:optimizer}), and the boundaries between regimes
(\cref{app:boundaries}). The short proofs of \cref{sec:setup} are not repeated.

\paragraph{Conventions.}
Notation is that of \cref{sec:setup}. Unless stated otherwise inputs are whitened ($\Sig=\Id$),
gradient flow has unit multipliers ($\lrm{v}=1$), so that $\Loss=\tfrac12\normF{\W-\Wt}^2$, and the
teacher is a CP teacher \eqref{eq:cp-teacher}. For a pattern teacher (\cref{sec:setup-loss}) and two of
its components $j,r$, $D_{jr}=\{i:\pi_j(i)\ne\pi_r(i)\}$ is the set of sites where they differ,
$N(D_{jr})$ the set of cores carrying a site of $D_{jr}$ (the \emph{terminal} cores),
$m_{jr}=\abs{N(D_{jr})}$, and $\mathrm{St}(N(D_{jr}))$ the smallest subtree of $\tree$ containing
$N(D_{jr})$ (its Steiner subtree; compare \cref{prop:teacher-spectra}(b)). At a learned saddle the
learned component is $j$, with per-core amplitude $q_j=\tw{j}^{1/\Kc}$ (the balanced amplitude of
\cref{prop:aligned}), and $r$ is a component still to be learned. A ratio of two teacher weights,
smaller over larger, is written $\varrho$; it is never a separation ratio $\sep{i}$. $\sigma_k(M)$ is
the $k$-th singular value of a matrix $M$ and always carries its argument; $\isc$ without argument is
the initialization scale. ``LO'' means leading order in the small quantities named in each
statement, and $\hat\params=\params/\norm{\params}$. A statement that combines a proven identity with a
derived consequence carries both tags, each at its part. The theory notes also use the label
\emph{heuristic} (a plausible argument whose assumptions are not controlled); the report has no such
tag, so these statements are tagged \conjecture{} and their argument is called heuristic. ``Checked''
refers to the numerical checks reported in the theory notes; the experimental tests are in
\cref{sec:regime-map,sec:s2s,sec:other-regimes,sec:covariance-teacher}.

\subsection{Conservation laws, gauge and invariant subspaces}\label{app:conservation}

\Cref{prop:conservation} fixes the differences of the core Gram matrices across every bond. Three
further facts are used below: the common value of the norm changes, the invariance of unused bond
channels, and the exact change of $\Q{e}$ under discrete steps.

\begin{lemma}[Equal norm shift]\label{lem:A-norm-shift}
  \proven{} \knownresult[deep linear networks: \citealp{arora2018optimization,du2018algorithmic}]
  Let $\Loss(\params)=\mathcal L(\W(\params))$ for a differentiable $\mathcal L$. Under gradient flow
  \eqref{eq:gf} (no mask), for every core $v$,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-norm-shift}
    \frac{1}{\lrm{v}}\ddt\normF{\G{v}}^2=-2\,\ip{\nabla_{\W}\mathcal L}{\W},
  \end{equation}
  which is the same for all cores. For the population loss \eqref{eq:loss} the right-hand side is
  $2\big(\ipS{\W}{\Wt}-\normS{\W}^2\big)$; for the leading-order flow $\dot\params=\nabla g$ of
  \cref{app:escape} it is $2g$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  Since $\W$ is linear in $\G{v}$,
  \[
    \ip{\G{v}}{\nabla_{\G{v}}\Loss}=\ip{\nabla_{\W}\mathcal L}{\W},\qquad
    \ddt\normF{\G{v}}^2=2\ip{\G{v}}{\dot{\G{v}}}=-2\lrm{v}\ip{\G{v}}{\nabla_{\G{v}}\Loss}.\qedhere
  \]
\end{proof}

\Cref{prop:conservation} gives only the differences $\normF{\G{u}}^2-\normF{\G{u'}}^2$; the lemma gives
the common shift $y(t)$ in $\normF{\G{v}(t)}^2=\normF{\G{v}(0)}^2+y(t)$ (unit multipliers). Along the
leading-order flow $\dot g=\norm{\nabla g}^2\ge0$, so $y(t)=2\int_0^tg$ is convex: \emph{all cores
shrink, by the same amount, and only while $g<0$}, that is, only if $\ip{\Wz}{\Wt}<0$ (half of all
initializations, by the symmetry $\G{v}\mapsto-\G{v}$), and the shrinking stops when $g$ changes sign.
The identity holds for any block of variables in which $g$ is multilinear, for example the reduced
cores of \cref{prop:A-reduced} (checked to $10^{-8}$--$10^{-11}$ relative on 2200 trajectories).

\begin{lemma}[Unused bond channels are invariant]\label{lem:A-unused}
  \proven{} \newresult[elementary]
  Let $e=\edge{v}$ with $p=\pa{v}$, and let $\mathcal B$ be a set of channels of $e$ such that every
  entry of $\G{v}$ and of $\G{p}$ whose $e$-index lies in $\mathcal B$ vanishes. Then, for every loss
  of the form $\mathcal L(\W(\params))$ (any input law, population or empirical), the gradient
  vanishes on these entries, so gradient flow, gradient descent and SGD keep them at zero. In
  particular, a critical point of the problem with bond dimensions $r_e\le\bd{e}$, padded with zeros,
  is a critical point of the problem with bond dimensions $\bd{e}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  The derivative of $\W$ with respect to an entry of $\G{v}$ with $e$-index $b\in\mathcal B$ contracts
  the other cores with $\G{p}$ fixed at $e$-index $b$, and that slice of $\G{p}$ vanishes. The same
  argument applies to $\G{p}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Conservation laws under discrete steps]\label{lem:A-gd-identity}
  \proven{} \knownresult[deep linear networks: \citealp{du2018algorithmic}; scalar form:
  \citealp{hofmann2026map}]
  Write $g_v=\nabla_{\Gm{v}{e}}\Loss$ and $g_p=\nabla_{\Gm{p}{e}}\Loss$ for the gradients, matricized
  like the cores in \eqref{eq:conserved}. One step $\params\mapsto\params-\lr\nabla\Loss$ (unit
  multipliers) changes the conserved quantity of bond $e$ by exactly
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-gd-identity}
    \Q{e}^{+}-\Q{e}=\lr^2\big(g_vg_v\T-g_pg_p\T\big).
  \end{equation}
  The same holds for SGD with the minibatch gradient. For one aligned component (\cref{prop:aligned})
  with amplitudes $\amp{v}{1}$ and product $\prodamp{1}$, the scalar $Q=\amp{v}{1}^2-\amp{p}{1}^2$ obeys
  $Q^{+}=Q\,\big(1-\lr^2(\tw{1}-\prodamp{1})^2\prodamp{1}^2/(\amp{v}{1}^2\amp{p}{1}^2)\big)$: every step
  contracts the imbalance.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  With $X=\Gm{v}{e}$ and $Y=\Gm{p}{e}$, the proof of \cref{prop:conservation} gives
  $g_vX\T=Yg_p\T=:C$ for every loss that depends on $\params$ through $\W$, minibatch losses included.
  In $(X-\lr g_v)(X-\lr g_v)\T-(Y-\lr g_p)(Y-\lr g_p)\T$ the terms linear in $\lr$ are
  $-\lr(C+C\T)+\lr(C\T+C)=0$. For the aligned component,
  $g_v=-(\tw{1}-\prodamp{1})\prodamp{1}/\amp{v}{1}$, and likewise for $p$.
\end{proof}
The change is negligible on plateaus, where $(\tw{1}-\prodamp{1})\prodamp{1}\approx0$, and large
during oscillations at large steps (\cref{app:optimizer}).

\paragraph{Expected imbalance at initialization.} By \cref{sec:setup-conservation}, fan-in
initialization with a common scale has $\E\,\Q{e}(0)=\isc^2(1-\bd{p}/\bd{v})\Id$ for $e=\edge{v}$,
where $\bd{p}$ is the bond dimension of the parent's own parent edge ($\bd{\vroot}=1$). Edges into the
root are therefore child-heavy, $\E\,\Q{e}=\isc^2(1-1/\bd{v})\Id\succ0$; internal edges of equal bond
dimension are balanced in expectation; and the leaf edges of an HT tree with $\bd{v}=d=2$ below a
parent with $\bd{p}=3$ are parent-heavy, $\E\,\Q{e}=-\tfrac12\isc^2\Id$. Fluctuations around these
means have both signs at any $\isc$. They matter for the stalls of \cref{prop:A-trap} and
\cref{app:boundaries}.

\subsection{Escape from the origin}\label{app:escape}

\paragraph{Setting.}
Whitened inputs, a CP teacher, and gradient flow from the fan-in initialization $\params_0=\isc Z$
(\cref{eq:init}; $Z$ denotes the initialization at $\isc=1$). \Cref{app:covariance} extends what follows
to product covariances by the substitution $\Wt\to\Sig\Wt$. Write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A-loss-g}
  \Loss(\params)=\tfrac12\normF{\Wt}^2-g(\params)+\tfrac12\normF{\W(\params)}^2,\qquad
  g(\params):=\ip{\W(\params)}{\Wt}.
\end{equation}
$g$ is homogeneous of degree $\Kc$ and linear in each core, while $\normF{\W}^2$ has degree $2\Kc$. The
\emph{leading-order (LO) flow} $\dot\params=\nabla g(\params)$ drops a term of relative size
$\normF{\W}/\normF{\Wt}$, which is $O(\isc^{\Kc})$ on the first plateau. Let $t^*$ be its blow-up time and
$\nabla_{\mathbb S}g(\hat\params)=\nabla g(\hat\params)-\Kc g(\hat\params)\hat\params$ the gradient of $g$
on the unit sphere. The first event $\tev{1}$ is defined in \cref{sec:setup-diagnostics}.

\begin{proposition}[Spherical--radial decomposition]\label{prop:A-spherical}
  \proven{} \knownresult[directional convergence: \citealp{kumar2025early}]
  Along the LO flow, with the time change $\dd\tau=\norm{\params}^{\Kc-2}\dd t$,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-spherical}
    \frac{\dd\hat\params}{\dd\tau}=\nabla_{\mathbb S}g(\hat\params),\qquad
    \frac{\dd\ln\norm{\params}}{\dd\tau}=\Kc\,g(\hat\params).
  \end{equation}
  The direction follows the gradient flow of $g$ on the sphere, which involves neither
  $\norm{\params}$ nor $\isc$. The conserved quantities obey
  $\Q{e}(\hat\params(\tau))=\Q{e}(\params_0)/\norm{\params(\tau)}^2$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  Euler's identity gives $\ip{\params}{\nabla g(\params)}=\Kc g(\params)$, and $\nabla g$ is homogeneous
  of degree $\Kc-1$, so $\nabla g(\params)=\norm{\params}^{\Kc-1}\nabla g(\hat\params)$. Hence
  $\ddt\norm{\params}=\ip{\hat\params}{\nabla g(\params)}=\Kc\norm{\params}^{\Kc-1}g(\hat\params)$ and
  $\ddt\hat\params=(\dot\params-\hat\params\ip{\hat\params}{\dot\params})/\norm{\params}
  =\norm{\params}^{\Kc-2}\nabla_{\mathbb S}g(\hat\params)$. Finally, $\Q{e}$ is quadratic in $\params$.
\end{proof}

As $\isc\to0$ the escape needs $\norm{\params}$ to grow by a factor of order $1/\isc$, i.e.\ a $\tau$ of
order $\ln(1/\isc)$, so the escape direction is $\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\hat\params(\tau)$, a KKT point of $g$
on the sphere (\citealp{kumar2025early}, Thm~1, which applies verbatim to TTNs with $\Kc\ge3$). The
conservation laws bind while $\norm{\params}\approx\norm{\params_0}$, which is the phase ($\tau=O(1)$) that
decides both the escape time and the KKT point reached; they do not change the balanced form of the
limit (\cref{prop:A-kkt}).

\begin{corollary}[Blow-up time]\label{cor:A-blowup}
  \proven{} \newresult[per-initialization form for TTNs; the exponent $\Kc-2$ is known in analogues:
  \citealp{jacot2022saddletosaddle,gissin2020implicit}]
  With $G(\tau)=\int_0^\tau g(\hat\params)\,\dd\tau'$,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-blowup}
    t^*=\norm{\params_0}^{-(\Kc-2)}\int_0^\infty e^{-(\Kc-2)\Kc G(\tau)}\,\dd\tau .
  \end{equation}
  For $\params_0=\isc Z$, $t^*=\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}\,\hat t_1(Z)/\tw{1}$, where the prefactor
  $\hat t_1(Z):=\tw{1}\,t^*|_{\isc=1}$ depends on the initialization direction and on $\Wt/\tw{1}$ but
  not on $\isc$. The exponent is exactly $\Kc-2$ and counts every core, with or without sites.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  By \eqref{eq:A-spherical}, $\norm{\params(\tau)}=\norm{\params_0}e^{\Kc G(\tau)}$ and
  $\dd t=\norm{\params}^{-(\Kc-2)}\dd\tau$. The LO vector field is homogeneous of degree $\Kc-1$, so
  $\params_\isc(t)=\isc\,\params_1(\isc^{\Kc-2}t)$, and replacing $\Wt$ by $c\Wt$ divides all times by $c$.
\end{proof}
There is no separate additive alignment time: the whole integral is alignment, because
$g(\hat\params)$ starts near $\ip{\Wz}{\Wt}/\norm{\params_0}^{\Kc}\approx0$ and grows gradually.

\begin{proposition}[From the blow-up to the first event]\label{prop:A-first-event}
  \derived{} \newresult[for TTNs]
  Assume that at the end of the LO phase the direction $\hat\params$ has converged to the balanced KKT
  point of the first component learned. Then, as $\isc\to0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-first-event}
    \tev{1}=t^*+\delta_\Kc\,\tw{1}^{-(2-2/\Kc)}+o(1),\qquad
    \delta_\Kc=-\frac{2^{(\Kc-2)/\Kc}}{\Kc-2}+\int_0^{2^{-1/\Kc}}\frac{b\,\dd b}{1-b^\Kc},
  \end{equation}
  with $\delta_3=-0.850$, $\delta_4=-0.266$, $\delta_5=-0.047$, $\delta_6=0.072$, $\delta_7=0.148$ and
  $\delta_8=0.201$. Hence $\tev{1}\isc^{\Kc-2}\tw1/\hat t_1(Z)=1+O(\isc^{\Kc-2})$ for every initialization.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Derivation]
  Once $\norm{\params}=O(1)$ the remaining time does not depend on $\isc$ and is set by the direction at
  that moment. Along a balanced KKT point the flow is the balanced mode $\dot q=q^{\Kc-1}(\tw1-q^\Kc)$ of
  \cref{prop:aligned}, whose half-time is $q_0^{-(\Kc-2)}/((\Kc-2)\tw1)+\delta_\Kc\tw1^{-(2-2/\Kc)}+O(q_0^2)$;
  the first term is the LO blow-up time. (With $b=q\,\tw1^{-1/\Kc}$ one has
  $\dot b=\tw1^{2-2/\Kc}b^{\Kc-1}(1-b^\Kc)$ and $1/(b^{\Kc-1}(1-b^\Kc))=b^{-(\Kc-1)}+b/(1-b^\Kc)$.)
\end{proof}
Checked per initialization: deviations from gradient flow at most $0.8\%$ ($\Kc=3,4$, $\isc\le0.03$),
with the residual equal to the $\delta_\Kc$ term to two digits; for $\Kc=6$--$8$ at $\isc=0.15$--$0.3$ the residual
is 2--30 times the $\delta_\Kc$ term (the direction has not converged), so a $1\%$ test needs
$\isc^{\Kc-2}\le10^{-3}$.

\begin{remark}[Per-initialization prediction]\label{rem:A-recipe}
  \proven{} at LO. The prefactor is computed per initialization by integrating, in $\tau$, the state
  $(u,\ell,T)$ with $u(0)=\params_0/\norm{\params_0}$ and $\ell(0)=T(0)=0$:
  $\dd u/\dd\tau=\nabla g(u)-\Kc g(u)u$ (renormalizing $u$), $\dd\ell/\dd\tau=\Kc g(u)$,
  $\dd T/\dd\tau=e^{-(\Kc-2)\ell}$, up to $\ell=40/(\Kc-2)+5$, and adding the tail
  $e^{-(\Kc-2)\ell}/((\Kc-2)\Kc g(u))$. Then $t^*=\norm{\params_0}^{-(\Kc-2)}T$ for the actual teacher (any
  number of components), and the dominant component of the final $u$ is the first one learned as
  $\isc\to0$. This is the parameter-free prediction of $\tev{1}$ tested in \cref{sec:s2s-escape}.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Maximum of $g$ on the sphere]\label{prop:A-gmax}
  \proven{} \newresult[for TTNs]
  Group the sites by the core that carries them (cores without sites form no group), and let
  $\norm{\Wt}_{\rm inj}=\max\{\ip{\Wt}{\bigotimes_vY_v}:\normF{Y_v}=1\}$ be the injective norm of $\Wt$
  seen as a tensor with one leg per group. Then
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-gmax}
    g(\params)\le\norm{\Wt}_{\rm inj}\prod_v\normF{\G{v}}\le\Kc^{-\Kc/2}\norm{\Wt}_{\rm inj}\norm{\params}^{\Kc},
  \end{equation}
  with equality on the unit sphere at the balanced bond-rank-one configurations
  $\G{v}=\Kc^{-1/2}\,Y_v\otimes\varepsilon_1\otimes\dots\otimes\varepsilon_1$ built from a maximizing
  $(Y_v)$. For odeco teachers $\norm{\Wt}_{\rm inj}=\tw1$ for every grouping; in general the grouping
  matters.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  First inequality, by induction on the number of cores. Take a leaf $\ell$ with parent $p$ and the SVD
  $\G{\ell}=\sum_k\mu_kY_k\otimes z_k$ across (physical legs $\mid$ bond leg). Then
  $g=\sum_k\mu_k\ip{\W'_k}{\Wt[Y_k]}$, where $\W'_k$ is the network without $\ell$ and with $\G{p}$
  contracted with $z_k$ on the $\ell$-leg, and $\Wt[Y_k]$ is $\Wt$ contracted with $Y_k$ on the sites of
  $\ell$. By induction, and since $\norm{\Wt[Y_k]}_{\rm inj}\le\norm{\Wt}_{\rm inj}$, the sum is at most
  $\norm{\Wt}_{\rm inj}\prod_{v\ne\ell,p}\normF{\G{v}}\sum_k\mu_k\normF{\G{p}\times_\ell z_k}$, and
  Cauchy--Schwarz with $\sum_k\normF{\G{p}\times_\ell z_k}^2\le\normF{\G{p}}^2$ closes the step. The second
  inequality is the AM--GM inequality $\prod_v\normF{\G{v}}\le(\sum_v\normF{\G{v}}^2/\Kc)^{\Kc/2}$.
\end{proof}
Example: for $\Wt=(\Id/\sqrt d)\otimes(\Id/\sqrt d)$ on the site pairs $(0,1)$ and $(2,3)$, the binary
tree with $\Ns=4$ reaches $g=3^{-3/2}$ on the unit sphere and the MPS only $4^{-2}/d$ (checked).

\begin{proposition}[KKT points are balanced]\label{prop:A-kkt}
  \proven{} \newresult[for TTNs] Every KKT point $\params^*$ of $g$ on the unit sphere with
  $g^*:=g(\params^*)>0$ has $\Q{e}=0$ on every bond and $\normF{\G{v}}^2=1/\Kc$ for every core.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  At a KKT point $\nabla g(\params^*)=\lambda\params^*$, and Euler's identity gives $\lambda=\Kc g^*>0$.
  $g$ is invariant under the gauge transformations \eqref{eq:gauge}, so $\ip{\nabla g}{\delta\params}=0$
  for every gauge direction $\delta\params=(M\Gm{v}{e},-M\T\Gm{p}{e})$, i.e.\
  $\lambda\tr\big((\Gm{v}{e}\Gm{v}{e}\T-\Gm{p}{e}\Gm{p}{e}\T)M\big)=0$ for all $M$; hence $\Q{e}=0$. Since $g$
  is linear in each core, $\ip{\G{v}}{\nabla_{\G{v}}g}=g^*$, i.e.\ $\lambda\normF{\G{v}}^2=g^*$.
\end{proof}
Numerically located KKT points satisfy the proposition to $10^{-15}$. Consequences: the near-origin
escape direction is balanced, hence of minimal norm in its fibre
\citep[Prop.~F.10]{furman2026benign}; and the imbalance of a random initialization, conserved in absolute
size ($O(\isc^2)$) while $\norm{\params}^2$ grows, does not affect the limit direction at LO.

\paragraph{Local maxima at component configurations.}
For a pattern teacher with distinct weights let $\params_j$ be the balanced bond-rank-one configuration of
component $j$ on the unit sphere: per-core amplitude $\Kc^{-1/2}$, the pattern of $j$ on the physical legs
and channel~1 on every bond (the aligned initialization of \cref{prop:aligned} with component $j$ only),
so that $g(\params_j)=\tw j\Kc^{-\Kc/2}$.

\begin{proposition}[Second-order structure at a component]\label{prop:A-component-hessian}
  \derived{} \newresult[for general trees; for binary $\Ns=4$, a deep linear network, it is known:
  \citealp{jacot2022saddletosaddle,achour2024loss}]
  (a) If some component $r$ has $m_{jr}=1$, then $\params_j$ is not a critical point of $g$ on the sphere.
  (b) Otherwise, assume that the components $r$ with $m_{jr}=2$ have pairwise different pairs of
  terminal cores. Each such $r$, with terminal cores $v,w$, contributes $n_{jr}$ eigenvalues
  $\Kc^{1-\Kc/2}(\tw r-\tw j)$ to the Riemannian Hessian of $g$ at $\params_j$, where $n_{jr}=\bd{e}$ if $v$
  and $w$ share the bond $e$ and $n_{jr}=1$ otherwise. All other eigenvalues are negative, except the
  $\sum_e(\bd{e}-1)$ zero modes along the gauge orbit. Hence $\params_j$ is a strict local maximum modulo
  gauge iff no $m_{jr}=1$ and every $r$ with $m_{jr}=2$ has $\tw r<\tw j$; otherwise it has
  $\sum_{r:\,m_{jr}=2,\,\tw r>\tw j}n_{jr}$ unstable directions. Ties $\tw r=\tw j$ are decided at higher
  order; partners that share a terminal core are treated by \cref{prop:A-piggyback-graph}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Derivation]
  The Riemannian Hessian is $\nabla^2g-\Kc g(\params_j)\Id=\nabla^2g-\tw j\Kc^{1-\Kc/2}\Id$ on the tangent
  space. Since $g$ is linear in each core, $\nabla^2g$ only has cross terms between two cores $v\ne w$, and
  such a term contracts $\Wt$ with the pattern of $j$ on every site outside $\sites v\cup\sites w$: only
  components with $D_{jr}\subseteq\sites v\cup\sites w$ contribute. If $m_{jr}=1$, $\nabla g(\params_j)$ has
  a component along the pattern of $r$ on the terminal core. Component $j$'s own cross terms vanish on the
  gauge orbit and give $-2\tw j\Kc^{1-\Kc/2}$ off it; every direction not coupled to another component has
  eigenvalue $-\tw j\Kc^{1-\Kc/2}$. For $m_{jr}=2$, rotate $\G{v}$ and $\G{w}$ toward the pattern of $r$ on
  their sites, with channel $c$ on the bond joining them if they are adjacent and channel~1 on every other
  bond. The cross term is $\tw r\Kc^{1-\Kc/2}$ for every channel $c$ if the cores are adjacent, and only
  for $c=1$ otherwise (every bond on the path between them meets channel~1 of a third core). Each
  surviving channel gives the block
  $\Kc^{1-\Kc/2}\big(\begin{smallmatrix}-\tw j&\tw r\\ \tw r&-\tw j\end{smallmatrix}\big)$, with eigenvalues
  $\Kc^{1-\Kc/2}(\tw r-\tw j)$ and $-\Kc^{1-\Kc/2}(\tw r+\tw j)$.
\end{proof}
Checked on 19 pattern-teacher cases and 7 odeco settings: numbers and values of the positive
eigenvalues as predicted in every case. For odeco teachers $m_{jr}$ is the number
of cores carrying sites: $m=\Ns$ on MPS and HT trees, $m=3,4$ on binary trees with $\Ns=6,8$, and $m=2$ on
binary $\Ns=4$, whose bottom cores are not adjacent ($n=1$). Every odeco component is therefore a local
maximum on MPS, HT and binary $\Ns\ge6$ trees (\emph{tensor-like}: one basin per component), while on
binary $\Ns=4$ only the largest one is (\emph{matrix-like}). Pattern teachers whose components differ on
exactly two cores are matrix-like on every tree. Spherical flows from 200 random initializations ended at
component configurations in every case \empirical; whether teachers that are not odeco have local
maxima of bond rank larger than one is open (\cref{app:teachers}), and even best rank-one approximation
has non-global local optima \citep{kofidis2002best}.

\begin{proposition}[Only the teacher slices move]\label{prop:A-reduced}
  \proven{} \newresult[for TTNs]
  For an odeco teacher, $\nabla_{\G{v}}g=\sum_r\tw r\big(\bigotimes_{i\in\sites v}\fac ri\big)\otimes E^{(r)}_v$,
  where $E^{(r)}_v$ contracts all other cores with $\fac ri$ on their sites. Hence, under the LO flow, the
  part of $\G{v}$ that is orthogonal on its physical legs to every $\bigotimes_{i\in\sites v}\fac ri$ is
  frozen. The moving variables are the reduced cores $H^{(r)}_v$ ($\G{v}$ contracted with the $\fac ri$ on
  its physical legs; for a core without sites, the whole core, shared by all components), and
  $g=\sum_r\tw r\,\mathcal W_{\rm red}(H^{(r)})$, with $\mathcal W_{\rm red}$ the scalar contraction of the
  reduced tree. Under \eqref{eq:init} the $H^{(r)}_v$ have \iid $\Normal(0,\isc^2/\fanin v)$ entries,
  independent across $r$: a fan-in-initialized scalar TTN with per-core scale $\isc d^{-\abs{\sites v}/2}$.
  If every core carries a site (MPS), different components have disjoint moving variables and evolve
  independently at LO.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  The physical legs of $\nabla_{\G{v}}g$ lie in the span of the teacher vectors, and $g$ depends on $\G{v}$
  only through the $H^{(r)}_v$. Contracting a core with \iid Gaussian entries with orthonormal vectors gives
  independent Gaussians of the same variance.
\end{proof}
The same holds for pattern teachers, with one slice per distinct pattern on each core. Most of
$\norm{\params_0}$ sits in frozen slices: $\E\normF{H^{(r)}_v}^2=\isc^2\bd{v}d^{-\abs{\sites v}}$ against
$\E\normF{\G{v}}^2=\isc^2\bd{v}$.

\begin{corollary}[The Kumar--Haupt time is a lower bound]\label{cor:A-kh}
  \proven{} (bounds) \empirical{} (sizes) \newresult[the escape time $t_{\rm KH}$ itself is
  \citealp{kumar2025towards}]
  Let the teacher be odeco and let $r_{\rm m}$ be the norm of the moving variables at initialization.
  Then, for every initialization,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-kh}
    t^*\ \ge\ \frac{\Kc^{\Kc/2-1}}{(\Kc-2)\tw1}\,r_{\rm m}^{-(\Kc-2)}\ \ge\
    \frac{\Kc^{\Kc/2-1}}{(\Kc-2)\tw1}\,\norm{\params_0}^{-(\Kc-2)}=:t_{\rm KH},
  \end{equation}
  with equality iff the initial direction already maximizes $g$. Here
  $t_{\rm KH}=1/(\Kc(\Kc-2)g_*\norm{\params_0}^{\Kc-2})$, $g_*=\tw1\Kc^{-\Kc/2}$, is the escape time of
  \citet{kumar2025towards} from an initialization exactly along a maximizer. Under fan-in initialization
  the median of $t^*/t_{\rm KH}$ is 2.5--5.2 (binary, $\Ns=4$, $d=2$--$4$), 5.2--18 (MPS, $\Ns=4$), 29--377
  (MPS, $\Ns=6$), 182--12378 (MPS, $\Ns=8$), 94--4463 (binary, $\Ns=8$) and 35--270 (HT, $\Ns=4$), growing
  with $d$, $\chi$ and $\Kc$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  By \cref{prop:A-gmax}, $g\le g_*$ on the unit sphere, so $\ln\norm{\params(\tau)}\le\ln\norm{\params_0}+\Kc g_*\tau$
  and \eqref{eq:A-blowup} gives $t^*\ge\norm{\params_0}^{-(\Kc-2)}/((\Kc-2)\Kc g_*)$. Frozen variables do not
  enter the dynamics of the moving ones, which is the LO flow of the same $g$ restricted to them; the
  same argument with their norm $r_{\rm m}$ gives the first inequality.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Escape along an imbalanced product path]\label{prop:A-product-mode}
  \derived{} \knownresult[the trichotomy: \citealp{rawal2026theory}, Thm~4.2; new: the TTN reduction and
  the constants]
  If the escape follows a rank-one path whose per-core amplitudes $x_v>0$ obey
  $\dot x_v=s\prod_{w\ne v}x_w$, then the $x_v^2-x_w^2$ are conserved and, with $x_v^2=c_v^2+y$,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-product-mode}
    \hat t=\frac1{2s}\int_0^\infty\prod_{v=1}^{\Kc}\big(y+c_v^2\big)^{-1/2}\dd y .
  \end{equation}
  Let $m$ internal cores start at amplitude $\gamma$ and the other $\Kc-m$ (the ends) at $c_e\gg\gamma$.
  As $\gamma\to0$: for $m=0$ and equal amplitudes $c$, $\hat t=c^{-(\Kc-2)}/((\Kc-2)s)$
  \citep{saxe2014exact}; for $m=1$ and ends at $c$, $\hat t\to\frac12\mathrm{B}(\frac12,\frac{\Kc-2}2)c^{-(\Kc-2)}/s$
  ($\mathrm B$ is Euler's beta function), a finite limit; for $m=2$,
  $\hat t=\ln(1/\gamma)/(s\prod_ec_e)+O(1)$; for $m\ge3$,
  $\hat t\simeq\gamma^{-(m-2)}/((m-2)s\prod_ec_e)$.
\end{proposition}
The integral follows from \cref{lem:A-norm-shift}: $\dot y=2s\prod_w(y+c_w^2)^{1/2}$. The binary tree with
$\Ns=4$ and the MPS with $\Ns=3$ have $m=1$, the MPS with $\Ns=4$ has $m=2$, the binary tree with $\Ns=8$
and the HT tree with $\Ns=4$ have $m=3$, and the MPS has $m=\Ns-2$. The escape time is set by the path
amplitudes of the internal cores, while $\norm{\params_0}$ is dominated by frozen slices and by internal
cores whose norm is spread over $\sim\chi^2$ directions; this is why $t_{\rm KH}$ fails most for large
$\Kc$. That the end cores keep their directions during the escape, which reduces the dynamics to such a
path, is empirical (median cosine with the initial direction $0.92$--$1.00$ at half the escape time).

\paragraph{Binary tree with $\Ns=4$.} \derived{} (given that the end directions are frozen)
\newresult. The reduced network has end vectors $a,b\in\R^{\bd b}$ with $\Normal(0,\isc^2/d^2)$ entries,
$\bd b=\min(\chi,d^2)$, and a root matrix $C$ with $\Normal(0,\isc^2/\bd b^2)$ entries, and
$g=\tw1a\T Cb$. With frozen end directions, \eqref{eq:A-product-mode} with $m=1$ and typical end norms
$\isc\sqrt{\bd b}/d$ gives the median $\tev1\approx\pi d/(2\tw1\isc\sqrt{\bd b})$ for $d\le\bd b\le d^2$, i.e.\
$\tev1\approx\pi/(2\tw1\isc)$ at full bond for every $d$, whereas $t_{\rm KH}\propto(2d^2+1)^{-1/2}$. Measured
medians are $0.80$--$0.95$ times the formula for
$d\le\bd b<2d$ and $0.92$--$1.00$ times it for $\bd b\ge2d$; at full bond
$\tev1\isc\tw1=1.55$--$1.57$ for $d=2,3,4$ \empirical. The log-spread is
$\mathrm{sd}\ln\hat t_1\approx1/(2\sqrt{\bd b})$ \derived.

\paragraph{Learning order.} Which component is learned first is decided in the spherical phase
($\tau=O(1)$) of \cref{prop:A-spherical}, by the basin of the initial direction. Two cases follow from
\cref{prop:A-component-hessian}.

\begin{proposition}[Race on trees whose cores all carry sites]\label{prop:A-race}
  \proven{} (at LO) \newresult{}
  Let the teacher be odeco and the tree an MPS. By \cref{prop:A-reduced} the components evolve
  independently at LO, and component $r$ blows up at $\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}\hat t_{1,r}/\tw r$, where the
  $\hat t_{1,r}$ are \iid copies of the prefactor of \cref{cor:A-blowup} for a single component of unit
  weight. The first component learned is $\arg\min_r\hat t_{1,r}/\tw r$, and for two components with
  $\varrho=\tw2/\tw1<1$
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-race}
    P(\text{larger first})=P\Big(\frac{\hat t_{1,2}}{\hat t_{1,1}}>\varrho\Big)
    =\frac12+\frac12P\Big(\Big|\ln\frac{\hat t_{1,2}}{\hat t_{1,1}}\Big|<\ln\frac1\varrho\Big),
  \end{equation}
  which does not depend on $\isc$. In the spherical flow the basin of component $r$ is exactly the set of
  initial directions with $\hat t_{1,r}/\tw r<\hat t_{1,r'}/\tw{r'}$ for all $r'\ne r$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  Independence of the components at LO and exchangeability of the $\hat t_{1,r}$; the law of
  $\ln(\hat t_{1,2}/\hat t_{1,1})$ is symmetric.
\end{proof}
The race agreed with the direct two-component LO flow and with full gradient flow on 100 of 100
initializations (MPS, $\Ns=4$) and with three components to $0.06\%$ in time. Further, \empirical:
$P(\text{larger first})\approx F_{\Normal}(\ln(1/\varrho)/S_{\hat t})$, with $F_{\Normal}$ the standard normal
distribution function and $S_{\hat t}$ the standard deviation of $\ln(\hat t_{1,2}/\hat t_{1,1})$, within
$\pm0.05$ in all tensor-like settings computed; $\mathrm{sd}\ln\hat t_1\approx c\sqrt{\Kc-2}$ on MPS
with $c=0.27$--$0.49$. Hence \conjecture: $P(\text{larger first})\to\frac12$ as $\Kc\to\infty$ at fixed
$\varrho$, $d$, $\chi$. The spread falls as the reduced cores get more entries but saturates once $\chi$
reaches its cap, so $P\to1$ needs $d\to\infty$ as well as large $\chi$ (\conjecture, heuristic; compare
the largest-first order of over-parameterized tensor decomposition, \citealp{ge2021understanding}).
When some cores carry no site (binary $\Ns=8$, HT), the components share the internal cores and the
race is approximate: it predicts the first component of the direct LO flow on $94$--$100\%$ of
initializations \empirical.

\begin{proposition}[Matrix-like pairs]\label{prop:A-matrix-like}
  Let the smaller component $j$ of a pair have a larger partner $r$ with $m_{jr}=2$, and let
  $n=n_{jr}$ and $\varrho=\tw j/\tw r$. (a) \derived{} \knownresult[binary $\Ns=4$:
  \citealp{jacot2022saddletosaddle}, Thm~2] $\params_j$ is a saddle of the spherical flow with $n$ unstable
  directions while $\params_r$ is a local maximum (\cref{prop:A-component-hessian}), so
  $P(\text{smaller first})\to0$ as $\isc\to0$. (b) \conjecture{} (heuristic) \newresult{} As $\isc\to0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-matrix-like}
    P(\text{smaller first})\propto\isc^{\,n(1-\varrho)/\varrho}.
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Heuristic argument for (b)]
  A trajectory that reaches $\norm{\params}=O(1)$ near $\params_j$ must pass close to it. There the
  deviations along the $n$ unstable directions grow in $\tau$ at rate $\Kc^{1-\Kc/2}(\tw r-\tw j)$, while
  $\dd\ln\norm{\params}/\dd\tau=\Kc^{1-\Kc/2}\tw j$; the deviations therefore grow like
  $(\norm{\params}/\norm{\params_0})^{(1-\varrho)/\varrho}$. At the escape
  $\norm{\params}/\norm{\params_0}\sim1/\isc$, so the trajectory escapes near $\params_j$ only if all $n$
  initial deviations are below $\sim\isc^{(1-\varrho)/\varrho}$, which for a smooth density of initial
  deviations has probability $\propto\isc^{n(1-\varrho)/\varrho}$.
\end{proof}
Checks of (b): in a leading-order emulation (MPS, $\Ns=4$, pattern teachers with $n=1,\dots,4$) the
fitted slopes $\dd\ln P/\dd\ln\isc$ are ordered as $n$ and are $0.67$--$1.03$ (typically $0.8$) of
$n(1-\varrho)/\varrho$ over $\isc=10^{-1}$--$10^{-8}$, steepening toward it at small $\isc$. Full gradient
flow (binary $\Ns=4$, $d=\chi=2$, $\varrho=0.7$) gives $P(\text{smaller first})=0.155$, $0.064$, $0.024$ at
$\isc=0.1$, $0.01$, $0.001$ (local slopes $0.38$, $0.43$ against $0.43$) \empirical. Gradient flow puts
the smaller component first on only $59$--$70\%$ of the initializations on which the emulation does,
because the term $\tfrac12\normF{\W}^2$ slows the component that is ahead; whether gradient flow has the
same exponent as the LO flow is open. When the wrong order occurs, it is corrected through a strict
saddle (\cref{rem:A-wrong-order}). These predictions are tested in \cref{sec:s2s-order}.

\subsection{Saddles after the first escape}\label{app:saddles}

\paragraph{Setting and assumptions.}
After the first escape, gradient flow passes near a \emph{learned saddle} $\params_S$, a critical point
that represents the partial fit $\W_S=\sum_{j\in S}\tw j\U j$ of \cref{sec:setup-loss}. We assume:
(A1) whitened inputs; (A2) a pattern teacher with distinct patterns, so that the $\U r$ are orthonormal;
(A3) $\params_S$ is balanced ($\Q{e}=0$ on every bond) and has bond ranks $r_e=\rank\mat{(\W_S)}{e}$,
padded with zeros to $\bd{e}$ (a critical point by \cref{lem:A-unused}; gradient flow from small
initialization has $\Q{e}=O(\isc^2)$, so plateaus are balanced to that order); (A4) $\bd{e}\ge r_e+1$ on
every bond whose rank must grow; (A5) leading order in the entries that vanish at $\params_S$, whose
size $\isc_{\rm eff}$ on the plateau is small. In a basis of each bond in which the learned channels come
first, entries of $\params$ whose bond indices are all learned are \emph{old} and the others \emph{new};
$c_e=\bd{e}-r_e$ is the number of new channels of bond $e$. Let $\mathcal R=\Wt-\W_S$ be the residual and
$\Loss_S=\frac12\normF{\mathcal R}^2$. With one learned component $j$, balancedness on bonds of rank one
forces equal core norms, so every core of $\params_S$ is $q_j$ times the pattern of $j$ on its sites times
channel~1 on its bonds, $q_j=\tw j^{1/\Kc}$ \proven.

\paragraph{Expansion around the saddle.}
For a set $\mathcal J$ of cores and perturbations $\delta=(\delta_v)_v$, let $\W_{\mathcal J}[\delta]$ be the
tensor obtained by replacing the cores in $\mathcal J$ by the $\delta_v$ and keeping the others at
$\params_S$. Multilinearity gives exactly $\W(\params_S+\delta)=\sum_{\mathcal J}\W_{\mathcal J}[\delta]$, with
$\W_\emptyset=\W_S$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A-expansion}
  \Loss(\params_S+\delta)=\Loss_S-\sum_{\mathcal J\neq\emptyset}\ip{\mathcal R}{\W_{\mathcal J}[\delta]}
  +\tfrac12\Big\|\sum_{\mathcal J\neq\emptyset}\W_{\mathcal J}[\delta]\Big\|_F^2 .
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Routes]\label{lem:A-routes}
  \proven{} \newresult{}
  (i) If $\delta_v$ has only new entries, then $\W_{\{v\}}[\delta_v]=0$, so new entries carry no
  Gauss--Newton cost at second order. (ii) For new entries, $\W_{\mathcal J}[\delta]\ne0$ requires every new
  index of every $\delta_v$ to sit on a bond inside $\mathcal J$ and to be matched there; hence every bond
  leaving $\mathcal J$ carries a learned index, and every connected component of $\mathcal J$ has at least
  two cores. For a connected set $\mathcal C$ (a \emph{route}),
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-route}
    \W_{\mathcal C}[\delta]=\mathcal W_{\mathcal C}(\delta)\otimes\bigotimes_{f\in\partial\mathcal C}\Sub{f},
  \end{equation}
  where $\mathcal W_{\mathcal C}(\delta)$ is the small TTN formed by the $\delta_v$ (internal bond dimensions
  $c_e$), $\partial\mathcal C$ is the set of bonds leaving $\mathcal C$, and $\Sub{f}$ is the contraction of
  the learned network beyond $f$, a map from the $r_f$ learned indices of $f$ to tensors on the sites $H_f$
  beyond $f$. Every proper subset $\mathcal C'\subsetneq\mathcal C$ has $\W_{\mathcal C'}[\delta]=0$: \emph{a route
  pays no Gauss--Newton cost at the order of its gain.}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  A new index of $\delta_v$ on a bond meets, in the neighbouring core, either a perturbation of that core
  or the zero block of a learned core (\cref{lem:A-unused}). In a proper subset of a route some new index
  meets such a zero block.
\end{proof}
Disconnected sets $\mathcal J$ also contribute at the same degree, but each connected component
$\mathcal J_i$ alone already gives $\W_{\mathcal J_i}\ne0$ through learned boundary indices, so it pays the
Gauss--Newton cost $\frac12\normF{\W_{\mathcal J_i}}^2$ at the same order as the gain. For two blocks this
makes the quadratic form positive semidefinite iff $\tw j\ge\tw r$, the condition of
\cref{prop:A-piggyback-graph} \derived{} (checked on MPS, $\Ns=5$); that disconnected sets never descend
before the single-core rotations is shown only for this case.

\begin{definition}[Local residual]\label{def:A-local-residual}
  For a route $\mathcal C$, $\mathcal R_{\mathcal C}:=\ip{\mathcal R}{\bigotimes_{f\in\partial\mathcal C}\Sub{f}}$
  (contraction over all sites beyond $\partial\mathcal C$) is a tensor with one mode per core of
  $\mathcal C$, made of its physical legs and its learned boundary indices; then
  $\ip{\mathcal R}{\W_{\mathcal C}[\delta]}=\ip{\mathcal R_{\mathcal C}}{\mathcal W_{\mathcal C}(\delta)}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Escape degree]\label{prop:A-escape-degree}
  \derived{} \knownresult[mechanism: \citealp{kumar2025learning}, Thm~8, and the accumulated utility of
  \citealp{kunin2025alternating}; new: the degree is set by the tree]
  The new entries first lower $\Loss$ at order
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-kstar}
    k^*=\min\{\abs{\mathcal C}:\ \mathcal C\text{ a route},\ \mathcal R_{\mathcal C}\ne0\}.
  \end{equation}
  At LO (old entries frozen at $\params_S$) the new entries of a minimizing route follow the gradient flow
  of a $k^*$-core TTN that fits the target $\mathcal R_{\mathcal C}$ from small initialization: the escape from
  the saddle is the first-escape problem of \cref{app:escape} for the route, with $\Kc\to k^*$ and
  $\Wt\to\mathcal R_{\mathcal C}$. This needs the old entries to sit at a local minimum of the fixed-rank
  problem; it fails at the strict saddles of \cref{prop:A-piggyback-graph}, where the old entries move
  first.
\end{proposition}
In \citet{kumar2025learning} dormant weights of lowest homogeneity $k$ stay small for a time
$\propto\delta^{-(k-2)}$, $\delta$ their initial size, provided the active weights sit at a local minimum; the
TTN content is which entries are dormant (the new entries of routes) and their degree $k^*$.

\begin{proposition}[Steiner identity]\label{prop:A-steiner}
  \proven{} (identity and upper bound on the order) \derived{} (order $=k^*$) \newresult[descent along a
  whole connected rank-deficient component: \citealp{furman2026benign}, Lemma~D.8]
  Let $S=\{j\}$. For every route $\mathcal C$,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-steiner}
    \mathcal R_{\mathcal C}=q_j^{\,\Kc-\abs{\mathcal C}}\sum_{r:\,N(D_{jr})\subseteq\mathcal C}\tw r\,\U r|_{\mathcal C},
  \end{equation}
  where $\U r|_{\mathcal C}$ is $\U r$ restricted to the sites of $\mathcal C$. Hence
  $k^*=\min_r\abs{\mathrm{St}(N(D_{jr}))}$; the route of component $r$ has degree
  $k=\abs{\mathrm{St}(N(D_{jr}))}$ and coupling
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-coupling}
    c_r=\tw r\,q_j^{\Kc-k}=\tw r\,\tw j^{(\Kc-k)/\Kc}.
  \end{equation}
  The bonds whose rank must grow to add $r$ are exactly the bonds of $\mathrm{St}(N(D_{jr}))$, so $k$ is one
  plus their number, and $k=1$ iff $D_{jr}$ lies on a single core. With route entries proportional to
  $t$, $\Loss-\Loss_S=-c_rt^{k}+O(t^{2k})$: the saddle has order at most $k^*$. That no curve descends
  faster is shown only up to order three (\cref{prop:A-hessian}); that the order equals $k^*$ is derived
  (\cref{prop:A-escape-degree}).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  The learned network beyond a boundary bond $f$ is $\Sub{f}=q_j^{n_f}\U j|_{H_f}$, with $n_f$ cores and
  sites $H_f$, and $\sum_fn_f=\Kc-\abs{\mathcal C}$. Since $\mathcal R=\sum_{r\ne j}\tw r\U r$, contracting
  $\U r$ with $\Sub{f}$ gives $q_j^{n_f}\prod_{i\in H_f}\ip{\fac ri}{\fac ji}$, which is $q_j^{n_f}$ if
  $\pi_r=\pi_j$ on $H_f$ and $0$ otherwise. So $\U r$ survives iff no site of $D_{jr}$ lies beyond
  $\mathcal C$, i.e.\ iff $N(D_{jr})\subseteq\mathcal C$, and a connected $\mathcal C$ containing $N(D_{jr})$
  contains its Steiner subtree. The rank statement is \cref{prop:teacher-spectra}(b) for the components
  $j,r$. For the descent curve, let $\delta_v$ be $t$ times the pattern of $r$ on $\sites v$, with a new
  channel on the bonds inside $\mathcal C$ and channel~1 on $\partial\mathcal C$. Then
  $\W_{\mathcal C}[\delta]=t^kq_j^{\Kc-k}\U r$, every other $\W_{\mathcal J}[\delta]$ vanishes by
  \cref{lem:A-routes}, and $\Loss-\Loss_S=-c_rt^k+\frac12q_j^{2(\Kc-k)}t^{2k}$.
\end{proof}
\Cref{tab:A-route-degree} lists $k$ for two-component pattern teachers. \citet{furman2026benign} bound
the order by the number of cores touching a connected component of rank-deficient bonds, which at a
rank-one saddle with $\chi\ge2$ is the whole tree ($\Kc$ cores), whereas $k^*$ can be as small as $2$. For
Tucker decompositions, \citet{frandsen2022optimization} exhibit descent of order 2, 3 and 4 when 2, 3 and
4 cores must move.

\begin{lemma}[Several learned components]\label{lem:A-general-S}
  \proven{} \newresult{}
  Let $E_+$ be the set of bonds $e$ with $\rank\mat{(\W_S+\U r)}{e}>\rank\mat{(\W_S)}{e}$. (i) $E_+$ is
  connected. (ii) If $E_+=\emptyset$, a single core can produce $\U r$, so $\nabla\Loss(\params_S)\ne0$ and
  $\params_S$ is not a saddle.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  (i) If the restriction of $\U r$ to one side of a bond lies outside the learned column space, so does its
  restriction to any larger side; hence every bond between two bonds of $E_+$ is in $E_+$. (ii) Walking
  from any core toward a branch on which $\U r$ is new, the walk cannot return, so it ends at a core all of
  whose branches are old; that core alone can produce $\U r$.
\end{proof}
With several learned components, adding $r$ exactly needs $k_{\rm ex}=\abs{E_+}+1$ cores, and $k^*\le k_{\rm ex}$
\derived. The inequality is strict when the learned space of a bond holds a superposition of learned
patterns that overlaps $\U r|_{H_f}$ without containing it; a cheaper route then first learns the
projection, which adds a plateau at the best fit of an intermediate rank profile (\emph{partial
learning}, \cref{rem:A-sequence}). When $k^*=k_{\rm ex}$ the coupling is
$c_r=\tw r\prod_{f\in\partial\mathcal C}\norm{\Sub{f}\T\,\U r|_{H_f}}$ in the balanced gauge \derived{}
(checked against exact Hessians at saddles with one and several learned components).

\paragraph{Saddle type.}

\begin{proposition}[Hessian at a balanced learned saddle]\label{prop:A-hessian}
  \proven{} (at $\params_S$) \newresult[that two-core descent gives a strict saddle is
  \citealp{furman2026benign}, Lemma~D.9; deep linear networks: \citealp{achour2024loss}]
  (a) The Hessian at $\params_S$ is block diagonal between the old entries and the new entries. The old
  block (the \emph{fixed-rank block}) is the Hessian of the problem with the bond ranks fixed at $r_e$:
  Gauss--Newton term plus the piggyback couplings of \cref{prop:A-piggyback-graph}.
  (b) The new block (the \emph{rank-increasing block}) vanishes except on routes with two cores: a route
  $\{u,v\}$ through the bond $e$ contributes $\big(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-B\\-B\T&0\end{smallmatrix}\big)$
  with $B=\mathcal R_{\{u,v\}}$, for each of the $c_e$ new channels, hence eigenvalues $\pm\sigma_k(B)$ with
  multiplicity $c_e$. With one learned component the negative one is $-\lambda$,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-lambda2}
    \lambda=\sigma_{\max}(\mathcal R_{\{u,v\}})=\tw r\,\tw j^{(\Kc-2)/\Kc}.
  \end{equation}
  (c) Hence $\params_S$ is a strict saddle iff the fixed-rank block has a negative eigenvalue or a route
  with $k^*=2$ exists. If $k^*=1$, $\params_S$ is not critical (with one learned component
  $\norm{\nabla\Loss(\params_S)}=\tw rq_j^{\Kc-1}$). If $k^*\ge3$ and the fixed-rank block is positive
  semidefinite, the Hessian has no negative eigenvalue: $\params_S$ is a higher-order saddle.
  (d) The gauge orbit through $\params_S$ has dimension $\sum_e[\bd{e}^2-(\bd{e}-r_e)^2]$, and the kernel of
  the Hessian has dimension $\sum_er_e^2+\dim\params_{\rm new}-2\sum_{k^*=2\text{ routes}}c_e$, where
  $\dim\params_{\rm new}$ counts the new entries.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
  An old--new cross term of $\ip{\mathcal R}{\W_{\mathcal J}}$ needs an old index to meet a new one, which gives
  zero, and the Gauss--Newton cross term vanishes by \cref{lem:A-routes}(i). On the new entries the only
  second-order terms are $-\ip{\mathcal R}{\W_{\{u,v\}}[\delta]}$ for adjacent pairs, and with one learned
  component $\mathcal R_{\{u,v\}}=q_j^{\Kc-2}\sum_r\tw r\U r|_{\{u,v\}}$ by \eqref{eq:A-steiner}. The gauge
  generators supported on the new--new blocks act trivially at $\params_S$, so the stabilizer has dimension
  $\sum_e(\bd{e}-r_e)^2$.
\end{proof}
Checked at exact saddles in 21 (tree, $D$) cases: $\norm{\nabla\Loss}=\tw rq_j^{\Kc-1}$ for $k=1$,
$\lambda$ to six digits with multiplicity $\chi-1$ for $k=2$, no negative eigenvalue for $k\ge3$ in the
correct order, and the kernel and orbit dimensions of (d). The negative curvature of a $k=2$ route is the
same $\lambda$ that sets its escape rate (\cref{prop:A-head-start}).

\begin{proposition}[Piggyback block and piggyback graph]\label{prop:A-piggyback-graph}
  \proven{} \newresult[for general trees; deep linear networks: \citealp{achour2024loss}, Thm~7; odeco
  components are local optima of rank-one approximation: \citealp{anandkumar2014tensor}]
  Let $S=\{j\}$. A \emph{piggyback direction} keeps the learned bond index and rotates the physical vectors
  of one terminal core $u$ toward a site pattern $w$ on $\sites u$. (i) For one residual component $r$ with
  $m_{jr}=2$ and terminal cores $u,v$ the rotation block is
  $\big(\begin{smallmatrix}q_j^{2(\Kc-1)}&-\tw rq_j^{\Kc-2}\\-\tw rq_j^{\Kc-2}&q_j^{2(\Kc-1)}\end{smallmatrix}\big)$,
  with eigenvalues $q_j^{\Kc-2}(\tw j\mp\tw r)$; for $m_{jr}\ge3$ it is $q_j^{2(\Kc-1)}\Id\succ0$. (ii) In
  general the rotation block is
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-piggyback}
    H_{\rm rot}=q_j^{\Kc-2}\big(\tw j\Id-\Gamma\big),
  \end{equation}
  where $\Gamma$ is the weighted adjacency matrix of the \emph{piggyback graph}: its nodes are the pairs
  $(u,w)$ of a terminal core and a rotated site pattern, with one edge between $(u,\pi_r|_u)$ and
  $(v,\pi_r|_v)$, of weight $\tw r$, for every residual component $r$ with $N(D_{jr})=\{u,v\}$. Hence
  $\lambda_{\min}(H_{\rm rot})=q_j^{\Kc-2}(\tw j-\lambda_{\max}(\Gamma))$, and $\params_j$ is a strict saddle iff
  $\lambda_{\max}(\Gamma)>\tw j$ or a route with $k^*=2$ exists.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  Rotating core $u$ by $\varepsilon_u$ toward $w$ creates the mixed tensor (pattern $w$ on $\sites u$,
  pattern of $j$ elsewhere) of norm $q_j^{\Kc-1}\varepsilon_u$. Mixed tensors of different nodes are
  orthonormal, so the Gauss--Newton part is diagonal, with entries $q_j^{2(\Kc-1)}=\tw jq_j^{\Kc-2}$. A
  residual $r$ with terminals $\{u,v\}$ couples the two rotations through
  $-\ip{\mathcal R}{\W_{\{u,v\}}}=-\tw rq_j^{\Kc-2}\varepsilon_u\varepsilon_v$; residuals with $m_{jr}\ge3$ add no
  quadratic term, and $\U r$ itself appears only at order $m_{jr}$. The radial part (rescaling the learned
  component) is positive semidefinite.
\end{proof}
A \emph{star} (residuals $r_1,\dots,r_n$ sharing one node) has $\lambda_{\max}(\Gamma)=(\sum_i\tw{r_i}^2)^{1/2}$,
and a triangle of equal weights $s$ has $\lambda_{\max}(\Gamma)=2s$. So $\params_j$ can be a strict saddle
although every residual is smaller than $\tw j$ and every route has $k^*\ge3$; with $d=2$, any two residuals
that differ from $j$ on the same single-site core share a node. The criterion was checked to six digits
in five strict cases and three sub-critical controls. Dynamics \empirical: for $j=0000$ (weight 2) and
$r=1010$, $r'=1001$ (weight $1.6$ each) on the MPS with $\Ns=4$, $\lambda_{\max}(\Gamma)=2.263>2$ and
$\lambda_{\min}=-0.3716$; gradient flow passes near $j$, forgets it ($\ov j$ falls to $0.01$--$0.10$), learns
$r$ and $r'$, and relearns $j$ (5 of 5 seeds), whereas with weights $1.2$ ($\lambda_{\max}(\Gamma)=1.70<2$) it
plateaus at $j$ (3 of 3 seeds). Learning-order experiments with three or more components should
therefore compute $\lambda_{\max}(\Gamma)$ for their teachers.

\begin{remark}[Binary tree with $\Ns=4$]\label{rem:A-binary4-dln}
  \proven{} \knownresult[\citealp{saxe2014exact,jacot2022saddletosaddle,achour2024loss}]
  Write the bottom cores as matrices $\tilde A,\tilde B\in\R^{d^2\times\chi}$ (sites $(0,1)$ and $(2,3)$
  as rows) and the root as a $\chi\times\chi$ matrix. Then
  $\mat{\W}{e}=\tilde A\,\G{\vroot}\tilde B\T$ on both bonds, and $\Loss$ is exactly the loss of a depth-3
  linear network fitting the $d^2\times d^2$ matrix $\mat{(\Wt)}{e}$. Its saddles are SVD truncations
  \citep{jacot2022saddletosaddle}; critical points that select non-top singular values are strict, while
  balanced ones that select the top values are not \citep[Thm~7; their full-rank assumption is not met by
  our low-rank targets, which the computation above covers]{achour2024loss}. Every statement of this
  appendix about this tree is therefore a statement about deep linear networks.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[After a wrong first escape]\label{rem:A-wrong-order}
  \derived{} \newresult{} Two-component teachers with the smaller component $j$ learned first; $\varepsilon$
  is the piggyback amplitude and $b$ the route amplitude when the plateau starts. (1) $k\ge3$, $m=2$
  (matrix-like pair): the learned bond index rotates from $j$ to $r$ after
  $\Delta t\approx\ln(1/\varepsilon)/((\tw r-\tw j)\tw j^{(\Kc-2)/\Kc})$, the loss plateaus at
  $\frac12\tw j^2$ with $\ov j\approx0$, and $j$ is relearned through its route after a time
  $\propto\isc_{\rm eff}^{-(k-2)}$. (2) $k=2$: $r$ is added directly, at the larger rate $\tw r\tw j^{(\Kc-2)/\Kc}$,
  and $j$ is kept. (3) $m\ge3$ (tensor-like pair): no rotation; $r$ is added through its route after
  $\Delta t\approx b^{-(k-2)}/((k-2)\tw r\tw j^{(\Kc-k)/\Kc})$ and $\ov j$ stays at $1$. Hence a correction
  through a strict saddle exists only for matrix-like pairs, exactly where the wrong order becomes rare
  as $\isc\to0$ (\cref{prop:A-matrix-like}). Checked on binary and MPS trees with $\Ns=4$ (rotation times
  within $9\%$ of the prediction; escape at $t=4999.97$ against $5000$), in runs made before the random
  streams of teacher and initialization were separated.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Escape times.}
Let the route of component $r$ have $k=\abs{\mathcal C}$ cores and amplitudes $b_v$, $v\in\mathcal C$ (its new
entries: the pattern of $r$ on the physical legs, a new channel on the internal bonds, channel~1 on
$\partial\mathcal C$). With the old entries frozen and one learned component, put $A:=q_j^{\Kc-k}$;
\eqref{eq:A-expansion} and \cref{lem:A-routes} give the closed route dynamics
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A-route-ode}
  \dot b_v=A\prod_{u\in\mathcal C\setminus v}b_u\Big(\tw r-A\prod_{u\in\mathcal C}b_u\Big),\qquad
  \ov r=\frac{A}{\tw r}\prod_{u\in\mathcal C}b_u ,
\end{equation}
a depth-$k$ mode with target $\tw r/A$ that conserves the differences $b_u^2-b_v^2$.

\begin{proposition}[Escape times with head start]\label{prop:A-head-start}
  \derived{} \newresult[for $k\ge3$; the $k=2$ exponent is known: \citealp{zhang2026saddletosaddle},
  Thm~4, and \citealp{kunin2025alternating}; the trichotomy in $k$: \citealp{rawal2026theory}, Thm~4.2]
  Let the initialization be aligned: every core of the learned component $j$ and of the route starts at
  amplitude $\isc$, and the hanging cores are aligned with the shared pattern. Let
  $\Delta t_2=t(\ov r=\frac12)-t(\ov j=\frac12)$. As $\isc\to0$,
  \begin{align}
    k=2:&\quad \Delta t_2=\frac{(1-\tw r/\tw j)\ln(1/\isc)+O(1)}{\tw r\,\tw j^{(\Kc-2)/\Kc}},\label{eq:A-dt-k2}\\
    k\ge3:&\quad \Delta t_2=\frac{\isc^{-(k-2)}\,(1-\tw r/\tw j)}{(k-2)\,\tw r\,\tw j^{(\Kc-k)/\Kc}}\big(1+o(1)\big),\label{eq:A-dt-k3}
  \end{align}
  and $\Delta t_2=O(1)$ (no plateau) for $k=1$. For $k\ge3$ the route thus enters the plateau with the
  effective amplitude $\isc_{\rm eff}=\isc(1-\tw r/\tw j)^{-1/(k-2)}$. For $k=\Kc$ the route never sees $j$, and
  $r$ escapes at the absolute time $\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}/((\Kc-2)\tw r)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Derivation]
  The route exists from the start, with the then-small learned cores in its hanging part, so it grows
  during the rise of $j$ with the instantaneous coupling $c(t)=\tw rq_j(t)^{\Kc-k}$, where
  $\dot q_j\simeq\tw jq_j^{\Kc-1}$ while $q_j$ is small. For $k=2$, $\ln b$ grows by
  $\int c\,\dd t=\int\tw rq_j^{\Kc-2}\,\dd q_j/(\tw jq_j^{\Kc-1})=(\tw r/\tw j)\ln(1/\isc)+O(1)$, so the route
  enters the plateau with $\ln b_{\rm eff}=-(1-\tw r/\tw j)\ln(1/\isc)+O(1)$ and needs a further time
  $\ln(1/b_{\rm eff})/\lambda$, with $\lambda$ from \eqref{eq:A-lambda2}. For $k\ge3$ the balanced route obeys
  $\dot b=c(t)\,b^{k-1}$, i.e.\ $\ddt b^{-(k-2)}=-(k-2)c(t)$, so that at the end of the rise of $j$
  \[
    b_{\rm eff}^{-(k-2)}=\isc^{-(k-2)}-(k-2)\int c\,\dd t=\isc^{-(k-2)}\big(1-\tw r/\tw j\big)+O(1),
  \]
  and the plateau lasts $b_{\rm eff}^{-(k-2)}/((k-2)\tw r\tw j^{(\Kc-k)/\Kc})$. The integrand
  $q_j^{\Kc-k}\dd t=q_j^{1-k}\dd q_j/\tw j$ is largest at $q_j\approx\isc$: for $k\ge3$ the head start accrues
  during the first plateau, while the learned cores are still of order $\isc$.
\end{proof}
The factor $1-\tw r/\tw j$ is the head start. Without it the slope of $\Delta t_2$ against $\ln(1/\isc)$ for
$k=2$ would be $1/\lambda$ instead of $(1-\tw r/\tw j)/\lambda$, which separates the two pictures
experimentally (\cref{sec:s2s-locality}). Checked: with aligned initialization, $k=2$ slopes $1.0067$ and
$0.78408$ against $1.0000$ and $0.78405$, and $\Delta t_2$ divided by \eqref{eq:A-dt-k3} tends to $1.0000$ for
$k=3,4,5$ down to $\isc=3\cdot10^{-5}$; with random fan-in initialization, $k=2$ slopes $1.05$ and $1.066$
against $1.00$ and $1.065$, and per-seed exponents $-0.97$ to $-1.02$ ($k=3$) and $-1.99$ to $-2.00$
($k=4$). For an unbalanced route with positive product,
$\Delta t=\int_0^\infty\dd\beta\big/\big(2c\prod_{v\in\mathcal C}(b_v^2+\beta)^{1/2}\big)$ \derived, as in
\cref{prop:A-product-mode}; if the product is negative, the smallest $\abs{b_v}$ first passes through
zero, which lengthens the plateau. With random initialization the prefactors are not predicted by $\isc$
alone: they are set by the route amplitudes at the start of each plateau, which vary between seeds by
factors above 20 for $k\ge4$ \empirical.

\begin{proposition}[Signatures of the route degree]\label{prop:A-signatures}
  \derived{} \knownresult[the exponent $2-2/k$ of a balanced product of $k$ factors:
  \citealp{arora2019implicit,razin2022implicit}; new: its use to read $k$ off TTN plateaus]
  (i) Per seed (fixed initialization direction, $\isc$ varied), $\Delta t_2\propto\isc^{-(k-2)}$ for $k\ge3$.
  (ii) On a balanced route, \eqref{eq:A-route-ode} gives
  $\dot{\ov r}=kA^{2/k}\tw r^{2-2/k}\ov r^{\,2-2/k}(1-\ov r)$, so the local growth exponent is
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-growth-exponent}
    \gamma(\ov r)=\frac{\dd\ln\dot{\ov r}}{\dd\ln\ov r}=2-\frac2k-\frac{\ov r}{1-\ov r},
  \end{equation}
  that is $1,\frac43,\frac32,\frac85$ for $k=2,\dots,5$ as $\ov r\to0$. The route is balanced only once
  $b^2\gg\abs{b_u^2-b_v^2}=O(\isc^2)$, so $k$ is read in the window $\ov r\in[10^{-4},10^{-2}]$; a smaller
  $\gamma$ at smaller $\ov r$ indicates an unbalanced route, not a smaller $k$. (iii) At a plateau point,
  where the route amplitudes are small but nonzero, each active route contributes one negative
  Hessian direction modulo gauge, with
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-plateau-lambda}
    \lambda_{\min}\simeq-\tw r\tw j^{(\Kc-2)/\Kc}\ \ (k=2),\qquad
    \lambda_{\min}\simeq-(k-1)\,\tw rA\,b^{k-2}=O(\isc_{\rm eff}^{k-2})\ \ (k\ge3),
  \end{equation}
  where $b=(\tw r\ov r/A)^{1/k}$. The count of one negative eigenvalue per route holds for $k\le3$ only: at
  $k=4$, new-index pairs that connect to learned structure add indefinite terms of order $\isc^2$, the
  same order as the route eigenvalue.
\end{proposition}
In (iii), the Hessian on the route block is $-\tw rAb^{k-2}(\mathbf 1\mathbf 1\T-\Id)$; its $k-1$ positive
directions rescale the amplitudes at fixed product, which is a gauge motion. Checked: $\gamma=0.995$--$1.000$,
$1.334$--$1.344$, $1.490$--$1.500$ for $k=2,3,4$, and $\abs{\lambda_{\min}}/\lambda_{\max}$ at plateau midpoints
scaling as $\isc^0$, $\isc^1$, $\isc^2$ for $k=2,3,4$ (MPS, $\Ns=4$). For the diagnostics of
\cref{sec:setup-diagnostics} this means that the fixed relative threshold $10^{-3}$ calls $k=3$ plateaus
strict for $\isc\gtrsim0.01$--$0.04$ (depending on the seed); the saddle type should be read from the
scaling of $\lambda_{\min}$ with $\isc$ (constant: strict; $\propto\isc^{k-2}$: higher order) or from
$\gamma$.

\begin{remark}[The whole sequence]\label{rem:A-sequence}
  \derived{} at LO for each step; \conjecture{} (heuristic) as an algorithm. \newresult{}
  (1) The first escape ends near a critical point of the bond-rank-one problem; it is a single component
  $j$ only if $\params_j$ is a local maximum of the spherical problem (\cref{prop:A-component-hessian}, with
  the piggyback graph when several partners share a core); a star with $\lambda_{\max}(\Gamma)>\tw j$ sends the
  flow past $j$ toward a superposition. (2) At every later saddle, if the fixed-rank block has a negative
  eigenvalue (wrong order, stars), the next event is a rotation of the learned structure or a $k=2$ route,
  whichever is more unstable, in time $\propto\ln(1/\isc)$, and \cref{prop:A-escape-degree} does not apply.
  Otherwise the next event is the route of smallest $k^*$ (logarithmic beats power law, smaller powers
  beat larger ones); ties are decided by the coupling and the head start, $c\,b_{\rm eff}^{k-2}$, which for
  $k\ge3$ are dominated by the random route amplitudes. (3) The escape raises the bond rank by one on the
  bonds inside the route, and the next saddle is the best approximation of $\Wt$ with the new rank profile
  (for pattern teachers without superpositions, the partial sum of the learned components).
  (4) Plateau levels are the optimal values of these rank-constrained problems.
\end{remark}
Because $k^*$ depends on the teacher, the tree and the learned set, \emph{locality beats weight}: a
smaller component with a smaller $k^*$ is learned first, and the same teacher gives different sequences
on different trees. Examples (checked numerically): for the teacher $0000$ (weight 1), $0110$ ($0.3$),
$1001$ ($0.6$), the MPS with $\Ns=4$ learns $0000$, then $0110$ ($k=2$, logarithmic gap), then $1001$ ($k=4$,
gap $\propto\isc^{-2}$), while the binary tree learns $0000$ and then $1001$ and $0110$ ($k=3$ each, gaps
$\propto\isc^{-1}$). For $0000$ (1), $1100$ ($0.6$), $1111$ ($0.4$) on the MPS with $\Ns=4$ a cheaper $k=2$ route
learns part of $1111$ first (partial learning): the levels are $0.76$, $0.26$, $0.08$, $0.0569$, $0$, and every
gap after the first is logarithmic, although adding $1111$ exactly would need three cores. The number of
cores that must change at once plays the role of the leap of \citet{abbe2023sgd}; what is specific to
the TTN is that it is set by the tree.

\begin{proposition}[Conservation-law trap]\label{prop:A-trap}
  \derived{} (mechanism, in a reduced model) \newresult{}
  Let a route end at a leaf core. On the bond $e$ of that end (child $v$, parent $p$) let $\nu$ be the new
  channel used by the route and $Q_{\nu\nu}=\nu\T\Q{e}\nu$. Let $x$ be the leaf's new column, and split the
  parent's slice along $\nu$ into its route entry $y_{\rm route}$ and the \emph{visible} entries $y_{\rm vis}$,
  which connect through the other side to learned structure. Conservation gives
  $\norm{x}^2-\norm{y_{\rm vis}}^2-\norm{y_{\rm route}}^2=Q_{\nu\nu}$. On the plateau the Gauss--Newton term makes
  $x$ and $y_{\rm vis}$ decay together, at a rate of order $\abs{Q_{\nu\nu}}=O(\isc^2)$, so the leaf column decays
  to zero iff the end is \emph{unfavourable},
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-trap}
    Q_{\nu\nu}+\norm{y_{\rm route}}^2<0 .
  \end{equation}
  The route's drive on $x$ is proportional to the product of the other route amplitudes, so while another
  end is favourable it pins $x$ at a positive value and the route escapes, slowly. A permanent stall
  ($x\to0$ exponentially, down to roundoff) therefore requires every end of the route to be unfavourable.
\end{proposition}
The dead state is linearly stable if $\gamma_1\gamma_2>\kappa^2$, with decay rates $\gamma_i\sim\abs{Q_i}\sim\isc^2$
and mutual drive $\kappa\sim\tw rA\prod_{\rm inner}b\sim\isc^{k-2}$ (\conjecture, heuristic): stable as
$\isc\to0$ for $k\ge5$, never for $k=3$, undecided for $k=4$. On HT leaf bonds ($\bd v=d=2$ below a parent
bond of dimension $3$), fan-in initialization gives $Q_{\nu\nu}=\isc^2(X_2/2-X_6/4)$ with independent
chi-squared variables $X_n$ of $n$ degrees of freedom (if $\nu$ is independent of the initialization), so
$\E Q_{\nu\nu}=-\isc^2/2$, $P(Q_{\nu\nu}>0)=(3/2)^{-3}\approx0.30$, and about half of the two-ended routes have
both ends unfavourable (\conjecture, heuristic). Data \empirical{} (HT, $\Ns=4$, the teacher $0000$, $0110$,
$1001$ above, $\isc=0.2$, nine fan-in initializations): every run whose route ends were all unfavourable
stalled with dead leaves (6 runs), each run with one favourable end had the other end pinned (2 runs), and
one run had no stall. The trapped point is not a local minimum, but gradient flow does not move along its
flat directions. Gauge-balanced initialization ($\Q{e}=0$, same $\Wz$) kept the routes alive (3 of 3 runs);
that it always does, and that the stalled fraction tends to $P(\text{all ends unfavourable})$ for
$k\ge5$ as $\isc\to0$, are conjectures. The trap is a property of unbalanced initialization, not of the
landscape; experiments on HT trees should report the stalled fraction and $Q_{\nu\nu}$ at every route end
(\cref{sec:s2s-locality}).

\subsection{Teachers that are not orthogonally decomposable}\label{app:teachers}

For a general teacher the saddles are no longer partial sums of components. Notation:
$\mathcal M_{\mathbf r}$ is the manifold of tensors with bond ranks exactly $\mathbf r=(r_e)_e$
\citep{holtz2012manifolds,uschmajew2013geometry}. For $\W\in\mathcal M_{\mathbf r}$ and $e=\edge v$, $p=\pa v$,
$U_e$ and $V_e$ are the column and row spaces of $\mat{\W}{e}$; the environment spaces of $e$ are
$E_e^-=(\bigotimes_{c\in\ch v}U_c)\otimes\R^{d_v}$ and $E_e^+=(\bigotimes_{c\in\ch p\setminus\{v\}}U_c)\otimes\R^{d_p}\otimes V_p$,
with $d_v=\prod_{i\in\sites v}\pd[i]$ (no $V_p$ factor at the root); and
$\widetilde W^*_e:=P_{E_e^-}\,\mat{(\Wt)}{e}\,P_{E_e^+}$ is the \emph{environment-projected teacher}.

\begin{proposition}[Critical points of the rank-constrained problem]\label{prop:A-critical-points}
  \proven{} \knownresult[in substance: the fixed-point conditions of HOOI and of one-site ALS;
  tangent spaces: \citealp{uschmajew2013geometry}]
  % TODO-CITE: De Lathauwer, De Moor & Vandewalle (2000, SIMAX 21; HOOI); Elden & Savas (2011, SIMAX
  % 32(4)); Holtz, Rohwedder & Schneider (2012, SISC 34, ALS in TT). Not in notes/literature.md sec. 7.
  $\W\in\mathcal M_{\mathbf r}$ is a critical point of $\Loss$ restricted to $\mathcal M_{\mathbf r}$ iff, for every bond $e$,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-critical-points}
    \mat{\W}{e}=P_{U_e}\widetilde W^*_e=\widetilde W^*_eP_{V_e},
  \end{equation}
  that is, iff $U_e$ is spanned by $r_e$ left singular vectors of $\widetilde W^*_e$ (not necessarily the top
  ones) and $\mat{\W}{e}$ is the corresponding SVD truncation; equivalently, iff $\W$ is a fixed point of
  one-site alternating least squares. Padded with zeros, such a point is a critical point of the
  parameterized loss for every $\bd{e}\ge r_e$ (\cref{lem:A-unused}).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
  At a parameterization with full bond ranks the differential with respect to $\G v$ spans the local space
  $\mathcal V_v=E^-_{e}\otimes V_e$ ($e=\edge v$), so $\W$ is critical iff $P_{\mathcal V_v}\Wt=\W$ for every $v$. For
  $e=\edge v$ this reads $\widetilde W^*_eP_{V_e}=\mat{\W}{e}$ at core $v$ and $P_{U_e}\widetilde W^*_e=\mat{\W}{e}$ at core
  $p$. If $P_UA=AP_V=X$ has rank $r$ for subspaces $U,V$ of dimension $r$, then $P_UAA\T=XX\T$ is
  symmetric, so $P_U$ commutes with $AA\T$ and $U$ is spanned by left singular vectors of $A$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[When is the hierarchical SVD critical?]\label{prop:A-hsvd}
  \proven{} (a, b) \derived{} (c) \newresult[the $\varepsilon^4$ gap; the HSVD is
  \citealp{grasedyck2010hierarchical}]
  Let $\W_H$ be the HSVD truncation of $\Wt$ at ranks $\mathbf r$. (a) $\W_H$ is critical iff its frames
  satisfy \eqref{eq:A-critical-points}; for generic teachers they do not, because the parts of $\Wt$
  discarded at other bonds leak into the environment of each bond. (b) If the best rank-$r_e$
  approximation of $\Wt$ across every bond $e$ is one and the same tensor $\W$ (consistent truncations:
  for example an odeco teacher truncated to the same components on every bond, or the binary tree with
  $\Ns=4$, whose two bonds induce the same bipartition), then $\W_H=\W$ is critical and is the global
  minimizer over bond ranks at most $\mathbf r$. (c) If $\Wt=\W+\varepsilon E$ with $\W$ consistent and $E$
  generic, the nearby critical point $\W_c$ satisfies $\normF{\W_H-\W_c}=O(\varepsilon^2)$ and
  $\Loss(\W_H)-\Loss(\W_c)=\tfrac12\normF{\W_H-\W_c}^2\,(1+O(\varepsilon))=O(\varepsilon^4)$.
\end{proposition}
For (b), $\normF{\Wt-X}\ge\normF{\Wt-\W}$ for every $X$ with a single bond rank at most $r_e$. For (c), both
sets of frames are first-order rotations of those of $\W$ with the same driving terms, and the residual
$\Wt-\W_c$ is normal to $\mathcal M_{\mathbf r}$ at $\W_c$ while $\W_H-\W_c$ is tangent up to curvature
terms. Checked: criticality defects of the HSVD between $0.004$ and $0.18$ for random TTN teachers,
independent of $\isc$; in (c) the exponents hold on every draw and the ratio
$(\Loss(\W_H)-\Loss(\W_c))/(\frac12\normF{\W_H-\W_c}^2)$ is within $3\%$ of 1 for $\varepsilon\le0.2$.

\paragraph{The rank-one plateau.} \derived{} \newresult{} By \cref{app:escape} the origin plateau ends along a
KKT direction of $g$. The global maximum of $g$ has bond rank one on every bond (\cref{prop:A-gmax}); that
local maxima do too is a conjecture, true at every rank-one plateau computed. The next plateau therefore
sits at a critical point of the bond-rank-one problem, not at an HSVD truncation. If every core carries at
most one site (MPS, HT), bond rank one means $\W=\bar s\bigotimes_iw_i$ with unit vectors $w_i$: the plateau
is a local best rank-one approximation of $\Wt$, with $\bar s=\ip{\Wt}{\bigotimes_iw_i}$ and level
$\Loss_1=\frac12(\normF{\Wt}^2-\bar s^2)$. On the binary tree with $\Ns=4$ it is the top singular pair of
the matricization (\cref{rem:A-binary4-dln}), a unique maximum. In the cases computed the visited plateau
lies below the HSVD truncation at the same ranks (by $6$--$10\%$ of its level) \empirical, but a plateau in
a minor basin is a worse critical point, so this is not general.

\begin{proposition}[Two non-orthogonal components]\label{prop:A-two-components}
  \proven{} (stationarity; threshold among symmetric critical points) \derived{} (fold)
  \newresult[elementary] Let $\Wt=\tw1\U1+\tw2\U2$ with $\Ns$ sites, $\tw1\ge\tw2$ and overlaps
  $\ip{\fac1i}{\fac2i}=\cos\phi_i\in[0,1)$. Every rank-one critical point has $w_i$ in the plane of
  $\fac1i,\fac2i$; with $\psi_i$ the angle of $w_i$ from $\fac1i$ its value is
  $f(\psi)=\tw1\prod_i\cos\psi_i+\tw2\prod_i\cos(\phi_i-\psi_i)$. For equal weights and equal overlaps
  $\cos\phi$, the symmetric point $\psi_i=\phi/2$ has the Hessian eigenvalue
  $-2\cos^{\Ns-2}\frac\phi2\,[\cos^2\frac\phi2-(\Ns-1)\sin^2\frac\phi2]$ along $(1,\dots,1)$ and
  $-2\cos^{\Ns-2}\frac\phi2$ on its complement (per unit weight), so there are two local maxima, one near
  each component, iff
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-cstar}
    \cos\phi<\frac{\Ns-2}{\Ns},
  \end{equation}
  and one merged maximum otherwise (asymmetric maxima were excluded numerically). For $\varrho=\tw2/\tw1<1$
  the maximum near $\U2$ disappears at a fold,
  $\cos\phi_{\rm fold}\approx\frac{\Ns-2}{\Ns-1}\big(\frac{\varrho}{\Ns-1}\big)^{1/(\Ns-2)}$ for small $\varrho$ ($1$--$13\%$
  below the exact values). The rank-one plateau is never one of the components.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Rates at the rank-one saddle of an MPS]\label{prop:A-mps-rates}
  \derived{} (rates) \proven{} (bound) \newresult{}
  Let the student be an MPS ($\Kc=\Ns$, core $k$ carries site $k$) at the balanced, zero-padded rank-one
  critical point $\W_1=\bar s\bigotimes_iw_i$, a local maximum of the rank-one problem, and let
  $M_e=\Wt\times_{i\notin\{k,k+1\}}w_i$ be the $d\times d$ matrix of the bond $e$ between cores $k$ and $k+1$.
  Then $(w_k,w_{k+1})$ is the top singular pair of $M_e$, with singular value $\bar s$, and the two-core
  route on $e$ contributes the Hessian eigenvalues $-\bar s^{(\Ns-2)/\Ns}\sigma_m(M_e)$, $m=2,\dots,d$, each
  with multiplicity $\bd e-1$. The escape rate of bond $e$ is
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-gamma-e}
    \gamma_e=\bar s^{(\Ns-2)/\Ns}\,\sigma_2(M_e),\qquad\text{with}\quad
    \sigma_m(M_e)\le\sigma_m\big(\mat{(\Wt)}{e}\big)
  \end{equation}
  by interlacing, since $M_e$ is a compression of $\mat{(\Wt)}{e}$ by isometries; equality holds when the
  teacher's Schmidt vectors are products with $w$ on the sites away from the bond.
\end{proposition}
The route block of $e$ is $\bar s^{(\Ns-2)/\Ns}(M_e-\bar s\,w_kw_{k+1}\T)$, whose singular values are those of
$M_e$ without the top one. For two components with $d=2$, $\det M_e=\tw1\tw2a_eb_e\sin\phi_k\sin\phi_{k+1}$ with
$a_e=\prod_{i\ne k,k+1}\cos\psi_i$ and $b_e=\prod_{i\ne k,k+1}\cos(\phi_i-\psi_i)$, so
$\sigma_2(M_e)=\det M_e/\bar s$: \emph{the bond that grows first is the one whose two sites differ most while
the other sites agree most}, not the one with the largest teacher Schmidt value. Checked: Hessian
eigenvalues at the exact saddle to four digits; at gradient-flow plateaus $\lambda_{\min}=-0.1474$ against
$-\gamma_e=-0.1474$.

\begin{corollary}[Second escape]\label{cor:A-second-escape}
  \derived{} \newresult[\cref{prop:A-head-start} with $\tw j\to\bar s$ and $\tw r\to\sigma_2(M_e)$]
  Let $\kappa_e=\sigma_2(M_e)/\bar s$. During the first escape the route of bond $e$ grows by the factor
  $\isc^{-\kappa_e}$, so the first bond to gain rank is $e^*=\arg\min_eh_e$ and
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-second-escape}
    \tev{e^*}-\tev1=h_{e^*}\ln(1/\isc)+O(1),\qquad h_e=\frac{1-\kappa_e}{\gamma_e}.
  \end{equation}
  On an MPS $\arg\min_eh_e=\arg\max_e\sigma_2(M_e)$.
\end{corollary}
Here $\tev e$ is the first time the second singular value across $e$ exceeds a fraction $f\in[0.03,0.1]$ of
the teacher's; the 50\%-of-final rule of \cref{sec:setup-diagnostics} can fire only after later escapes,
because the intermediate saddles have second singular values well below the final ones. Checked: slopes
$5.853$ and $17.255$ (two components, equal overlaps $0.5$ and $0.3$) measured within $1\%$; for overlaps
$(0.2,0.5,0.7,0.3)$ the teacher's second Schmidt values are nearly tied ($0.567,0.566,0.548$) while
$\sigma_2(M_e)=(0.127,0.036,0.059)$, and bond 0 grows first in every run.

\begin{proposition}[Later escapes on an MPS: an accumulated-utility race]\label{prop:A-agf-race}
  \derived{} (as $\isc\to0$, under (A-carry)) \knownresult[the rule: alternating gradient flows,
  \citealp{kunin2025alternating}; new: the TTN routes and their rates]
  Give every bond $e$ that has not yet grown the log-amplitude $\ell_e=\ln x_e/\ln(1/\isc)\in[-1,0]$ of its
  route and use the clock $\vartheta=(t-\tev1)/\ln(1/\isc)$. Then $\ell_e(0)=-1+\kappa_e$; while the flow sits
  at the saddle $S_n$, $\dd\ell_e/\dd\vartheta=\gamma_e(S_n)$, the rate of the route at the exact critical point
  $S_n$; bond $e$ escapes when $\ell_e=0$, after which the flow moves in time $O(1)$ to $S_{n+1}$, the
  critical point with the rank of $e$ raised by one. (A-carry): at each transition a pending route's
  amplitude projects onto its new unstable direction with a $\isc$-independent factor $p\ne0$.
\end{proposition}
Event times are $\tev e-\tev1=\vartheta_e\ln(1/\isc)+C_e+o(1)$ with offsets $C_e$ that depend on the
initialization (measured up to about 50 time units), so near-tied later escapes come in a seed-dependent
order at accessible $\isc$ (\conjecture, heuristic, at finite $\isc$). Unlike greedy low-rank learning
\citep{li2021towards}, the race keeps memory across saddles. Checked (two components, overlaps
$(0.2,0.5,0.7,0.3)$, 8 seeds): slopes $6.823$, $12.178$, $14.467$ for the three bonds, confirmed to $0.5\%$ at
$\isc\le10^{-6}$ in 7 of 8 seeds; (A-carry) holds with $p=0.775$ at one transition.

\paragraph{Generic teachers: dependence on the tree.} \derived{} \newresult{} At any critical point, the local
residual of an adjacent pair $\{u,v\}$ through the bond $e$ annihilates the learned subspaces on both
sides, so it lives on complements of dimensions $\mathsf d_u-r_e$ and $\mathsf d_v-r_e$, where $\mathsf d_u$ is
the dimension of the legs of $u$ other than $e$ (physical legs and learned outer bonds). For a generic
teacher it is nonzero there: \emph{a route with $k^*=2$ (strict saddle, logarithmic escape) exists whenever
some adjacent pair has $\mathsf d_u>r_e$ and $\mathsf d_v>r_e$.} Consequences: (i) on an MPS every core has a
physical leg, so a generic teacher gives one polynomial plateau (the origin plateau) and then only
logarithmic ones, with ranks growing bond by bond (consistent with the plateaus and abrupt rank growth
seen with gradient descent on tensor-train factorizations, \citealp{milanesi2021implicit}); (ii) on binary
and HT trees at the rank-one saddle the
internal cores carry no site and have rank-one outer legs, so the cheapest routes join two sibling bottom
cores through their parent ($k=3$): later escapes are polynomial even for generic teachers, and the root
bipartition grows only after both halves have (bottom-up order); (iii) odeco and pattern teachers suppress
the two-core routes by orthogonality, so the power-law plateaus of \cref{prop:A-head-start} are a property
of teachers whose components are orthogonal on the relevant site blocks, not of TTNs in general. Checked
on one random CP teacher: on the MPS with $\Ns=4$, $\lambda_{\min}=-0.129,-0.132,-0.136$ at
$\isc=0.05,0.025,0.0125$ (strict) with logarithmic gaps; on the binary tree $\lambda_{\min}\propto\isc$ and gaps
$\propto\isc^{-1}$.

\paragraph{Teacher on another tree.} \proven{} \knownresult[standard; cf.\ \citealp{hackbusch2009new}]
A student bond $e$ must reach
$\rank\mat{(\Wt)}{e}$, which for a TTN teacher is at most the minimum, over cuts of the teacher's tree that
separate the two sides of $e$, of the product of the cut dimensions (bond dimensions and physical
dimensions). For example, a binary student with $\Ns=8$ of an MPS teacher with bond dimension $r$ needs
rank $\min(d^2,r^2)$ on the bonds of sites $\{2,3\}$ and $\{4,5\}$, i.e.\ about $r^2$ rank increments, each
a separate saddle. A binary student with $\Ns=4$ is the deep linear network of \cref{rem:A-binary4-dln}
for any teacher: its plateaus sit at $\frac12\sum_{i>m}\sigma_i(\mat{(\Wt)}{(01|23)})^2$, every escape has
degree three ($\propto\isc^{-1}$), and the number of plateaus is the rank of the matricization. The same
teacher thus has different plateau levels and exponents on an MPS and on a binary tree. These
predictions are tested in \cref{sec:teacher-cp,sec:teacher-ttn,sec:teacher-mismatch}.

\subsection{Input covariance}\label{app:covariance}

Let $\Sig=\Sigi1\otimes\dots\otimes\Sigi\Ns$ (independent sites; cross-site correlations are treated at the
end). Let $\Xi$ act on every core by multiplying the physical leg of each site $i\in\sites v$ by
$\Sigi i^{1/2}$, leaving bond legs and cores without sites unchanged. $\Xi$ is linear, symmetric and
invertible, and $\W(\Xi\params)=\Sig^{1/2}\W(\params)$ because a leg-wise linear map commutes with the
contraction.

\begin{proposition}[A product covariance is a preconditioned whitened problem]\label{prop:A-precond}
  \proven{} \newresult[for TTNs]
  (a) $\Loss(\params)=\tilde\Loss(\Xi\params)$ with $\tilde\Loss(\tilde\params)=\frac12\normF{\W(\tilde\params)-\Sig^{1/2}\Wt}^2$.
  (b) In the variables $\tilde\params=\Xi\params$ gradient flow reads $\dot{\tilde\params}=-\Xi^2\nabla\tilde\Loss(\tilde\params)$ from
  the anisotropic initialization $\Xi\params_0$: a whitened problem with teacher $\Sig^{1/2}\Wt$ and the
  preconditioner $\Sigi i$ on every physical leg.
  (c) $\nabla\Loss(\params)=\Xi\nabla\tilde\Loss(\Xi\params)$ and $\nabla^2\Loss(\params)=\Xi\,\nabla^2\tilde\Loss(\Xi\params)\,\Xi$ at every
  $\params$. Critical points, critical values, Morse indices and saddle types are therefore those of the
  whitened problem with teacher $\Sig^{1/2}\Wt$ (Sylvester's law of inertia), but escape rates are
  eigenvalues of $\Xi^2\nabla^2\tilde\Loss$, not of $\nabla^2\tilde\Loss$.
  (d) The gauge group acts on bond legs only, so \cref{prop:conservation} holds unchanged in $\params$: small
  initialization is balanced in $\params$, not in $\tilde\params$.
  (e) Plain whitened gradient flow is recovered iff $\Xi^2\propto\Id$, i.e.\ iff every $\Sigi i=\kappa_i\Id$ and
  $\prod_{i\in\sites v}\kappa_i$ is the same for every core; on binary and HT trees, whose internal cores have
  factor 1, this forces that product to equal 1. For $\Sigi i=\kappa_i\Id$, $\Loss=(\prod_i\kappa_i)\Loss_{\Sig=\Id}$, so
  time is rescaled by $\prod_i\kappa_i$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  (a) follows from $\W(\Xi\params)=\Sig^{1/2}\W(\params)$; (b) and (c) are the chain rule through the symmetric
  linear map $\Xi$.
\end{proof}
Checked: the preconditioned whitened flow reproduces $\Sig^{1/2}\W(\params(t))$ to $10^{-8}$ on binary, MPS
and HT trees with $\Ns=4$, while the naive whitened flow (same teacher and initialization, no
preconditioner) deviates by 4--100\%.

\begin{proposition}[Aligned covariance: exact clocks]\label{prop:A-aligned-cov}
  \proven{} \knownresult[Saxe-type modes: \citealp{saxe2014exact}; depth two: \citealp{gidel2019implicit};
  new: every tree]
  Let the teacher be odeco with factors that are eigenvectors of the site covariances,
  $\Sigi i\fac ri=\lambda_{r,i}\fac ri$, and let $\Lambda_r=\prod_i\lambda_{r,i}$. On the aligned family of
  \cref{prop:aligned}: (a) $\Loss=\frac12\sum_r\Lambda_r(\prodamp r-\tw r)^2$; (b) the family is invariant and
  $\dot q_{v,r}=\Lambda_r(\tw r-\prodamp r)\prod_{u\ne v}\amp ur$, so each component evolves as in the whitened
  problem with its own clock, $\prodamp r(t;\Sig)=\prodamp r(\Lambda_rt;\Id)$, with the same factor $\Lambda_r$ for every
  core, with or without sites; (c) from a balanced start $q_0$,
  $t_{1/2,r}=q_0^{-(\Kc-2)}/((\Kc-2)\Lambda_r\tw r)+\delta_\Kc/(\Lambda_r\tw r^{2-2/\Kc})+O(q_0^2)$, with $\delta_\Kc$ of
  \cref{prop:A-first-event}; (d) with the set $S$ learned the loss is $\frac12\sum_{r\notin S}\Lambda_r\tw r^2$, and the
  events of $j$ and then $r$ are separated by
  $\big(1-\frac{\Lambda_r\tw r}{\Lambda_j\tw j}\big)\big/\big((\Kc-2)\Lambda_r\tw rq_0^{\Kc-2}\big)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  $\ip{\U r}{\Sig\U{r'}}=\delta_{rr'}\Lambda_r$ and $\Sig(\W-\Wt)=\sum_r\Lambda_r(\prodamp r-\tw r)\U r$; the rest is the proof of
  \cref{prop:aligned} with this residual.
\end{proof}
Three orderings therefore coexist \derived{} \newresult: by escape speed $\Lambda_r\tw r$ (which component is
learned first), by loss drop $\frac12\Lambda_r\tw r^2$, and the whitened order by $\tw r$. With $\tw1>\tw2$,
component 2 escapes first iff $\Lambda_2/\Lambda_1>\tw1/\tw2$, and in the \emph{mismatch window}
$\tw1/\tw2<\Lambda_2/\Lambda_1<(\tw1/\tw2)^2$ the faster component is the smaller loss drop, so the first plateau
($\frac12\Lambda_1\tw1^2$) lies above the reduced-rank-regression optimum ($\frac12\Lambda_2\tw2^2$). The visited saddles
are then not the reduced-rank solutions of \citet{gidel2019implicit}, whose Corollary~2 states no ordering
condition. With power-law spectra $\lambda_k\propto k^{-\beta}$ and component $r$ on eigenvector $k_r$ at every site,
$\Lambda_2/\Lambda_1=(k_1/k_2)^{\beta\Ns}$: the natural pairing $k_r=r$ reinforces the whitened order.

\begin{proposition}[Random initialization: what replaces the teacher]\label{prop:A-escape-cov}
  \derived{} \newresult[TTN form of the covariance-weighted first escape of
  \citealp{jacot2022saddletosaddle,atanasov2022neural,rawal2026theory}]
  Near the origin $\Loss=\Loss_0-\ip{\W}{\Sig\Wt}+\frac12\normS{\W}^2$, and the fan-in initialization law of $\params$
  does not depend on $\Sig$. Hence: (a) the escape from the origin (direction, time and learning order of
  \cref{app:escape}) is that of the whitened problem with the \emph{escape tensor}
  $\Sig\Wt=\E[f^*(x)\Feat(x)]$ in place of $\Wt$; for aligned covariance $\tw r\to\Lambda_r\tw r$, and
  $\dd\ln\tev1/\dd\ln\Lambda=-1$ exactly, for every $\Kc$, tree and split of $\Lambda$ over the sites;
  (b) the landscape (critical points, plateau levels, saddle types) is that of $\Sig^{1/2}\Wt$
  (\cref{prop:A-precond}); for aligned covariance $\tw r\to\Lambda_r^{1/2}\tw r$; (c) the problems $(\Sig,\Wt)$ and
  $(\Id,\Sig\Wt)$, which have the same escape tensor, have the same escape at LO and differ only by a
  saturation offset of order one: for an aligned component the half-times, each relative to its own
  target, differ by $-\delta_\Kc\tw r^{-(2-2/\Kc)}(\Lambda_r^{-(2-2/\Kc)}-\Lambda_r^{-1})$.
\end{proposition}
Plain gradient flow on the whitened teacher without the preconditioner predicts the exponent $-1/2$
instead of $-1$. Checked: $\dd\ln\tev1/\dd\ln\Lambda=-1.0000$ in 18 fits (binary and MPS trees, three splits,
three seeds) and $-1.0005$ to $-1.0011$ on the HT tree; same-escape-tensor offset $-0.2218$ against
$-0.2219$. Random initialization keeps the random learning order of \cref{prop:A-race}, at the rate ratio
$\Lambda_2\tw2/(\Lambda_1\tw1)$.

\begin{proposition}[Piggyback block under aligned covariance]\label{prop:A-piggyback-cov}
  \proven{} (at the saddle) \newresult{}
  Let $j$ be learned (balanced, $q_j=\tw j^{1/\Kc}$) and let $r$ differ from $j$ on $D=D_{jr}$, with two terminal
  cores $c_1,c_2$. With $\mu_x=\prod_{i\in D\cap\sites{c_1}}\lambda_{x,i}$, $\nu_x=\prod_{i\in D\cap\sites{c_2}}\lambda_{x,i}$ for
  $x\in\{j,r\}$ and $\lambda_{\rm sh}=\prod_{i\notin D}\lambda_{j,i}$, the Hessian on the two rotations (per unit
  parameter displacement) is
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-piggyback-cov}
    H_{\rm pb}=\frac{\lambda_{\rm sh}}{\tw j^{2/\Kc}}\begin{pmatrix}\tw j^2\mu_r\nu_j&-\tw r\tw j\mu_r\nu_r\\
    -\tw r\tw j\mu_r\nu_r&\tw j^2\mu_j\nu_r\end{pmatrix}.
  \end{equation}
  It has a negative eigenvalue iff $\Lambda_r\tw r^2>\Lambda_j\tw j^2$, whatever the split of the eigenvalues over the
  cores. If $c_1,c_2$ are adjacent ($k=2$), its rate is below the add-route rate
  $b=\Lambda_r\tw r\tw j^{(\Kc-2)/\Kc}$, so $r$ is added directly: a rotation needs $m=2$ and $k\ge3$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  At the learned amplitude, and up to constants,
  $\Loss=\frac12\tw j^2\lambda_{\rm sh}(\mu_j\cos^2\varphi+\mu_r\sin^2\varphi)(\nu_j\cos^2\psi+\nu_r\sin^2\psi)
  -\tw j(\Lambda_j\tw j\cos\varphi\cos\psi+\Lambda_r\tw r\sin\varphi\sin\psi)$, where $\varphi,\psi$ rotate the two terminal cores
  and decouple from the amplitude at second order; $\det H_{\rm pb}\propto\Lambda_j\tw j^2-\Lambda_r\tw r^2$. For adjacent
  terminals $\lambda_{\rm sh}\mu_r\nu_r=\Lambda_r$, so the off-diagonal entry is $-b$; a symmetric $2\times2$ matrix with
  positive diagonal $h_1,h_2$ and off-diagonal $-b$ has $\lambda_{\min}>-b$, because
  $(\frac{h_1+h_2}2+b)^2-(\frac{h_1-h_2}2)^2-b^2=h_1h_2+b(h_1+h_2)>0$.
\end{proof}
In the mismatch window, conditionally on the faster component winning the first escape \derived: on the
binary tree with $\Ns=4$ ($m=2$, $k=3$) the learned term rotates from the faster to the other component at
bond rank one, the loss plateaus at $\frac12\Lambda_2\tw2^2$, and the faster component is relearned later; on the
MPS with $\Ns=4$ and on every tree with $m\ge3$ there is no rotation, and the loss plateaus at the higher
level $\frac12\Lambda_1\tw1^2$. Checked: rotation rates $0.1263$, $0.1130$, $0.0548$ for three splits of the same
$\Lambda$, as predicted.

\paragraph{Misaligned covariance.} \proven{} (identities and inequality) \derived{} (order, to leading
order in the cross overlaps) \newresult{} For every CP teacher, exactly,
$\Sig\Wt=\sum_r\tw r\Lambda^{\rm esc}_r\widetilde U_r$ with $\widetilde U_r=\bigotimes_i\Sigi i\fac ri/\norm{\Sigi i\fac ri}$ and
$\Lambda^{\rm esc}_r=\prod_i\norm{\Sigi i\fac ri}$, while $\Sig^{1/2}\Wt=\sum_r\tw r(\Lambda^{\rm loss}_r)^{1/2}\widehat U_r$ with
$\Lambda^{\rm loss}_r=\prod_i\ip{\fac ri}{\Sigi i\fac ri}$. By Cauchy--Schwarz $\Lambda^{\rm esc}_r\ge\Lambda^{\rm loss}_r$, with equality
iff every $\fac ri$ is an eigenvector of $\Sigi i$. Components escape in the order of $\tw r\Lambda^{\rm esc}_r$ along
$\widetilde U_r$ and then realign to $\U r$ without an extra plateau (the TTN form of the early alignment of
\citealp{atanasov2022neural}), while the loss drops are $\frac12\tw r^2\Lambda^{\rm loss}_r$; misalignment alone can
reverse the order. Misalignment also opens routes of degree below $k^*$, with couplings of order
$\epsilon^{n_{\rm out}}$ at per-site misalignment $\epsilon$, $n_{\rm out}$ being the number of sites outside the route.

\paragraph{Cross-site correlations.} Consider the one-factor model $x^{(i)}=\sqrt{1-c}\,z_i+\sqrt c\,z_0$, with
independent $z_0,\dots,z_\Ns\sim\Normal(0,\Id_d)$: every marginal is standard and the covariance between two
sites is $c\Id$. (i) \proven{} \newresult[the model is the standard one-factor Gaussian model; new: its tree
contraction] Given $z_0$ the sites are independent with second moments $M_i(z_0)=(1-c)\Id+cz_0z_0\T$, so
$\Loss=\E_{z_0}\big[\frac12\norm{\W-\Wt}^2_{\otimes_iM_i(z_0)}\big]$, a polynomial of degree $2\Ns$ in $z_0$; tensor
Gauss--Hermite quadrature with $\Ns+1$ nodes per dimension evaluates it exactly with $(\Ns+1)^d$ product-metric
tree contractions. A site-aligned component has the effective weight
$\Lambda_{\rm eff}(c,\Ns)=\sum_k\binom{\Ns}{k}(1-c)^{\Ns-k}c^k(2k-1)!!$. (ii) \derived{} The first plateau survives for
every $c<1$, because the origin is a degree-$\Kc$ critical point for any input law, and the
bond-rank-constrained critical points remain critical (\cref{lem:A-unused}). (iii) \proven{} (rates at the
saddle) \newresult{} On an MPS with even $\Ns$ and a site-aligned odeco teacher, the correlations open strict
two-core routes at the product saddle on every bond, with rate $\lambda_2\approx\tw2c^{\Ns-2}((\Ns-3)!!)^2$ at small
$c$. Whitened power-law plateaus therefore become logarithmic once $c$ exceeds a crossover
$c^*=O(\isc)$, up to logarithms (\conjecture, heuristic, for $\Ns\ge6$; checked for $\Ns=4$ and on one example
with $\Ns=6$). (iv) \derived{} On the MPS with $\Ns=4$ this escape has two stages: it passes through the first
plateau point of the binary tree (a point with junk pairing components, which is a higher-order saddle on
the binary tree and a strict one on the MPS), and the second plateau grows by
$\ln2\,[(1-\kappa_{\rm c})/\lambda_2+1/\lambda'']$ per halving of $\isc$, where $\lambda''$ is the rate at the intermediate
saddle and $\kappa_{\rm c}\in(0,1)$ the head start accrued during the first escape ($2.128$ predicted, $2.13$
measured, at $c=0.4$). These predictions are tested in \cref{sec:cov-aligned,sec:cov-misaligned,sec:cov-correlated}.

\subsection{Discrete steps and gradient noise}\label{app:optimizer}

Odeco teacher with $\tw1>\tw2>\dots$, whitened Gaussian inputs, $q_r=\tw r^{1/\Kc}$; gradient descent (GD) and
online SGD as in \cref{sec:setup-dynamics}, recorded at $t=\lr\times(\text{number of steps})$.

\begin{proposition}[Hessian at aligned global minima: the pattern rule]\label{prop:A-pattern-rule}
  \proven{} \knownresult[binary $\Ns=4$, a deep linear network: \citealp{ghosh2025learning}, Lemma~1;
  new: general trees]
  At a global minimum $\Hess=J\T J$ with $J=\partial\W/\partial\params$. At an aligned minimum (\cref{prop:aligned}
  with $\prodamp r=\tw r$), an elementary perturbation of core $w$ changes $\W$ by a gain times a unit rank-one
  \emph{pattern}: the chosen vectors at the sites of $w$, the teacher vector $\ell_c$ at every site below a
  child $c$ whose bond index is $\ell_c$, and the teacher vector $\ell_p$ at every site outside the subtree of
  $w$. The gain is $\prod_{u\ne w}\amp u{\ell(u)}$, with $\ell(u)$ the label of the branch of $w$ that contains
  $u$ (zero if a label exceeds $\ncomp$). Distinct patterns are orthonormal, so
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-pattern-rule}
    \mathrm{spec}(\Hess)\setminus\{0\}=\Big\{\lambda_P=\sum_{w\in\mathrm{prod}(P)}\mathrm{gain}_w(P)^2\ :\ P\text{ a pattern}\Big\},
  \end{equation}
  where $\mathrm{prod}(P)$ is the set of cores that can produce $P$. The radial mode of component $r$ (pattern
  $\U r$, produced by every core) has $\lambda=\tw r^2\sum_v\amp vr^{-2}\ge\Kc\tw r^{2-2/\Kc}$, with equality iff the
  component is balanced. At the balanced minimum every pattern has $\lambda_P\le\abs{\mathrm{prod}(P)}\,q_1^{2(\Kc-1)}$,
  hence
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-sharpness}
    \lambda_{\max}(\Hess)=\Kc\,\tw1^{2-2/\Kc},
  \end{equation}
  with a nondegenerate eigenvector if $\tw2<\tw1$.
\end{proposition}
Consequences \derived{} \newresult: GD is linearly stable at the balanced minimum iff
$\lr<\lr_c:=2/(\Kc\tw1^{2-2/\Kc})$, the stability limit tested in \cref{sec:other-optim}. Imbalanced aligned
minima are sharper (AM--GM; \proven), and with $\bd e=\ncomp$ so is every gauge-equivalent minimum
(\derived; random $\GL$ gauges were at least $1.43$ times sharper), so for $\lr>\lr_c$ no global minimum is
linearly stable (\derived{} for $\bd e=\ncomp$, \conjecture{} for $\bd e>\ncomp$). Because the conservation laws
fix the imbalance, the minimum reached by gradient flow and its sharpness are functions of $\Q e(0)$; a
strongly imbalanced weaker component can then carry the top mode. If that minimum is too sharp for the
step ($2/\lr$ below its sharpness), GD oscillates, the imbalance contracts by \cref{lem:A-gd-identity}, and
GD converges to a flatter, more balanced minimum with sharpness just below $2/\lr$ (\empirical{} on binary
$\Ns=4$; \knownresult[scalar and deep linear networks:
\citealp{kreisler2023gradient,wang2022large,ghosh2025learning}]).
Checked: autograd Hessians against \eqref{eq:A-pattern-rule} to $6\cdot10^{-15}$ on five trees with
$\Kc=3$--$7$.

\paragraph{Sharpness along the saddle-to-saddle path.} \derived{} \newresult{} On the first plateau the Hessian
of the degree-$\Kc$ polynomial $\W$ at $\norm{\params}\sim\isc$ is indefinite and $O(\isc^{\Kc-2})$. During the
event of a balanced component of amplitude $a$, its radial eigenvalue
$(2\Kc-1)a^{2\Kc-2}-(\Kc-1)\tw ra^{\Kc-2}$ rises to $\Kc\tw r^{2-2/\Kc}$ without overshoot, and the $\Kc-1$ transverse
eigenvalues $(\tw r-a^\Kc)a^{\Kc-2}$ stay below it. At a later plateau that has learned the set $S$, the radial
mode of the largest learned component is an exact eigenvector, so the sharpness is
$\Kc\max_{r\in S}\tw r^{2-2/\Kc}$. A finite initialization adds a relative excess $O(\isc^4)$: balance minimizes
the radial eigenvalue, so the excess is quadratic in an imbalance of order $\isc^2$. Hence, at a fixed step
$\lr$, a plateau is linearly unstable iff it has learned a component with
$\tw r>s_c(\lr)=(2/(\lr\Kc))^{\Kc/(2\Kc-2)}$: GD first becomes unstable at that component's event.

\paragraph{Edge of stability.} \derived{} \knownresult[the scalar map: \citealp{ghosh2025learning}; new: the
period-two amplitude] For $\lr_c<\lr<2/\lambda_2(\Hess)$ only the radial mode of component 1 is unstable, and GD
restricted to it (exactly for aligned initialization) is the balanced scalar map
$x\mapsto x+hx^{\Kc-1}(1-x^\Kc)$, $x=q/\tw1^{1/\Kc}$, $h=\lr\tw1^{2-2/\Kc}$, which doubles its period at $h\Kc=2$, i.e.\
at $\lr=\lr_c$. Expanding the map to third order around $x=1$, the period-two orbit $1\pm\delta_{\rm p2}$ has
$\delta_{\rm p2}^2\simeq3(h\Kc-2)/(4(\Kc-1)(5\Kc-4))$, and the time-averaged loss is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A-eos-loss}
  \Loss_{\rm EoS}\simeq\frac{3\Kc^2\tw1^2}{4(\Kc-1)(5\Kc-4)}\Big(\frac{\lr}{\lr_c}-1\Big).
\end{equation}
Later components still converge and later events still lower the loss. Checked (binary, $\Ns=4$, weights
$(1,0.6,0.3)$, $\lr=1.05\lr_c$): late loss $1.45\cdot10^{-2}$, against $1.452\cdot10^{-2}$ from the map and
$1.53\cdot10^{-2}$ from \eqref{eq:A-eos-loss}; the full network diverges between $1.5\lr_c$ and $1.65\lr_c$.

\begin{proposition}[Shift of event times under gradient descent]\label{prop:A-gd-shift}
  \derived{} \newresult[for TTNs; the modified flow is known]
  % TODO-CITE: modified equation / implicit gradient regularization, Barrett & Dherin (ICLR 2021); not in
  % notes/literature.md section 7.
  To order $\lr$, GD follows the modified flow $\dot\params=-(\Id+\frac\lr2\Hess)\nabla\Loss$. Its component along the
  gradient-flow path reparametrizes time, and since $\ddt\ln\norm{\nabla\Loss}=-\nabla\Loss\T\Hess\nabla\Loss/\norm{\nabla\Loss}^2$ along
  gradient flow,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-gd-shift}
    t_{\rm GD}-t_{\rm GF}=\frac\lr2\ln\frac{\norm{\nabla\Loss(t_{\rm event})}}{\norm{\nabla\Loss(0)}}
    +(\text{transverse part})+O(\lr^2).
  \end{equation}
  For aligned initialization the path is exact, and the event of component $r$ moves by
  \[
    \frac\lr2\ln\frac{f(a_1)}{f(a_0)},\qquad f(a)=a^{\Kc-1}(\tw r-a^\Kc),
  \]
  with $a_0$ and $a_1$ the per-core amplitudes at initialization and at the event: plateau exponents and
  prefactors are those of gradient flow, and no saddle is skipped or reordered while the gradient-flow
  gaps exceed the differences of the shifts.
\end{proposition}
For generic initialization $\norm{\nabla\Loss(0)}\propto\isc^{\Kc-1}$, so $\Delta\tev1/\lr\approx\frac{\Kc-1}2\ln(1/\isc)+{\rm const}$
asymptotically (\conjecture, heuristic: seeds with a long alignment phase are pre-asymptotic). At a strict
saddle the growth per step is $1+\lr\abs{\lambda_-}$ instead of $e^{\lr\abs{\lambda_-}}$, so
$t_{\rm GD}/t_{\rm GF}=\lr\abs{\lambda_-}/\ln(1+\lr\abs{\lambda_-})\in[1,1.82]$ for $\lr\abs{\lambda_-}<2$. Checked: aligned shifts
within $1.3\%$; generic slopes $0.69$--$0.70$ per halving of $\isc$ on binary $\Ns=4$ (predicted
$\frac{\Kc-1}2\ln2=0.69$).

\paragraph{SGD on the first plateau.} \derived{} \newresult[framework: stochastic differential equations
for SGD and Type-II saddles, \citealp{ziyin2023typeii}; consistent with \citealp{corlouer2026stochastic}]
% TODO-CITE: SDE approximation of SGD (Li, Tai & E, 2017/2019); not in notes/literature.md section 7.
For online SGD with batch $\batch$, the per-sample gradient is $\ip{\W-\Wt}{\Feat(x)}J\T\Feat(x)$. Near the origin
$J=O(\norm{\params}^{\Kc-1})$ and $\W-\Wt\approx-\Wt$, so the gradient covariance is
$O(\tw1^23^\Ns\norm{\params}^{2\Kc-2})/\batch$ while the drift is $O(\tw1\norm{\params}^{\Kc-1})$: the noise vanishes at the
origin at the same order as the drift, and in the clock $\dd\tau=\norm{\params}^{\Kc-2}\dd t$ its amplitude tends
to zero, so noise cannot drive the first escape. For one aligned balanced component,
$\dd a=\tw1a^{\Kc-1}\dd t+\sqrt{\lr/\batch}\,\tw1\kappa_{\rm n}^{1/2}a^{\Kc-1}\dd\mathcal B_t$, with $\mathcal B_t$ a
standard Brownian motion and $\kappa_{\rm n}=3^\Ns-1+\sum_{r\ge2}\tw r^2/\tw1^2$; its hitting time is inverse Gaussian to leading order:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A-sgd-first}
  \E\tev1^{\rm SGD}-\tev1^{\rm GF}\simeq\frac\lr2(\Kc-1)\Big(1+\frac{\kappa_{\rm n}}\batch\Big)\ln\frac{a_{1/2}}{a_0}>0,\qquad
  \mathrm{sd}\big(\tev1^{\rm SGD}\big)\simeq\Big(\kappa_{\rm n}\frac\lr\batch\tev1^{\rm GF}\Big)^{1/2},
\end{equation}
with $a_{1/2}=(\tw1/2)^{1/\Kc}$. Both corrections vanish relative to $\tev1^{\rm GF}\propto a_0^{-(\Kc-2)}$, so the
plateau exponent $\Kc-2$ is unchanged. Checked with 160--2000 seeds: within 10--15\% for aligned
initialization; for generic initialization the spread is about $1.2$ times the formula and the delay
smaller.

\paragraph{SGD at the minimum.} \derived{} (exact mean-square threshold) \proven{} (the inequality)
\newresult[the reduction; general criteria are known: \citealp{ziyin2023typeii}]
% TODO-CITE: exact mean-square stability criterion (Mulayoff & Michaeli, COLT 2024); radial 1D criterion
% (Wu, Ma & E, NeurIPS 2018); top Lyapunov exponent criterion (Chemnitz & Engel, JMLR 2025). None is in
% notes/literature.md section 7.
In the eigenbasis of \eqref{eq:A-pattern-rule}, linearized SGD at the minimum acts on one coordinate per
pattern, with the products $Z_P(x)=\prod_i(t_{P,i}\cdot x^{(i)})$ of the pattern's site vectors. Its
second-moment operator maps diagonal matrices to diagonal ones (since $\E[Z_R^2Z_PZ_Q]=0$ for $P\ne Q$) and
preserves the positive semidefinite cone, which gives the exact mean-square threshold
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A-ms-threshold}
  \lr^*_{\rm ms}(\batch)=\frac{2}{\lambda_{\max}(G_\batch)},\qquad
  G_\batch=\Big(1-\frac1\batch\Big)\Lambda_\Hess+\frac1\batch\Lambda_\Hess^{1/2}\mathcal K\Lambda_\Hess^{1/2},\qquad
  \mathcal K_{PQ}=\prod_im(t_{P,i},t_{Q,i}),
\end{equation}
with $\Lambda_\Hess=\diag(\lambda_P)$ and $m=3$ if the two site vectors coincide, $1$ if they are orthogonal
(Gaussian sites). Jensen's inequality gives $\lr_{\rm as}\ge\lr^*_{\rm ms}$ for the almost-sure divergence
threshold; computed ratios are about $2$ for $\Ns=4$ and $8$--$21$ for the binary tree with $\Ns=8$
\empirical. Full nonlinear SGD started near the minimum diverges at $\lr_{\rm as}$, not at $\lr^*_{\rm ms}$.

\begin{proposition}[SGD lengthens later plateaus]\label{prop:A-sgd-collapse}
  \conjecture{} (heuristic law) \knownresult[mechanism: stochastic collapse, \citealp{chen2023stochastic}]
  At a later saddle with a route of degree $k\ge3$ the population Hessian vanishes on the route and the
  per-sample gradients vanish at zero route amplitude. Zero-mean multiplicative noise coupling the route
  entries to mixed learned entries gives the log-amplitude of the coherent route a negative drift
  $-\gamma_c$, $\gamma_c=c_\gamma\lr/\batch$. Adding it to the gradient-flow route equation, $\dot b=\tw rb^{k-1}-\gamma_cb$; for
  $k=3$, with $T_{\rm GF}=1/(\tw rb_0)$,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-sgd-collapse}
    \frac{T_{\rm SGD}}{T_{\rm GF}}=\frac{-\ln(1-x)}{x},\qquad x=\gamma_cT_{\rm GF},
  \end{equation}
  and the route is trapped (it decays) for $x\ge1$; for general $k$ escape needs roughly
  $(k-2)\gamma_cT_{\rm GF}<1$. Since $b_0$ scales with the initialization, at fixed $\lr/\batch$ SGD stalls on a later
  degenerate plateau once $\isc$ is small enough. Strict saddles escape if $\abs{\lambda_-}>\gamma_c$.
\end{proposition}
Solving $\dot w=-\tw r+\gamma_cw$ for $w=1/b$ gives \eqref{eq:A-sgd-collapse}. Checked on binary $\Ns=4$ only
(four noise levels, 21 runs): delays $1.02$--$2$ times $T_{\rm GF}$, trapping for $x\ge1$, no escape faster than
gradient flow, $c_\gamma\approx1.35$ (range $1.0$--$2.5$) \empirical, against $1.08$ from a commuting-pair estimate.
Near the stability limit SGD breaks the saddle sequence instead of compressing it: no learned component
was erased for $\lr\le0.25\lr^*_{\rm ms}$, while at $0.7$--$1.2\,\lr^*_{\rm ms}$ components were repeatedly erased and
relearned, or the run diverged \empirical. Tested in \cref{sec:other-optim}.

\subsection{Regime boundaries}\label{app:boundaries}

Here $\pd[i]=d$, and $\TK$ denotes the tensor-space tangent kernel $\TK=\sum_v\lrm vJ_vJ_v\T$ on
$\R^{d^\Ns}$, $J_v=\partial\W/\partial\G v$, which drives the function-space dynamics of \eqref{eq:gf}; for whitened
inputs the probe-set kernel of \cref{tab:raw-diagnostics} is $\Feat(x_p)\T\TK\Feat(x_{p'})$. Recall
$\mat{\W}{\edge v}=\Sub vC_v\T$ (\cref{sec:setup-model}); the initialization is \eqref{eq:init} with scales
$\iscv v$ and $\fsc=\prod_v\iscv v$. A bond $e$ is \emph{full} if $\bd e=\min(d^{n_e},d^{\Ns-n_e})$, its cut dimension,
with $n_e$ the number of sites below $e$.

\begin{proposition}[Tangent kernel at initialization]\label{prop:A-kernel}
  \proven{} \newresult[exact at every bond dimension and on every tree; Gaussian-process and NTK limits of
  MPS and TT models: \citealp{guo2021neural,wesel2025tensor}]
  (a) $J_vJ_v\T=\bigotimes_{c\in\ch v}\Sub c\Sub c\T\otimes\Id_{d_v}\otimes C_vC_v\T$ (legs in site order).
  (b) For all bond dimensions, $\E\,\TK=\frac{\fsc^2}{d^\Ns}\sum_v\frac{\lrm v\fanin v}{\iscv v^2}\,\Id$ and
  $\E[\Wz\otimes\Wz]=\frac{\fsc^2}{d^\Ns}\Id$. With $\iscv v=\isc$ and $\lrm v=1$ the kernel scale is
  $\bar\lambda:=\E\tr\TK/d^\Ns=\isc^{2(\Kc-1)}\sum_v\fanin v/d^\Ns$, and core $v$ carries the share
  $\lrm v\fanin v\iscv v^{-2}/\sum_u\lrm u\fanin u\iscv u^{-2}$ of $\tr\TK$.
  (c) If the bond dimensions are consistent (each $\bd e$ at most the product of the other leg dimensions of
  both of its cores) and the initialization is generic,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-kernel-rank}
    \rank\TK=\sum_v\fanin v\bd v-\sum_e\bd e^2\quad(\text{capped at }d^\Ns),
  \end{equation}
  the dimension of the manifold $\mathcal M_\chi$ of tensors with bond ranks at most $\bd e$ (for inconsistent bond
  dimensions, first reduce each $\bd e$ to its effective value). $\rank\TK=d^\Ns$ iff every bond is full.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
  (a) $\W$ is linear in $\G v$. (b) The cores are independent, $\Sub c$ and $C_v$ involve disjoint cores, and the
  law of the initialization is invariant under orthogonal maps of each physical leg, which act irreducibly;
  the traces follow by induction from the leaves as in \cref{prop:init-norm}. (c) $\rank\TK$ is the dimension of
  the tangent space of $\mathcal M_\chi$ at $\Wz$: parameters minus the dimension of the gauge group, as for
  hierarchical Tucker tensors \citep{uschmajew2013geometry}.
\end{proof}
Consequences \derived: with a bond that is not full, the linearized (lazy) dynamics fits only the
projection of the target on the range of $\TK$ and stops at $\frac12\normF{P_\perp\Wt}^2$, with
$\E\normF{P_\perp\Wt}^2/\normF{\Wt}^2=(1-\chi/d^2)^2$ for the binary tree with $\Ns=4$ (compare
\citealp{chizat2019lazy}, Thm~2.5, and \citealp{ghorbani2019limitations}). $\TK\approx\bar\lambda\Id$ needs every
$\Sub c\Sub c\T$ and $C_vC_v\T$ to be nearly isotropic, i.e.\ every $\bd e$ far above its cut dimension; at capped full
bonds $\lambda_{\min}(\TK)/\bar\lambda\sim10^{-4}$ \empirical, so lazy runs need uncapped $\chi\ge2d^2$
(\cref{sec:other-lazy}). At practical $\chi$ the lazy regime therefore learns along the eigenvectors of a
random, tree-dependent kernel.

\begin{proposition}[Lazy boundary of the centred model, $\Ns=4$]\label{prop:A-lazy}
  \derived{} \newresult[the TTN constants; the criterion is that of \citealp{chizat2019lazy}; a coherent
  second-order kernel change is known in wide networks: \citealp{bordelon2022selfconsistent}]
  Binary tree with $\Ns=4$, standard parameterization, centred model; let $\xi=\normF{\Wt}/\fsc$, assume
  $\chi\gg d^2$ and keep the leading order in $d^2/\chi$. Then
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-lazy-dk}
    \dTK^2\approx(c_1\xi)^2+2\zeta c_1c_2\xi^3+(c_2\xi^2)^2,\qquad
    c_1=\frac{4d^3\sqrt{d^2+1}}{\chi^3},\quad c_2=\frac{2d^4\sqrt{2d^2\nu+2}}{\chi^3},
  \end{equation}
  with $\nu=\sum_r\tw r^4/(\sum_r\tw r^2)^2$ and a seed-dependent correlation $\zeta\in[-1,1]$. The first-order term
  is linear in $\Wz$ and has zero mean; the second-order term is coherent, proportional to
  $\mat{(\Wt)}{e}\big(\mat{(\Wt)}{e}\big)\T$, because the bottom cores drift toward the singular directions of
  the target.
  The boundary $\dTK=\delta_*$ lies in $[\xi_c^+,\xi_c^-]$ with $\xi_c^\pm=(\mp c_1+\sqrt{c_1^2+4c_2\delta_*})/(2c_2)$, and for
  large $\chi$ ($c_1^2\ll c_2\delta_*$)
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-sigma-lazy}
    \isc_{\rm lazy}=\Big(\frac{\normF{\Wt}^2c_2}{\delta_*}\Big)^{1/6}\propto\chi^{-1/2}
  \end{equation}
  ($6.50\,\chi^{-1/2}$ for $d=3$, weights $(3,2,1)$, $\delta_*=0.1$), not $\chi^{-1}$ as the first-order term alone
  would give. In the two-copy model the first-order term cancels, $\dTK\approx\frac14c_2\xi^2$, and
  $\isc^{(2)}_{\rm lazy}=2^{-1/3}\isc_{\rm lazy}$.
\end{proposition}
Checked \empirical: at $\chi=32$ the predicted $\xi_c\in[2.17,2.81]$ against $2.25$ and $2.82$ in gradient flow;
at $\chi=64$, $[6.67,7.31]$ against $6.73$ and $7.53$; two-copy model at $\chi=32$, $4.94$ against $4.96$ and
$5.15$. The boundary moves with the threshold as $\isc_{\rm lazy}\propto\delta_*^{-1/(\Kc s)}$, where
$s=\dd\ln\dTK/\dd\ln\xi\in[1,2]$ is the local slope (measured $1.5$--$1.8$). Under the standard
parameterization the root can move by more than $100\%$ while $\dTK<0.1$ (it acts as a trained readout of
random features), so per-core changes should be reported next to $\dTK$.

\paragraph{Deficient bonds.} \derived{} \newresult{} If some bond is not full, $\Wt+\Wz$ generically has more
bond rank than the student can carry, so the centred model is unrealizable at every $\isc$. At small $\isc$ the
best reachable point is $\Wt+P_T\Wz+O(\normF{\Wz}^2)$, with $P_T$ and $P_\perp$ the tangent and normal projectors of
$\mathcal M_\chi$ at $\Wt$, so the loss floors at $\frac12\normF{P_\perp\Wz}^2$, with mean, by \cref{prop:A-kernel}(b),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A-centred-floor}
  \frac{\E\Loss_{\rm end}}{\Loss(0)}\approx\Big(1-\frac{\dim\mathcal M_\chi}{d^\Ns}\Big)\frac{\isc^{2\Kc}}{\normF{\Wt}^2}.
\end{equation}
A typical run meets the convergence rule $\Loss<10^{-3}\Loss(0)$ only below
$\isc_{\rm floor}=[10^{-3}\normF{\Wt}^2/(1-\dim\mathcal M_\chi/d^\Ns)]^{1/(2\Kc)}$: $0.56$, $0.60$, $0.71$, $1.02$ for the binary
tree with $\Ns=4$, $d=3$, weights $(3,2,1)$ and $\chi=3,4,6,8$, and $0.74$ for $\Ns=8$, $d=3$, $\chi=3$. At large $\isc$
the floor tends to $\normF{P_\perp\Wt}^2/\normF{\Wt}^2$, with $P_\perp$ now the normal projector at $\Wz$. Runs above
$\isc_{\rm floor}$ should be labelled stalled (unrealizable). In the two-copy model a deficient bond gives a
lazy plateau at that level and then an escape by rotation of the bond subspaces: adiabatic elimination of
the fast antisymmetric part gives an escape time $t_{\rm esc}=2\vartheta\isc^2/\normF{\Wt}^2$, with a seed-dependent
$\vartheta\approx0.5$, i.e.\ a kernel time $\propto\isc^{2\Kc}$, and no saddle: the loss first falls linearly in $t$
(compare the mixed regimes of \citealp{tu2024mixed} and \citealp{lyu2024dichotomy}).

\begin{proposition}[Saddle-to-saddle boundary for aligned initialization]\label{prop:A-s2s-aligned}
  \proven{} (given the exact aligned reduction) \knownresult[mode equation: \citealp{saxe2014exact}; threshold
  exponent $\Kc/(\Kc-2)$: \citealp{gissin2020implicit}, Thm~2; new: the constants for the criterion of
  \cref{sec:setup-diagnostics} and the exact $\Kc\to\infty$ limit]
  Let every component of an odeco teacher start at product amplitude $u_0=q_0^\Kc$, let
  $I_\Kc(v_0,v_1)=\frac1\Kc\int_{v_0}^{v_1}v^{-2+2/\Kc}(1-v)^{-1}\dd v$ and $\omega_\Kc=I_\Kc(0.1,0.9)$. Then
  $\tev r=\tw r^{-(2-2/\Kc)}I_\Kc(u_0/\tw r,\frac12)$ and $\wev r=\tw r^{-(2-2/\Kc)}\omega_\Kc$, so, with $\varrho_i=\tw{i+1}/\tw i$,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-s2s-ratio}
    \sep i=\frac{I_\Kc(u_0/\tw{i+1},\frac12)-\varrho_i^{\,2-2/\Kc}I_\Kc(u_0/\tw i,\frac12)}{\omega_\Kc}
    \simeq\Big(\frac{u_0}{\tw{i+1}}\Big)^{-(1-2/\Kc)}\frac{1-\varrho_i}{(\Kc-2)\,\omega_\Kc}\quad(u_0\to0),
  \end{equation}
  and the boundary $\sep i=3$ lies at $u_{0c}\simeq\tw{i+1}[(1-\varrho_i)/(3(\Kc-2)\omega_\Kc)]^{\Kc/(\Kc-2)}$. As $\Kc\to\infty$,
  $\Kc I_\Kc(v_0,v_1)\to\frac1{v_0}-\frac1{v_1}+\ln\frac{v_1}{v_0}+\ln\frac{1-v_0}{1-v_1}$ and $\Kc\omega_\Kc\to\frac{80}9+2\ln9=13.283$: the
  function-space boundary $u_{0c}$ stays finite ($1.306\cdot10^{-2}$ for weights $(3,2,1)$), while the per-core
  boundary $a_{0c}=u_{0c}^{1/\Kc}=1+\ln u_{0c}/\Kc+O(\Kc^{-2})$ tends to 1.
\end{proposition}
Values: $\omega_3=2.009$, $\omega_5=1.612$, $\omega_7=1.318$; for weights $(3,2,1)$, $a_{0c}=0.066$, $0.324$, $0.480$
at $\Kc=3,5,7$. Aligned gradient flow reproduces \eqref{eq:A-s2s-ratio} to four decimals at $\Kc=3$ and 7.

\paragraph{Large bond dimension.} \derived{} \newresult[the spectral condition is
\citealp{yang2024spectral}; factor-wise rules for structured layers:
\citealp{qiu2024compute,potapczynski2024searching}] The input of a core is always a product of vectors, so
the norm that controls its forward and backward signals is its injective norm, not the spectral norm of its
matricization. Imposing the spectral condition with the injective norm keeps the fan-in initialization of
every non-root core, multiplies the root by $(\sum_\ell m_\ell)^{-1/2}$, where $m_\ell$ are the dimensions of its
child legs (physical and bond), and sets the
learning-rate multipliers $\lrm v\propto\bd v/\fanin v$ (``$\mu$P-TTN''). At fixed $\xi$ ($\Ns=4$) this gives
$\dTK\propto\chi^0$, against $\chi^{-3}$ for the standard and $\chi^{-1}$ for the NTK parameterization
(compare \citealp{guo2021neural} for the MPS NTK); the factor-wise rule applied to TTN cores makes the initial
kernel vanish like $\chi^{-2}$ and slows the initial fit about linearly in $\chi$ \empirical.

\paragraph{Imbalance.} \derived{} (aligned mode) \newresult[compare two-layer linear networks:
\citealp{kunin2024get}] With the root at scale $\lambda_{\rm root}\isc$ and the other cores at $\isc$ (fixed $\fsc$), the
root bonds have $\E\Q e=\isc^2(1-\lambda_{\rm root}^2/\bd e)\Id$, balanced at $\lambda_{\rm root}=\sqrt{\bd e}$ (default fan-in
initialization is root-light). A small root ($\lambda_{\rm root}\ll1$) makes training lazy, with
