\section{Derivations}\label{app:derivations}

This appendix derives the theoretical statements of the main text not proved in \cref{sec:setup}.

\paragraph{Conventions.}
Notation is that of \cref{sec:setup}. Unless stated otherwise inputs are whitened ($\Sig=\Id$),
gradient flow has unit multipliers ($\lrm{v}=1$), so that $\Loss=\tfrac12\normF{\W-\Wt}^2$, and the
teacher is a CP teacher \eqref{eq:cp-teacher}. For two components $j,r$ of a pattern teacher,
$D_{jr}=\{i:\pi_j(i)\ne\pi_r(i)\}$ is the set of sites where they differ, $N(D_{jr})$ the set of
(\emph{terminal}) cores carrying a site of $D_{jr}$, $m_{jr}=\abs{N(D_{jr})}$, and $\mathrm{St}(N(D_{jr}))$ the
smallest subtree of $\tree$ containing $N(D_{jr})$ (its Steiner subtree; compare
\cref{prop:teacher-spectra}(b)). At a learned saddle the learned component is $j$, with per-core
amplitude $q_j=\tw{j}^{1/\Kc}$ (\cref{prop:aligned}), and $r$ is a component still to be learned. A ratio of
two teacher weights, smaller over larger, is written $\varrho$. $\sigma_k(M)$ is the $k$-th singular value of
a matrix $M$; $\isc$ is the initialization scale. ``LO'' means leading order in the small quantities of each
statement; $\hat\params=\params/\norm{\params}$. A statement that combines a proven identity with a derived
consequence carries both tags. Arguments the theory notes call \emph{heuristic} (assumptions not
controlled) are tagged \conjecture. ``Checked'' refers to the numerical checks of the theory notes
(\path{notes/theory_*.md}), which give all values; only the key ones are quoted here.

\subsection{Conservation laws, gauge and invariant subspaces}\label{app:conservation}

Besides \cref{prop:conservation}, three facts are used below.

\begin{lemma}[Equal norm shift]\label{lem:A-norm-shift}
  \proven{} \knownresult[deep linear networks: \citealp{arora2018optimization,du2018algorithmic}]
  Let $\Loss(\params)=\mathcal L(\W(\params))$ for a differentiable $\mathcal L$. Under gradient flow \eqref{eq:gf} (no
  mask), $\lrm{v}^{-1}\ddt\normF{\G{v}}^2=-2\ip{\nabla_{\W}\mathcal L}{\W}$ for every core $v$, the same for all cores. For the
  population loss \eqref{eq:loss} the right-hand side is $2(\ipS{\W}{\Wt}-\normS{\W}^2)$; for the leading-order
  flow $\dot\params=\nabla g$ of \cref{app:escape} it is $2g$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  $\W$ is linear in $\G{v}$, so $\ip{\G{v}}{\nabla_{\G{v}}\Loss}=\ip{\nabla_{\W}\mathcal L}{\W}$, and
  $\ddt\normF{\G{v}}^2=-2\lrm{v}\ip{\G{v}}{\nabla_{\G{v}}\Loss}$.
\end{proof}

The lemma gives the common shift $y(t)$ in $\normF{\G{v}(t)}^2=\normF{\G{v}(0)}^2+y(t)$. Along the leading-order
flow $\dot g=\norm{\nabla g}^2\ge0$, so $y=2\int_0^tg$ is convex: \emph{all cores shrink, by the same amount,
and only while $g<0$}, i.e.\ only if $\ip{\Wz}{\Wt}<0$. The identity holds for any block of variables in
which $g$ is multilinear, e.g.\ the reduced cores of \cref{prop:A-reduced} (checked).

\begin{lemma}[Unused bond channels are invariant]\label{lem:A-unused}
  \proven{} \newresult[elementary]
  Let $e=\edge{v}$ with $p=\pa{v}$, and let $\mathcal B$ be a set of channels of $e$ such that every
  entry of $\G{v}$ and of $\G{p}$ whose $e$-index lies in $\mathcal B$ vanishes. Then, for every loss
  of the form $\mathcal L(\W(\params))$ (any input law, population or empirical), the gradient
  vanishes on these entries, so gradient flow, gradient descent and SGD keep them at zero. In
  particular, a critical point of the problem with bond dimensions $r_e\le\bd{e}$, padded with zeros,
  is a critical point of the problem with bond dimensions $\bd{e}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  The derivative of $\W$ with respect to an entry of $\G{v}$ with $e$-index $b\in\mathcal B$ contracts the
  other cores with $\G{p}$ at $e$-index $b$, a vanishing slice; likewise for $\G{p}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Conservation laws under discrete steps]\label{lem:A-gd-identity}
  \proven{} \knownresult[deep linear networks: \citealp{du2018algorithmic}; scalar form:
  \citealp{hofmann2026map}]
  With the gradients $g_v=\nabla_{\Gm{v}{e}}\Loss$, $g_p=\nabla_{\Gm{p}{e}}\Loss$ matricized as in \eqref{eq:conserved},
  one step $\params\mapsto\params-\lr\nabla\Loss$ (unit multipliers) changes $\Q{e}$ by exactly
  $\Q{e}^{+}-\Q{e}=\lr^2(g_vg_v\T-g_pg_p\T)$; likewise for SGD. For one aligned component (\cref{prop:aligned}), $Q=\amp{v}{1}^2-\amp{p}{1}^2$ obeys
  $Q^{+}=Q(1-\lr^2\Delta)$, $\Delta=(\tw{1}-\prodamp{1})^2\prodamp{1}^2/(\amp{v}{1}^2\amp{p}{1}^2)$: a step contracts the imbalance iff $\lr^2\Delta<2$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  With $X=\Gm{v}{e}$, $Y=\Gm{p}{e}$, the proof of \cref{prop:conservation} gives $g_vX\T=Yg_p\T=:C$ for
  every loss that depends on $\params$ through $\W$; so the terms linear in $\lr$ cancel in
  $(X-\lr g_v)(X-\lr g_v)\T-(Y-\lr g_p)(Y-\lr g_p)\T$. For the aligned component
  $g_v=-(\tw{1}-\prodamp{1})\prodamp{1}/\amp{v}{1}$, and likewise for $p$.
\end{proof}
The change is negligible on plateaus and large during oscillations at large steps (\cref{app:optimizer}).
At initialization with a common scale,
$\E\,\Q{e}(0)=\isc^2(1-\bd{p}/\bd{v})\Id$ (\cref{sec:setup-conservation}; $\bd p$ is the parent's own bond
dimension, $\bd{\vroot}=1$): edges into the root are child-heavy ($\E\,\Q{e}\succ0$), and HT leaf edges with
$\bd{v}=d=2$ below $\bd p=3$ are parent-heavy ($\E\,\Q{e}=-\tfrac12\isc^2\Id$).

\subsection{Escape from the origin}\label{app:escape}

\paragraph{Setting.}
Whitened inputs, a CP teacher, and gradient flow from the fan-in initialization $\params_0=\isc Z$
(\cref{eq:init}; $Z$ is the initialization at $\isc=1$); \cref{app:covariance} extends this to product
covariances by $\Wt\to\Sig\Wt$. Write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A-loss-g}
  \Loss(\params)=\tfrac12\normF{\Wt}^2-g(\params)+\tfrac12\normF{\W(\params)}^2,\qquad
  g(\params):=\ip{\W(\params)}{\Wt}.
\end{equation}
$g$ is homogeneous of degree $\Kc$ and linear in each core, while $\normF{\W}^2$ has degree $2\Kc$. The
\emph{leading-order (LO) flow} $\dot\params=\nabla g(\params)$ drops a term of relative size
$\normF{\W}/\normF{\Wt}=O(\isc^{\Kc})$ on the first plateau. Let $t^*$ be its blow-up time and
$\nabla_{\mathbb S}g(\hat\params)=\nabla g(\hat\params)-\Kc g(\hat\params)\hat\params$ the gradient of $g$
on the unit sphere.

\begin{proposition}[Spherical--radial decomposition]\label{prop:A-spherical}
  \proven{} \knownresult[directional convergence: \citealp{kumar2025early}]
  Along the LO flow, with the time change $\dd\tau=\norm{\params}^{\Kc-2}\dd t$,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-spherical}
    \frac{\dd\hat\params}{\dd\tau}=\nabla_{\mathbb S}g(\hat\params),\qquad
    \frac{\dd\ln\norm{\params}}{\dd\tau}=\Kc\,g(\hat\params).
  \end{equation}
  The direction follows the spherical gradient flow of $g$, independent of $\norm{\params}$ and $\isc$, and
  $\Q{e}(\hat\params(\tau))=\Q{e}(\params_0)/\norm{\params(\tau)}^2$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  By Euler's identity and the homogeneity of $\nabla g$ (degree $\Kc-1$),
  $\ddt\norm{\params}=\Kc\norm{\params}^{\Kc-1}g(\hat\params)$ and
  $\ddt\hat\params=\norm{\params}^{\Kc-2}\nabla_{\mathbb S}g(\hat\params)$; $\Q{e}$ is quadratic in $\params$.
\end{proof}

As $\isc\to0$ the escape needs $\norm{\params}$ to grow by a factor of order $1/\isc$ ($\tau$ of order
$\ln(1/\isc)$), so the escape direction is $\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\hat\params(\tau)$, a KKT point of $g$ on
the sphere (\citealp{kumar2025early}, Thm~1, which applies verbatim to TTNs with $\Kc\ge3$). The
conservation laws bind only while $\norm{\params}\approx\norm{\params_0}$ ($\tau=O(1)$), the phase that
decides the escape time and the KKT point reached; they do not change the balanced form of the
limit (\cref{prop:A-kkt}).

\begin{corollary}[Blow-up time]\label{cor:A-blowup}
  \proven{} \newresult[per-initialization form for TTNs; the exponent $\Kc-2$ is known in analogues:
  \citealp{jacot2022saddletosaddle,gissin2020implicit}]
  With $G(\tau)=\int_0^\tau g(\hat\params)\,\dd\tau'$,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-blowup}
    t^*=\norm{\params_0}^{-(\Kc-2)}\int_0^\infty e^{-(\Kc-2)\Kc G(\tau)}\,\dd\tau .
  \end{equation}
  For $\params_0=\isc Z$, $t^*=\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}\,\hat t_1(Z)/\tw{1}$, where $\hat t_1(Z):=\tw{1}\,t^*|_{\isc=1}$ depends on the
  initialization direction and on $\Wt/\tw{1}$ but not on $\isc$; the exponent $\Kc-2$ counts every core.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  By \eqref{eq:A-spherical}, $\norm{\params(\tau)}=\norm{\params_0}e^{\Kc G(\tau)}$ and
  $\dd t=\norm{\params}^{-(\Kc-2)}\dd\tau$. The LO field is homogeneous of degree $\Kc-1$, so
  $\params_\isc(t)=\isc\,\params_1(\isc^{\Kc-2}t)$; replacing $\Wt$ by $c\Wt$ divides all times by $c$.
\end{proof}
There is no separate alignment time: the whole integral is alignment ($g(\hat\params)$ starts near $0$).

\begin{proposition}[From the blow-up to the first event]\label{prop:A-first-event}
  \derived{} \newresult[for TTNs]
  Assume that at the end of the LO phase the direction $\hat\params$ has converged to the balanced KKT
  point of the first component learned, of weight $s$. Then, as $\isc\to0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-first-event}
    \tev{1}=t^*+\delta_\Kc\,s^{-(2-2/\Kc)}+o(1),\qquad
    \delta_\Kc=-\frac{2^{(\Kc-2)/\Kc}}{\Kc-2}+\int_0^{2^{-1/\Kc}}\frac{b\,\dd b}{1-b^\Kc},
  \end{equation}
  with $\delta_3=-0.850$, $\delta_4=-0.266$, $\delta_5=-0.047$, $\delta_6=0.072$, $\delta_7=0.148$ and
  $\delta_8=0.201$. Hence $\tev{1}\isc^{\Kc-2}\tw1/\hat t_1(Z)=1+O(\isc^{\Kc-2})$ for every initialization.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Derivation]
  Once $\norm{\params}=O(1)$ the remaining time does not depend on $\isc$. Along a balanced KKT point the
  flow is the balanced mode $\dot q=q^{\Kc-1}(s-q^\Kc)$ of \cref{prop:aligned}, whose half-time is
  $q_0^{-(\Kc-2)}/((\Kc-2)s)+\delta_\Kc s^{-(2-2/\Kc)}+O(q_0^2)$ (write $b=qs^{-1/\Kc}$ and
  $1/(b^{\Kc-1}(1-b^\Kc))=b^{-(\Kc-1)}+b/(1-b^\Kc)$); the first term is the LO blow-up time.
\end{proof}
Checked per initialization (within $0.8\%$ of gradient flow for $\Kc=3,4$, $\isc\le0.03$); for $\Kc=6,7$ at
$\isc=0.15$--$0.5$ the direction has not converged (heuristically), so a $1\%$ test needs $\isc^{\Kc-2}\le10^{-3}$.

\begin{remark}[Per-initialization prediction]\label{rem:A-recipe}
  \proven{} at LO. Integrate in $\tau$ the state $(u,\ell,T)$ from $u(0)=\params_0/\norm{\params_0}$,
  $\ell(0)=T(0)=0$: $\dd u/\dd\tau=\nabla g(u)-\Kc g(u)u$ (renormalizing $u$), $\dd\ell/\dd\tau=\Kc g(u)$,
  $\dd T/\dd\tau=e^{-(\Kc-2)\ell}$, up to $\ell=40/(\Kc-2)+5$, and add the tail
  $e^{-(\Kc-2)\ell}/((\Kc-2)\Kc g(u))$. Then $t^*=\norm{\params_0}^{-(\Kc-2)}T$ for the actual teacher (any
  number of components), and the dominant component of the final $u$ is the first one learned as
  $\isc\to0$: a parameter-free prediction of $\tev{1}$ (tested in \cref{sec:s2s-escape}).
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Maximum of $g$ on the sphere]\label{prop:A-gmax}
  \proven{} \newresult[for TTNs]
  Group the sites by the core that carries them (cores without sites form no group), and let
  $\norm{\Wt}_{\rm inj}=\max\{\ip{\Wt}{\bigotimes_vY_v}:\normF{Y_v}=1\}$ be the injective norm of $\Wt$
  seen as a tensor with one leg per group. Then
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-gmax}
    g(\params)\le\norm{\Wt}_{\rm inj}\prod_v\normF{\G{v}}\le\Kc^{-\Kc/2}\norm{\Wt}_{\rm inj}\norm{\params}^{\Kc},
  \end{equation}
  with equality on the unit sphere at the balanced bond-rank-one configurations
  $\G{v}=\Kc^{-1/2}\,Y_v\otimes\varepsilon_1\otimes\dots\otimes\varepsilon_1$ built from a maximizing
  $(Y_v)$. For odeco teachers $\norm{\Wt}_{\rm inj}=\tw1$ for every grouping; in general the grouping
  matters (for $\Wt=(\Id/\sqrt d)\otimes(\Id/\sqrt d)$ on the site pairs $(0,1)$, $(2,3)$ the unit-sphere maximum
  is $3^{-3/2}$ on binary $\Ns=4$ and $4^{-2}/d$ on the MPS).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  First inequality, by induction on the number of cores: for a leaf $\ell$ with parent $p$ and the SVD
  $\G{\ell}=\sum_k\mu_kY_k\otimes z_k$ across (physical legs $\mid$ bond leg),
  $g=\sum_k\mu_k\ip{\W'_k}{\Wt[Y_k]}$, with $\W'_k$ the network without $\ell$ and $\G{p}$ contracted
  with $z_k$ on the $\ell$-leg, and $\Wt[Y_k]$ the contraction of $\Wt$ with $Y_k$ on the sites of $\ell$.
  Since $\norm{\Wt[Y_k]}_{\rm inj}\le\norm{\Wt}_{\rm inj}$, induction bounds the sum by
  $\norm{\Wt}_{\rm inj}\prod_{v\ne\ell,p}\normF{\G{v}}\sum_k\mu_k\normF{\G{p}\times_\ell z_k}$, and Cauchy--Schwarz with
  $\sum_k\normF{\G{p}\times_\ell z_k}^2\le\normF{\G{p}}^2$ closes the step. The second inequality is AM--GM.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[KKT points are balanced]\label{prop:A-kkt}
  \proven{} \newresult[for TTNs] Every KKT point $\params^*$ of $g$ on the unit sphere with
  $g^*:=g(\params^*)>0$ has $\Q{e}=0$ on every bond and $\normF{\G{v}}^2=1/\Kc$ for every core.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  At a KKT point $\nabla g(\params^*)=\lambda\params^*$, and Euler's identity gives $\lambda=\Kc g^*>0$.
  $g$ is invariant under the gauge transformations \eqref{eq:gauge}, so $\ip{\nabla g}{\delta\params}=0$
  for every gauge direction $\delta\params=(M\Gm{v}{e},-M\T\Gm{p}{e})$, i.e.\
  $\lambda\tr\big((\Gm{v}{e}\Gm{v}{e}\T-\Gm{p}{e}\Gm{p}{e}\T)M\big)=0$ for all $M$; hence $\Q{e}=0$. Since $g$
  is linear in each core, $\ip{\G{v}}{\nabla_{\G{v}}g}=g^*$, i.e.\ $\lambda\normF{\G{v}}^2=g^*$.
\end{proof}
Hence (checked to $10^{-15}$) the near-origin escape direction is balanced, so of minimal norm in its fibre
\citep[Prop.~F.10]{furman2026benign}, and the $O(\isc^2)$ initial imbalance does not affect it at LO.

\begin{proposition}[Second-order structure at a component]\label{prop:A-component-hessian}
  \derived{} \newresult[for general trees; for binary $\Ns=4$, a deep linear network, it is known:
  \citealp{jacot2022saddletosaddle,achour2024loss}]
  For a pattern teacher with distinct weights let $\params_j$ be the balanced bond-rank-one configuration of
  component $j$ on the unit sphere (per-core amplitude $\Kc^{-1/2}$, the pattern of $j$ on the physical legs,
  channel~1 on every bond), so that $g(\params_j)=\tw j\Kc^{-\Kc/2}$. (a) If some component $r$ has $m_{jr}=1$,
  $\params_j$ is not a critical point of $g$ on the sphere. (b) Otherwise, assume that no two components $r$
  with $m_{jr}=2$ share a terminal core. Each such $r$, with terminal cores $v,w$, contributes $n_{jr}$
  eigenvalues $\Kc^{1-\Kc/2}(\tw r-\tw j)$ to the Riemannian Hessian of $g$ at $\params_j$, where $n_{jr}=\bd{e}$ if
  $v$ and $w$ share the bond $e$ and $n_{jr}=1$ otherwise; all other eigenvalues are negative, except the
  $\sum_e(\bd{e}-1)$ gauge zero modes. So $\params_j$ is a strict local maximum modulo gauge iff no $m_{jr}=1$ and
  every $r$ with $m_{jr}=2$ has $\tw r<\tw j$; otherwise it has $\sum_{r:\,m_{jr}=2,\,\tw r>\tw j}n_{jr}$ unstable
  directions. Ties are decided at higher order; partners sharing a terminal core: \cref{prop:A-piggyback-graph}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Derivation]
  The Riemannian Hessian is $\nabla^2g-\tw j\Kc^{1-\Kc/2}\Id$. As $g$ is linear in each core, $\nabla^2g$ has only
  cross terms between cores $v\ne w$, which contract $\Wt$ with the pattern of $j$ outside $\sites v\cup\sites w$:
  only components with $D_{jr}\subseteq\sites v\cup\sites w$ contribute. If $m_{jr}=1$, $\nabla g(\params_j)$ has a
  component along the pattern of $r$ on the terminal core. Component $j$'s own cross terms vanish on the
  gauge orbit and give $-2\tw j\Kc^{1-\Kc/2}$ off it; uncoupled directions give $-\tw j\Kc^{1-\Kc/2}$. For $m_{jr}=2$,
  rotating $\G{v},\G{w}$ toward the pattern of $r$ (channel $c$ on the bond joining them if adjacent, channel~1
  elsewhere) gives the cross term $\tw r\Kc^{1-\Kc/2}$ for every $c$ if $v,w$ are adjacent and for $c=1$ only
  otherwise; each surviving channel gives the block
  $\Kc^{1-\Kc/2}\big(\begin{smallmatrix}-\tw j&\tw r\\ \tw r&-\tw j\end{smallmatrix}\big)$, with eigenvalues $\Kc^{1-\Kc/2}(\pm\tw r-\tw j)$.
\end{proof}
Checked on 19 pattern-teacher cases and 7 odeco settings. For odeco teachers $m_{jr}$ is the number of
cores carrying sites ($\Ns$ on MPS and HT trees, $3,4$ on binary $\Ns=6,8$, and $2$ on binary $\Ns=4$, whose
bottom cores are not adjacent, $n=1$): every odeco component is a local maximum on MPS, HT and binary
$\Ns\ge6$ trees (\emph{tensor-like}: one basin per component), only the largest on binary $\Ns=4$
(\emph{matrix-like}); pattern teachers whose components differ on exactly two cores are matrix-like on
every tree. Spherical flows from 200 random initializations (odeco teachers, five trees) ended at
component configurations \empirical; other local maxima for non-odeco teachers: \cref{app:teachers}.

\begin{proposition}[Only the teacher slices move]\label{prop:A-reduced}
  \proven{} \newresult[for TTNs]
  For an odeco teacher $\nabla_{\G{v}}g=\sum_r\tw r(\bigotimes_{i\in\sites v}\fac ri)\otimes E^{(r)}_v$, $E^{(r)}_v$ the contraction
  of all other cores with $\fac ri$ on their sites. Under the LO flow the part of $\G{v}$ orthogonal on its
  physical legs to every $\bigotimes_{i\in\sites v}\fac ri$ is therefore frozen; the moving variables are the
  reduced cores $H^{(r)}_v$ ($\G{v}$ contracted with the $\fac ri$ on its physical legs; a core without sites is
  shared by all components), and $g=\sum_r\tw r\,\mathcal W_{\rm red}(H^{(r)})$, $\mathcal W_{\rm red}$ the scalar
  contraction of the reduced tree. Under \eqref{eq:init} the $H^{(r)}_v$ have \iid $\Normal(0,\isc^2/\fanin v)$
  entries, independent across $r$ (a fan-in scalar TTN with per-core scale $\isc d^{-\abs{\sites v}/2}$); on an MPS
  the components have disjoint moving variables and evolve independently at LO.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  The physical legs of $\nabla_{\G{v}}g$ lie in the teacher span, so $g$ depends on $\G{v}$ only through the
  $H^{(r)}_v$, which are \iid Gaussian as orthonormal contractions of \iid Gaussians.
\end{proof}
The same holds for pattern teachers (one slice per distinct pattern on each core). Most of $\norm{\params_0}$ is
frozen: $\E\normF{H^{(r)}_v}^2=\isc^2\bd{v}d^{-\abs{\sites v}}$, against $\E\normF{\G{v}}^2=\isc^2\bd{v}$.

\begin{corollary}[The Kumar--Haupt time is a lower bound]\label{cor:A-kh}
  \proven{} (bounds) \empirical{} (sizes) \newresult[the escape time $t_{\rm KH}$ itself is
  \citealp{kumar2025towards}]
  Let the teacher be odeco and let $r_{\rm m}$ be the norm of the moving variables at initialization.
  Then, for every initialization,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-kh}
    t^*\ \ge\ \frac{\Kc^{\Kc/2-1}}{(\Kc-2)\tw1}\,r_{\rm m}^{-(\Kc-2)}\ \ge\
    \frac{\Kc^{\Kc/2-1}}{(\Kc-2)\tw1}\,\norm{\params_0}^{-(\Kc-2)}=:t_{\rm KH},
  \end{equation}
  with equality iff the initial direction already maximizes $g$; $t_{\rm KH}=1/(\Kc(\Kc-2)g_*\norm{\params_0}^{\Kc-2})$,
  $g_*=\tw1\Kc^{-\Kc/2}$, is the escape time of \citet{kumar2025towards} from an initialization along a
  maximizer. Under fan-in initialization the median of $t^*/t_{\rm KH}$ ranges from 2.5--5.2 (binary,
  $\Ns=4$, $d=2$--$4$) to 182--12378 (MPS, $\Ns=8$), growing with $d$, $\chi$ and $\Kc$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  By \cref{prop:A-gmax}, $g\le g_*$ on the unit sphere, so $\ln\norm{\params(\tau)}\le\ln\norm{\params_0}+\Kc g_*\tau$
  and \eqref{eq:A-blowup} gives $t^*\ge t_{\rm KH}$. The moving variables follow the LO flow of the same $g$
  restricted to them, so the same argument with their norm $r_{\rm m}\le\norm{\params_0}$ gives the first
  inequality.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Escape along an imbalanced product path]\label{prop:A-product-mode}
  \derived{} \knownresult[the trichotomy: \citealp{rawal2026theory}, Thm~4.2; new: the TTN reduction and
  the constants]
  If the escape follows a rank-one path whose per-core amplitudes $x_v>0$ obey
  $\dot x_v=s\prod_{w\ne v}x_w$, then the $x_v^2-x_w^2$ are conserved and, with $x_v^2=c_v^2+y$,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-product-mode}
    \hat t=\frac1{2s}\int_0^\infty\prod_{v=1}^{\Kc}\big(y+c_v^2\big)^{-1/2}\dd y .
  \end{equation}
  Let $m$ internal cores start at amplitude $\gamma$ and the other $\Kc-m$ (the ends) at $c_e\gg\gamma$.
  As $\gamma\to0$: for $m=0$ and equal amplitudes $c$, $\hat t=c^{-(\Kc-2)}/((\Kc-2)s)$
  \citep{saxe2014exact}; for $m=1$ and ends at $c$, $\hat t\to\frac12\mathrm{B}(\frac12,\frac{\Kc-2}2)c^{-(\Kc-2)}/s$
  ($\mathrm B$ is Euler's beta function), a finite limit; for $m=2$,
  $\hat t=\ln(1/\gamma)/(s\prod_ec_e)+O(1)$; for $m\ge3$,
  $\hat t\simeq\gamma^{-(m-2)}/((m-2)s\prod_ec_e)$.
\end{proposition}
The integral follows from \cref{lem:A-norm-shift} ($\dot y=2s\prod_w(y+c_w^2)^{1/2}$). Binary $\Ns=4$ and MPS
$\Ns=3$ have $m=1$, MPS $\Ns=4$ $m=2$ (only qualitatively: its internal amplitude is not small), binary $\Ns=8$
and HT $\Ns=4$ $m=3$, the MPS $m=\Ns-2$. The escape time is set by the internal path amplitudes, while
$\norm{\params_0}$ is dominated by frozen slices and by internal cores whose norm is spread over $\sim\chi^2$
directions: hence $t_{\rm KH}$ fails most at large $\Kc$. That the end directions stay frozen is empirical
(median cosine $0.92$--$1.00$; a minority rotate strongly). Given it, on binary $\Ns=4$ (reduced network
$g=\tw1a\T Cb$, $a,b\in\R^{\bd b}$, $\bd b=\min(\chi,d^2)$) the median is $\tev1\approx\pi d/(2\tw1\isc\sqrt{\bd b})$ for
$d\le\bd b\le d^2$, with $\mathrm{sd}\ln\hat t_1\approx1/(2\sqrt{\bd b})$ \derived{} \newresult{} ($0.80$--$1.00$ of measured medians \empirical).

\paragraph{Learning order} is decided in the spherical phase by the basin of the initial direction.

\begin{proposition}[Race on trees whose cores all carry sites]\label{prop:A-race}
  \proven{} (at LO) \newresult{}
  Let the teacher be odeco and the tree an MPS. By \cref{prop:A-reduced} the components evolve
  independently at LO, and component $r$ blows up at $\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}\hat t_{1,r}/\tw r$, where the
  $\hat t_{1,r}$ are \iid copies of the prefactor of \cref{cor:A-blowup} for a single component of unit
  weight. The first component learned is $\arg\min_r\hat t_{1,r}/\tw r$, and for two components with
  $\varrho=\tw2/\tw1<1$
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-race}
    P(\text{larger first})=P\Big(\frac{\hat t_{1,2}}{\hat t_{1,1}}>\varrho\Big)
    =\frac12+\frac12P\Big(\Big|\ln\frac{\hat t_{1,2}}{\hat t_{1,1}}\Big|<\ln\frac1\varrho\Big),
  \end{equation}
  which does not depend on $\isc$. In the spherical flow the basin of component $r$ is exactly the set of
  initial directions with $\hat t_{1,r}/\tw r<\hat t_{1,r'}/\tw{r'}$ for all $r'\ne r$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  The components are independent at LO and the $\hat t_{1,r}$ are exchangeable.
\end{proof}
The race agreed with gradient flow on 100 of 100 initializations (MPS, $\Ns=4$). \empirical:
$P(\text{larger first})\approx F_{\Normal}(\ln(1/\varrho)/S)$ within $\pm0.05$ in all tensor-like settings computed ($F_{\Normal}$ the
standard normal distribution function, $S$ the standard deviation of $\ln(\hat t_{1,2}/\hat t_{1,1})$), and
$\mathrm{sd}\ln\hat t_1\approx c\sqrt{\Kc-2}$ on MPS ($c=0.27$--$0.49$). Hence \conjecture: $P(\text{larger first})\to\frac12$ as
$\Kc\to\infty$ at fixed $\varrho$, $d$, $\chi$, and $P\to1$ needs $d\to\infty$ as well as large $\chi$ (compare the largest-first
order of over-parameterized tensor decomposition, \citealp{ge2021understanding}). When some cores carry no
site (binary $\Ns=8$, HT) the race predicts the first component on $94$--$100\%$ of initializations \empirical.

\begin{proposition}[Matrix-like pairs]\label{prop:A-matrix-like}
  Let the teacher have two components, the smaller $j$ and the larger $r$, with $m_{jr}=2$, and let
  $n=n_{jr}$ and $\varrho=\tw j/\tw r$. (a) \derived{} \knownresult[binary $\Ns=4$:
  \citealp{jacot2022saddletosaddle}, Thm~2] $\params_j$ is a saddle of the spherical flow with $n$ unstable
  directions while $\params_r$ is a local maximum (\cref{prop:A-component-hessian}), so
  $P(\text{smaller first})\to0$ as $\isc\to0$. (b) \conjecture{} (heuristic) \newresult{} As $\isc\to0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-matrix-like}
    P(\text{smaller first})\propto\isc^{\,n(1-\varrho)/\varrho}.
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Heuristic argument for (b)]
  A trajectory that escapes near $\params_j$ passes close to it, where the deviations along the $n$ unstable
  directions grow in $\tau$ at rate $\Kc^{1-\Kc/2}(\tw r-\tw j)$ while $\dd\ln\norm{\params}/\dd\tau=\Kc^{1-\Kc/2}\tw j$, i.e.\
  like $(\norm{\params}/\norm{\params_0})^{(1-\varrho)/\varrho}$. As $\norm{\params}/\norm{\params_0}\sim1/\isc$ at the escape, all $n$
  initial deviations must be below $\sim\isc^{(1-\varrho)/\varrho}$, which for a smooth density has probability
  $\propto\isc^{n(1-\varrho)/\varrho}$.
\end{proof}
Checks of (b): in an LO emulation (MPS, $\Ns=4$, $n=1,\dots,4$) the slopes $\dd\ln P/\dd\ln\isc$ are ordered as $n$
and are $0.67$--$1.03$ of $n(1-\varrho)/\varrho$, steepening toward it as $\isc\to0$. For gradient flow (binary $\Ns=4$,
$\varrho=0.7$) the estimated $P(\text{smaller first})=0.155$, $0.064$, $0.024$ at $\isc=0.1$, $0.01$, $0.001$ (emulation
probability times gradient flow's agreement rate) has local slopes $0.38$, $0.43$ against $0.43$ \empirical;
whether gradient flow has the LO exponent is open. A wrong order is corrected through a strict saddle
(\cref{rem:A-wrong-order}); tests in \cref{sec:s2s-order}.

\subsection{Saddles after the first escape}\label{app:saddles}

\paragraph{Setting and assumptions.}
After the first escape, gradient flow passes near a \emph{learned saddle} $\params_S$ representing the
partial fit $\W_S=\sum_{j\in S}\tw j\U j$. We assume: (A1) whitened inputs; (A2) a
pattern teacher with distinct patterns (orthonormal $\U r$); (A3) $\params_S$ is a balanced ($\Q{e}=0$)
critical point with bond ranks $r_e=\rank\mat{(\W_S)}{e}$, padded with zeros to $\bd{e}$ (\cref{lem:A-unused}
lifts criticality from $r_e$ to $\bd{e}$; with one learned component $\params_S$ is critical iff no residual
has $k=1$, \cref{prop:A-hessian}(c); small initialization gives $\Q{e}=O(\isc^2)$); (A4) $\bd{e}\ge r_e+1$ on
every bond whose rank must grow; (A5) leading order in the entries that vanish at $\params_S$ (of size
$\isc_{\rm eff}$ on the plateau). In a basis of each bond with the learned channels first, entries whose bond
indices are all learned are \emph{old}, the others \emph{new}; $c_e=\bd{e}-r_e$. Let $\mathcal R=\Wt-\W_S$ and
$\Loss_S=\frac12\normF{\mathcal R}^2$. With one learned component $j$, balance forces equal core norms: each core is
$q_j=\tw j^{1/\Kc}$ times the pattern of $j$ on its sites and channel~1 on its bonds \proven.

\paragraph{Expansion around the saddle.}
For a set $\mathcal J$ of cores and perturbations $\delta=(\delta_v)_v$, let $\W_{\mathcal J}[\delta]$ be the
tensor with the cores in $\mathcal J$ replaced by the $\delta_v$ and the others at $\params_S$. Multilinearity
gives exactly $\W(\params_S+\delta)=\sum_{\mathcal J}\W_{\mathcal J}[\delta]$, $\W_\emptyset=\W_S$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A-expansion}
  \Loss(\params_S+\delta)=\Loss_S-\sum_{\mathcal J\neq\emptyset}\ip{\mathcal R}{\W_{\mathcal J}[\delta]}
  +\tfrac12\Big\|\sum_{\mathcal J\neq\emptyset}\W_{\mathcal J}[\delta]\Big\|_F^2 .
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Routes]\label{lem:A-routes}
  \proven{} \newresult{}
  (i) If $\delta_v$ has only new entries, $\W_{\{v\}}[\delta_v]=0$: new entries carry no Gauss--Newton cost at
  second order. (ii) For new entries, $\W_{\mathcal J}[\delta]\ne0$ requires every new index of every $\delta_v$ to
  sit on a bond inside $\mathcal J$ and to be matched there; so every bond leaving $\mathcal J$ carries a
  learned index, and every connected component of $\mathcal J$ has at least two cores. For a connected set
  $\mathcal C$ (a \emph{route}), $\W_{\mathcal C}[\delta]=\mathcal W_{\mathcal C}(\delta)\otimes\bigotimes_{f\in\partial\mathcal C}\Sub{f}$,
  where $\mathcal W_{\mathcal C}(\delta)$ is the small TTN formed by the $\delta_v$ (internal bond dimensions $c_e$),
  $\partial\mathcal C$ the set of bonds leaving $\mathcal C$, and $\Sub{f}$ the contraction of the learned network
  beyond $f$ (a map from the $r_f$ learned indices of $f$ to tensors on the sites $H_f$ beyond $f$). Every
  proper subset $\mathcal C'\subsetneq\mathcal C$ has $\W_{\mathcal C'}[\delta]=0$: \emph{a route pays no Gauss--Newton
  cost at the order of its gain.}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  A new index of $\delta_v$ meets, in the neighbouring core, a perturbation or the zero block of a learned
  core (\cref{lem:A-unused}), and in a proper subset of a route some new index meets a zero block.
\end{proof}
Disconnected sets $\mathcal J$ also contribute, possibly below the route degree (MPS $\Ns=5$, $D=\{0,4\}$:
degree 4), but each connected component $\mathcal J_i$ pays the Gauss--Newton cost $\frac12\normF{\W_{\mathcal J_i}}^2$ at the
order of the gain; for one learned component and two blocks the quadratic form is positive
semidefinite iff $\tw j\ge\tw r$, the condition of \cref{prop:A-piggyback-graph} \derived. That disconnected sets never
descend before the single-core rotations is shown only in this case.

\begin{definition}[Local residual]\label{def:A-local-residual}
  For a route $\mathcal C$, $\mathcal R_{\mathcal C}:=\ip{\mathcal R}{\bigotimes_{f\in\partial\mathcal C}\Sub{f}}$
  (contraction over all sites beyond $\partial\mathcal C$) is a tensor with one mode per core of
  $\mathcal C$, made of its physical legs and its learned boundary indices; then
  $\ip{\mathcal R}{\W_{\mathcal C}[\delta]}=\ip{\mathcal R_{\mathcal C}}{\mathcal W_{\mathcal C}(\delta)}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Escape degree]\label{prop:A-escape-degree}
  \derived{} \knownresult[mechanism: \citealp{kumar2026learning}, Thm~8, and the accumulated utility of
  \citealp{kunin2025alternating}; new: the degree is set by the tree]
  The new entries first lower $\Loss$ at order
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-kstar}
    k^*=\min\{\abs{\mathcal C}:\ \mathcal C\text{ a route},\ \mathcal R_{\mathcal C}\ne0\}.
  \end{equation}
  At LO (old entries frozen at $\params_S$) the new entries of a minimizing route follow the gradient flow
  of a $k^*$-core TTN that fits the target $\mathcal R_{\mathcal C}$ from small initialization: the escape from
  the saddle is the first-escape problem of \cref{app:escape} for the route, with $\Kc\to k^*$ and
  $\Wt\to\mathcal R_{\mathcal C}$. This needs the old entries to sit at a local minimum of the fixed-rank
  problem; it fails at the strict saddles of \cref{prop:A-piggyback-graph}, where the old entries move
  first.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Steiner identity]\label{prop:A-steiner}
  \proven{} (identity and upper bound on the order) \derived{} (order $=k^*$) \newresult[descent along a
  whole connected rank-deficient component: \citealp{furman2026benign}, Lemma~D.8]
  Let $S=\{j\}$. For every route $\mathcal C$,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-steiner}
    \mathcal R_{\mathcal C}=q_j^{\,\Kc-\abs{\mathcal C}}\sum_{r:\,N(D_{jr})\subseteq\mathcal C}\tw r\,\U r|_{\mathcal C},
  \end{equation}
  where $\U r|_{\mathcal C}$ is $\U r$ restricted to the sites of $\mathcal C$. Hence
  $k^*=\min_r\abs{\mathrm{St}(N(D_{jr}))}$; the route of component $r$ has degree
  $k=\abs{\mathrm{St}(N(D_{jr}))}$ and coupling
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-coupling}
    c_r=\tw r\,q_j^{\Kc-k}=\tw r\,\tw j^{(\Kc-k)/\Kc}.
  \end{equation}
  The bonds whose rank must grow to add $r$ are exactly the bonds of $\mathrm{St}(N(D_{jr}))$, so $k$ is one
  plus their number, and $k=1$ iff $D_{jr}$ lies on a single core. With route entries proportional to
  $t$, $\Loss-\Loss_S=-c_rt^{k}+O(t^{2k})$: the saddle has order at most $k^*$. That no curve descends
  faster is shown only up to order three (\cref{prop:A-hessian}); that the order equals $k^*$ is derived
  (\cref{prop:A-escape-degree}).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  Beyond a boundary bond $f$ the learned network is $\Sub{f}=q_j^{n_f}\U j|_{H_f}$ ($n_f$ cores, sites $H_f$,
  $\sum_fn_f=\Kc-\abs{\mathcal C}$). As $\mathcal R=\sum_{r\ne j}\tw r\U r$ and $\U r$ contracted with $\Sub{f}$ gives
  $q_j^{n_f}\prod_{i\in H_f}\ip{\fac ri}{\fac ji}$ ($q_j^{n_f}$ if $\pi_r=\pi_j$ on $H_f$, else $0$), $\U r$ survives iff
  $N(D_{jr})\subseteq\mathcal C$, and a connected $\mathcal C$ containing $N(D_{jr})$ contains its Steiner subtree.
  The rank statement is \cref{prop:teacher-spectra}(b). For the descent curve let $\delta_v$ be $t$ times the
  pattern of $r$ on $\sites v$, with a new channel on the bonds inside $\mathcal C$ and channel~1 on
  $\partial\mathcal C$: then $\W_{\mathcal C}[\delta]=t^kq_j^{\Kc-k}\U r$, every other $\W_{\mathcal J}[\delta]$ vanishes
  (\cref{lem:A-routes}), and $\Loss-\Loss_S=-c_rt^k+\frac12q_j^{2(\Kc-k)}t^{2k}$.
\end{proof}
\Cref{tab:A-route-degree} lists $k$ for two-component teachers (in Tucker decompositions descent has order $n$
when $n=2,3,4$ cores must move, \citealp{frandsen2022optimization}).

\begin{lemma}[Several learned components]\label{lem:A-general-S}
  \proven{} \newresult{}
  Let $E_+$ be the set of bonds $e$ with $\rank\mat{(\W_S+\U r)}{e}>\rank\mat{(\W_S)}{e}$. (i) $E_+$ is connected.
  (ii) If $E_+=\emptyset$, one core can produce $\U r$: $\nabla\Loss(\params_S)\ne0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  (i) If the restriction of $\U r$ to one side of a bond lies outside the learned column space, so does its
  restriction to any larger side. (ii) A walk from any core toward a branch on which $\U r$ is new cannot
  return, so it ends at a core all of whose branches are old, which alone can produce $\U r$.
\end{proof}
With several learned components, adding $r$ exactly needs $k_{\rm ex}=\abs{E_+}+1$ cores, and $k^*\le k_{\rm ex}$
\derived, strictly when the learned space of a bond holds a superposition of learned patterns that
overlaps $\U r|_{H_f}$ without containing it: a cheaper route then first learns the projection, adding a
plateau at the best fit of an intermediate rank profile (\emph{partial learning}, \cref{rem:A-sequence}).
When $k^*=k_{\rm ex}$ the coupling is $c_r=\tw r\prod_{f\in\partial\mathcal C}\norm{\Sub{f}\T\,\U r|_{H_f}}$ in the balanced gauge \derived.

\begin{proposition}[Hessian at a balanced learned saddle]\label{prop:A-hessian}
  \proven{} (at $\params_S$) \newresult[that two-core descent gives a strict saddle is
  \citealp{furman2026benign}, Lemma~D.9; deep linear networks: \citealp{achour2024loss}]
  (a) The Hessian at $\params_S$ is block diagonal between old and new entries; the old (\emph{fixed-rank})
  block is the Hessian of the problem with bond ranks fixed at $r_e$ (Gauss--Newton term plus the
  piggyback couplings of \cref{prop:A-piggyback-graph}). (b) The new (\emph{rank-increasing}) block vanishes
  except on two-core routes: a route $\{u,v\}$ through the bond $e$ contributes
  $\big(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-B\\-B\T&0\end{smallmatrix}\big)$, $B=\mathcal R_{\{u,v\}}$, for each of the $c_e$ new channels, i.e.\
  eigenvalues $\pm\sigma_k(B)$ with multiplicity $c_e$; with one learned component the negative one is $-\lambda$,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-lambda2}
    \lambda=\sigma_{\max}(\mathcal R_{\{u,v\}})=\tw r\,\tw j^{(\Kc-2)/\Kc}.
  \end{equation}
  (c) Hence $\params_S$ is a strict saddle iff the fixed-rank block has a negative eigenvalue or a route with
  $k^*=2$ exists. If $k^*=1$ it is not critical (one learned component: $\norm{\nabla\Loss(\params_S)}=\tw rq_j^{\Kc-1}$);
  if $k^*\ge3$ and the fixed-rank block is positive semidefinite, the Hessian has no negative eigenvalue: a
  higher-order saddle. (d) The gauge orbit through $\params_S$ has dimension $\sum_e[\bd{e}^2-(\bd{e}-r_e)^2]$, and
  the Hessian kernel has dimension $\sum_er_e^2+\dim\params_{\rm new}-2\sum_{k^*=2\text{ routes}}c_e$
  ($\dim\params_{\rm new}$: the number of new entries).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
  An old--new cross term of $\ip{\mathcal R}{\W_{\mathcal J}}$ needs an old index to meet a new one, which gives
  zero; the Gauss--Newton cross term vanishes by \cref{lem:A-routes}(i). On the new entries the only
  second-order terms are $-\ip{\mathcal R}{\W_{\{u,v\}}[\delta]}$ for adjacent pairs, $\mathcal R_{\{u,v\}}=q_j^{\Kc-2}\sum_r\tw r\U r|_{\{u,v\}}$
  for one learned component \eqref{eq:A-steiner}. Gauge generators on new--new blocks fix $\params_S$.
\end{proof}
Checked in 21 (tree, $D$) cases ($\lambda$ to six digits); $\lambda$ is also the escape rate of a $k=2$ route.

\begin{proposition}[Piggyback block and piggyback graph]\label{prop:A-piggyback-graph}
  \proven{} \newresult[for general trees; deep linear networks: \citealp{achour2024loss}, Thm~7; odeco
  components are local optima of rank-one approximation: \citealp{anandkumar2014tensor}]
  Let $S=\{j\}$. A \emph{piggyback direction} keeps the learned bond index and rotates the physical vectors
  of one terminal core $u$ toward a site pattern $w$ on $\sites u$. (i) For one residual $r$ with $m_{jr}=2$
  and terminal cores $u,v$ the rotation block has diagonal $q_j^{2(\Kc-1)}$, off-diagonal $-\tw rq_j^{\Kc-2}$ and
  eigenvalues $q_j^{\Kc-2}(\tw j\mp\tw r)$; for $m_{jr}\ge3$ it is $q_j^{2(\Kc-1)}\Id\succ0$. (ii) In general it is
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-piggyback}
    H_{\rm rot}=q_j^{\Kc-2}\big(\tw j\Id-\Gamma\big),
  \end{equation}
  where $\Gamma$ is the weighted adjacency matrix of the \emph{piggyback graph}: its nodes are the pairs
  $(u,w)$ of a terminal core and a rotated site pattern, with one edge between $(u,\pi_r|_u)$ and
  $(v,\pi_r|_v)$, of weight $\tw r$, for every residual component $r$ with $N(D_{jr})=\{u,v\}$. Hence
  $\lambda_{\min}(H_{\rm rot})=q_j^{\Kc-2}(\tw j-\lambda_{\max}(\Gamma))$, and $\params_j$ is a strict saddle iff
  $\lambda_{\max}(\Gamma)>\tw j$ or a route with $k^*=2$ exists.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  Rotating core $u$ by $\varepsilon_u$ toward $w$ creates a mixed tensor (pattern $w$ on $\sites u$, $j$ elsewhere) of norm
  $q_j^{\Kc-1}\varepsilon_u$; those of different nodes are orthonormal, so the Gauss--Newton part is diagonal
  ($q_j^{2(\Kc-1)}=\tw jq_j^{\Kc-2}$). A residual $r$ with terminals $\{u,v\}$ couples two rotations through
  $-\ip{\mathcal R}{\W_{\{u,v\}}}=-\tw rq_j^{\Kc-2}\varepsilon_u\varepsilon_v$; residuals with $m_{jr}\ge3$ enter at order $m_{jr}$; the radial part is $\succeq0$.
\end{proof}
With one learned component $j$ and one residual $r$ the saddle is thus not critical for $k=1$, strict for
$k=2$ ($\lambda_{\min}=-\tw rq_j^{\Kc-2}$), of higher order for $k\ge3$ if $m\ge3$ or $\tw j>\tw r$, and strict via the
piggyback block ($\lambda_{\min}=-(\tw r-\tw j)q_j^{\Kc-2}$) for $k\ge3$, $m=2$, $\tw j<\tw r$ \proven. A \emph{star}
(residuals $r_1,\dots,r_n$ sharing one node) has $\lambda_{\max}(\Gamma)=(\sum_i\tw{r_i}^2)^{1/2}$ and a triangle of equal
weights $s$ has $2s$, so $\params_j$ can be strict although every residual is smaller than $\tw j$ and every route
has $k^*\ge3$ (checked to six digits). Dynamics \empirical: for $j=0000$ (weight 2) and $r=1010$, $r'=1001$
(weight $1.6$ each) on MPS $\Ns=4$ ($\lambda_{\max}(\Gamma)=2.263$), gradient flow passes near $j$ in 4 of 5 seeds and in
all 5 forgets it, learns $r$, $r'$ and relearns $j$; with weights $1.2$ ($\lambda_{\max}(\Gamma)=1.70$) it plateaus at $j$.

\begin{remark}[Binary tree with $\Ns=4$]\label{rem:A-binary4-dln}
  \proven{} \knownresult[\citealp{saxe2014exact,jacot2022saddletosaddle,achour2024loss}]
  With the bottom cores as matrices $\tilde A,\tilde B\in\R^{d^2\times\chi}$ (sites $(0,1)$ and $(2,3)$ as
  rows), $\mat{\W}{e}=\tilde A\,\G{\vroot}\tilde B\T$ on both bonds, and $\Loss$ is exactly the loss of a depth-3
  linear network fitting the $d^2\times d^2$ matrix $\mat{(\Wt)}{e}$. Its saddles are SVD truncations
  \citep{jacot2022saddletosaddle}; critical points that select non-top singular values are strict, balanced
  ones that select the top values are not (\citealp{achour2024loss}, Thm~7, for full-rank targets; the
  computation above covers ours).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[After a wrong first escape]\label{rem:A-wrong-order}
  \derived{} \newresult{} Two-component teachers, smaller component $j$ learned first; $\varepsilon$ and $b$ are the
  piggyback and route amplitudes when the plateau starts. (1) $k\ge3$, $m=2$ (matrix-like pair), if the
  rotation beats the direct add route (always as $\isc\to0$): the learned bond index rotates from $j$ to $r$
  after $\Delta t\approx\ln(1/\varepsilon)/((\tw r-\tw j)\tw j^{(\Kc-2)/\Kc})$, the loss plateaus at $\frac12\tw j^2$ with
  $\ov j\approx0$, and $j$ is relearned through its route after a time $\propto\isc_{\rm eff}^{-(k-2)}$, $\isc_{\rm eff}$ set
  by the largest new-index seed. (2) $k=2$: $r$ is added directly, at the larger rate $\tw r\tw j^{(\Kc-2)/\Kc}$,
  and $j$ is kept. (3) $m\ge3$ (tensor-like pair): no rotation; $r$ is added through its route after
  $\Delta t\approx b^{-(k-2)}/((k-2)\tw r\tw j^{(\Kc-k)/\Kc})$, and $\ov j$ stays at $1$. So a correction through a
  strict saddle exists only for matrix-like pairs, where the wrong order becomes rare as $\isc\to0$
  (\cref{prop:A-matrix-like}). Checked on binary and MPS $\Ns=4$, before the random streams of teacher and
  initialization were separated.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Escape times.}
Let the route of component $r$ have $k=\abs{\mathcal C}$ cores and amplitudes $b_v$ (its new entries: the pattern
of $r$ on the physical legs, a new channel on internal bonds, channel~1 on $\partial\mathcal C$). With the old
entries frozen, one learned component and $A:=q_j^{\Kc-k}$, \eqref{eq:A-expansion} and \cref{lem:A-routes} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A-route-ode}
  \dot b_v=A\prod_{u\in\mathcal C\setminus v}b_u\Big(\tw r-A\prod_{u\in\mathcal C}b_u\Big),\qquad
  \ov r=\frac{A}{\tw r}\prod_{u\in\mathcal C}b_u ,
\end{equation}
a depth-$k$ mode with target $\tw r/A$ that conserves the differences $b_u^2-b_v^2$.

\begin{proposition}[Escape times with head start]\label{prop:A-head-start}
  \derived{} \newresult[for $k\ge3$; the $k=2$ exponent is known: \citealp{zhang2026saddletosaddle},
  Thm~4, and \citealp{kunin2025alternating}; the trichotomy in $k$: \citealp{rawal2026theory}, Thm~4.2]
  Let the initialization be aligned: every core of the learned component $j$ and of the route starts at
  amplitude $\isc$, and the hanging cores are aligned with the shared pattern. Let
  $\Delta t_2=t(\ov r=\frac12)-t(\ov j=\frac12)$. As $\isc\to0$,
  \begin{align}
    k=2:&\quad \Delta t_2=\frac{(1-\tw r/\tw j)\ln(1/\isc)+O(1)}{\tw r\,\tw j^{(\Kc-2)/\Kc}},\label{eq:A-dt-k2}\\
    k\ge3:&\quad \Delta t_2=\frac{\isc^{-(k-2)}\,(1-\tw r/\tw j)}{(k-2)\,\tw r\,\tw j^{(\Kc-k)/\Kc}}\big(1+o(1)\big),\label{eq:A-dt-k3}
  \end{align}
  and $\Delta t_2=O(1)$ (no plateau) for $k=1$. For $k\ge3$ the route enters the plateau with the effective
  amplitude $\isc_{\rm eff}=\isc(1-\tw r/\tw j)^{-1/(k-2)}$; for $k=\Kc$ it never sees $j$, and $r$ escapes at
  $\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}/((\Kc-2)\tw r)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Derivation]
  The route exists from the start, with the then-small learned cores in its hanging part, so it grows
  during the rise of $j$ with the coupling $c(t)=\tw rq_j(t)^{\Kc-k}$, where $\dot q_j\simeq\tw jq_j^{\Kc-1}$
  while $q_j$ is small. For $k=2$, $\ln b$ grows by $\int c\,\dd t=\int\tw rq_j^{-1}\dd q_j/\tw j=(\tw r/\tw j)\ln(1/\isc)+O(1)$,
  so the route enters the plateau at $\ln b_{\rm eff}=-(1-\tw r/\tw j)\ln(1/\isc)+O(1)$ and needs a further
  $\ln(1/b_{\rm eff})/\lambda$, with $\lambda$ from \eqref{eq:A-lambda2}. For $k\ge3$ the balanced route obeys
  $\ddt b^{-(k-2)}=-(k-2)c(t)$, so at the end of the rise of $j$
  $b_{\rm eff}^{-(k-2)}=\isc^{-(k-2)}-(k-2)\int c\,\dd t=\isc^{-(k-2)}(1-\tw r/\tw j)+O(1)$, and the plateau lasts
  $b_{\rm eff}^{-(k-2)}/((k-2)\tw r\tw j^{(\Kc-k)/\Kc})$; as $q_j^{\Kc-k}\dd t=q_j^{1-k}\dd q_j/\tw j$, this head start accrues while $q_j\approx\isc$.
\end{proof}
The factor $1-\tw r/\tw j$ is the head start (without it the $k=2$ slope would be $1/\lambda$;
\cref{sec:s2s-locality}). Checked to $10^{-4}$ with aligned initialization; with fan-in initialization the $k=2$
slope is $1.066$ against $1.065$, per-seed exponents $-0.97$ to $-1.02$ ($k=3$) and $-1.99$ to $-2.00$ ($k=4$).
An unbalanced route with positive product has $\Delta t=\int_0^\infty\dd\beta/(2c\prod_{v\in\mathcal C}(b_v^2+\beta)^{1/2})$ \derived{}
(as in \cref{prop:A-product-mode}); a negative product lengthens the plateau. With random initialization the
prefactors are set by the route amplitudes at each plateau start (seed-to-seed factors above 20 for $k\ge4$) \empirical.

\begin{proposition}[Signatures of the route degree]\label{prop:A-signatures}
  \derived{} \knownresult[the exponent $2-2/k$ of a balanced product of $k$ factors:
  \citealp{arora2019implicit,razin2022implicit}; new: its use to read $k$ off TTN plateaus]
  (i) Per seed (fixed initialization direction, $\isc$ varied), $\Delta t_2\propto\isc^{-(k-2)}$ for $k\ge3$.
  (ii) On a balanced route, \eqref{eq:A-route-ode} gives
  $\dot{\ov r}=kA^{2/k}\tw r^{2-2/k}\ov r^{\,2-2/k}(1-\ov r)$, so the local growth exponent is
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-growth-exponent}
    \gamma(\ov r)=\frac{\dd\ln\dot{\ov r}}{\dd\ln\ov r}=2-\frac2k-\frac{\ov r}{1-\ov r},
  \end{equation}
  that is $1,\frac43,\frac32,\frac85$ for $k=2,\dots,5$ as $\ov r\to0$. The route is balanced only once
  $b^2\gg\abs{b_u^2-b_v^2}=O(\isc^2)$, so $k$ is read in the window $\ov r\in[10^{-4},10^{-2}]$ (a smaller $\gamma$ at
  smaller $\ov r$ indicates an unbalanced route). (iii) At a plateau point (route amplitudes small but
  nonzero) each active route contributes one negative Hessian direction modulo gauge, with
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-plateau-lambda}
    \lambda_{\min}\simeq-\tw r\tw j^{(\Kc-2)/\Kc}\ \ (k=2),\qquad
    \lambda_{\min}\simeq-(k-1)\,\tw rA\,b^{k-2}=O(\isc_{\rm eff}^{k-2})\ \ (k\ge3),
  \end{equation}
  where $b=(\tw r\ov r/A)^{1/k}$ (for $k\ge3$ on a balanced route). The count of one negative eigenvalue
  per route holds for $k\le3$ only: at $k=4$, new-index pairs that connect to learned structure add
  indefinite terms of order $\isc^2$, the same order as the route eigenvalue.
\end{proposition}
In (iii) the route block of the Hessian is $-\tw rAb^{k-2}(\mathbf 1\mathbf 1\T-\Id)$, whose $k-1$ positive directions
are gauge. Checked on MPS $\Ns=4$ ($\gamma$ within $0.01$; $\abs{\lambda_{\min}}/\lambda_{\max}\propto\isc^0,\isc^1,\isc^2$ for $k=2,3,4$;
\eqref{eq:A-plateau-lambda} within a factor 1.4 for $k=3$, 3.5 for $k=4$), so the saddle type is read from the
$\isc$-scaling of $\lambda_{\min}$ or from $\gamma$.

\begin{remark}[The whole sequence]\label{rem:A-sequence}
  \derived{} at LO for each step; \conjecture{} (heuristic) as an algorithm. \newresult{}
  (1) The first escape ends near a critical point of the bond-rank-one problem; it is a single component
  $j$ only if $\params_j$ is a local maximum of the spherical problem (\cref{prop:A-component-hessian}, with
  the piggyback graph when several partners share a core); a star with $\lambda_{\max}(\Gamma)>\tw j$ sends the
  flow past $j$ toward a superposition. (2) At a later saddle whose fixed-rank block has a negative
  eigenvalue (wrong order, stars) the next event is a rotation of the learned structure or a $k=2$ route,
  whichever is more unstable, in time $\propto\ln(1/\isc)$ (\cref{prop:A-escape-degree} does not apply);
  otherwise it is the route of smallest $k^*$ (logarithmic beats power law, smaller powers beat larger
  ones), ties being decided by the coupling and head start $c\,b_{\rm eff}^{k-2}$, dominated for $k\ge3$ by the
  random route amplitudes. (3) The escape raises the bond rank by one on the bonds inside the route, and
  the next saddle is the best approximation of $\Wt$ with the new rank profile (for pattern teachers
  without superpositions, the partial sum of the learned components), whose loss is the plateau level. Since $k^*$
  depends on the tree, \emph{locality beats weight} (teacher B4, \cref{sec:s2s-locality}); the number of cores that
  must change at once plays the role of the leap of \citet{abbe2023sgd}.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Conservation-law trap]\label{prop:A-trap}
  \derived{} (mechanism, in a reduced model) \newresult{}
  Let a route end at a leaf core, on the bond $e$ (child $v$, parent $p$); let $\nu$ be the new channel used by
  the route, $Q_{\nu\nu}=\nu\T\Q{e}\nu$, $x$ the leaf's new column, and split the parent's slice along $\nu$ into
  its route entry $y_{\rm route}$ and the \emph{visible} entries $y_{\rm vis}$, which connect through the other side
  to learned structure. Conservation gives $\norm{x}^2-\norm{y_{\rm vis}}^2-\norm{y_{\rm route}}^2=Q_{\nu\nu}$. On the
  plateau the Gauss--Newton term makes $x$ and $y_{\rm vis}$ decay together, at a rate of order
  $\abs{Q_{\nu\nu}}=O(\isc^2)$, so the leaf column decays to zero iff the end is \emph{unfavourable},
  $Q_{\nu\nu}+\norm{y_{\rm route}}^2<0$. The route's drive on $x$ is proportional to the product of the other route amplitudes, so while another
  end is favourable it pins $x$ at a positive value and the route escapes, slowly. In the reduced model a
  permanent stall ($x\to0$ down to roundoff) therefore requires every end of the route to be unfavourable.
\end{proposition}
The dead state is linearly stable if $\gamma_1\gamma_2>\kappa^2$, with decay rates $\gamma_i\sim\abs{Q_i}\sim\isc^2$ and mutual
drive $\kappa\sim\isc^{k-2}$ (\conjecture, heuristic): stable as $\isc\to0$ for $k\ge5$, never for $k=3$. On HT leaf bonds
($\bd v=d=2$ below a parent bond of dimension $3$) fan-in initialization gives $Q_{\nu\nu}=\isc^2(X_2/2-X_6/4)$ ($X_n$
independent chi-squared, $n$ degrees of freedom; $\nu$ independent of the initialization), so
$\E Q_{\nu\nu}=-\isc^2/2$; then $P(Q_{\nu\nu}>0)\approx0.30$ and about half of the two-ended routes have both ends
unfavourable (\conjecture, heuristic). Data \empirical{} (HT $\Ns=4$, teacher B4, $\isc=0.2$, nine fan-in
initializations): all 6 runs with all ends unfavourable stalled with dead leaves, the 2 with one
favourable end had the other end pinned; balanced initialization ($\Q{e}=0$, same $\Wz$) kept the routes
alive (3 of 3, before the random streams were separated). \conjecture: balanced initialization always
avoids the trap (a property of unbalanced initialization, not of the landscape), and for $k\ge5$ the
stalled fraction tends to $P(\text{all ends unfavourable})$ as $\isc\to0$. The locality experiments contradict
parts of this picture (dead runs with a live route end, no stall in 144 fan-in HT4 runs with $k=5$;
\cref{sec:s2s-failure}).

\subsection{Teachers that are not orthogonally decomposable}\label{app:teachers}

For a general teacher the saddles are no longer partial sums of components. $\mathcal M_{\mathbf r}$ is the
manifold of tensors with bond ranks exactly $\mathbf r=(r_e)_e$ \citep{holtz2012manifolds,uschmajew2013geometry}.
For $\W\in\mathcal M_{\mathbf r}$ and $e=\edge v$, $p=\pa v$, $U_e$ and $V_e$ are the column and row spaces of
$\mat{\W}{e}$, the environment spaces of $e$ are $E_e^-=(\bigotimes_{c\in\ch v}U_c)\otimes\R^{d_v}$ and
$E_e^+=(\bigotimes_{c\in\ch p\setminus\{v\}}U_c)\otimes\R^{d_p}\otimes V_p$ ($d_v=\prod_{i\in\sites v}\pd[i]$; no
$V_p$ at the root), and $\widetilde W^*_e:=P_{E_e^-}\,\mat{(\Wt)}{e}\,P_{E_e^+}$ is the
\emph{environment-projected teacher}.

\begin{proposition}[Critical points of the rank-constrained problem]\label{prop:A-critical-points}
  \proven{} \knownresult[in substance: the fixed-point conditions of HOOI,
  \citealp{delathauwer2000best,elden2011perturbation}, and of one-site ALS, \citealp{holtz2012alternating};
  tangent spaces: \citealp{uschmajew2013geometry}]
  $\W\in\mathcal M_{\mathbf r}$ is a critical point of $\Loss$ restricted to $\mathcal M_{\mathbf r}$ iff, for every bond $e$,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-critical-points}
    \mat{\W}{e}=P_{U_e}\widetilde W^*_e=\widetilde W^*_eP_{V_e},
  \end{equation}
  i.e.\ iff $U_e$ is spanned by $r_e$ left singular vectors of $\widetilde W^*_e$ (not necessarily the top ones)
  and $\mat{\W}{e}$ is the corresponding SVD truncation, i.e.\ iff $\W$ is a fixed point of one-site
  alternating least squares. Padded with zeros it is critical for every $\bd{e}\ge r_e$ (\cref{lem:A-unused}).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
  At full bond ranks the differential with respect to $\G v$ spans $\mathcal V_v=E^-_{e}\otimes V_e$ ($e=\edge v$), so $\W$
  is critical iff $P_{\mathcal V_v}\Wt=\W$ for every $v$, i.e.\ $\widetilde W^*_eP_{V_e}=\mat{\W}{e}$ at $v$ and $P_{U_e}\widetilde W^*_e=\mat{\W}{e}$
  at $p$. If $P_UA=AP_V=X$ has rank $\dim U$, $P_UAA\T=XX\T$ is symmetric: $U$ is a left singular space of $A$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[When is the hierarchical SVD critical?]\label{prop:A-hsvd}
  \proven{} (a, b) \derived{} (c) \newresult[the $\varepsilon^4$ gap; the HSVD is
  \citealp{grasedyck2010hierarchical}]
  Let $\W_H$ be the HSVD truncation of $\Wt$ at ranks $\mathbf r$. (a) $\W_H$ is critical iff its frames satisfy
  \eqref{eq:A-critical-points}, which generic teachers violate (parts discarded at other bonds leak into
  each bond's environment). (b) If the best rank-$r_e$
  approximation of $\Wt$ across every bond $e$ is one and the same tensor $\W$ (consistent truncations,
  e.g.\ an odeco teacher truncated to the same components on every bond, or binary $\Ns=4$), then
  $\W_H=\W$ is critical and the global minimizer over bond ranks at most $\mathbf r$. (c) If $\Wt=\W+\varepsilon E$
  with $\W$ consistent and $E$ generic, the nearby critical point $\W_c$ has $\normF{\W_H-\W_c}=O(\varepsilon^2)$
  and $\Loss(\W_H)-\Loss(\W_c)=\tfrac12\normF{\W_H-\W_c}^2(1+O(\varepsilon))=O(\varepsilon^4)$.
\end{proposition}
For (b), $\normF{\Wt-X}\ge\normF{\Wt-\W}$ for every $X$ with one bond rank at most $r_e$; for (c), both sets of
frames are first-order rotations of those of $\W$ with the same driving terms, $\Wt-\W_c$ is normal to
$\mathcal M_{\mathbf r}$ at $\W_c$ and $\W_H-\W_c$ tangent up to curvature (checked for $\varepsilon\le0.2$).

\paragraph{The rank-one plateau.} \derived{} \newresult{} The origin plateau ends along a KKT direction of $g$,
and the global maximum of $g$ has bond rank one (\cref{prop:A-gmax}). That local maxima do too, and that
the flow reaches one, are conjectures (true at every rank-one plateau computed); then the next plateau is
a critical point of the bond-rank-one problem, not an HSVD truncation. If every core carries at most one
site (MPS, HT) it is a local best rank-one approximation $\bar s\bigotimes_iw_i$, $\bar s=\ip{\Wt}{\bigotimes_iw_i}$, at level
$\frac12(\normF{\Wt}^2-\bar s^2)$; on binary $\Ns=4$ it is the top singular pair of the matricization. In the cases
computed it lies $6$--$12\%$ below the HSVD truncation \empirical, which is not general.

\begin{proposition}[Two non-orthogonal components]\label{prop:A-two-components}
  \proven{} (stationarity; threshold among symmetric critical points) \derived{} (fold)
  \newresult[elementary] Let $\Wt=\tw1\U1+\tw2\U2$ with $\Ns$ sites, $\tw1\ge\tw2$ and overlaps
  $\ip{\fac1i}{\fac2i}=\cos\phi_i\in[0,1)$, at most one of them zero. Every rank-one critical point has $w_i$
  in the plane of $\fac1i,\fac2i$, at angle $\psi_i$ from $\fac1i$, with value $\tw1\prod_i\cos\psi_i+\tw2\prod_i\cos(\phi_i-\psi_i)$.
  For equal weights and overlaps $\cos\phi$, the symmetric point $\psi_i=\phi/2$ has Hessian eigenvalues
  $-2c^{\Ns-2}[c^2-(\Ns-1)(1-c^2)]$ along $(1,\dots,1)$ and $-2c^{\Ns-2}$ on its complement ($c=\cos\frac\phi2$, unit
  weight), so there are two local maxima, one near each component, iff $\cos\phi<(\Ns-2)/\Ns$, and one merged
  maximum otherwise (asymmetric maxima excluded numerically). For $\varrho=\tw2/\tw1<1$ the
  maximum near $\U2$ disappears at a fold, $\cos\phi_{\rm fold}\approx\frac{\Ns-2}{\Ns-1}(\frac{\varrho}{\Ns-1})^{1/(\Ns-2)}$
  for small $\varrho$ ($1$--$13\%$ below the exact values). The rank-one plateau is never one of the components.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Rates at the rank-one saddle of an MPS]\label{prop:A-mps-rates}
  \derived{} (rates) \proven{} (bound) \newresult{}
  Let the student be an MPS ($\Kc=\Ns$, core $k$ carries site $k$) at the balanced, zero-padded rank-one
  critical point $\W_1=\bar s\bigotimes_iw_i$, a local maximum of the rank-one problem, and let
  $M_e=\Wt\times_{i\notin\{k,k+1\}}w_i$ be the $d\times d$ matrix of the bond $e$ between cores $k$ and $k+1$. Then
  $(w_k,w_{k+1})$ is the top singular pair of $M_e$ (singular value $\bar s$), the route block of $e$ is
  $\bar s^{(\Ns-2)/\Ns}(M_e-\bar s\,w_kw_{k+1}\T)$, with Hessian eigenvalues $-\bar s^{(\Ns-2)/\Ns}\sigma_m(M_e)$, $m=2,\dots,d$ (each
  $\bd e-1$ times), and the escape rate of bond $e$ is
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-gamma-e}
    \gamma_e=\bar s^{(\Ns-2)/\Ns}\,\sigma_2(M_e),\qquad\text{with}\quad
    \sigma_m(M_e)\le\sigma_m\big(\mat{(\Wt)}{e}\big)
  \end{equation}
  by interlacing ($M_e$ compresses $\mat{(\Wt)}{e}$ by isometries).
\end{proposition}
For two components with $d=2$, $\sigma_2(M_e)=\det M_e/\bar s$ with $\det M_e=\tw1\tw2\sin\phi_k\sin\phi_{k+1}a_eb_e$,
$a_e=\prod_{i\ne k,k+1}\cos\psi_i$, $b_e=\prod_{i\ne k,k+1}\cos(\phi_i-\psi_i)$: \emph{the bond that grows first is the one
whose two sites differ most while the other sites agree most}, not the one with the largest teacher
Schmidt value (checked to four digits).

\begin{corollary}[Second escape]\label{cor:A-second-escape}
  \derived{} \newresult[\cref{prop:A-head-start} with $\tw j\to\bar s$ and $\tw r\to\sigma_2(M_e)$]
  Assume $\isc\to0$, balanced cores and route projections $\propto\isc$ at initialization, and let
  $\kappa_e=\sigma_2(M_e)/\bar s$. During the first escape the route of bond $e$ grows by the factor
  $\isc^{-\kappa_e}$, so the first bond to gain rank is $e^*=\arg\min_eh_e=\arg\max_e\sigma_2(M_e)$, and
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-second-escape}
    \tev{e^*}-\tev1=h_{e^*}\ln(1/\isc)+O(1),\qquad h_e=\frac{1-\kappa_e}{\gamma_e}.
  \end{equation}
\end{corollary}
Here $\tev e$ is when $\sigma_2$ across $e$ first exceeds $f\in[0.03,0.1]$ times the teacher's (slopes checked to $1\%$).

\begin{proposition}[Later escapes on an MPS: an accumulated-utility race]\label{prop:A-agf-race}
  \derived{} (as $\isc\to0$, under (A-carry)) \knownresult[the rule: alternating gradient flows,
  \citealp{kunin2025alternating}; new: the TTN routes and their rates]
  Give every pending two-core route $\rho$ the log-amplitude $\ell_\rho=\ln x_\rho/\ln(1/\isc)\in[-1,0]$ and use
  the clock $\vartheta=(t-\tev1)/\ln(1/\isc)$. Then $\ell_\rho(0)=-1+\kappa_\rho$; while the flow sits at the
  saddle $S_n$, $\dd\ell_\rho/\dd\vartheta=\gamma_\rho(S_n)$, the rate of the route at the exact critical point $S_n$;
  $\rho$ escapes when $\ell_\rho=0$, and the flow moves in time $O(1)$ to $S_{n+1}$, with the rank of its bond raised
  by one. (A-carry): at each transition a pending route's amplitude projects onto its new unstable
  direction with a $\isc$-independent factor $p\ne0$.
\end{proposition}
Event times are $\tev e-\tev1=\vartheta_e\ln(1/\isc)+C_e+o(1)$ with initialization-dependent offsets $C_e$ (up to
about 50 time units), so near-tied later escapes come in a seed-dependent order at accessible $\isc$
(\conjecture, heuristic). Unlike greedy low-rank learning \citep{li2021towards}, the race keeps memory
across saddles. Checked (two components, overlaps $(0.2,0.5,0.7,0.3)$): predicted bond order 0, 2, 1 with
slopes $6.823$, $12.178$, $14.467$, confirmed to $0.5\%$ at $\isc\le10^{-6}$ in 7 of 8 seeds.

\paragraph{Generic teachers: dependence on the tree.} \derived{} \newresult{} At any critical point the local
residual of an adjacent pair $\{u,v\}$ through the bond $e$ annihilates the learned subspaces on both sides,
so it lives on complements of dimensions $\mathsf d_u-r_e$, $\mathsf d_v-r_e$ ($\mathsf d_u$: the dimension of the
legs of $u$ other than $e$), where a generic teacher makes it nonzero: \emph{a route with $k^*=2$ (strict
saddle, logarithmic escape) exists whenever some adjacent pair has $\mathsf d_u,\mathsf d_v>r_e$} (and
$\bd e>r_e$). (i) On an MPS every core has a physical leg, so a generic teacher gives one polynomial plateau
(at the origin) and then only logarithmic ones, with ranks growing bond by bond (compare the abrupt
rank growth in tensor-train factorization, \citealp{milanesi2021implicit}). (ii) On binary and HT trees
the internal cores carry no site and, at the rank-one saddle, have rank-one outer legs, so the cheapest
routes join two sibling bottom cores through their parent ($k=3$): escapes from the rank-one saddle are
polynomial even for generic teachers, and the root bipartition grows only after both halves (bottom-up
order); two-core routes can open later on bottom edges, never for the root bipartition. (iii) Odeco and
pattern teachers suppress two-core routes by orthogonality, so power-law later plateaus need components
orthogonal on the relevant site blocks; for nearly orthogonal ones (overlaps below a crossover
$c_\times=O(\isc)$ up to logarithms) they persist at accessible $\isc$ (\conjecture, heuristic). Checked on one
random CP teacher: strict saddles with logarithmic gaps on MPS $\Ns=4$, $\lambda_{\min}\propto\isc$ and gaps
$\propto\isc^{-1}$ on the binary tree.

\paragraph{Teacher on another tree.} \proven{} \knownresult[standard; cf.\ \citealp{hackbusch2009new}]
A student bond $e$ must reach $\rank\mat{(\Wt)}{e}$, which for a TTN teacher is at most the minimum, over
cuts of the teacher's tree that separate the two sides of $e$, of the product of the cut dimensions; a
binary student with $\Ns=8$ of an MPS teacher with bond dimension $r$, for example, needs rank
$\min(d^2,r^2)$ on the bonds of sites $\{2,3\}$, $\{4,5\}$, i.e.\ about $r^2$ separate saddles. A binary student
with $\Ns=4$ is the deep linear network of \cref{rem:A-binary4-dln} for any teacher: plateaus at
$\frac12\sum_{i>m}\sigma_i(\mat{(\Wt)}{(01|23)})^2$, escapes of degree three ($\propto\isc^{-1}$), as many plateaus as the rank
of the matricization. One teacher thus has different plateau levels and exponents on an MPS and on
a binary tree (tests:
\cref{sec:teacher-cp,sec:teacher-ttn,sec:teacher-mismatch}).

\subsection{Input covariance}\label{app:covariance}

Let $\Sig=\Sigi1\otimes\dots\otimes\Sigi\Ns$ (cross-site correlations: end of this section), and let $\Xi$ multiply the
physical leg of each site $i$ of every core by $\Sigi i^{1/2}$; $\Xi$ is linear, symmetric and invertible, and
$\W(\Xi\params)=\Sig^{1/2}\W(\params)$.

\begin{proposition}[A product covariance is a preconditioned whitened problem]\label{prop:A-precond}
  \proven{} \newresult[for TTNs]
  (a) $\Loss(\params)=\tilde\Loss(\Xi\params)$ with $\tilde\Loss(\tilde\params)=\frac12\normF{\W(\tilde\params)-\Sig^{1/2}\Wt}^2$.
  (b) In the variables $\tilde\params=\Xi\params$ gradient flow reads $\dot{\tilde\params}=-\Xi^2\nabla\tilde\Loss(\tilde\params)$ from
  the anisotropic initialization $\Xi\params_0$: a whitened problem with teacher $\Sig^{1/2}\Wt$ and the
  preconditioner $\Sigi i$ on every physical leg.
  (c) $\nabla\Loss(\params)=\Xi\nabla\tilde\Loss(\Xi\params)$ and $\nabla^2\Loss(\params)=\Xi\,\nabla^2\tilde\Loss(\Xi\params)\,\Xi$ at every
  $\params$. Critical points, critical values, Morse indices and saddle types are therefore those of the
  whitened problem with teacher $\Sig^{1/2}\Wt$ (Sylvester's law of inertia), but escape rates are
  eigenvalues of $\Xi^2\nabla^2\tilde\Loss$, not of $\nabla^2\tilde\Loss$.
  (d) The gauge group acts on bond legs only, so \cref{prop:conservation} holds unchanged in $\params$: small
  initialization is balanced in $\params$, not in $\tilde\params$.
  (e) Plain whitened gradient flow is recovered iff $\Xi^2\propto\Id$, i.e.\ iff every $\Sigi i=\kappa_i\Id$ and
  $\prod_{i\in\sites v}\kappa_i$ is the same for every core (equal to 1 on binary and HT trees, whose internal
  cores have factor 1); for $\Sigi i=\kappa_i\Id$ time is rescaled by $\prod_i\kappa_i$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  (a) is $\W(\Xi\params)=\Sig^{1/2}\W(\params)$; (b) and (c) are the chain rule through the symmetric map $\Xi$
  (checked to $2\cdot10^{-8}$; without the preconditioner the flow deviates by 4--100\%).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Aligned covariance: exact clocks]\label{prop:A-aligned-cov}
  \proven{} \knownresult[Saxe-type modes: \citealp{saxe2014exact}; depth two: \citealp{gidel2019implicit};
  new: every tree]
  Let the teacher be odeco with factors that are eigenvectors of the site covariances,
  $\Sigi i\fac ri=\lambda_{r,i}\fac ri$, and let $\Lambda_r=\prod_i\lambda_{r,i}$. On the aligned family of
  \cref{prop:aligned}: (a) $\Loss=\frac12\sum_r\Lambda_r(\prodamp r-\tw r)^2$; (b) the family is invariant and
  $\dot q_{v,r}=\Lambda_r(\tw r-\prodamp r)\prod_{u\ne v}\amp ur$, so each component evolves as in the whitened
  problem with its own clock, $\prodamp r(t;\Sig)=\prodamp r(\Lambda_rt;\Id)$, the same for every core; (c) from a
  balanced start $q_0$, $t_{1/2,r}=q_0^{-(\Kc-2)}/((\Kc-2)\Lambda_r\tw r)+\delta_\Kc/(\Lambda_r\tw r^{2-2/\Kc})+O(q_0^2)$
  (\cref{prop:A-first-event}); (d) with the set $S$ learned the loss is $\frac12\sum_{r\notin S}\Lambda_r\tw r^2$, and
  the events of $j$ and then $r$ are separated by
  $(1-\frac{\Lambda_r\tw r}{\Lambda_j\tw j})/((\Kc-2)\Lambda_r\tw rq_0^{\Kc-2})$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  $\ip{\U r}{\Sig\U{r'}}=\delta_{rr'}\Lambda_r$, so $\Sig(\W-\Wt)=\sum_r\Lambda_r(\prodamp r-\tw r)\U r$; argue as for \cref{prop:aligned}.
\end{proof}
Three orderings coexist \derived{} \newresult: by escape speed $\Lambda_r\tw r$, by loss drop $\frac12\Lambda_r\tw r^2$, and
the whitened one by $\tw r$. With $\tw1>\tw2$ and aligned initialization, component 2 escapes first iff
$\Lambda_2/\Lambda_1>\tw1/\tw2$ (LO in $q_0$); in the \emph{mismatch window} $\tw1/\tw2<\Lambda_2/\Lambda_1<(\tw1/\tw2)^2$ the faster
component is the smaller loss drop, so the first plateau lies above the reduced-rank-regression optimum:
the visited saddles are not the reduced-rank solutions of \citet{gidel2019implicit}, whose Corollary~2
states no ordering condition. With power-law spectra $\lambda_k\propto k^{-\beta}$ and component $r$ on eigenvector
$k_r$ at every site, $\Lambda_2/\Lambda_1=(k_1/k_2)^{\beta\Ns}$, so the pairing $k_r=r$ reinforces the whitened order.

\begin{proposition}[Random initialization: what replaces the teacher]\label{prop:A-escape-cov}
  \derived{} \newresult[TTN form of the covariance-weighted first escape of
  \citealp{jacot2022saddletosaddle,atanasov2022neural,rawal2026theory}]
  Near the origin $\Loss=\Loss_0-\ip{\W}{\Sig\Wt}+\frac12\normS{\W}^2$, and the fan-in initialization law of $\params$
  does not depend on $\Sig$. Hence: (a) the escape from the origin (direction, time and learning order of
  \cref{app:escape}) is that of the whitened problem with the \emph{escape tensor}
  $\Sig\Wt=\E[f^*(x)\Feat(x)]$ in place of $\Wt$; for aligned covariance $\tw r\to\Lambda_r\tw r$, and for a single
  (or dominant) aligned component $\dd\ln\tev1/\dd\ln\Lambda=-1$ exactly, for every $\Kc$, tree and split of
  $\Lambda$ over the sites; (b) the landscape (critical points, plateau levels, saddle types) is that of
  $\Sig^{1/2}\Wt$ (\cref{prop:A-precond}); for aligned covariance $\tw r\to\Lambda_r^{1/2}\tw r$; (c) the problems
  $(\Sig,\Wt)$ and $(\Id,\Sig\Wt)$ have the same escape at LO; for an aligned component their half-times, each
  relative to its own target, differ by the $O(1)$ offset $-\delta_\Kc\tw r^{-(2-2/\Kc)}(\Lambda_r^{-(2-2/\Kc)}-\Lambda_r^{-1})$.
\end{proposition}
Without the preconditioner the exponent would be wrong ($-1/2$ with isotropic initialization). Checked:
exponent $-1.000$ (binary, MPS, HT), offset $-0.2218$ against $-0.2219$. The order stays random
(\cref{prop:A-race}), at rate ratio $\Lambda_2\tw2/(\Lambda_1\tw1)$.

\begin{proposition}[Piggyback block under aligned covariance]\label{prop:A-piggyback-cov}
  \proven{} (at the saddle) \newresult{}
  Let $j$ be learned (balanced, $q_j=\tw j^{1/\Kc}$) and let $r$ differ from $j$ on $D=D_{jr}$, with two terminal
  cores $c_1,c_2$. With $\mu_x=\prod_{i\in D\cap\sites{c_1}}\lambda_{x,i}$, $\nu_x=\prod_{i\in D\cap\sites{c_2}}\lambda_{x,i}$ for
  $x\in\{j,r\}$ and $\lambda_{\rm sh}=\prod_{i\notin D}\lambda_{j,i}$, the Hessian on the two rotations is
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-piggyback-cov}
    H_{\rm pb}=\frac{\lambda_{\rm sh}}{\tw j^{2/\Kc}}\begin{pmatrix}\tw j^2\mu_r\nu_j&-\tw r\tw j\mu_r\nu_r\\
    -\tw r\tw j\mu_r\nu_r&\tw j^2\mu_j\nu_r\end{pmatrix}.
  \end{equation}
  It has a negative eigenvalue iff $\Lambda_r\tw r^2>\Lambda_j\tw j^2$, for any split of the eigenvalues over the cores.
  If $c_1,c_2$ are adjacent ($k=2$), its rate is below the add-route rate $b=\Lambda_r\tw r\tw j^{(\Kc-2)/\Kc}$, so with
  comparable seeds $r$ is added directly: a rotation needs $m=2$ and $k\ge3$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  At the learned amplitude, up to constants,
  $\Loss=\frac12\tw j^2\lambda_{\rm sh}(\mu_j\cos^2\varphi+\mu_r\sin^2\varphi)(\nu_j\cos^2\psi+\nu_r\sin^2\psi)
  -\tw j(\Lambda_j\tw j\cos\varphi\cos\psi+\Lambda_r\tw r\sin\varphi\sin\psi)$ in the terminal-core rotations $\varphi,\psi$, so
  $\det H_{\rm pb}\propto\Lambda_j\tw j^2-\Lambda_r\tw r^2$. For adjacent terminals the off-diagonal entry is $-b$
  ($\lambda_{\rm sh}\mu_r\nu_r=\Lambda_r$), and a symmetric $2\times2$ matrix with positive diagonal $h_1,h_2$ and off-diagonal $-b$
  has $\lambda_{\min}>-b$, since $(\frac{h_1+h_2}2+b)^2-(\frac{h_1-h_2}2)^2-b^2=h_1h_2+b(h_1+h_2)>0$.
\end{proof}
In the mismatch window, if the faster component wins the first escape \derived: on binary $\Ns=4$ ($m=2$,
$k=3$) the learned term rotates to the other component at bond rank one, the loss plateaus at
$\frac12\Lambda_2\tw2^2$, and the faster component is relearned later; on MPS $\Ns=4$ and every tree with $m\ge3$
there is no rotation, and the loss plateaus at the higher level $\frac12\Lambda_1\tw1^2$ (rotation rates checked).

\paragraph{Misaligned covariance.} \proven{} (identities and inequality) \derived{} (order, to leading
order in the cross overlaps) \newresult{} For every CP teacher, exactly,
$\Sig\Wt=\sum_r\tw r\Lambda^{\rm esc}_r\widetilde U_r$ and $\Sig^{1/2}\Wt=\sum_r\tw r(\Lambda^{\rm loss}_r)^{1/2}\widehat U_r$, with
$\widetilde U_r=\bigotimes_i\Sigi i\fac ri/\norm{\Sigi i\fac ri}$, $\widehat U_r=\bigotimes_i\Sigi i^{1/2}\fac ri/\norm{\Sigi i^{1/2}\fac ri}$,
$\Lambda^{\rm esc}_r=\prod_i\norm{\Sigi i\fac ri}$ and $\Lambda^{\rm loss}_r=\prod_i\ip{\fac ri}{\Sigi i\fac ri}$; by Cauchy--Schwarz
$\Lambda^{\rm esc}_r\ge\Lambda^{\rm loss}_r$, with equality iff every $\fac ri$ is an eigenvector of $\Sigi i$. Components escape in the order of $\tw r\Lambda^{\rm esc}_r$ along $\widetilde U_r$
and then realign to $\U r$ without an extra plateau (the TTN form of the early alignment of
\citealp{atanasov2022neural}), while the loss drops are $\frac12\tw r^2\Lambda^{\rm loss}_r$: misalignment alone can
reverse the order. It also opens routes of degree below $k^*$, with couplings of order $\epsilon^{n_{\rm out}}$ at
per-site misalignment $\epsilon$ ($n_{\rm out}$ sites outside the route).

\paragraph{Cross-site correlations.} In the one-factor model $x^{(i)}=\sqrt{1-c}\,z_i+\sqrt c\,z_0$, with
independent $z_0,\dots,z_\Ns\sim\Normal(0,\Id_d)$, every marginal is standard and two sites have covariance $c\Id$.
(i) \proven{} \newresult[the model is the standard one-factor Gaussian model; new: its tree
contraction] Given $z_0$ the sites are independent with second moments $M_i(z_0)=(1-c)\Id+cz_0z_0\T$, so
$\Loss=\E_{z_0}[\frac12\norm{\W-\Wt}^2_{\otimes_iM_i(z_0)}]$, a polynomial of degree $2\Ns$ in $z_0$, is exact by
Gauss--Hermite quadrature with $(\Ns+1)^d$ product-metric tree contractions; a site-aligned component has
the weight $\Lambda_{\rm eff}(c,\Ns)=\sum_k\binom{\Ns}{k}(1-c)^{\Ns-k}c^k(2k-1)!!$. (ii) \derived{} The first plateau survives
for every $c<1$ (the origin is a degree-$\Kc$ critical point for any input law; rank-constrained critical
points remain critical, \cref{lem:A-unused}). (iii) \proven{} (rates at the saddle) \newresult{} On an MPS
with even $\Ns$ and a site-aligned odeco teacher, the correlations open strict two-core routes at the
product saddle on every bond, with rate $\lambda_2\approx\tw1^{(\Ns-2)/\Ns}\tw2c^{\Ns-2}((\Ns-3)!!)^2$ at small $c$; whitened
power-law plateaus therefore become logarithmic once $c$ exceeds a crossover $c^*=O(\isc)$, up to logarithms
(\conjecture, heuristic, for $\Ns\ge6$; checked for $\Ns=4$ and on one example with $\Ns=6$). (iv) \derived{} On
MPS $\Ns=4$ this escape passes through the first plateau point of the binary tree (with junk pairing
components; strict on the MPS), and with spectral events the second plateau grows by
$\ln2\,[(1-\kappa_{\rm c})/\lambda_2+1/\lambda'']$ per halving of $\isc$, with $\lambda''$ the rate at that intermediate saddle and
$\kappa_{\rm c}\in(0,1)$ the head start of the first escape ($2.128$ predicted, $2.13$ measured, at $c=0.4$).

\subsection{Discrete steps and gradient noise}\label{app:optimizer}

Odeco teacher with $\tw1>\tw2>\dots$, whitened Gaussian inputs, $q_r=\tw r^{1/\Kc}$; gradient descent (GD) and
online SGD as in \cref{sec:setup-dynamics}, recorded at $t=\lr\times(\text{number of steps})$.

\begin{proposition}[Hessian at aligned global minima: the pattern rule]\label{prop:A-pattern-rule}
  \proven{} \knownresult[binary $\Ns=4$, a deep linear network: \citealp{ghosh2025learning}, Lemma~1;
  new: general trees]
  At a global minimum $\Hess=J\T J$, $J=\partial\W/\partial\params$. At an aligned minimum (\cref{prop:aligned}
  with $\prodamp r=\tw r$), an elementary perturbation of core $w$ changes $\W$ by a gain times a unit rank-one
  \emph{pattern}: the chosen vectors at the sites of $w$, the teacher vector $\ell_c$ at every site below a
  child $c$ whose bond index is $\ell_c$, and $\ell_p$ at every site outside the subtree of $w$. The gain is
  $\prod_{u\ne w}\amp u{\ell(u)}$, $\ell(u)$ the label of the branch of $w$ that contains $u$ (zero if a label
  exceeds $\ncomp$). Distinct patterns are orthonormal, so
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-pattern-rule}
    \mathrm{spec}(\Hess)\setminus\{0\}=\Big\{\lambda_P=\sum_{w\in\mathrm{prod}(P)}\mathrm{gain}_w(P)^2\ :\ P\text{ a pattern}\Big\},
  \end{equation}
  where $\mathrm{prod}(P)$ is the set of cores that can produce $P$. The radial mode of component $r$ (pattern
  $\U r$, produced by every core) has $\lambda=\tw r^2\sum_v\amp vr^{-2}\ge\Kc\tw r^{2-2/\Kc}$, with equality iff the
  component is balanced. At the balanced minimum $\lambda_P\le\abs{\mathrm{prod}(P)}\,q_1^{2(\Kc-1)}$, hence (nondegenerate if $\tw2<\tw1$)
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-sharpness}
    \lambda_{\max}(\Hess)=\Kc\,\tw1^{2-2/\Kc}.
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}
Consequences \derived{} \newresult: GD is linearly stable at the balanced minimum iff
$\lr<\lr_c:=2/(\Kc\tw1^{2-2/\Kc})$ (tested in \cref{sec:other-optim}). Imbalanced aligned minima are at least as
sharp (AM--GM; \proven; deep linear networks: \citealp{mulayoff2020unique}), and with $\bd e=\ncomp$ so is every
gauge-equivalent minimum (\derived; random $\GL$ gauges were at least $1.43$ times sharper): for $\lr>\lr_c$ no
global minimum is linearly stable (\derived{} for $\bd e=\ncomp$, \conjecture{} for $\bd e>\ncomp$). The minimum
reached by gradient flow, and its sharpness $\lambda_{\rm GF}$, are fixed by $\Q e(0)$ (a strongly imbalanced weaker
component can carry the top mode); for $2/\lambda_{\rm GF}<\lr<\lr_c$ GD oscillates, the imbalance contracts
(\cref{lem:A-gd-identity}), and GD converges to a flatter, more balanced minimum, with sharpness just below
$2/\lr$ on binary $\Ns=4$ (\empirical; \knownresult[scalar and deep linear networks:
\citealp{kreisler2023gradient,wang2022large,ghosh2025learning}]). Hessians checked to $6\cdot10^{-15}$ on five trees.

\paragraph{Sharpness along the saddle-to-saddle path.} \derived{} \newresult{} On the first plateau the Hessian
is indefinite and $O(\isc^{\Kc-2})$. During the event of a balanced component of amplitude $a$ its radial
eigenvalue $(2\Kc-1)a^{2\Kc-2}-(\Kc-1)\tw ra^{\Kc-2}$ rises to $\Kc\tw r^{2-2/\Kc}$ without overshoot, and the $\Kc-1$
transverse ones $(\tw r-a^\Kc)a^{\Kc-2}$ stay below $\Kc\tw r^{2-2/\Kc}$. At a later plateau that has learned the set
$S$ the radial mode of the largest learned component is an exact eigenvector: the sharpness is
$\Kc\max_{r\in S}\tw r^{2-2/\Kc}$, up to a relative excess $O(\isc^4)$ from the $O(\isc^2)$ imbalance. So at a fixed step
$\lr$ a plateau is linearly unstable iff it has learned a component with $\tw r>s_c(\lr)=(2/(\lr\Kc))^{\Kc/(2\Kc-2)}$,
and GD first becomes unstable at that component's event.

\paragraph{Edge of stability.} \derived{} \knownresult[the scalar map: \citealp{ghosh2025learning}; new: the
period-two amplitude] For $\lr_c<\lr<2/\lambda_2(\Hess)$ only the radial mode of component 1 is unstable, and GD
restricted to it (exactly for aligned initialization) is the balanced scalar map
$x\mapsto x+hx^{\Kc-1}(1-x^\Kc)$, $x=a/\tw1^{1/\Kc}$ ($a$ the per-core amplitude), $h=\lr\tw1^{2-2/\Kc}$, which doubles
its period at $h\Kc=2$, i.e.\ at $\lr=\lr_c$. To third order around $x=1$ the period-two orbit $1\pm\delta_{\rm p2}$
has $\delta_{\rm p2}^2\simeq3(h\Kc-2)/(4(\Kc-1)(5\Kc-4))$, so for $\lr_c<\lr<\min(2/\lambda_2,1.245\lr_c)$ the
time-averaged loss is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A-eos-loss}
  \Loss_{\rm EoS}\simeq\frac{3\Kc^2\tw1^2}{4(\Kc-1)(5\Kc-4)}\Big(\frac{\lr}{\lr_c}-1\Big).
\end{equation}
Later events still occur and lower the loss. Checked (binary $\Ns=4$, $\lr=1.05\lr_c$): late loss $1.45\cdot10^{-2}$
against $1.452\cdot10^{-2}$ (map) and $1.53\cdot10^{-2}$ \eqref{eq:A-eos-loss}; divergence at $1.5$--$1.65\lr_c$.

\begin{proposition}[Shift of event times under gradient descent]\label{prop:A-gd-shift}
  \derived{} \newresult[for TTNs; the modified flow is known: \citealp{barrett2021implicit}]
  To order $\lr$, GD follows the modified flow $\dot\params=-(\Id+\frac\lr2\Hess)\nabla\Loss$. Its component along the
  gradient-flow path reparametrizes time, and since $\ddt\ln\norm{\nabla\Loss}=-\nabla\Loss\T\Hess\nabla\Loss/\norm{\nabla\Loss}^2$ along
  gradient flow,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-gd-shift}
    t_{\rm GD}-t_{\rm GF}=\frac\lr2\ln\frac{\norm{\nabla\Loss(t_{\rm event})}}{\norm{\nabla\Loss(0)}}
    +(\text{transverse part})+O(\lr^2).
  \end{equation}
  For aligned initialization the path is exact and the event of component $r$ moves by $\frac\lr2\ln(f(a_1)/f(a_0))$,
  $f(a)=a^{\Kc-1}(\tw r-a^\Kc)$, $a_0$, $a_1$ the per-core amplitudes at initialization and at the event: plateau
  exponents and prefactors are those of gradient flow, and no saddle is skipped or reordered while the
  gradient-flow gaps exceed the differences of the shifts $+O(\lr^2)$.
\end{proposition}
For generic initialization $\norm{\nabla\Loss(0)}\propto\isc^{\Kc-1}$, so $\Delta\tev1/\lr\approx\frac{\Kc-1}2\ln(1/\isc)+{\rm const}$
asymptotically (\conjecture, heuristic). At a strict saddle the growth per step is $1+\lr\abs{\lambda_-}$ instead
of $e^{\lr\abs{\lambda_-}}$, so $t_{\rm GD}/t_{\rm GF}=\lr\abs{\lambda_-}/\ln(1+\lr\abs{\lambda_-})\in[1,1.82]$ for $\lr\abs{\lambda_-}<2$. Checked: aligned
shifts within $1.3\%$, generic slopes $0.69$--$0.70$ per halving of $\isc$ (predicted $0.69$).

\paragraph{SGD on the first plateau.} \derived{} \newresult[framework: stochastic differential equations
for SGD, \citealp{li2017stochastic,li2019stochastic}, and Type-II saddles, \citealp{ziyin2024typeii};
consistent with \citealp{corlouer2026stochastic}]
For online SGD with batch $\batch$ the per-sample gradient is $\ip{\W-\Wt}{\Feat(x)}J\T\Feat(x)$; near the origin
the gradient covariance is $O(\tw1^23^\Ns\norm{\params}^{2\Kc-2})/\batch$ and the drift $O(\tw1\norm{\params}^{\Kc-1})$, so in
the clock $\dd\tau=\norm{\params}^{\Kc-2}\dd t$ the noise vanishes: noise cannot drive the first escape. For one
aligned balanced component,
$\dd a=\tw1a^{\Kc-1}\dd t+\sqrt{\lr/\batch}\,\tw1\kappa_{\rm n}^{1/2}a^{\Kc-1}\dd\mathcal B_t$ ($\mathcal B_t$ a standard Brownian
motion, $\kappa_{\rm n}=3^\Ns-1+\sum_{r\ge2}\tw r^2/\tw1^2$), whose hitting time is inverse Gaussian at leading order:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A-sgd-first}
  \E\tev1^{\rm SGD}-\tev1^{\rm GF}\simeq\frac\lr2(\Kc-1)\Big(1+\frac{\kappa_{\rm n}}\batch\Big)\ln\frac{a_{1/2}}{a_0}>0,\qquad
  \mathrm{sd}\big(\tev1^{\rm SGD}\big)\simeq\Big(\kappa_{\rm n}\frac\lr\batch\tev1^{\rm GF}\Big)^{1/2},
\end{equation}
with $a_{1/2}=(\tw1/2)^{1/\Kc}$. Both corrections vanish relative to $\tev1^{\rm GF}\propto a_0^{-(\Kc-2)}$: the exponent is
unchanged. Checked: within 10--15\% for aligned, order of magnitude for generic initialization.

\paragraph{SGD at the minimum.} \derived{} (exact mean-square threshold) \proven{} (the inequality)
\newresult[the reduction; general criteria are known: \citealp{ziyin2024typeii}; exact mean square,
\citealp{mulayoff2024exact}; one-dimensional, \citealp{wu2018how}; top Lyapunov exponent,
\citealp{chemnitz2025characterizing}]
In the eigenbasis of \eqref{eq:A-pattern-rule} linearized SGD at the minimum acts on one coordinate per
pattern, with the products $Z_P(x)=\prod_i(t_{P,i}\cdot x^{(i)})$ of its site vectors; the second-moment
operator maps diagonal matrices to diagonal ones ($\E[Z_R^2Z_PZ_Q]=0$ for $P\ne Q$) and preserves the
positive semidefinite cone, which gives the exact mean-square threshold
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A-ms-threshold}
  \lr^*_{\rm ms}(\batch)=\frac{2}{\lambda_{\max}(G_\batch)},\qquad
  G_\batch=\Big(1-\frac1\batch\Big)\Lambda_\Hess+\frac1\batch\Lambda_\Hess^{1/2}\mathcal K\Lambda_\Hess^{1/2},\qquad
  \mathcal K_{PQ}=\prod_im(t_{P,i},t_{Q,i}),
\end{equation}
with $\Lambda_\Hess=\diag(\lambda_P)$, $m=3$ if the two site vectors coincide and $1$ if they are orthogonal (Gaussian
sites). Jensen's inequality gives $\lr_{\rm as}\ge\lr^*_{\rm ms}$ for the almost-sure divergence threshold; \empirical: the
ratios are about $2$ ($\Ns=4$, $\batch\le16$) and $8$--$21$ (binary $\Ns=8$), and SGD near the minimum diverges at $\lr_{\rm as}$.

\begin{proposition}[SGD lengthens later plateaus]\label{prop:A-sgd-collapse}
  \conjecture{} (heuristic law) \knownresult[mechanism: stochastic collapse, \citealp{chen2023stochastic}]
  At a later saddle with a route of degree $k\ge3$ the population Hessian vanishes on the route and the
  per-sample gradients vanish at zero route amplitude. Zero-mean multiplicative noise coupling the route
  entries to mixed learned entries gives the log-amplitude of the coherent route a drift $-\gamma_c$,
  $\gamma_c=c_\gamma\lr/\batch$; added to the route equation, $\dot b=\tw rb^{k-1}-\gamma_cb$. For $k=3$, with $T_{\rm GF}=1/(\tw rb_0)$,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-sgd-collapse}
    \frac{T_{\rm SGD}}{T_{\rm GF}}=\frac{-\ln(1-x)}{x},\qquad x=\gamma_cT_{\rm GF},
  \end{equation}
  and the route is trapped (decays) for $x\ge1$; for general $k$ escape needs roughly $(k-2)\gamma_cT_{\rm GF}<1$. As
  $b_0$ scales with the initialization, at fixed $\lr/\batch$ SGD stalls on a later degenerate plateau once $\isc$ is
  small enough; strict saddles escape if $\abs{\lambda_-}>\gamma_c$.
\end{proposition}
Solving $\dot w=-\tw r+\gamma_cw$ for $w=1/b$ gives \eqref{eq:A-sgd-collapse}. Checked on binary $\Ns=4$ only (weights
$(1,0.6)$, Gaussian sites; 21 runs): delays $1.02$--$2$ times $T_{\rm GF}$, trapping for $x\ge1$, never faster than
gradient flow, $c_\gamma\approx1.35$ \empirical{} ($1.08$ from a commuting-pair estimate; about 33 for weights
$(3,2,1)$, $d=\chi=3$: $c_\gamma$ depends on teacher and tree). Near the stability limit SGD breaks the saddle
sequence instead \empirical: with weights $(1,0.6)$ no learned component was erased in 13 runs at
$\lr\le0.25\lr^*_{\rm ms}$, but with $(3,2,1)$ one was erased (mostly relearned) in 20 of 36 longer runs at
$0.25$--$0.30\,\lr^*_{\rm ms}$ (\cref{sec:other-optim}); at $0.7$--$1.2\,\lr^*_{\rm ms}$ components were repeatedly erased and
relearned, or runs diverged.

\subsection{Regime boundaries}\label{app:boundaries}

Here $\pd[i]=d$, and $\TK=\sum_v\lrm vJ_vJ_v\T$ on $\R^{d^\Ns}$, $J_v=\partial\W/\partial\G v$, is the tensor-space
tangent kernel that drives the function-space dynamics of \eqref{eq:gf} (for whitened inputs the probe-set
kernel of \cref{tab:raw-diagnostics} is $\Feat(x_p)\T\TK\Feat(x_{p'})$). Recall $\mat{\W}{\edge v}=\Sub vC_v\T$
(\cref{sec:setup-model}); the initialization is \eqref{eq:init} with scales $\iscv v$, $\fsc=\prod_v\iscv v$. A bond
$e$ is \emph{full} if $\bd e$ reaches its cut dimension $\min(d^{n_e},d^{\Ns-n_e})$ ($n_e$ sites below $e$).

\begin{proposition}[Tangent kernel at initialization]\label{prop:A-kernel}
  \proven{} \newresult[exact at every bond dimension and on every tree; Gaussian-process and NTK limits of
  MPS and TT models: \citealp{guo2021neural,wesel2025tensor}]
  (a) $J_vJ_v\T=\bigotimes_{c\in\ch v}\Sub c\Sub c\T\otimes\Id_{d_v}\otimes C_vC_v\T$ (legs in site order).
  (b) For all bond dimensions, $\E\,\TK=\frac{\fsc^2}{d^\Ns}\sum_v\frac{\lrm v\fanin v}{\iscv v^2}\,\Id$ and
  $\E[\Wz\otimes\Wz]=\frac{\fsc^2}{d^\Ns}\Id$; with $\iscv v=\isc$ and $\lrm v=1$ the kernel scale is
  $\bar\lambda:=\E\tr\TK/d^\Ns=\isc^{2(\Kc-1)}\sum_v\fanin v/d^\Ns$ (core $v$ contributes the term $\fanin v$).
  (c) If the bond dimensions are consistent (each $\bd e$ at most the product of the other leg dimensions of
  both of its cores) and the initialization is generic, $\rank\TK=\sum_v\fanin v\bd v-\sum_e\bd e^2$ (capped at $d^\Ns$),
  the dimension of the manifold $\mathcal M_\chi$ of tensors with bond ranks at most $\bd e$ (for inconsistent bond
  dimensions, first reduce each $\bd e$ to its effective value); $\rank\TK=d^\Ns$ iff every bond is full.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
  (a) $\W$ is linear in $\G v$. (b) The cores are independent, $\Sub c$ and $C_v$ involve disjoint cores, and the
  initialization law is invariant under orthogonal maps of each physical leg (irreducible); the traces
  follow by induction from the leaves (\cref{prop:init-norm}). (c) $\rank\TK$ is the dimension of the tangent
  space of $\mathcal M_\chi$ at $\Wz$, parameters minus gauge \citep{uschmajew2013geometry}.
\end{proof}
Consequences \derived: with a bond that is not full, the linearized (lazy) dynamics fits only the
projection of the target on the range of $\TK$ and stops at $\frac12\normF{P_\perp\Wt}^2$, with
$\E\normF{P_\perp\Wt}^2/\normF{\Wt}^2=(1-\chi/d^2)^2$ for binary $\Ns=4$ (compare \citealp{chizat2019lazy}, Thm~2.5,
and \citealp{ghorbani2019limitations}). $\TK\approx\bar\lambda\Id$ needs every $\bd e$ far above its cut dimension; at
capped full bonds $\lambda_{\min}(\TK)/\bar\lambda\sim10^{-4}$ \empirical, so lazy runs need uncapped $\chi\ge2d^2$
(\cref{sec:other-lazy}); at practical $\chi$ the lazy regime follows a random, tree-dependent kernel.

\begin{proposition}[Lazy boundary of the centred model, $\Ns=4$]\label{prop:A-lazy}
  \derived{} \newresult[the TTN constants; the criterion is that of \citealp{chizat2019lazy}; a coherent
  second-order kernel change is known in wide networks: \citealp{bordelon2022selfconsistent}]
  Binary tree with $\Ns=4$, standard parameterization, centred model; let $\xi=\normF{\Wt}/\fsc$, assume
  $\chi\gg d^2$ and keep the leading order in $d^2/\chi$. Then
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-lazy-dk}
    \dTK^2\approx(c_1\xi)^2+2\zeta c_1c_2\xi^3+(c_2\xi^2)^2,\qquad
    c_1=\frac{4d^3\sqrt{d^2+1}}{\chi^3},\quad c_2=\frac{2d^4\sqrt{2d^2\nu+2}}{\chi^3},
  \end{equation}
  with $\nu=\sum_r\tw r^4/(\sum_r\tw r^2)^2$ and a seed-dependent correlation $\zeta\in[-1,1]$: a zero-mean first-order
  term, linear in $\Wz$, and a coherent second-order term $\propto\mat{(\Wt)}{e}(\mat{(\Wt)}{e})\T$ (the bottom cores drift
  toward the singular directions of the target). The boundary $\dTK=\delta_*$ lies in $[\xi_c^+,\xi_c^-]$,
  $\xi_c^\pm=(\mp c_1+\sqrt{c_1^2+4c_2\delta_*})/(2c_2)$, and for large $\chi$ ($c_1^2\ll c_2\delta_*$)
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-sigma-lazy}
    \isc_{\rm lazy}=\Big(\frac{\normF{\Wt}^2c_2}{\delta_*}\Big)^{1/6}\propto\chi^{-1/2}
  \end{equation}
  ($6.50\,\chi^{-1/2}$ for $d=3$, weights $(3,2,1)$, $\delta_*=0.1$), not $\chi^{-1}$ as the first-order term alone
  would give. In the two-copy model the first-order term cancels, $\dTK\approx\frac14c_2\xi^2$, and
  $\isc^{(2)}_{\rm lazy}=2^{-1/3}\isc_{\rm lazy}$.
\end{proposition}
Checked \empirical: predicted $\xi_c\in[2.17,2.81]$, $[6.67,7.31]$ at $\chi=32$, $64$ against $2.25$--$2.82$, $6.73$--$7.53$ in
gradient flow. The boundary moves with the threshold as $\isc_{\rm lazy}\propto\delta_*^{-1/(\Kc s)}$, $s=\dd\ln\dTK/\dd\ln\xi\in[1,2]$
($1.5$--$1.8$ measured); the root can move by more than $100\%$ while $\dTK<0.1$.

\paragraph{Deficient bonds.} \derived{} \newresult{} If some bond is not full, $\Wt+\Wz$ generically has more
bond rank than the student can carry: the centred model is unrealizable at every $\isc$. At small $\isc$
(first order in $\Wz$ around a smooth point $\Wt$ of $\mathcal M_\chi$, i.e.\ $\bd e=\rank\mat{(\Wt)}{e}$, with $\isc^\Kc$ far
below the smallest teacher Schmidt value) the best reachable point is $\Wt+P_T\Wz+O(\normF{\Wz}^2)$ ($P_T$,
$P_\perp$: tangent and normal projectors of $\mathcal M_\chi$ at $\Wt$), so the loss floors at $\frac12\normF{P_\perp\Wz}^2$,
with mean, by \cref{prop:A-kernel}(b),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A-centred-floor}
  \frac{\E\Loss_{\rm end}}{\Loss(0)}\approx\Big(1-\frac{\dim\mathcal M_\chi}{d^\Ns}\Big)\frac{\isc^{2\Kc}}{\normF{\Wt}^2}.
\end{equation}
A typical run meets the convergence rule $\Loss<10^{-3}\Loss(0)$ only below
$\isc_{\rm floor}=[10^{-3}\normF{\Wt}^2/(1-\dim\mathcal M_\chi/d^\Ns)]^{1/(2\Kc)}$ ($0.56$ for binary $\Ns=4$, $0.74$ for $\Ns=8$;
$d=\chi=3$, weights $(3,2,1)$); runs above it are stalled (unrealizable). At large $\isc$ the floor tends to
$\normF{P_\perp\Wt}^2/\normF{\Wt}^2$ ($P_\perp$ at $\Wz$). In the two-copy model (realizable iff $2\bd e\ge\rank\mat{(\Wt)}{e}$) a
deficient bond gives, at large $\isc$, a lazy plateau at that level and then a saddle-free escape by rotation
of the bond subspaces: adiabatic elimination gives $t_{\rm esc}=2\vartheta\isc^2/\normF{\Wt}^2$, $\vartheta=0.37$--$0.70$, a kernel
time $\propto\isc^{2\Kc}$ (explicit for $\Ns=4$, heuristic on other trees; compare \citealp{tu2024mixed,lyu2024dichotomy}).

\begin{proposition}[Saddle-to-saddle boundary for aligned initialization]\label{prop:A-s2s-aligned}
  \proven{} (given the exact aligned reduction) \knownresult[mode equation: \citealp{saxe2014exact}; threshold
  exponent $\Kc/(\Kc-2)$: \citealp{gissin2020implicit}, Thm~2; new: the constants for the criterion of
  \cref{sec:setup-diagnostics} and the exact $\Kc\to\infty$ limit]
  Let every component of an odeco teacher start at product amplitude $u_0=q_0^\Kc$, let
  $I_\Kc(v_0,v_1)=\frac1\Kc\int_{v_0}^{v_1}v^{-2+2/\Kc}(1-v)^{-1}\dd v$ and $\omega_\Kc=I_\Kc(0.1,0.9)$. Then
  $\tev r=\tw r^{-(2-2/\Kc)}I_\Kc(u_0/\tw r,\frac12)$ and $\wev r=\tw r^{-(2-2/\Kc)}\omega_\Kc$, so, with $\varrho_i=\tw{i+1}/\tw i$,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-s2s-ratio}
    \sep i=\frac{I_\Kc(u_0/\tw{i+1},\frac12)-\varrho_i^{\,2-2/\Kc}I_\Kc(u_0/\tw i,\frac12)}{\omega_\Kc}
    \simeq\Big(\frac{u_0}{\tw{i+1}}\Big)^{-(1-2/\Kc)}\frac{1-\varrho_i}{(\Kc-2)\,\omega_\Kc}\quad(u_0\to0),
  \end{equation}
  and the boundary $\sep i=3$ lies at $u_{0c}\simeq\tw{i+1}[(1-\varrho_i)/(3(\Kc-2)\omega_\Kc)]^{\Kc/(\Kc-2)}$. As $\Kc\to\infty$,
  $\Kc I_\Kc(v_0,v_1)\to\frac1{v_0}-\frac1{v_1}+\ln\frac{v_1}{v_0}+\ln\frac{1-v_0}{1-v_1}$ and $\Kc\omega_\Kc\to\frac{80}9+2\ln9=13.283$:
  $u_{0c}$ stays finite ($1.306\cdot10^{-2}$ for weights $(3,2,1)$), and $a_{0c}=u_{0c}^{1/\Kc}=1+\ln u_{0c}/\Kc+O(\Kc^{-2})\to1$.
\end{proposition}
Values: $\omega_3=2.009$, $\omega_5=1.612$, $\omega_7=1.318$; for weights $(3,2,1)$, $a_{0c}=0.066$, $0.324$, $0.480$
at $\Kc=3,5,7$. Aligned gradient flow reproduces \eqref{eq:A-s2s-ratio} to four decimals at $\Kc=3$ and 7.

\paragraph{Random initialization.} \derived{} ($\tev1$) \conjecture{} (later gaps; large $\Kc$) \empirical{}
(boundary) By \cref{cor:A-blowup,prop:A-first-event}, $\tev1=\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}\hat t_1(Z)/\tw1+O(1)$. For teachers whose
escape routes span the whole tree (odeco), later gaps scale the same way if the dormant amplitudes
entering each plateau stay $\propto\isc$ (exact for aligned initialization), so $\sep i\approx R_i\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}$ with
seed-dependent $R_i$; in general a later gap scales as $\isc^{-(k-2)}$, $k$ the route degree (\cref{app:saddles}).
The boundary is a seed distribution: median per-seed $\isc_c=0.27$--$0.31$ for binary $\Ns=4$, $d=\chi=3$, weights
$(3,2,1)$, and $0.71$ for $\Ns=8$, $d=2$, $\chi=16$, weights $(3,2)$; as aligned initialization with per-core
amplitude $\kappa_\Kc\isc$, $\kappa_3\approx0.22$ ($d=3$) and $\kappa_7\approx0.69$ ($d=2$).
\conjecture: $\kappa_\Kc\to1$, so $\isc_c(\Kc)\to a_{0c}(\Kc)\to1$; the data (all at $d=2$ for large $\Kc$) do not distinguish
this from $\kappa_\Kc\to d^{-1/2}$, i.e.\ $\isc_c\to\sqrt d$ (\cref{sec:regime-K}). For matrix-like pairs the transient
overlap of a component not yet learned peaks at a value $\propto\isc^{2(1-\varrho)}$ (\conjecture, heuristic), hence
events are timed by their final rise. With $\chi$ equal to the teacher rank $\ncomp$ and $\Kc\ge5$, the last
component needs a route through all $\Kc$ cores, which the child-heavy root bonds of fan-in
initialization (\cref{app:conservation}) can kill before it escapes (\conjecture, heuristic). \empirical: it
was not learned in 2 of 4 runs at $\Kc=5$ and 2 of 2 at $\Kc=7$ ($d=3$); balanced initialization removed these
stalls at $\Kc=5$ (leaving long plateaus) but not at $\Kc=7$; spare channels ($\chi\ge2\ncomp$) avoided stalls in
the runs made (one at $d=3$; 26 at $d=2$).

\paragraph{Large bond dimension.} \derived{} \newresult[the spectral condition is
\citealp{yang2024spectral}; factor-wise rules for structured layers:
\citealp{qiu2024compute,potapczynski2024searching}] The input of a core is always a product of vectors, so
its signals are controlled by its injective norm, not by the spectral norm of its matricization. The
spectral condition with the injective norm keeps the fan-in initialization of every non-root core,
multiplies the root by $(\sum_\ell m_\ell)^{-1/2}$ ($m_\ell$: its child-leg dimensions) and sets $\lrm v\propto\bd v/\fanin v$
(``$\mu$P-TTN''). At fixed $\xi$ ($\Ns=4$) this gives $\dTK\propto\chi^0$, against $\chi^{-3}$ (standard) and $\chi^{-1}$ (NTK
parameterization; compare \citealp{guo2021neural}); the factor-wise rule makes the initial kernel of binary
$\Ns=8$ vanish like $\chi^{-2}$ and slows the initial fit about linearly in $\chi$ \empirical.

\paragraph{Imbalance.} \derived{} (aligned mode) \newresult[compare two-layer linear networks:
\citealp{kunin2024get}] With the root at scale $\lambda_{\rm root}\isc$ and the other cores at $\isc$ (fixed $\fsc$), the
root bonds have $\E\Q e=\isc^2(1-\lambda_{\rm root}^2/\bd e)\Id$, balanced at $\lambda_{\rm root}=\sqrt{\bd e}$ (fan-in initialization
is root-light). A small root ($\lambda_{\rm root}\ll1$) makes training lazy when $s\ll\fsc/\lambda_{\rm root}$ ($s$ a teacher
weight), with $\dTK\propto\lambda_{\rm root}^2$, but only with full bonds (with a deficient bond: lazy, then rich). A
large root freezes, leaving a degree-$(\Kc-1)$ model, which does not make training lazy for $\Kc\ge3$
(unlike the two-layer case). Tests: \cref{sec:regime-sigma-chi,sec:regime-K,sec:regime-imbalance,sec:other-large-chi}.
