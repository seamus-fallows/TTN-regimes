\subsection{Covariance and teacher structure: supplementary results}\label{app:supp-cov}

Material moved from \cref{sec:covariance-teacher}: per-teacher values and secondary checks. Settings are
in \cref{app:exp-data-teacher,app:exp-real-data}; notation as in \cref{sec:covariance-teacher}.

\paragraph{Aligned covariance (\cref{sec:cov-aligned}).}
\empirical{} $\Ns=4$, $d=\chi=4$, $\beta=0.25$, so the high-variance eigenvector $e_1$ has twice the $\Lambda$ of
$e_2$. Teachers T1 $(1,0.8)$ and T2 $(1,0.6)$ put the smaller component on $e_1$. Forty seeds, binary4 at
$\isc=0.02$--$0.1$, MPS4 at $0.05$--$0.2$, four variants from the same initialization: $(\Sig,\tw{})$, $(\Id,\Lambda\tw{})$,
$(\Id,\Lambda^{1/2}\tw{})$, and the last from the mapped initialization (same landscape and starting point as
$(\Sig,\tw{})$, whitened flow).
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Clocks.} From aligned initialization half-times match the clock $\Lambda_rt$ to $10^{-9}$; the
    naive substitution $\tw r\to\Lambda_r\tw r$ misses the widths by up to $47\%$.
  \item \emph{Race.} Paired $(\Sig,\tw{})$ and $(\Id,\Lambda\tw{})$ runs learn the same component first in all
    MPS4 and $95$--$100\%$ of the binary4 pairs, with first-escape times within $1.3\%$. The covariance
    reverses the order: in T1 the $0.8$ component comes first in $90$--$98\%$ (binary4) and $78\%$ (MPS4) of
    the runs. The mapped-initialization control picks a different first component in $10$--$15\%$ (MPS4)
    and $12$--$53\%$ (binary4) of the seeds and escapes $11$--$23\%$ later.
  \item \emph{Exponents.} $\tev1\propto\isc^{-1.010\pm0.002}$ (binary4) and $\isc^{-1.999\pm0.001}$ (MPS4);
    $\tev1\propto(\Lambda\tw{})^{-1}$ with per-seed exponents $-1.000$ to $-1.003$ within each eigenvector direction
    ($\Lambda=0.008$--$33$, ten seeds).
  \item \emph{Landscape.} On binary4 no swap follows a landscape-correct first component in any of the 24
    settings, while in T2 the probability of a swap after the $0.6$ component came first is $0.33$, $0.68$,
    $0.94$ at $\isc=0.1$, $0.05$, $0.02$. The Hessian at the saddle equals the formula to $10^{-15}$ in the 6
    strict binary4 cases; MPS4 has no negative eigenvalue (12 of 12) and no swap in 960 runs. Plateau
    levels are $\frac12\sum_{r\notin S}\Lambda_r\tw r^2$ (median relative error $10^{-8}$--$10^{-3}$; binary4 runs of T2 on
    their way to a swap sit $1$--$3\%$ from it).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Generic teachers (\cref{sec:teacher-generic}).}
\Cref{tab:dt-saddle} lists the thirteen teachers and the escape from the exact saddle. Where the realized
edge is not the fastest (two random teachers with $\Ns=8$), the slope follows the realized edge, not
$1/\abs{\lambda_{\min}}$. Changing the onset
threshold from $3\%$ to $10\%$ of the teacher's singular value moves the MPS slopes by at most $0.4\%$; fits
over all $\varepsilon$, not only the five smallest, deviate by up to $11\%$ (pre-asymptotic points). The misaligned
teacher with $\Ns=8$ at $20^\circ$, timed at its intermediate level, gives exponents $1.07$ and $1.05$ (binary,
HT) over $1.3$ decades of $\varepsilon$, and $1.03$ and $1.02$ over its three smallest $\varepsilon$.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Random initialization, MPS4.} Slopes over the three smallest $\isc$ against $h_e$: $33.95\pm0.18$
    ($34.14$), $5.674\pm0.059$ ($5.682$), $12.005\pm0.071$ ($12.014$), $1.038\pm0.005$ ($1.051$; $2.78$ without the
    head start), $1.401\pm0.013$ ($1.386$). The misaligned teacher, which has no prediction under the
    preconditioned flow, gives $52.46\pm0.10$, below the bound $1/\abs{\lambda_{\min}}=52.94$. The curvature at the
    plateau is $-0.1510$ against $-0.1509$ at the exact saddle (overlap $0.45$). The first escape keeps its
    $\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}$ law: per-seed exponents $1.998$--$2.014$ (MPS4) and $1.002$--$1.017$ (binary4) over all nine
    $\isc$, $2.0000$ and $1.0003$ over the three smallest.
  \item \emph{A failure mode of onset events} \empirical{} \newresult[methodological]. The misaligned teacher
    with $\Ns=8$ at $20^\circ$ escapes at the predicted rate to an intermediate saddle with second singular values
    $3.7$--$3.9\cdot10^{-4}$ on every bond, nine times below the onset threshold, so the threshold rule times the
    next escape (a slope 24 times the prediction). Timed at the intermediate level, the slope is
    $1979.6\pm1.8$ against $1/\lambda_e=1974.8$. A threshold relative to the teacher's spectrum misses escapes that
    end below it.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Non-orthogonal components (\cref{sec:cov-misaligned}).}
\empirical{} Single-$\isc$ runs: $\isc=0.05$ on binary4 and $0.1$ on MPS4, 20 seeds.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Overlapping CP components} ($d=\chi=4$, weights $(1,\tw2)$, $\tw2\in\{0.7,1\}$, overlaps $0$--$0.8$; an MPS4
    sweep with $\tw2=0.7$, overlaps $0$, $0.05$, $0.2$, $0.45$, nine $\isc$ from $3\cdot10^{-4}$ to $0.3$, ten seeds).
    MPS4 has two rank-one minima for overlaps up to $0.45$ and one from $0.55$ ($\tw2=1$), two up to $0.35$ and one
    from $0.45$ ($\tw2=0.7$); binary4 one. Median relative level errors of the first plateaus: $5\cdot10^{-5}$--$2\cdot10^{-3}$
    on binary4, up to $3\%$ on barely resolved MPS4 plateaus; some binary4 runs with $\tw2=1$ sit at the second
    singular pair, a strict saddle.
  \item \emph{Misaligned covariance} ($d=\chi=4$, weights $(1,0.8)$, $\beta\in\{0.5,1\}$, angles $0$--$45^\circ$; an MPS4 sweep
    with $\beta=1$, angles $0$, $5^\circ$, $20^\circ$, nine $\isc$ from $3\cdot10^{-4}$ to $0.3$, ten seeds). For angles up to $30^\circ$
    the median $\abs{\cos}$ between the first plateau and the critical tensor is at least $0.998$, over the resolved
    plateaus ($20$--$50\%$ of the MPS4 runs per angle have none at $\isc=0.1$). At $45^\circ$, $65\%$ ($\beta=0.5$) and
    $90\%$ ($\beta=1$) of the MPS4 runs have no resolved plateau; for the two resolved ones at $\beta=1$ the medians
    are a level error of $5.8\%$ and $\abs{\cos}=0.53$. Binary4 resolves every plateau at every angle.
  \item \emph{Correlated sites.} On binary4 ($d=\chi=4$, weights $(1,0.7)$) the first plateau is at $0.33$, $0.29$,
    $0.20$, $0.14$, $0.10\,\Loss(0)$ for $c=0$, $0.2$, $0.3$, $0.4$, $0.5$ (median relative error $\le5\cdot10^{-4}$). The
    $\isc$ sweep ($d=3$, weights $(1,0.6)$, six $\isc$ from $0.0016$ to $0.05$, five seeds) dates events on the
    singular values across $01|23$; seed 2 of MPS4 sits on the minor saddle at every $c$ and is excluded.
    Online SGD (binary4, $c=0.3$, $\isc=0.05$, three seeds) crosses the same loss levels within $2\%$ of gradient
    flow at batch size $4096$ and within $7\%$ at batch size $256$ (event times on a grid of $0.2$).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Random TTN teachers (\cref{sec:teacher-ttn}).}
\empirical{} Per teacher, the distance of the rank-one plateau to the bond-rank-one critical point falls as
$\isc^{0.72}$, $\isc^{0.83}$, $\isc^{0.41}$ (one seed), $\isc^{0.93}$, $\isc^{0.80}$ ($\pm0.01$--$0.03$), against the heuristic exponents
$0.64$, $0.68$, $0.34$, $0.80$, $0.72$. For the three separated teachers, at $\isc=10^{-3}$ the plateau lies
$10^{-4}$--$2.5\cdot10^{-3}$ from the critical point and $1.5\cdot10^{-3}$--$0.028$ from the nearest SVD-type truncation
(relative to $\normF{\Wt}$); the SVD-type truncations compared are the hierarchical SVD and the TT-SVD in both
directions. Example of a single-route slope: $5.188\pm0.008$ against $h_{e^*}=5.175$.

\paragraph{Teacher and student on different trees (\cref{sec:teacher-mismatch}).}
\empirical{} An MPS8 student of binary8 teachers at $\isc=0.45$ grows the predicted bond first in 2 of 24
runs with onset events at $3\%$ or $5\%$ of the teacher's singular value (1 of 24 at $10\%$; a rule on the
bond spectra gives 6 of 24); with $\ln(1/\isc)=0.8$ this is no test of the asymptotic race of
\cref{prop:A-agf-race}.

\paragraph{Star teachers (\cref{sec:teacher-star}).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Hessian.} At $\tw r=1.6$, $\lambda_{\min}=-0.3716$, $-0.3310$, $-0.4311$, $-0.4311$ on MPS4, binary4, HT4
    and the binary8 analogue, as the formula gives.
  \item \emph{Near the threshold} (MPS4, $\tw r=1.42$, $1.44$) the blended rank-one point keeps $\ov q=0.98$, $0.93$;
    with $\lambda_b$ its rate ($-\lambda_{\min}$ there), the measured slopes $130.0\pm0.2$ and $29.39\pm0.02$ equal
    $1/\lambda_q+1/\lambda_b=129.9$ and $29.3$ (one stage would give $86.4$ and $19.4$). On HT4 leaving the blended point takes a time $\propto\varepsilon^{-1}$ (fitted exponents
    $0.995$, $0.998$); at $\tw r=1.6$, $\ov q$ falls below $\frac12$ after $3.4\cdot10^3$, $3.3\cdot10^4$ and $3.3\cdot10^6$ time
    units for $\varepsilon=10^{-3}$, $10^{-4}$, $10^{-6}$.
  \item \emph{Random initialization} ($\tw r=1.6$; $\isc=0.01$--$0.1$ on MPS4, $0.005$--$0.05$ on binary4, ten seeds).
    The flow spends at most $0.13$ time units at the loss level of $\params_q$ (one seed $1.7$), and up to 538 at
    ``$q$ forgotten''. Classes, set by the seed: $q$ learned last (binary4 7 of 10 seeds, MPS4 1 of 10); $\ov q$
    rising to $0.78$--$1.00$ (binary4 3, MPS4 4 of 10), then decaying except on MPS4 for seed 6 at $\isc\ge0.025$ and
    seeds 3, 5 and 8 at $\isc=0.1$, which keep $q$ ($\min\ov q>0.5$); a blended start with $\ov q\le0.68$ (MPS4, 5 of
    10). Sub-critical stars ($\tw r=1.2$): the smallest $\min\ov q$ over all runs is $0.9918$ (MPS4, $\isc=0.1$).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Real data (\cref{sec:real-data}).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Route couplings.} The largest sibling coupling rises first in 22 of the 26 critical-point
    units with our grouping of states, and in 19--20 of 23--24 with other groupings; 10 of the 11 independent
    runs use new orders or seeds.
  \item \emph{Robustness of the $k=3$ gap law.} The verdicts of \cref{fig:real-gaps} also hold on the full sweep of
    124 binary runs, each point attributed to the drop that contains the route's rank crossing (91 points
    kept, 33 excluded with their reason; ten seed series). The one whitened series that meets the selection rule has $t_0=+10.8$, so $t_0<0$ holds for
    raw features only.
  \item \emph{Tree shape on the two clocks} \empirical. On the clock $\tau$ started at the first escape the
    snake MPS is fastest (MNIST, $\isc=0.1$: $\Loss\le0.2$ after 56 time units, the quadtree after 333). On the
    absolute clock the first escape dominates ($t_a\ge10^5$), $t_a\propto\isc^{-14}$ on MPS16 against $\isc^{-13}$ on
    binary16 \proven, and the MPS escapes later than the quadtree in 4 of 5 seeds at $\isc=0.1$; on
    Fashion-MNIST it is the slowest tree to $\Loss\le0.25$ at $\isc\le0.14$ but the first to reach $\Loss\le0.12$.
    Only snake chains were swept (three chains in other orders, one seed, are independent runs), so tree
    shape and pixel order are confounded; that the logarithmic routes
    make the MPS fast on the $\tau$ clock is a \conjecture.
  \item \emph{Other task and bond dimension} \empirical. A sibling pair rises first at $\chi=8$ (14 of 15 runs
    per binary order) and on Fashion-MNIST (sneaker against ankle boot; first two rising edges siblings in
    15 and 14 of 15 runs, rising together in only 3 and 2). There a strict saddle
    ($\lambda_{\min}\propto\isc^{0.03\pm0.09}$, error of the pooled fit) separates two rank-one states, and the
    quadtree has no advantage.
  \item \emph{Large initialization} \empirical. The centred sweep ($\isc=0.5$--$4$, quadtree) gives no regime
    map: a rich label would rest on $\isc=1.4$ alone, the boundary of the saddle-to-saddle label moves over
    $\isc=0.07$--$1$ with the separation threshold, and the lazy runs are unconverged at their horizon; the
    small-initialization runs end below the floor of the linearized model for four of five seeds. From
    $\isc=2.8$ on the runs follow the linearized model to $5\cdot10^{-5}$ (at $\isc=2$ up to $1.5\%$, one run labelled
    rich); its tangent kernel has rank $304=\sum_v\fanin v\bd v-\sum_e\bd e^2$ of 528 parameters
    (\cref{prop:A-kernel}(c)).
  \item \emph{Procedural points of the order test.} The run configuration and the analysis script were
    committed four minutes after the runs started (before any outcome), and an interim summary of 72 of the
    81 runs (all 60 random-order runs) was written, and probably analysed, before the last runs finished.
    The registration (hypotheses, orders, outcomes, analysis) and the predictor values of all 27 orders
    were committed before the runs.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Provenance and corrections after review.}
Both experiments were checked by independent verifications that recomputed the numbers from the committed
outputs (Hessians at 12 stored saddles with independent code; the saddle-start escape times rerun on
another platform). The two-stage law of the correlated-input escape and the star Hessian formula were
checked numerically when they were derived, in the same settings, so their experiments are replications
with more seeds rather than independent tests (on binary4 the star formula is the known deep-linear-network
result). After review, the early plateau durations at a single $\isc$ were replaced by $\isc$ sweeps; claims
about later plateaus were narrowed to the escape from the rank-one saddle, the only one timed; the
binary/HT contrast was limited to the settings run (no correlated inputs on HT, no random initialization
on HT); a fit over all $\varepsilon$ that suggested a slow crossover was traced to pre-asymptotic points; a
teacher first reported as degenerate turned out to be the event-rule failure above; the bottom-up count
was corrected after de-duplication of runs; and power laws for the forgetting depth and the relearning
time of stars from random initialization were withdrawn (they pooled two classes of runs and describe runs
that never visit $\params_q$). For the real data, an early first-route exponent came from an attribution bug;
counts per run across $\isc$ were replaced by counts per (order, seed) or critical-point unit; statements
about the tree shape on every clock, about locality as a small-initialization effect and about later
routes of higher degree, and the exponents of the Fashion-MNIST and $\chi=8$ sweeps, which are in no
committed output, were withdrawn (the $\chi=8$ comparison at $\isc=0.5$ is also confounded, since all five
quadtree runs there stopped at the wall-clock limit; 23 of the 45 runs did); and the two procedural points
of the order test were found by the
verification, not listed by the experiment. The order test was registered in the commit 2f4d99e, the
predictor values in 44feb2f, and the results are in 01144a5.
