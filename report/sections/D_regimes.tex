\subsection{Map of regimes and the lazy and rich regimes}\label{app:D-regimes}

Values behind \cref{sec:regime-map} and \cref{sec:other-lazy,sec:other-rich,sec:other-large-chi}; settings in
\cref{app:exp-regimes}. Boundaries are geometric means $\times/$ their geometric standard error over six seeds,
or medians with quartiles or brackets.

\paragraph{Kernels and boundaries.}
The binary4 maps ($\isc\times\chi$, $\chi$ sweep, imbalance) are labelled with the tensor kernel; the $\Kc$ sweeps,
the maps of the binary tree with $\Ns=8$ and the regime labels of the large-$\chi$ sweep with the probe kernel (its
exponents, \cref{tab:large-chi}, use the tensor kernel). Near the lazy boundary
$\dTK^{\rm probe}$ is $0.54$--$1.11$ times $\dTK$ (median $0.78$; 43 runs at $\chi\ge32$) \empirical, so relabelling the
binary4 maps from the probe to the tensor kernel changed 65 of their 5,298 labels ($1.2\%$): 59 from lazy to
non-lazy at the lazy boundary and 6 plain runs to lazy. It moved the per-seed lazy boundaries up by $1$--$9\%$ in
$\isc$ (centred: $4$--$9\%$; $14$--$15\%$ at $\chi=6$--$9$); the saddle-to-saddle boundaries do not depend on the
kernel. Per seed, the saddle-to-saddle boundary is the
crossing of $\min_i\sep i=3$, or a bracket from the last saddle-to-saddle run to the next run when that run is
stalled; the lazy boundary is the crossing of $0.1$ by the kernel change among converged runs.

\paragraph{Saddle-to-saddle boundary on binary4.}
\empirical{} $0.237\times/1.32$ at $\chi=3$, $0.099\times/1.51$ at $\chi=4$, $0.137\times/1.19$ at $\chi=6$,
$0.136\times/1.11$ at $\chi=9$, $0.075\times/1.33$ at $\chi=12$, $0.072\times/1.07$ at $\chi=18$, $0.067\times/1.12$
at $\chi=24$ and $0.043\times/1.05$ at $\chi=36$ ($\isc\times\chi$ sweep), falling to $0.023$ at $\chi=128$ ($\chi$
sweep). At $\chi=3$ it lies within the spread of 30 plain-model seeds (median $0.27$--$0.31$,
\cref{app:boundaries}). Per seed, $\min_i\sep i$ falls like a power of $\isc$ with fitted exponents $1.015$--$1.048$
in 96 of the 101 fits (all $\chi$ of both sweeps), against $\Kc-2=1$; the five exceptions ($1.09$--$1.27$) are seeds
with transients above $\frac12$. Deep in the saddle-to-saddle regime ($\chi=36$, $\isc=0.01$) the widths of the final
rises are $0.464$--$0.468$, $0.798$--$0.801$ and $2.010$ against the aligned-mode values $0.464$, $0.797$ and
$2.009$ of \cref{prop:A-s2s-aligned} for $\tw r=3$, $2$, $1$. Extrapolating $\sep{}\propto1/\isc$ from each seed's
deepest run gives $0.86$--$1.23$ times the measured boundary for 53 of 54 seeds: a consistency check of the
$\isc^{-1}$ law, not a prediction of the boundary.

\paragraph{Lazy boundary on binary4.}
\empirical{} With the tensor kernel (medians of six seeds) the lazy boundary is $1.13$, $0.94$, $0.82$, $0.66$
and $0.571$ at $\chi=32$, $48$, $64$, $96$, $128$, inside the predicted brackets $[1.10,1.20]$, $[0.92,0.96]$,
$[0.80,0.82]$, $[0.66,0.67]$, and $0.07\%$ below the lower edge of $[0.571,0.577]$. At $\chi=9$, $12$, $16$, $24$,
$\isc_{\rm lazy}\sqrt\chi=8.96$, $7.99$, $7.43$, $6.75$ ($6.3\%$, $2.9\%$, $1.5\%$ above the bracket at $\chi=9$, $12$,
$16$).

\paragraph{Slopes against $\chi$.}
\empirical{} Saddle-to-saddle boundary: $-0.528\pm0.053$ over $\chi=16$--$128$ (42 values, $0.9$ decade; per-seed
fits $-0.528\pm0.073$) and $-0.552\pm0.049$ over $\chi=9$--$128$. Lazy boundary: $-0.504\pm0.011$ over
$\chi=32$--$128$ and $-0.553\pm0.013$ over $\chi=16$--$128$, with $\chi=16$, $24$ not yet asymptotic. The slope
$-\frac13$ of $\isc^3\chi/d^2$ lies $2.7$ and $2.9$ standard errors from the saddle-to-saddle slope in per-seed
fits over $\chi\ge16$ and $\chi\ge32$ ($3.7$ in the pooled fit over $\chi\ge16$, which treats seeds sharing a
teacher across $\chi$ as independent). The rich window $\isc_{\rm lazy}/\isc_c$ at $\chi\ge32$ spans $19.5$--$24.2$
with the tensor kernel and $18.4$--$22.8$ with the probe kernel; the per-bond Gram scale $\chi\isc^2/d^2$ is
$0.010$ at $\isc\sqrt\chi=0.30$ and $4.69$ at $\isc\sqrt\chi=6.50$.

\paragraph{Number of cores.}
\empirical{} Centred model, $d=2$, $\chi=4$: medians (quartiles) of the per-seed saddle-to-saddle boundaries are
$0.17$ ($0.11$--$0.20$) on binary4, $0.49$ ($0.32$--$0.58$) on MPS4, $0.61$ ($0.48$--$0.80$) on MPS5, $0.68$
($0.68$--$0.74$; four seeds) on MPS6 and $0.75$ ($0.61$--$0.93$) on binary8. Read as aligned initialization with
per-core amplitude $a_{0c}$, the ratio $a_{0c}/\isc_c$ grows from $0.39$ ($\Kc=3$) to $0.65$ ($\Kc=7$), as in the runs of
\cref{app:boundaries}. At $\Kc=8$, $10$, $12$, $15$ only 1, 1, 0, 0 of six seeds are resolved; the
brackets have medians $[0.88,1.16]$, $[0.80,1.00]$, $[0.83,1.03]$, $[0.63,1.36]$. Classified by $\min_i\sep i$ instead
of the label, the run after the last saddle-to-saddle run is saddle-to-saddle-like ($\sep{}=3.9$--$642$) for 13 of
the 18 unresolved MPS seeds. At $\Kc=8$ the block then ends at a crossing in 5 of 6 seeds (median $1.01$); at
$\Kc=10$ and $12$ such runs persist up to $\isc\approx1.0$--$1.3$ and the next runs learn nothing within the budget,
so a crossing to rich is seen in 2 of 6 seeds at $\Kc=10$ (both at $0.90$) and in none at $\Kc=12$. All these runs
end on the floor ($\Loss/\Loss(0)=0.001$--$0.074$), and 9 of the 13 learn the smaller component first: they do not
measure the boundary of a realizable problem. On $\Kc=4$--$7$ both $\isc_c^\Kc=\text{const}$ and
$\isc_c^\Kc/\sqrt{d^\Ns}=\text{const}$ fit the medians ($\chi^2=0.79$ and $0.59$, three degrees of freedom); adding the
per-seed values whose bracket ends at a crossing ($\Kc=8$, two seeds at $\Kc=10$) leaves them indistinguishable
($\chi^2=7.5$ and $7.3$, five degrees of freedom). At the smallest $\isc$, 0, 1, 0, 3, 0, 5, 3 and 6 of six seeds
stall on binary4, MPS4, MPS5, MPS6, binary8, MPS8, MPS10 and MPS12, and 5 of 5 on binary16, each at a partial fit
with one component learned ($\Loss/\Loss(0)=4/13$ or $9/13$).

\paragraph{Imbalance.}
\empirical{} At $\chi=16$, $\fsc=3.375$ the per-seed local slopes of $\dTK$ against $\lambda$ are $1.97\pm0.01$,
$1.85\pm0.06$, $1.55\pm0.11$ and $1.22\pm0.09$ between $\lambda=0.01$, $0.03$, $0.1$, $0.3$ and $1$; the saturation
level for $\lambda\ge10$ is $0.59$--$0.61$ in the medians and $0.51$--$0.75$ over seeds.

\paragraph{Threshold sensitivity and the two-copy lazy boundary.}\label{sec:regime-sensitivity}
\empirical{} (predictions \derived) Raising the separation threshold from $2$ to $5$ moves the saddle-to-saddle
boundary of binary4 by a factor $2.14$--$2.34$ ($\chi=3$--$36$; means of six seeds, s.e.\ at most $0.08$), against
$(5/2)^{1/(\Kc-2)}=2.5$: the $O(1)$ part of $\sep{}$ lowers the ratio at finite $\isc$. Raising the kernel threshold
from $0.05$ to $0.2$ moves the lazy boundary by $1.97\pm0.08$ at $\chi=9$, down to $1.33\pm0.01$ at $\chi=36$ and
$1.30$--$1.35$ at $\chi=32$--$128$: with $\isc_{\rm lazy}\propto\delta_*^{-1/(\Kc\beta)}$ (\cref{app:boundaries}), the local slope
$\beta=\dd\ln\dTK/\dd\ln\xi$ grows from 1 (first order, ratio $1.59$) towards 2 (second order, $1.26$), and the slopes
$1.5$--$1.8$ at $\chi\ge32$ predict $1.27$--$1.36$. Only the boundaries move: across the nine threshold pairs at most
70 of the 912 centred binary4 labels change ($7.7\%$), each to a neighbouring regime. The two-copy model has the
same tangent space as the centred one, but its kernel changes only at second order, so its lazy boundary is
$2^{-1/3}=0.794$ times lower (\cref{prop:A-lazy}): measured $0.79$ at $\chi=32$, $64$, $128$ (tensor kernel). Its
saddle-to-saddle side is not comparable (merged first events, \cref{sec:other-rich}).

\paragraph{Large $\Kc$ in realizable problems.}
\empirical{} \newresult{} Pre-registered sweep: binary trees with $\Ns=4$--$16$ ($\Kc=3$--$15$), odeco teacher $(3,2)$ at
$d=2$ and $3$, six seeds per cell; per seed, $\isc_c$ is the first crossing of $\sep{}=3$ coming from small $\isc$, on
an adaptive grid refined until the bracket is a factor $2^{1/(\Kc-2)}$ in $\isc$. A run whose initial function
already overlaps a teacher component by $0.1$ is outside the domain of the event rule, and a seed that is
saddle-to-saddle up to that scale gives only a lower bound. Plain model, $\chi=4=2\ncomp$ (1,070 runs, 156 seeds): at
$\Kc\ge8$, 85 of the 96 seeds are censored from above, 76 at the domain limit and 9 still saddle-to-saddle when the
budget ended (at $d=3$, 47 of 48); the other 11 cross at $\isc_c=0.86$--$1.37$ ($d=2$, $\Kc=8$--$12$, including 4 of 6
seeds at $\Kc=12$; and $1.08$ at $d=3$, $\Kc=10$). Every censored lower bound at $\Kc\ge8$ ($1.07$--$2.03$;
$1.18$--$2.03$ at $\Kc\ge11$) exceeds 1 and both laws extrapolated from the resolved cells, so $\isc_c\to1$ is rejected
for the plain model at $\Kc\le15$. The bounds sit a median $3\%$ below each seed's own domain limit, whose medians are
$1.50$ ($d=2$) and $1.80$ ($d=3$) at $\Kc\ge11$, still rising: the domain limit is a product of $\Kc$ per-core factors
and tends to about $1.15\sqrt d$ \derived{} (checked by simulation), and only 38 of the 85 bounds exceed $\sqrt d$.
The bounds rest on the sampled $\isc$ (a median of four runs inside the domain per seed); 14 of them, at $d=3$,
$\Kc=12$--$15$, rest on the rule for runs whose second component was not learned by the end of the budget. On the
resolved cells ($d=2$: $\Kc=3$--$7$ and $12$; $d=3$: $\Kc=3$--$5$) the slope of $\ln(\isc_c^\Kc/a_{0c}^\Kc)$ against
$\ln\sqrt{d^\Ns}$ is $1.21\pm0.22$ at the separation threshold 3, favouring the fixed per-component amplitude by the
pre-registered rule; at threshold 5 it favours it only with standard errors scaled by the fit's $\chi^2$, which was
not pre-registered (unresolved without); it is $0.72\pm0.56$ at threshold 2 and $0.61\pm0.51$ without the cells where
the cores shrink (both unresolved): a censored selection that does not answer the large-$\Kc$ question.

Before the first escape the plain model's cores lose a median $0.1\%$ of their squared norm where
$x_W=\isc^\Kc\sqrt{d^\Ns}/(\tw2d)<0.1$, $10\%$ where $x_W$ is between 1 and 10, $34\%$, $52\%$ and $72\%$ in the next
three decades and $92\%$ where $x_W\ge10^4$, and none in the centred model; that the cores shrink while
$\normF{\W}^2>\ip{\W}{\Wt}$ follows from the exact identity $\ddt\normF{\G v}^2=-2(\normF{\W}^2-\ip{\W}{\Wt})$
\proven{} (standard). Near the top of the saddle-to-saddle block $\sep{}$ flattens against $\isc$ (local exponents
$-3.7$, $-4.9$, $-6.4$ at $\Kc=8$--$10$, $d=2$, against $-6$, $-7$, $-8$). That this unlearning of $\Wz$ lengthens the
plateaus and lifts the plain boundary above both laws is consistent with these data but not tested causally
\conjecture; its registered signature also required the centred arm to follow the fixed per-component amplitude,
which it did not (slope $-0.07\pm0.51$, $2.1$ standard errors from 1). Centred model, full bonds (360 runs): medians
$0.16$, $0.39$, $0.50$, $0.65$, $0.81$, $0.83$ at $\Kc=3$--$8$ ($d=2$) and $0.15$, $0.28$ at $\Kc=3$, $4$ ($d=3$, every seed
resolved); per-seed exponents of $\sep{}$ against $\isc$ are $-1.05$, $-2.13$, $-3.21$, $-4.93$, $-5.18$, $-6.93$ at
$\Kc=3$--$8$, against $-(\Kc-2)$ \knownresult[the scaling; new as a check]. The plain model agrees with the centred one
up to $\Kc=4$ and lies above it from $\Kc=5$.

\paragraph{Transients.}
\empirical{} During an escape, components not yet learned rise transiently (for matrix-like pairs the peak falls
like $\isc^{2(1-w)}$, $w$ the weight ratio, \conjecture, \cref{app:boundaries}). In 116 of the $9{,}165$ runs of the
regime maps such a transient crossed $\frac12$ before the component was learned (14 more cross $\frac12$ repeatedly
only in a component never learned); with events dated at the first crossing, 15 runs would be rich instead of
saddle-to-saddle. Events are therefore dated by the final rise (\cref{sec:setup-diagnostics}).

\paragraph{The floor below the cut dimension.}
\empirical{} With $\chi$ equal to the teacher's bond rank ($\chi=3$) the small-$\isc$ floor has mean
$(1-\dim\mathcal M_\chi/d^\Ns)\,\isc^{2\Kc}/\normF{\Wt}^2$ \eqref{eq:A-centred-floor}, and the median of the six seeds is
$0.48$ times it; at $\chi=4$, $6$, where the spare bond rank absorbs part of $\Wz$, the median floor is $0.18$ and
$0.044$ times that expression, which applies only at $\chi=3$ (centred runs at $\isc\le0.4$). At $\chi=3$ the expression
reaches the convergence threshold at $\isc=0.56$, against the measured onset $\isc=1.0$ from which every seed stalls.
At large $\isc$ the floor saturates at the lazy residual: at $\isc=40$ the final loss of each of the 18 centred runs
at $\chi=3$, $4$, $6$ equals its residual computed at initialization to $0.13\%$, with a kernel change of at most
$1.1\cdot10^{-3}$.

\paragraph{Lazy runs of the binary tree with $\Ns=8$.}
\empirical{} With bonds over-parameterized twice ($d=2$, bonds $8$ above two sites, $32$ above four), every run from
$\isc=1.42$ on reaches the step limit, but the runs are lazy (below $10^{-3}\Loss(0)$ by then) in 2 of 5 at
$\isc=1.42$, 2 of 3 at $1.87$, 4 of 5 or 6 at each $\isc$ from $2.47$ to $7.6$ and 2 of 5 at $10$; the others end at
$\Loss/\Loss(0)=0.0014$--$0.0054$ with $\dTK^{\rm probe}<0.02$. The kernel boundary is $1.45$ (probe kernel, median of
four seeds).

\paragraph{Two-copy model at small $\isc$.}
\empirical{} On binary4 at $\chi=18$, $24$, $36$ every two-copy run at $\isc\le0.04$ still has three events, but
$\min_i\sep i$ at $\isc=0.010$ is only $2.7$--$14$ (centred: $12$--$29$) and grows per seed like $\isc^{-0.1}$ to
$\isc^{-1.0}$ between $\isc=0.04$ and $0.01$ (centred: about $\isc^{-1}$); the labels (5, 4 and 5 of six seeds
saddle-to-saddle at $\isc=0.010$, 4, 3 and 2 at $0.016$) do not follow the $\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}$ law.

\paragraph{Large bond dimension.}
\empirical{} Fitted exponents of the measurements of \cref{sec:other-large-chi}: \cref{tab:large-chi}. At $\isc=1$
($\xi=3.74$ under SP, further from the lazy limit) the kernel slopes are shallower: SP $-2.58\pm0.06$, NTK
$-0.62\pm0.02$, $\mu$P-TTN $+0.089\pm0.024$. The plain model gives the same exponents within the errors ($\isc=1.5$:
$-3.06\pm0.07$, $-0.83\pm0.04$, $+0.095\pm0.024$): with full, over-parameterized bonds, centring does not change the
scaling. The factor-wise rule of structured layers \citep{qiu2024compute,potapczynski2024searching}, applied to a TTN,
makes the initial kernel of the binary tree with $\Ns=8$ vanish like $\chi^{-2}$ and slows the initial fit about
linearly in $\chi$ (theory checks, two seeds, $\chi=4$--$16$; \cref{app:boundaries}).
