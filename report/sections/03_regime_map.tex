\section{Map of regimes}\label{sec:regime-map}\label{sec:regime-summary}

Which regime of \cref{tab:regimes} does a run fall into, and which parameters decide it? This section maps the
regimes of gradient flow on the centred model over the per-core initialization scale $\isc$, the bond dimension
$\chi$, the number of cores $\Kc$ and the imbalance between the root and the other cores (\cref{fig:regime-map});
\cref{tab:regime-summary} at its end lists which parameter moves which boundary. On the binary tree with $\Ns=4$,
training is saddle-to-saddle below $\isc_c\approx0.3/\sqrt\chi$, rich in a window of about 20 in $\isc$, and lazy
above $\isc_{\rm lazy}\approx6.5/\sqrt\chi$ (values reached at $\chi\ge32$), but lazy only when every bond reaches
its cut dimension; below it the runs end on a floor. With more cores the saddle-to-saddle boundary rises (in the
plain model above $\isc=1$ for most seeds at $\Kc\ge8$) and stalled runs invade its side, and a light root makes
training lazy at an up to 100 times smaller function-space scale. \Cref{sec:other-regimes} gives the detail of each regime other than saddle-to-saddle, and
\cref{sec:other-optim} adds discrete steps and gradient noise. \newresult[for TTNs] \knownresult[maps for linear
and diagonal networks: \citealp{woodworth2020kernel,tu2024mixed,kunin2024get,domine2025lazy}]

\subsection{Protocol}\label{sec:regime-protocol}

The main family is the \emph{centred} model (\cref{sec:setup-init}, variant (b)): its target $\Wt+\Wz$ removes the
initial function at every $\isc$, so $\isc$ alone moves a run from small to large initialization; the
\emph{plain} and \emph{two-copy} models are cross-checks. Odeco teachers with weights $(3,2,1)$ at $d=3$ or $(3,2)$
at $d=2$, exact population loss, fan-in initialization with one scale $\isc$ for every core unless stated, and
gradient flow until the loss falls below $10^{-6}\cdot\frac12\normF{\Wt}^2$ or a budget ends. On the binary tree with
$\Ns=4$ ($\Kc=3$, \emph{binary4}) the bonds are not capped: $\chi=9=d^2$ is the cut dimension, and larger $\chi$
over-parameterizes the bonds. Labels follow \cref{tab:regimes}, with the tensor kernel ($\dTK$) on binary4 and the
probe kernel ($\dTK^{\rm probe}$) on the larger trees (\cref{sec:setup-diagnostics}). Boundaries
are found per seed (six seeds; saddle-to-saddle where $\min_i\sep i$ crosses $3$, lazy where the kernel change
crosses $0.1$) and reported as medians, or as geometric means $\times/$ their geometric standard error $f$ (the
interval from the mean divided by $f$ to the mean times $f$). Settings: \cref{app:exp-regimes}; supplementary
results: \cref{app:D-regimes}.

\subsection{Initialization scale and bond dimension}\label{sec:regime-sigma-chi}

\paragraph{Setup.}
Binary4, $d=3$, teacher $(3,2,1)$: $\chi=3$--$36$ with 19 values of $\isc\in[0.01,40]$, and $\chi=9$--$128$ with 22
values of $\isc\in[0.004,4]$ (\cref{fig:regime-map}(a)).

\paragraph{Three regimes, ordered in $\isc$.}
\empirical{} At every $\chi$ the runs are saddle-to-saddle at small $\isc$ and rich (converged, $\dTK\ge0.1$,
overlapping events) at intermediate $\isc$; at large $\isc$ they are lazy if $\chi\ge9$ and stalled on a floor if
$\chi<9$. The saddle-to-saddle boundary falls from $0.24$ at $\chi=3$ to $0.136$ at $\chi=9$ and $0.043$ at
$\chi=36$ (not monotone at small $\chi$: $0.099$ at $\chi=4$ and $0.075$ at $\chi=12$, where per-seed values
spread by up to a factor 16; \cref{sec:other-rich}). Below it the smallest separation ratio grows like
$\isc^{-1}$ per seed (fitted exponents $1.015$--$1.048$ in 96 of 101 fits, against $\Kc-2=1$, \derived{} for the
first gap, \cref{app:boundaries}): the gaps grow while the event widths stay at the values of aligned
initialization (\cref{prop:A-s2s-aligned}). The $\chi$-dependence of $\isc_c$ is not derived. Per-$\chi$ values
and fits: \cref{app:D-regimes}.

\paragraph{The lazy boundary.}
\empirical{} confirming \derived{} \cref{prop:A-lazy} With the tensor kernel and $\chi=32$--$128$ the lazy
boundary lies inside the predicted brackets, $0.07\%$ below the lower edge at $\chi=128$:
$\isc_{\rm lazy}\sqrt\chi=6.40$--$6.56$ against the asymptote $6.50$ of \eqref{eq:A-sigma-lazy}. At $\chi=9$--$16$,
where the isotropic constants are not expected to hold, it lies $1.5$--$6\%$ above the bracket
($\isc_{\rm lazy}\sqrt\chi=8.96$ at $\chi=9$). At large $\chi$ a coherent second-order kernel change sets the
boundary (\cref{sec:other-lazy}). \knownresult[the criterion, a threshold on the kernel change:
\citealp{geiger2020disentangling}; a coherent second-order kernel change in wide networks:
\citealp{bordelon2022selfconsistent}] \newresult[the TTN constants]

\paragraph{No lazy regime below the cut dimension.}
\derived{} \empirical{} With $\chi<9$ the centred target has more bond rank than the student can carry: from
$\isc=1.0$, $1.0$, $1.6$ at $\chi=3$, $4$, $6$ every seed stalls on the floor, the best approximation of $\Wt+\Wz$
across the cut $01|23$, and no run is lazy (\cref{sec:other-lazy}).

\paragraph{Both boundaries scale like $\chi^{-1/2}$.}
\empirical{} With full bonds, fits to the per-seed boundaries give the slope $-0.53\pm0.05$ against $\chi$ for the
saddle-to-saddle boundary ($\chi=16$--$128$) and $-0.504\pm0.011$ for the lazy boundary ($\chi=32$--$128$). At
$\chi=32$--$128$, $\isc_c\sqrt\chi=0.27$--$0.33$, and the rich window $\isc_{\rm lazy}/\isc_c$ spans a factor
$19$--$24$ in $\isc$ ($18$--$23$ with the probe kernel), about $10^4$ in the function-space scale $\fsc=\isc^3$.
At large $\chi$ the map thus depends on $\isc\sqrt\chi$, or on the per-bond Gram scale $\chi\isc^2/d^2$ (the
eigenvalue scale of the subtree Grams $\Sub v\T\Sub v$): saddle-to-saddle below about $0.01$, lazy above about
$4.7$. Neither $\isc^\Kc$ (slope $0$) nor $\isc^3\chi/d^2$, the amplitude per path times the number of paths (slope
$-\frac13$, $2.7$--$2.9$ standard errors from the saddle-to-saddle slope), collapses the data. Scope: one tree (a
three-factor matrix product), fan-in initialization, less than one decade of $\chi$. The saddle-to-saddle
scaling is not derived; heuristically, the leaf Grams grow like $\chi\isc^2/d^2$ while the root is light by
$\sqrt\chi$, so the effective per-core amplitude of the escape grows like $\isc\sqrt\chi$ \conjecture.
\knownresult[as a scaling: the width-dependent scales $\isc^2w$ of \citealp{tu2024mixed} and $\fsc\sqrt h$ of
\citealp{geiger2020disentangling}]

\paragraph{Seven cores.}
\empirical{} On the binary tree with $\Ns=8$ ($\Kc=7$) the regimes come in the same order. At $d=3$ no centred run
with $\chi\le24$ is lazy (the top bonds have cut dimension $81$; floor from $\isc=0.91$--$1.34$). At $d=2$ (cut
dimensions $4$ and $16$) the saddle-to-saddle boundary is $0.83$ $[0.72,0.97]$ for $\chi=4$, $8$, $16$ and $0.53$
$[0.46,0.61]$ for full bonds over-parameterized twice (medians of six seeds and of their brackets), and only the
latter reach a lazy regime within the step budget, from $\isc\approx1.4$: the rich window spans a factor $2.7$
in $\isc$, against about 20 at $\Kc=3$ (with another $d$ and teacher). At $\chi=16$, where the bonds are exactly
full, the runs above $\isc=2$ stop at the step limit unconverged (\cref{sec:other-lazy}).

\subsection{Number of cores}\label{sec:regime-K}

\paragraph{Setup.}
$d=2$, teacher $(3,2)$, $\chi=4$ (twice the teacher rank) on every tree, so the bonds are full for $\Kc\le5$ and
deficient from $\Kc=6$ on: binary trees with $\Ns=4$, $8$, $16$ ($\Kc=3$, $7$, $15$) and MPS with $\Ns=4$, $5$, $6$,
$8$, $10$, $12$; 11--14 values of $\isc$ from $\max(10^{-8/\Kc},0.02)$ to $2$ (\cref{fig:regime-map}(b)).

\paragraph{Resolved up to seven cores.}
\empirical{} \newresult[for TTNs] The saddle-to-saddle boundary rises with $\Kc$: medians of the per-seed
boundaries $0.17$, $0.49$, $0.61$, $0.68$ and $0.75$ at $\Kc=3$--$7$ (quartiles in \cref{app:D-regimes}), with all
six seeds resolved except at $\Kc=6$ (four). It stays above the aligned-initialization boundary
$a_{0c}(\Kc)=0.066$--$0.49$ of \cref{prop:A-s2s-aligned}. The plain model gives the same boundaries within one grid
step up to $\Kc=7$; beyond, it must first remove $\Wz$.

\paragraph{Beyond seven cores.}
\empirical{} At $\Kc=8$, $10$, $12$, $15$ at most one of six seeds is resolved: the run after the last
saddle-to-saddle run stalls on the floor of the unrealizable target, so the label rule gives only brackets near
1 (median brackets between $0.63$ and $1.36$), lower bounds rather than crossings (\cref{app:D-regimes}).

\paragraph{The limit at large $\Kc$, in realizable problems.}
\empirical{} \newresult{} Two laws were candidates: a fixed $\isc_c^\Kc$, which gives $\isc_c\to\lim_\Kc a_{0c}(\Kc)=1$
like the per-layer threshold of aligned initialization \knownresult[\citealp{gissin2020implicit}, Thm~2], and a fixed
per-component amplitude $\isc_c^\Kc/\sqrt{d^\Ns}$ (\cref{prop:A-kernel}(b)), which gives $\isc_c\to\sqrt d$. A
pre-registered second sweep with realizable targets (binary trees with $\Ns=4$--$16$, teacher $(3,2)$, $d=2$ and $3$,
six seeds per cell) tests them. In the plain model with spare channels ($\chi=4$), 85 of the 96 seeds at $\Kc\ge8$
find no crossing: they stay saddle-to-saddle up to the scale at which the initial function itself overlaps a teacher
component, where the event rule stops applying, or to the end of the budget. Their lower bounds on $\isc_c$,
$1.07$--$2.03$, all exceed 1 and both laws fitted on the resolved cells, so in the plain model $\isc_c$ does not tend
to 1. A proposed mechanism, consistent with the data but not tested causally, is that the plain model unlearns
$\Wz$ while it waits on the plateau, which lowers the effective amplitude \conjecture. The centred model with full
bonds has no $\Wz$ to unlearn but is affordable only up to $\Kc=8$ at $d=2$ and $\Kc=4$ at $d=3$: there $\isc_c$ rises
from $0.16$ to $0.83$ over $\Kc=3$--$8$, and the slope of $\ln(\isc_c^\Kc/a_{0c}^\Kc)$ against $\ln\sqrt{d^\Ns}$ ($0$ for
the first law, $1$ for the second) is $-0.07\pm0.51$, which resolves neither law. Whether $\isc_c\to\sqrt d$ remains a
\conjecture{} (details: \cref{app:D-regimes}).

\paragraph{Stalls on the saddle-to-saddle side.}
\empirical{} Below the boundary stalled runs take a growing part of the map (plain grey in
\cref{fig:regime-map}(b)): at the smallest $\isc$, from none of six seeds on binary4 to 6 of 6 on MPS12 and 5 of 5
on binary16, each at a partial fit with one component learned. A stalling MPS seed stalls up to a seed-dependent
$\isc$, then turns saddle-to-saddle. Such stalls are dead routes or slow plateaus (the centred runs did not
record the spectra that tell the two apart), the failure modes of \cref{sec:s2s-failure}, which also covers
the more frequent stalls with $\chi$ equal to the teacher rank.

\subsection{Imbalance between cores}\label{sec:regime-imbalance}

\paragraph{Setup.}
Binary4, $d=3$, teacher $(3,2,1)$. The root starts at scale $\lambda\isc$, the other cores at $\isc$, so
$\fsc=\lambda\isc^3$ is fixed while $\lambda$ sets the conserved imbalance of the root bonds,
$\E\Q e=\isc^2(1-\lambda^2/\chi)\Id$ (\cref{sec:setup-conservation}): balance at $\lambda=\sqrt\chi$; the fan-in
default $\lambda=1$ is root-light. Grids: $\chi=24$ (full bonds) and $\chi=3$, nine values of $\fsc$ from $10^{-6}$
to $10^2$ times ten of $\lambda/\sqrt\chi$ from $0.01$ to $100$; and $\chi=16$, $\fsc=3.375$
($\xi=\normF{\Wt}/\fsc=1.11$), $\lambda=0.01$--$100$ (\cref{fig:regime-map}(c), (d)).

\paragraph{A light root makes training lazy; a heavy one does not.}
\empirical{} \newresult[for TTNs] At $\chi=24$ every seed is lazy from $\fsc=1$ for a light root
($\lambda/\sqrt\chi=0.01$, $0.03$), from $\fsc=10$ for $\lambda/\sqrt\chi=0.1$--$0.32$ (the fan-in default is $0.2$),
and only from $\fsc=100$ at balance (2 of 6 lazy at $\fsc=10$) and for a heavy root ($\lambda/\sqrt\chi\ge3$, none
at $\fsc=10$): a light root lowers the lazy boundary by a factor 100 in $\fsc$, a heavy one does not move it. The
saddle-to-saddle majority reaches $\fsc=10^{-4}$ for $0.1\le\lambda/\sqrt\chi\le10$ and $10^{-5}$ or $10^{-6}$
outside; on this one-decade grid no widest point near balance can be read off. With $\chi=3$ every run stalls on
the floor from $\fsc=1$.

\paragraph{Kernel change at fixed $\fsc$.}
\empirical{} confirming \derived{} (\cref{app:boundaries}) At $\chi=16$, $\fsc=3.375$, $\dTK$ grows like $\lambda^2$
from small $\lambda$ (local slope $1.85\pm0.06$ between $\lambda=0.03$ and $0.1$, predicted $1.8$--$1.9$) and
saturates at $0.59$--$0.61$ for $\lambda\ge10$ (medians; at the lower edge of the predicted $0.6$--$0.7$); the runs
are lazy up to $\lambda=0.3$ and rich from $\lambda=1$. In the aligned mode a small root is the only core that
moves, so the model is linear in it, while a large root freezes and leaves a degree-$(\Kc-1)$ model \derived.
Two-layer linear networks share the first effect, a lazy phase that turns rich when the features are
incomplete, but there a large output weight also gives lazy training
\knownresult[\citealp{kunin2024get,domine2025lazy}]; for $\Kc\ge3$ the model left by a frozen root is not linear.
