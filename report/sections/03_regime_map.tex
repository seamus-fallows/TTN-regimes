\section{Map of regimes}\label{sec:regime-map}

Which regime of \cref{tab:regimes} does a run fall into, and which parameters decide it? This section maps the
regimes of gradient flow on the centred model over the per-core initialization scale $\isc$, the bond dimension
$\chi$, the number of cores $\Kc$ and the imbalance between the root and the other cores
(\cref{fig:regime-map}; \cref{tab:regime-summary} lists which parameter moves which boundary). On the binary
tree with $\Ns=4$, training is saddle-to-saddle below $\isc_c\approx0.3/\sqrt\chi$, rich in a window of about 20
in $\isc$, and lazy above $\isc_{\rm lazy}\approx6.5/\sqrt\chi$, but lazy only when every bond reaches its cut
dimension; below it the runs end on a floor. With more cores the saddle-to-saddle boundary rises and stalled
runs invade its side, and a light root makes training lazy at a 100 times smaller function-space scale. Maps of
this kind are known for linear and diagonal networks
\knownresult[\citealp{woodworth2020kernel,tu2024mixed,kunin2024get,domine2025lazy}]; for tree tensor networks
they are new \newresult[for TTNs]. \Cref{sec:other-lazy,sec:other-rich,sec:other-stalled,sec:other-large-chi}
look at the regimes other than saddle-to-saddle in more detail.

% ===========================================================================================
\subsection{Protocol}\label{sec:regime-protocol}

The main family is the \emph{centred} model (\cref{sec:setup-init}, variant (b)): its target $\Wt+\Wz$ removes the
initial function at every $\isc$ without changing the parameterization, so $\isc$ alone moves a run from small to
large initialization; the \emph{plain} and \emph{two-copy} models are cross-checks. Whitened Gaussian inputs,
exact population loss, double precision; odeco teachers with weights $(3,2,1)$ at $d=3$ or $(3,2)$ at $d=2$,
Haar-random site bases from a seed stream independent of the initialization; fan-in initialization with one
scale $\isc$ for every core unless stated. On the binary tree with $\Ns=4$ ($\Kc=3$, \emph{binary4}) the bonds are
not capped: $\chi=9=d^2$ is the cut dimension, larger $\chi$ over-parameterizes the bonds; other trees are capped
by \eqref{eq:bond-cap}. Gradient flow (Dormand--Prince 5(4), or LSODA for long plateaus) runs until the loss
falls below $10^{-6}\cdot\frac12\normF{\Wt}^2$ or a time or step budget ends; every final rise is re-integrated
on a linear grid, so the separation ratios are measured in linear time. Labels follow \cref{tab:regimes} at all
nine threshold pairs, with events at the last upward crossing of $\ov r=\frac12$ before $\ov r$ first reaches
$0.9$. The lazy label uses the 128-probe kernel of \cref{sec:setup-diagnostics} ($\dTK^{\rm probe}$); comparisons
with theory use the tensor-space kernel $\Jac\Pre\Jac\T$ ($\dTK$). Near the lazy boundary the probe value is
$0.54$--$1.11$ times the tensor value (median $0.78$; 43 runs at $\chi\ge32$) \empirical, so probe-kernel lazy
boundaries lie about $5\%$ lower in $\isc$. Six seeds per point; boundaries are found per seed (saddle-to-saddle
where $\min_i\sep i$ crosses $3$, or a bracket where a stalled run follows the last saddle-to-saddle run; lazy
where $\dTK$ crosses $0.1$) and reported as geometric means $\times/$ their standard-error factor, or medians with
quartiles or brackets. Details and checks: \cref{app:exp-regimes}.

\subsection{Initialization scale and bond dimension}\label{sec:regime-sigma-chi}

\paragraph{Setup.}
Binary4, $d=3$, teacher $(3,2,1)$: $\chi=3$--$36$ with 19 values of $\isc\in[0.01,40]$, and $\chi=9$--$128$ with 22
values of $\isc\in[0.004,4]$ (\cref{fig:regime-map}(a)).

\paragraph{Three regimes, ordered in $\isc$.}
\empirical{} \newresult[for TTNs] At every $\chi$ the runs are saddle-to-saddle at small $\isc$ and rich
(converged, $\dTK\ge0.1$, overlapping events) at intermediate $\isc$; at large $\isc$ they are lazy if $\chi\ge9$
and stalled if $\chi<9$. The saddle-to-saddle boundary falls from $0.237\times/1.32$ at $\chi=3$ to
$0.136\times/1.11$ at $\chi=9$ and $0.043\times/1.05$ at $\chi=36$; at $\chi=3$ it lies within the spread of 30
plain-model seeds (median $0.27$--$0.31$, \cref{app:boundaries}). Below it, $\min_i\sep i\propto\isc^{-p}$ per seed
with $p=1.015$--$1.048$ in 96 of the 101 fits (all $\chi$ of both sweeps), against $\Kc-2=1$ (\derived{} for the first
gap, \cref{app:boundaries}); the exceptions ($1.09$--$1.27$) are seeds with transients above $\frac12$. The gaps grow
while the widths stay at the aligned-mode values of \cref{prop:A-s2s-aligned} ($0.464$--$0.468$,
$0.798$--$0.801$, $2.010$ against $0.464$, $0.797$, $2.009$ for $\tw r=3,2,1$; $\chi=36$, $\isc=0.01$). Extrapolating
$\sep{}\propto1/\isc$ from each seed's deepest run gives $0.86$--$1.23$ times the measured boundary for 53 of 54
seeds, a consistency check, not a prediction: the $\chi$-dependence of $\isc_c$ is not derived.

\paragraph{The lazy boundary.}
\empirical{} confirming \derived{} \cref{prop:A-lazy} \knownresult[the criterion, a threshold on the kernel change:
\citealp{geiger2020disentangling}; a coherent second-order kernel change in wide networks:
\citealp{bordelon2022selfconsistent}] \newresult[the TTN constants] With the tensor kernel the lazy boundary is
$1.13$, $0.94$, $0.82$, $0.66$ and $0.571$ at $\chi=32$, $48$, $64$, $96$, $128$ (medians of six seeds), inside the
predicted brackets $[1.10,1.20]$, $[0.92,0.96]$, $[0.80,0.82]$, $[0.66,0.67]$ and $0.07\%$ below the lower edge of
$[0.571,0.577]$: $\isc_{\rm lazy}\sqrt\chi=6.40$--$6.56$ against the asymptote $6.50$ of \eqref{eq:A-sigma-lazy}. At
$\chi=9$--$16$, where the isotropic constants are not expected to hold, it lies $1.5$--$6\%$ above the bracket. Near
the boundary $\dTK$ is $0.96$ (median; $0.69$--$1.82$) times the law $\sqrt{(c_1\xi)^2+(c_2\xi^2)^2}$ of
\eqref{eq:A-lazy-dk}: at large $\chi$ the coherent second-order change sets the boundary, and the first-order
estimate holds only up to $\chi=12$ ($0.59$ against the measured $1.09$ at $\chi=36$).

\paragraph{No lazy regime below the cut dimension.}
\derived{} (\cref{app:boundaries}) \empirical{} With $\chi<9$ the centred target has more bond rank than the
student can carry. All 159 stalled centred runs at $\chi=3$, $4$, $6$ end on the Eckart--Young minimum of
$\Wt+\Wz$ across the cut $\{1,2\}|\{3,4\}$ (to $0.04\%$), and every seed stalls from $\isc=1.0$, $1.0$, $1.6$,
$1.7$--$2.2$ times the small-$\isc$ estimate \eqref{eq:A-centred-floor} (\cref{sec:other-lazy}).

\paragraph{Both boundaries scale like $\chi^{-1/2}$.}
\empirical{} \knownresult[as a scaling: the width-dependent scales $\isc^2w$ of \citealp{tu2024mixed} and
$\fsc\sqrt h$ of \citealp{geiger2020disentangling}] Fits to the per-seed boundaries with full bonds give the
slope $-0.528\pm0.053$ against $\chi$ for the saddle-to-saddle boundary ($\chi=16$--$128$, 42 values, $0.9$
decade; per-seed fits $-0.528\pm0.073$; $-0.552\pm0.049$ over $\chi=9$--$128$) and $-0.504\pm0.011$ for the lazy
boundary ($\chi=32$--$128$;
$-0.553\pm0.013$ over $\chi=16$--$128$, with $\chi=16$, $24$ not yet asymptotic). The measured $\isc_c\sqrt\chi$ is
$0.27$--$0.33$ at $\chi=32$--$128$, and the rich window $\isc_{\rm lazy}/\isc_c$ spans a factor $18$--$23$ in $\isc$
(probe kernel; $19$--$24$ with the tensor kernel), about $10^4$ in $\fsc=\isc^3$. At large $\chi$ the map thus
depends on $\isc\sqrt\chi$, the per-bond Gram scale $p=\chi\isc^2/d^2$ (the eigenvalue scale of the subtree
Grams $\Sub v\T\Sub v$): saddle-to-saddle below $p\approx0.01$, lazy above $p\approx4.7$. Neither $\isc^\Kc$ (slope
$0$) nor the amplitude per path times the number of paths, $\isc^3\chi/d^2$ (slope $-\frac13$), collapses the data:
$-\frac13$ lies $2.7$--$2.9$ standard errors from the saddle-to-saddle slope in per-seed fits ($\chi\ge16$, $\chi\ge32$;
$3.7$ in the pooled fit, which treats seeds sharing a teacher across $\chi$ as independent).
Scope: one tree (a three-factor matrix product), fan-in initialization, less than one decade of $\chi$. The
saddle-to-saddle scaling is not derived; heuristically, the leaf Grams grow like $p$ while the root is light by
$\sqrt\chi$, so the effective per-core amplitude of the escape grows like $\isc\sqrt\chi$ \conjecture.

\paragraph{Seven cores.}
\empirical{} On the binary tree with $\Ns=8$ ($\Kc=7$) the regimes come in the same order. At $d=3$ no centred run
with $\chi\le24$ is lazy (the top bonds have cut dimension $81$; floor from $\isc=0.91$--$1.34$). At $d=2$ (cut
dimensions $4$ and $16$) the saddle-to-saddle boundary is $0.83$ $[0.72,0.97]$ for $\chi=4$, $8$, $16$ and $0.53$
$[0.46,0.61]$ for full bonds over-parameterized twice (medians of six seeds and of their brackets), and only the
latter reach a lazy regime, from $\isc\approx1.4$ (\cref{sec:other-lazy}): the rich window spans a factor $2.7$ in
$\isc$, against $18$--$23$ at $\Kc=3$ (with another $d$ and teacher).

\subsection{Number of cores}\label{sec:regime-K}

\paragraph{Setup.}
$d=2$, teacher $(3,2)$, $\chi=4$ (twice the teacher rank) on every tree, so the bonds are full for $\Kc\le5$ and
deficient from $\Kc=6$ on: binary trees with $\Ns=4$, $8$, $16$ ($\Kc=3$, $7$, $15$) and MPS with $\Ns=4$, $5$, $6$,
$8$, $10$, $12$; 11--14 values of $\isc$ from $\max(10^{-8/\Kc},0.02)$ to $2$ (\cref{fig:regime-map}(b)).

\paragraph{Resolved up to seven cores.}
\empirical{} \newresult[for TTNs] The saddle-to-saddle boundary rises with $\Kc$: medians of the per-seed
boundaries $0.17$ (quartiles $0.11$--$0.20$), $0.49$, $0.61$, $0.68$ and $0.75$ at $\Kc=3$--$7$, with all six seeds
resolved except at $\Kc=6$ (four). It stays above the aligned-initialization boundary
$a_{0c}(\Kc)=0.066$--$0.49$ of \cref{prop:A-s2s-aligned}; read as aligned initialization with per-core amplitude
$\kappa_\Kc\isc$, $\kappa_\Kc=a_{0c}/\isc_c$ grows from $0.39$ to $0.65$, as in the runs of \cref{app:boundaries}. The
plain model gives the same boundaries within one grid step up to $\Kc=7$; beyond, it must first remove $\Wz$.

\paragraph{Beyond seven cores: brackets.}
\empirical{} At $\Kc=8$, $10$, $12$, $15$ only 1, 1, 0, 0 of six seeds are resolved: the run after the last
saddle-to-saddle run is stalled on the floor of the unrealizable target, so the label rule gives brackets, with
medians $[0.88,1.16]$, $[0.80,1.00]$, $[0.83,1.03]$, $[0.63,1.36]$: lower bounds, not crossings. Classified by
$\min_i\sep i$ instead, that run is saddle-to-saddle-like ($\sep{}=3.9$--$642$) for 13 of the 18 unresolved MPS
seeds. At $\Kc=8$ the block then ends at a crossing in 5 of 6 seeds, median $1.01$; at $\Kc=10$ and $12$ such runs
persist up to $\isc\approx1.0$--$1.3$ and the next runs learn nothing within the budget, so a
saddle-to-saddle-to-rich crossing is seen in 2 of 6 seeds at $\Kc=10$ (both at $0.90$) and in none at $\Kc=12$. All these runs end on the floor ($\Loss/\Loss(0)=0.001$--$0.074$), and 9 of the 13
learn the smaller component first: they do not measure the boundary of a realizable problem.

\paragraph{Which scale stays fixed?}
\empirical{} On $\Kc=4$--$7$ both $\isc_c^\Kc=\text{const}$ and $\isc_c^\Kc/\sqrt{d^\Ns}=\text{const}$ (a fixed
per-component amplitude, \cref{prop:A-kernel}(b)) fit the medians ($\chi^2=0.79$ and $0.59$, three degrees of
freedom), and adding the per-seed values whose bracket ends at a crossing ($\Kc=8$, two seeds at $\Kc=10$) leaves
them indistinguishable ($\chi^2=7.5$ and $7.3$, five degrees of freedom). If $\kappa_\Kc\to1$,
$\isc_c\to\lim_\Kc a_{0c}(\Kc)=1$, like the per-layer threshold of aligned initialization
\knownresult[\citealp{gissin2020implicit}, Thm~2]; a fixed per-component amplitude would give $\isc_c\to\sqrt d$.
With all runs at $d=2$ and brackets beyond $\Kc=7$, the data distinguish neither limit \conjecture.

\paragraph{Stalls on the saddle-to-saddle side.}
\empirical{} Below the boundary stalled runs take a growing part of the map (plain grey): at the smallest $\isc$,
0, 1, 0, 3, 0, 5, 3 and 6 of six seeds stall on binary4, MPS4, MPS5, MPS6, binary8, MPS8, MPS10 and MPS12, and
5 of 5 on binary16, each at a partial fit with one component learned ($\Loss/\Loss(0)=4/13$ or $9/13$). A stalling
MPS seed stalls up to a seed-dependent $\isc$, then turns saddle-to-saddle; with $\chi$ equal to the teacher rank
stalls are more frequent still (\cref{sec:other-stalled}).

\paragraph{The boundary at large $\Kc$.}
\placeholder{To be filled from the second round of the regime-map experiment (remote, running): a realizable
$\Kc$ sweep (spare channels or full bonds) at $d=2$ and $d=3$ with longer budgets at $\Kc\ge8$, and a
pre-registered comparison of $\isc_c^\Kc=\text{const}$ with $\isc_c^\Kc/\sqrt{d^\Ns}=\text{const}$. Until then the
values for $\Kc\ge8$ are brackets on an unrealizable target, and the limit of $\isc_c(\Kc)$ is a conjecture.}

\subsection{Imbalance between cores}\label{sec:regime-imbalance}

\paragraph{Setup.}
Binary4, $d=3$, teacher $(3,2,1)$. The root starts at scale $\lambda\isc$, the other cores at $\isc$, so
$\fsc=\lambda\isc^3$ is fixed while $\lambda$ sets the conserved imbalance of the root bonds,
$\E\Q e=\isc^2(1-\lambda^2/\chi)\Id$ (\cref{sec:setup-conservation}): balance at $\lambda=\sqrt\chi$; the fan-in
default $\lambda=1$ is root-light. Grids: $\chi=24$ (full bonds) and $\chi=3$, nine values of $\fsc$ from $10^{-6}$
to $10^2$ times ten of $\lambda/\sqrt\chi$ from $0.01$ to $100$; and $\chi=16$, $\fsc=3.375$
($\xi=\normF{\Wt}/\fsc=1.11$), $\lambda=0.01$--$100$ (\cref{fig:regime-map}(c), (d)).

\paragraph{A light root makes training lazy; a heavy one does not.}
\empirical{} \newresult[for TTNs] At $\chi=24$ the runs are lazy from $\fsc=0.1$ at $\lambda/\sqrt\chi=0.01$ (4 of 6
seeds) and from $\fsc=1$ at $0.03$ (6 of 6; at $0.1$ a tie, 3 of 6), against $\fsc=10$ at balance (4 of 6): the
lazy boundary moves by up to a factor 100 in $\fsc$. A heavy root ($\lambda/\sqrt\chi\ge3$) is lazy from $\fsc=10$ (3 of
6; 6 of 6 at $\fsc=100$), as at balance. The saddle-to-saddle majority reaches $\fsc=10^{-4}$ for
$0.1\le\lambda/\sqrt\chi\le10$ and $10^{-5}$ or $10^{-6}$ outside; on this one-decade grid no widest point near
balance can be read off. With $\chi=3$ every run stalls on the floor from $\fsc=1$.

\paragraph{Kernel change at fixed $\fsc$.}
\empirical{} confirming \derived{} (\cref{app:boundaries}) At $\chi=16$, $\fsc=3.375$, $\dTK$ grows like $\lambda^2$
from small $\lambda$ (per-seed local slopes $1.97\pm0.01$, $1.85\pm0.06$, $1.55\pm0.11$, $1.22\pm0.09$ between
$\lambda=0.01$, $0.03$, $0.1$, $0.3$, $1$; predicted $1.8$--$1.9$ between $0.03$ and $0.1$) and saturates at
$0.59$--$0.61$ for $\lambda\ge10$ (medians, seeds $0.51$--$0.75$; at the lower edge of the predicted $0.6$--$0.7$); the
runs are lazy up to
$\lambda=0.3$ and rich from $\lambda=3$. In the aligned mode a small root is the only core that moves, so the
model is linear in it, while a large root freezes and leaves a degree-$(\Kc-1)$ model \derived. Two-layer linear
networks share the first effect, a lazy phase that turns rich when the features are incomplete, but there a
large output weight also gives lazy training
\knownresult[\citealp{kunin2024get,domine2025lazy}]; for $\Kc\ge3$ the model left by a frozen root is not linear.

\subsection{Threshold sensitivity and cross-checks}\label{sec:regime-sensitivity}

\empirical{} (predictions \derived) Raising the separation threshold from $2$ to $5$ moves the saddle-to-saddle
boundary of binary4 by a factor $2.14$--$2.34$ ($\chi=3$--$36$; means of six seeds, s.e.\ at most $0.08$), against
$(5/2)^{1/(\Kc-2)}=2.5$: the $O(1)$ part of $\sep{}$ lowers the ratio at finite $\isc$. Raising the kernel threshold
from $0.05$ to $0.2$ moves the lazy boundary by $1.97\pm0.08$ at $\chi=9$, down to $1.33\pm0.01$ at $\chi=36$ and
$1.30$--$1.35$ at $\chi=32$--$128$: with $\isc_{\rm lazy}\propto\delta_*^{-1/(\Kc s)}$, the local slope
$s=\dd\ln\dTK/\dd\ln\xi$ grows from 1 (first order, ratio $1.59$) towards 2 (second order, $1.26$), and the
slopes $1.5$--$1.8$ at $\chi\ge32$ predict $1.27$--$1.36$. Only the boundaries move: across the nine threshold
pairs at most 65 of the 912 centred binary4 labels change ($7.1\%$), each to a neighbouring regime. The
two-copy model has the same tangent space as the centred one, but its kernel changes only at second order, so
its lazy boundary is $2^{-1/3}=0.794$ times lower (\cref{prop:A-lazy}): measured $0.79$ at $\chi=32$, $64$, $128$
(tensor kernel). Its saddle-to-saddle side is not comparable (merged first events, \cref{sec:other-rich}).

\subsection{Step size}\label{sec:regime-lr}

The map is that of gradient flow; \cref{sec:other-optim} adds the step size. Gradient descent keeps gradient
flow's saddle sequence, with event times shifted by $\lr c_e+O(\lr^2)$, up to a stability limit \derived: at small
initialization $\lr_c=2/(\Kc\tw1^{2-2/\Kc})$ \eqref{eq:optim-etac}, independent of $\isc$ ($0.154$ on binary4), and on
the lazy side $2/\lambda_{\rm GF}$, which falls like $\isc^{-3.965\pm0.014}$, close to the kernel scale
$\isc^{-2(\Kc-1)}$ (centred binary4, $\chi=9$) \empirical. Beyond the limit GD oscillates at the edge of stability or
diverges from small initialization, and catapults to flatter minima from $\isc\ge1$ (\cref{fig:optim-gd}(c)). Online
SGD adds a mean-square limit and lengthens or traps later plateaus at fixed $\lr/\batch$, turning slow escapes into
stalls.
