\section{Experimental details}\label{app:experiments}

This appendix gives the settings of every experiment: the problems (trees, dimensions, teachers), the
initializations, the dynamics and their tolerances, the sweeps, seeds and budgets, how runs are
classified, and which runs were excluded. \Cref{app:exp-common} lists what all experiments share.
Each later subsection names its experiment folder, whose README lists every command in order and whose
\path{configs/} holds the YAML configurations, the notes with its findings, and the reviews whose
errata are applied in the main text. Run counts are of finished runs. Raw outputs are not in the
repository; \cref{app:reproducibility} says how to regenerate them.

\subsection{Common settings}\label{app:exp-common}

\paragraph{Model and loss.}
Every experiment trains the model of \cref{sec:setup-model} on the exact population loss
\eqref{eq:loss}, computed by tree contractions (\cref{sec:setup-loss}), except where stated: the
finite-sample sweeps (empirical loss), the correlated-input runs (dense second-moment matrix) and the
real-data runs (empirical loss on a fixed training set). Inputs are whitened Gaussians ($\Sigi i=\Id$)
unless stated, so the loss is half the squared Frobenius distance to the teacher, and only the second
moments of the features enter. The three terms of \eqref{eq:loss-expanded} are exact contractions
(\path{ttn.losses.population_loss}, with \path{ttn.network.inner} and \path{ttn.general.inner_general}): for two TTNs $\W,\W'$ on the
same tree (bond dimensions may differ), $\ipS{\W}{\W'}=E_{\vroot}$, with environments
$E_v\in\R^{\bd{v}\times\bd{v}'}$ computed from the leaves to the root at a cost linear in $\Kc$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:environment}
  (E_v)_{aa'}=\sum\G{v}\big[(j_i),(b_u),a\big]\,\G{v}'\big[(j'_i),(b'_u),a'\big]
  \prod_{i\in\sites{v}}(\Sigi{i})_{j_ij'_i}\prod_{u\in\ch{v}}(E_u)_{b_ub'_u};
\end{equation}
for a CP teacher, $\ipS{\W}{\U{r}}$ is the student evaluated at the site vectors $\Sigi{i}\fac{r}{i}$, so on any
tree
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cp-inner}
  \ipS{\W}{\Wt}=\sum_r\tw{r}\,f_\params\big(\Sigi{1}\fac{r}{1},\dots,\Sigi{\Ns}\fac{r}{\Ns}\big),
  \qquad
  \ipS{\Wt}{\Wt}=\sum_{r,r'}\tw{r}\tw{r'}\prod_i\fac{r}{i}{}\T\Sigi{i}\fac{r'}{i},
\end{equation}
where $f_\params(z_1,\dots,z_\Ns)$ is \eqref{eq:messages} with $\feat{i}(x)$ replaced by $z_i$; a TTN teacher on
another tree is glued to the student along the physical legs, and the loopy network is contracted exactly
along an optimized path. The reference model and the reasons for it are in \path{notes/model.md}.

\paragraph{Precision and processes.}
All computations are in double precision: with $\isc=10^{-2}$ and $\Kc=7$ the initial tensor has norm
about $10^{-14}$ and the gradients are of order $10^{-12}$. A dormant component whose escape signal falls
below about $10^{-15}$ relative is seeded by roundoff, not by the initialization; the experiments that can
reach this floor scan for it and flag such runs (\cref{sec:setup-dynamics}). Every process runs PyTorch
with a single thread (\path{torch.set_num_threads(1)}): the tensors are small, and many threads slow
their linear algebra down by large factors on a loaded machine. Parallelism comes from running many
single-threaded processes.

\paragraph{Integrators and tolerances.}
The reference integrator is the adaptive Dormand--Prince 5(4) pair of \path{ttn.dynamics.integrate_gf}
with relative and absolute tolerances $10^{-8}$ and $10^{-12}$ (\cref{sec:setup-dynamics}). Past the first
escape the flow is stiff: learned directions have Hessian eigenvalues of order one while a plateau lasts
$\propto\isc^{-(k-2)}$, so an explicit method needs a number of steps that grows with the plateau. Most
experiments therefore integrate with SciPy's LSODA and the exact Hessian (by automatic differentiation)
as its Jacobian; short runs and references use Dormand--Prince or DOP853; Radau is a fallback.
\Cref{tab:exp-integrators} gives each experiment's choice and its seeds. Event times come from root finding on
the integrator's dense output, from re-integrating the event window on a fine linear grid, or from dense
merged records (log-spaced and linear output times plus the fine rows of \cref{sec:setup-dynamics});
\cref{tab:exp-common} says which. Accuracy is checked by the conservation drift \eqref{eq:drift},
below $10^{-6}$ (exceptions are reported per experiment), by the exact aligned solutions
(\cref{sec:s2s-aligned}), by reruns at tighter tolerances, and by independent implementations in the
reviews.

\paragraph{Seeds and random streams.}
Teacher and initialization draw from independent random streams: the teacher's generator is
\path{ttn.teachers.teacher_generator}$(s)$, which mixes the teacher seed $s$ through a seed sequence, so
that a teacher and an initialization with equal seeds share no Gaussian draws. Before this separation
(library commit 57d5262), equal seeds made a random TTN teacher proportional to the student's
initialization and tied an odeco teacher's first site basis to the first core; experiment runs from
that time support no claim and were discarded or rerun (each subsection lists its exclusions), and two
numerical checks of the theory that predate the separation are marked as such in
\cref{rem:A-wrong-order,prop:A-trap}. Initialization seeds are consecutive integers, the same at every
$\isc$ of a sweep, so that a seed fixes the initialization direction while $\isc$ varies.

\paragraph{Initializations.}
\emph{Fan-in}: \eqref{eq:init} with one scale $\isc$ for all cores, or per-core scales where stated.
\emph{Balanced}: the same tensor $\Wz$ in the gauge with $\Q e=0$ on every bond. For each bond, with
$M=\Gm{v}{e}\Gm{v}{e}\T$ and $N=\Gm{p}{e}\Gm{p}{e}\T$, the symmetric positive definite $P$ with $PMP=N$
gives the gauge transformation $P^{1/2}$ that makes the two Gram matrices equal; since balancing one bond
changes its neighbours, sweeps over the bonds repeat until $\max_e\normmax{\Q e}<10^{-13}\max_v\normF{\G v}^2$
(\path{gauge_balance} in \path{experiments/s2s_core/s2s.py}; the locality, regime-map and optimizer
experiments have their own implementations). A gauge transformation leaves $\W$ unchanged, so a balanced run and its fan-in twin start
from the same function and differ only in the conserved quantities. \emph{Aligned}: cores diagonal in
the teacher's basis (\cref{prop:aligned}; \path{ttn.network.aligned_init}). \emph{Saddle starts}: an exact
balanced saddle, aligned for pattern teachers or found by L-BFGS on the rank-constrained problem for
generic teachers, plus a fan-in perturbation of scale $\isc$ or $\varepsilon$.

\paragraph{Centred and two-copy models.}
The centred model trains on the target $\Wt+\Wz$ (\path{ttn.teachers.ShiftedTeacher}), so that
$f_\params-f_{\params_0}$ is trained on $f^*$ (\cref{sec:setup-init}). Written directly, its loss is a
difference of terms of size $\normF{\Wz}^2$, with a cancellation error of about $10^{-16}\normF{\Wz}^2$
that is harmless at small $\fsc$ but ruins large ones. The library therefore writes $\W(\params)-\Wz$ as
one TTN on doubled bonds, in half-sums and half-differences of the two networks, so that nothing cancels
(\path{ttn.dynamics.Problem}); the regime map uses the direct form only for $\fsc<10^{-2}$
(\cref{app:exp-regimes}). The two-copy model $\W(\params^+)-\W(\params^-)$ is stored as one TTN with doubled bonds
$2\bd{v}$ and block-diagonal cores (\path{ttn.network.antisymmetric_double}): $\G{v}^\pm$ on the block where all bond
indices of $v$ are in the ``$\pm$'' half, and the root holds $\G{\vroot}^+$ and $-\G{\vroot}^-$. It is trained with the
gradient mask of \cref{rem:two-copy-mask} and contracted in the same half-sum and half-difference form
(\path{ttn.network.sum_difference_cores}). Why the centred model is the main construction and the two-copy
model a cross-check: \path{notes/model.md} and \path{notes/decisions.md}.

\paragraph{Recording, kernels and Hessians.}
Diagnostics are recorded at $t=0$ and on a logarithmic time grid, by default 200 times from
$10^{-3}\min(\tau_0,\tau_\Loss,\tmax)$ to $\tmax$, where $\tau_0=\norm{\params}/\norm{\dot\params}$ and
$\tau_\Loss=\Loss/\abs{\dot\Loss}$ at $t=0$ (other grids where a subsection says so), plus the fine rows of
\cref{sec:setup-dynamics} (\path{Recorder} in \path{ttn/dynamics.py}), and merged for events
(\path{ttn.dynamics.merged_rows}). Hessians are computed by automatic differentiation at plateau midpoints
(minimum parameter speed between two events), raw and with the gauge directions projected out
(\path{ttn.diagnostics.project_out_gauge}): the projection keeps the left singular vectors of the
unnormalized generator matrix with singular values above $10^{-3}$ times the largest. An earlier cutoff of
$10^{-10}$ also normalized the tiny generators of nearly unused bond channels, removed part of the escape
direction and biased $\lambda_{\min}$ near rank-deficient saddles; all Hessians reported were recomputed
with the fixed cutoff, and the raw $\lambda_{\min}$ is reported too (\cref{app:exp-locality}). The saddle type is
read from how $\lambda_{\min}$ scales with $\isc$; the single-$\isc$ fallback (strict if
$\lambda_{\min}(\Hperp)<-10^{-3}\max_j\abs{\lambda_j(\Hperp)}$) calls $k=3$ plateaus strict for $\isc\gtrsim0.01$--$0.04$
and is not used for claims. The relative kernel change uses the probe kernel (in the regime map, 128
Gaussian inputs from generator seed 12345; on real data, the first 128 test images) and, where the dense
Jacobian fits in memory ($d^\Ns\dim\params\le2\cdot10^7$), the tensor-space kernel $\Jac\Pre\Jac\T$, with
$\boldsymbol\lr$ the per-parameter multipliers, set to zero where a gradient mask applies.

\paragraph{Event rules.}
\Cref{tab:exp-common} gives the rule of every experiment. For CP teachers whose components are orthogonal
in the metric of the loss, event $r$ is dated by a crossing of $\ov r=\frac12$. The \emph{final-rise} rule
(recommended) takes the last upward crossing before $\ov r$ first reaches $0.9$ (a component that never reaches
$0.9$ has no event); the \emph{persistent} rule takes the last upward crossing after which $\ov r$ stays at least
$\frac12$ to the end of the record (none if $\ov r$ ends below $\frac12$); the \emph{first-crossing} rule takes the
first upward crossing. The persistent rule differs from the final-rise rule only when a learned component
later dips below $\frac12$ (star teachers, gradient noise, rotations to a better component, a few deep-tree
runs): it then dates the relearning, or gives no event. The first-crossing rule differs only when a transient
crosses $\frac12$ before the final rise (non-learned overlaps rise transiently while another component is
learned, up to $0.41$ on binary4). For other teachers one escape can raise several overlaps and later escapes
none, so event $(e,n)$ comes from the bond spectra. The \emph{onset} rule (recommended) takes the first time
$\bs en$ reaches $f\,\bst en$ with $f=0.05$ (sensitivity $0.03$, $0.1$), without width: a half-value rule can skip
intermediate saddles whose spectra stay below the final ones (\cref{cor:A-second-escape}). The
\emph{half-teacher} rule takes a crossing of half the teacher's value. The \emph{library} rule
(\path{ttn.diagnostics.spectral_events}) takes the last upward crossing of half the run's final value and then
merges consecutive events whose gap is at most the larger width into one, with their mean time and largest
width. Widths: the \emph{final rise} runs from the last upward crossing of $0.1$ before the first reach of $0.9$
to that reach, within the rise that contains the event; the \emph{first rise} (\path{ttn.diagnostics.rise_times})
is the same for the first rise to $0.9$, which belongs to another rise than a persistent event after a dip (no
reported number depends on it: no gradient-flow reference of \cref{app:exp-optim} dips, and the 45 dipping
optimizer traces keep their labels and median ratios with final-rise widths). Crossings are interpolated on the merged log and fine
rows or found by root finding on dense output; an event narrower than three sample intervals is
\emph{unresolved}, and separation ratios involving it are lower bounds. Code per experiment:
\path{notes/diagnostics.md}.

\paragraph{Regime labels and run classes.}
The labels of \cref{tab:regimes} follow \path{notes/diagnostics.md} and are computed by
\path{classify_regime} in \path{ttn/diagnostics.py}, which adds two labels: \emph{lazy (projected fit)}, checked
before stalled, for $\dTK(T)<0.1$ and a final loss within $10\%$ of that of the linearized fit (positive when a
bond is deficient), and \emph{undetermined} when every $\sep{i}\le3$ involves an unresolved event.
A run is \emph{stalled} when its loss is still above $10^{-3}\Loss(0)$ when the budget ends,
and the subclass names the cause: \emph{dead route} (an end of the route the next component needs is at
roundoff, the conservation-law trap of \cref{prop:A-trap} being the proposed mechanism), \emph{slow} (every
route is live, but the plateau outlasts the budget) or \emph{floor} (a centred target that the bond ranks
cannot represent). The experiments test the subclass as follows. s2s-core: a never-learned component's
route-end slice below $10^{-8}\isc$ (dead route), otherwise slow; runs stopped by the wall clock or the
amplitude stop are \emph{unresolved}. Locality: every gradient entry below $10^{-12}$ for 5,000 evaluations
flags a stall, and a rerun with 60 times the patience found a dead route in all 37 flagged runs it
covered. Order: a component slice below $10^{-14}$ times the largest core norm (dead route). Regime map: the
$\ncomp$-th bond singular value of $\W$ at the end, minimized over edges, at roundoff (dead route), between
$10^{-12}$ and $10^{-4}$, or above (\path{analysis.stall_type}; most centred runs recorded only the spectra of
$\W-\Wz$ and are not typed); centred runs with a deficient bond end at a floor (\cref{app:boundaries}).
Steps and noise: no persistent event within $4T_{\rm GF}$ on the aligned route (the route amplitude decays,
the SGD trap of \cref{prop:A-sgd-collapse}). Optimizers: runs that hit their budget are reported as
budget-limited (slow), and route ends at roundoff separately. Runs whose subclass is not determined are
reported as stalled.

\paragraph{Compute.}
Each remote experiment ran on one rented CPU machine with 32 to 384 virtual cores, as many
single-threaded processes in parallel; some fix rounds ran on the same machines (machines, hours and
costs in \path{notes/spend.md}). Pilots, reviews, the fix rounds of the regime-map and real-data
experiments, and the pre-registered order test of \cref{sec:real-data} ran on a local workstation. No GPU
was used.

\subsection{Saddle-to-saddle experiments}\label{app:exp-s2s}
Settings of \cref{sec:s2s-phenomenology,sec:s2s-aligned,sec:s2s-escape} and of the odeco runs of
\cref{sec:s2s-failure} (experiment s2s-core; the locality experiment is in \cref{app:exp-locality}).
Experiment folder \path{experiments/s2s_core/} (README with every command in order; YAML configurations
in \path{configs/}); findings in \path{notes/s2s_core.md}; reviews in \path{notes/reviews/s2s_core_round1.md}
and \path{notes/reviews/s2s_core_round2_verification.md}, whose errata E1--E21 are applied in \cref{sec:s2s}.
Numbers that are not single entries of a results table are computed by \path{report_numbers.py} into
\path{results/report_numbers.csv}.
The order experiment of \cref{sec:s2s-order} is described in \cref{app:exp-order}.

\paragraph{Problems.}
Whitened Gaussian inputs, exact population loss, double precision. Odeco teachers
(\path{ttn.teachers.odeco_teacher}) with $\ncomp=3$, $d=4$ at every site and Haar-random site bases from
teacher seed 1000, a stream independent of the initialization seeds; weights $(3,2,1)$ (separated) and
$(1,0.85,0.7)$ (close). Trees binary4, MPS4, binary8, HT4 and MPS8 ($\Kc=3,4,7,7,8$); nominal bond
dimension $\chi=3$ or $6$, capped by \eqref{eq:bond-cap} (at $\chi=6$: MPS4 bonds $(4,6,4)$, MPS8 end bonds
and HT4 leaf bonds $4$, all other bonds $6$). Initializations: fan-in \eqref{eq:init} with seeds $0$--$9$;
\emph{balanced}, the same $\Wz$ re-gauged to $\Q e=0$ on every bond (\path{s2s.gauge_balance}); \emph{aligned},
the same per-core amplitude on every core and component (\path{ttn.network.aligned_init}). The
leading-order sweeps also use MPS, binary and HT trees with up to 16 sites (\cref{tab:exp-s2s}).

\paragraph{Dynamics.}
Gradient flow by SciPy's LSODA (\path{ttn.dynamics.integrate_gf}) with the exact Hessian as Jacobian,
instead of the default integrator of \cref{sec:setup-dynamics}: past the first escape the flow is stiff,
and an explicit method needs a number of steps $\propto\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}$ per plateau (Radau, tried on one
$\Kc=7$ run, did not finish in 25 minutes). Tolerances: relative $10^{-8}$ and absolute $10^{-11}\isc$
(sweeps \texttt{main}, \texttt{first}, \texttt{balanced}), or $10^{-6}$ and $10^{-9}\isc$ (\texttt{large},
\texttt{k78}, whose plateaus last up to $10^{7}$); on binary8 at $\chi=6$, $\isc=0.07$, the looser tolerances
move the event times by $7\cdot10^{-7}$ relative. Aligned runs: Dormand--Prince 5(4) and LSODA with
tolerances $10^{-10}$ and $10^{-14}$. Events and widths follow the final-rise rule (\cref{tab:exp-common}), both
refined by re-integrating the event window on linear grids until the rise holds at least 40 samples
(\path{s2s.zoom_rise}); no duration is read off the
logarithmic output grid. The plateau midpoint is the time of minimum parameter speed between two events;
the parameters there are saved, and the Hessian, raw and gauge-projected
(\path{ttn.diagnostics.project_out_gauge}), was recomputed from them after the library's projection fix
(\path{recompute_hessians.py}). Bond spectra are recorded on the output grid and, in all but the first 332
complete runs of \texttt{main}, in the event windows. The conservation drift (per integration chunk, as in
\eqref{eq:drift}) has median $9\cdot10^{-10}$ and exceeds $10^{-6}$ in 11 of 1,576 runs (at most
$3.3\cdot10^{-6}$; ten MPS8 runs and one binary8 run).

\paragraph{Leading-order flow.}
$\mathcal T(Z)$ and $r_1$ (\cref{cor:A-blowup,rem:A-recipe}) come from \path{s2s.correlation_flow_blowup}: the flow
$\dot\params=\nabla g$ from the initialization at $\isc=1$, by DOP853 with relative tolerance $10^{-10}$,
integrated in segments until the second-largest component amplitude $\abs{\ip{\W}{\U r}}$ is below $10^{-6}$
of the largest and no longer growing relative to it, with a cap at amplitude $10^{30}$; the remaining time
of a balanced mode from the stop is added. The 40 binary4 close-teacher jobs and 2 separated binary4 jobs
at $\chi=6$ stopped at the cap with the direction still converging; $\mathcal T$ is converged regardless (the time
left beyond amplitude $A$ is $\propto A^{-(\Kc-2)/\Kc}$), and an independent integration of the spherical
flow agrees to $1.5\cdot10^{-11}$ (binary4) and $1.7\cdot10^{-10}$ (MPS4) relative on all 160 binary4 and
MPS4 jobs (verification review). Round-1 values of $\mathcal T$, from a stop at amplitude $10^6$, were biased by up to
$2.2\cdot10^{-3}$ relative (binary4, close teacher) and are superseded.

\paragraph{Budgets and run classes.}
Runs stop at $\Loss\le10^{-9}\Loss(0)$; at $t_{\max}$, $10^3$ times the RMS-overlap escape time of the weakest
component; at a wall-clock limit ($300$\,s in \texttt{main}, $400$\,s in \texttt{balanced}, $2400$\,s in
\texttt{large} and \texttt{k78}, so the number of unresolved runs depends on the machine) or a step limit per
output chunk; and, except in the first ${\sim}310$ runs of \texttt{main}, when a component never learned has a
route-end slice below $10^{-8}\isc$ for a whole output chunk (a dead route; the rest of the run carries no
information). \texttt{main} also stopped when every dormant amplitude $\abs{\ip{\W}{\U r}}$ stayed below $10^{-15}$
for a chunk; at $\Kc=7,8$ this was too strict (the amplitude is a product of $\Kc$ per-core amplitudes and can be
$10^{-15}$ while each of them is $10^{-3}$), so those runs are classed unresolved unless a slice is dead, and
the cells were rerun without it in \texttt{k78}. Classes (\path{analyze_main.py}): \emph{complete} (all three
components learned); \emph{dead route} (\texttt{trapped} in the tables: a component never learned has a
route-end slice below $10^{-8}\isc$ after the first escape); \emph{slow} (\texttt{stalled}: the time budget ended
with every such slice above it); \emph{unresolved} (wall-clock limit or the amplitude stop). The classes do not
depend on the tolerance: the 9 runs of the tolerance check keep their class and order, and their event times
change by at most $3.6\cdot10^{-6}$ relative. At $\Kc=7,8$ the dead-route fractions are lower bounds, since some
slow runs end with slices still decaying (\path{slice_trends.py}).

\paragraph{Analysis.}
Exponents are fitted per seed, $\ln$ of the time against $\ln\isc$ with at least four values of $\isc$, on run
sets classified by the saddle sequence (\path{analyze_main.fit_sets}): $\tev1$ on the runs whose first
component is the seed's most frequent one; $\tev2-\tev1$ on the runs whose first two components are the
seed's most frequent pair, with $\sep1>3$; $\tev3-\tev2$ on complete runs in the seed's most frequent order
with $\sep1,\sep2>3$. The Hessian slopes use the same sets. Each cell comes from one sweep (\texttt{large} at
$\Kc=7,8$ with $\chi=6$, \texttt{main} otherwise); sweeps with different tolerances are never pooled. The cut
$\sep i>3$ matters only for binary4 with the close teacher, where it leaves some seeds with four or five
values of $\isc$ (about $0.8$ decade); without it, overlapping and wrong-order runs are mixed in and the
second gap gives $0.92\pm0.11$ at $\chi=3$ and $1.12\pm0.05$ at $\chi=6$ (verification review, E9).
First-escape exponents are also given with the offset $\delta_\Kc\tw{r_1}^{-(2-2/\Kc)}$ of
\eqref{eq:s2s-first} subtracted from $\tev1$.

\paragraph{Continued runs.}
\path{continue_stalled.py} integrates a slow or unresolved run further from its saved final state, with the
escape time resolved like every event. Three samples: the 8 slow \texttt{k78} runs with the shortest predicted
remaining plateau (3 seeds); a seeded random sample (\texttt{-{}-seed 1}) of 8 of the 74 \texttt{k78} runs (73
slow, 1 dead route) whose estimate is at most 100 times the time already integrated, one of them also in the
first sample; and 8 MPS8 runs of \texttt{large}, spread over the ratio of estimate to elapsed time
($0.2$--$420$). The budget multiplier of the third sample was not recorded, and the README command selects a
different set (verification review, E13); its results are reported as measured.

\paragraph{Excluded and superseded runs.}
The scipy pilots made before the library existed, part of them before the teacher and initialization streams
were separated, support no claim. Superseded after review round 1: $\mathcal T$ from the amplitude-$10^6$ stop (kept as
\texttt{T\_v1} in the raw records), gap fits on pooled run sets, Hessians with the earlier gauge projection, and
decay rates fitted into the floating-point floor (now fitted above $10^{-9}$ absolute). No run was dropped:
runs that do not complete are reported in their class. The raw records (3,417 files, about 220\,MB; local copy
\path{data/s2s_core/}) are not in the repository; \cref{app:reproducibility} says how to regenerate them.

Settings of \cref{sec:s2s-locality,sec:s2s-failure}. Experiment folder \path{experiments/locality/}

\paragraph{Problems.}
Whitened Gaussian inputs, exact population loss, double precision, the library model
(\path{ttn.dynamics.Problem}). Pattern teachers (\path{ttn.teachers.pattern_teacher}) with $d=2$ and
random orthonormal site bases, teacher seed 1000, from a stream independent of the initialization
seeds. Two-component teachers have patterns $0^\Ns$ and the indicator of $D$, weights $(1,0.5)$, also
$(1,0.25)$, $(1,0.75)$ and $(2,1)$, on 60 (tree, $D$) pairs. Multi-component teachers (patterns as
site values, weights in brackets): TA $0000$ ($1$), $1100$ ($0.6$), $1110$ ($0.35$); TB as TA plus
$1111$ ($0.2$); N4 $0000$ ($1$), $1100$ ($0.6$), $1111$ ($0.4$); B4 $0000$ ($1$), $0110$ ($0.3$), $1001$

$\chi=2$, except $\chi=3$ for B4, N4 and E8, and $\chi\in\{4,8\}$, $d=4$ in the robustness sweep. Trees:
MPS4, MPS6, MPS8 ($\Kc=4,6,8$), binary4, binary8 ($\Kc=3,7$), HT4, HT8 ($\Kc=7,15$), star3 ($\Kc=4$: a
root without sites and three children carrying sites $\{0,1\}$, $\{2,3\}$, $\{4,5\}$) and midroot6

\paragraph{Dynamics.}
Protocols: \emph{small}, fan-in initialization \eqref{eq:init}; \emph{saddle}, the exact balanced saddle $\tw1\U1$
(\path{ttn.network.aligned_init}) plus a fan-in perturbation of per-core scale $\isc$;
\emph{balanced}, the fan-in $\Wz$ re-gauged so that $\Q e=0$ on every bond. Gradient flow by SciPy's
LSODA (\path{solve_ivp}) with the exact Hessian as Jacobian, relative and absolute tolerances $10^{-9}$
and $10^{-14}$, instead of the default integrator of \cref{sec:setup-dynamics}; Radau as fallback
(fewer than $1\%$ of runs). Event times are located by root finding on the dense output (persistent rule,
\cref{tab:exp-common}); a run with an overlap that crosses $\frac12$ more than once is a \emph{rotation}.
Budgets: $t_{\max}=10^{14}$ and a wall-clock limit of $1500$\,s per run ($900$\,s for the $k\ge6$ rows of the
main sweep, $2400$\,s in the $k\ge6$ extension and the stall diagnosis). A run is flagged \emph{stalled} when,
after an overlap has passed $\frac12$, every gradient entry stays below $10^{-12}$ for more than 5,000
consecutive evaluations (300,000 in the stall diagnosis, which also keeps the trajectory); the stall
diagnosis found a dead route in every flagged run it covered. The Hessian is computed by automatic differentiation at the plateau
midpoint (minimum gradient norm between consecutive events), raw and with the gauge directions
projected out (\path{ttn.diagnostics.project_out_gauge}, relative cutoff $10^{-3}$). An earlier cutoff,
$10^{-10}$, kept the generators of nearly unused bond channels, whose normalized direction overlaps the
escape route, and biased the $k=2$ values to $-0.44$ to $-0.46$ instead of $-0.5000$; all Hessians were
recomputed (\path{hess_recompute.py}). The fixed projection is still biased on the short
small-initialization $k=2$ plateaus at $\isc\ge10^{-3}$ ($-0.411$ and $-0.502$ against raw $-0.437$ and
$-0.581$), so raw values are quoted there; raw and projected $\isc$-exponents of $\lambda_{\min}$ differ by
up to $0.11$.

\paragraph{Analysis.}
Run classes: greedy, other order, rotation, stalled (flagged as above; a dead route wherever the stall
diagnosis checked), timeout (wall-clock limit: slow or undetermined), incomplete, error. Fits as in
\cref{sec:s2s-locality} (per seed, at least three $\isc$; rotations excluded; the log law is fitted on
the residuals of $\ln\Delta t_2$, like the power law). Several components: plateaus detected by
\path{ttn.diagnostics.find_plateaus} on $\Loss(t)$ (linear time, separation ratio above $3$), matched to
a predicted level within $5\%$; laws of levels too short to detect from the time spent within $2\%$ of
the level. Bond events: effective ranks just after the event ($\ov2=0.9$) against the plateau midpoint,
threshold $0.05$ times the teacher's singular value ($0.03$, $0.1$ as sensitivity); rank profiles of
several components in the middle of the time spent within $2\%$ of each predicted level. Growth
exponent: least squares of $\ln\dot{\ov2}$ against $\ln\ov2$ over $\ov2\in[10^{-4},10^{-2}]$.

\paragraph{Checks.}
The conservation drift $\max_e\normmax{\Q e(T)-\Q e(0)}$ is at most $10^{-6}$ in every run ($99.9\%$
below $3.5\cdot10^{-8}$, median $1.2\cdot10^{-9}$), against Gram entries of order one after learning.
Relative to the initial imbalance $\abs{\Q e(0)}\sim\isc^2$ it is at most $1.6\cdot10^{-4}$ in every run
with $k\ge4$, where the imbalance matters (\cref{sec:s2s-failure}), but up to $0.5$ for $k\le3$ at
$\isc\le10^{-3}$, where $\isc^2$ is below the absolute tolerance; those runs never stall, and their gaps
agree with an independent integration to $10^{-7}$--$10^{-9}$. LSODA (tolerances $10^{-9}$ and
$10^{-10}$), Radau and RK45 agree on a $k=6$ gap to $3\cdot10^{-8}$. A reviewer's independent code (dense
contraction, automatic differentiation, Radau at relative tolerance $10^{-10}$) reproduced the gaps of
11 rows to $10^{-7}$--$10^{-9}$, the gauge-projected $\lambda_{\min}$ to $7\cdot10^{-11}$ and the Steiner
counts of all 60 (tree, $D$) pairs.

\subsection{Order of learning}\label{app:exp-order}
Settings of \cref{sec:s2s-order}. Experiment folder \path{experiments/order/} (README with every command and
the run order; one YAML configuration per sweep in \path{configs/}); findings in \path{notes/order.md}, whose
sections 0 and 6--11 were revised after the review \path{notes/reviews/order_round1.md} (section 11 lists the
changed claims); verification of the revision in \path{notes/reviews/order_round2_verification.md}, whose errata
E1--E10 are applied in \cref{sec:s2s-order}; running report \path{agent-runs/order/REPORT.md}.

\paragraph{Problems.}
Whitened Gaussian inputs, exact population loss, double precision. Teachers with weights $(1,w)$, or
$(1,w,\dots,w^{\ncomp-1})$ with $\ncomp=3,4$: odeco, or pattern teachers whose first component is the pattern
$0^\Ns$ and whose second differs from it on the sites $D$ (MPS6: $D=\{0,5\}$, $\{2,3\}$,
$\{0,1,2\}$, $\{0,1,2,3\}$; MPS4: $\{0,2\}$, $\{1,2\}$). All teachers use the standard site basis: the fan-in
initialization is invariant in law under an orthogonal rotation of any physical leg, so this changes no order
statistic, and no teacher randomness enters. Trees binary4, MPS4, MPS6, binary8 and MPS8 ($\Kc=3,4,6,7,8$);
$d\in\{2,4\}$; nominal $\chi\in\{2,4,8\}$ capped by \eqref{eq:bond-cap} (28 distinct settings), or uncapped
$\chi=1$--$32$. Fan-in initialization \eqref{eq:init} with seeds $0,\dots,n-1$, the same for every $w$ and
$\isc$; the $d$ test uses the disjoint ranges from $200000$ ($d=2$) and $300000$ ($d=4$), the exchangeability check
the range from $100000$.

\paragraph{Dynamics.}
Events follow the first-crossing rule (\cref{tab:exp-common}). \emph{First events} (\path{sweep.py}): gradient flow by SciPy's RK45
(Dormand--Prince 5(4)) with relative and absolute tolerances $10^{-9}$ and $10^{-14}$, stopped by event location at
the first crossing of $\ov r=\frac12$ by any component; 1,105 MPS8 runs of the main sweep that reached the float64 resolution of $t$ were re-integrated
with a restarted clock (\path{repair.py}; no winner changed). \emph{Leading-order race}
(\path{common.lo_blowup}): for each component $r$, the flow of $\ip{\W}{\U r}$ from the initialization at $\isc=1$
until $\norm{\params}=10^4\norm{\params_0}$, plus the radial remainder of the blow-up time; the predicted winner is
$\arg\min_r\hat t_{1,r}/\tw r$. \emph{Full runs} (\path{h1b.py}): LSODA with the exact Hessian as Jacobian,
tolerances $10^{-10}$ and $10^{-15}$, dense output, event times by root finding on it and a fine record window
around the events; a run stops below $10^{-10}\Loss(0)$, at $\tmax=10^9$ ($10^{10}$ for the MPS6 and binary8 saddle
starts, $10^{12}$ for the natural MPS4 runs), or when some component's slice of some core falls to $10^{-14}$ times
the largest core norm (\emph{stalled}, a dead route). The threshold is justified empirically: in the 22 runs without
any stall stop, every learned component kept a slice ratio of at least $6.7\cdot10^{-6}$ before its event, while 25
of the 29 unlearned ones fell below $10^{-14}$ (17 to an exact zero by underflow), so the rule had no false
positive; the slices keep decaying rather than stopping at a roundoff floor (verification review; this margin, the
fits over the three smallest $\isc$ and the McNemar test quoted in \cref{sec:s2s-order} come from its scripts in
\path{notes/reviews/scripts/verify_order/}). The Hessian is computed at the
plateau midpoint (minimum parameter speed between the two events), raw and gauge-projected
(\path{ttn.diagnostics}, relative cutoff $10^{-3}$); at $\isc=10^{-2}$ the projection is biased for the adjacent MPS4
pair ($-0.58$ to $-0.80$ against the raw $-0.71$ to $-0.95$), so raw values are quoted. In the natural adjacent runs
57 of 150 wrong plateaus are near-ties without a resolved midpoint; no claim uses their Hessians.
\emph{Several components} (\path{multi.py}): LSODA with the exact Hessian, tolerances $10^{-9}$ and $10^{-14}$, exact
location of every first crossing; a run stops below $10^{-8}\Loss(0)$ (converged), at $t=10^9$ (unfinished: slow or
undetermined) or by the slice criterion (stalled, a dead route). The round-1 run \texttt{multi} (raw folder \texttt{multi2}) stopped instead when an
overlap fell below $10^{-25}$, which can also fire while a component is forgotten, and flagged runs with an
overlap below $10^{-14}$ as seeded by roundoff, which wrongly excluded 18--19 binary8 runs per $w$. The
three-component rerun \texttt{multi3} reproduces it run for run (same status and event times) and changes only
that flag; \texttt{multi} is used only for the four-component counts, which \texttt{multi\_nostop} checks without any
stall stop.

Experiment folder \path{experiments/regime_map/} (README with the run order; YAML configurations in

\paragraph{Problems.}
Whitened Gaussian inputs, exact population loss, double precision, the library model
(\path{ttn.dynamics.Problem}). Odeco teachers with weights $(3,2,1)$ at $d=3$ or $(3,2)$ at $d=2$ (round 2: $(3,2)$
at $d=2$ and $3$), Haar-random
site bases from teacher seed $1000+s$ for initialization seed $s$ (an independent stream); fan-in initialization
\eqref{eq:init}. Bond dimensions: binary4 uncapped ($\chi=3$--$128$); the other trees capped by
\eqref{eq:bond-cap}, except the variant of the binary tree with $\Ns=8$ with bonds $8$ above two sites and $32$

\paragraph{Dynamics.}

$10^{-12}$, or LSODA with the exact Hessian as Jacobian for long plateaus (the $\isc\times\Kc$ sweeps, the stall test,
and $\isc<0.7$ in the sweeps of the binary tree with $\Ns=8$). A run stops when $\Loss<10^{-6}\cdot\frac12\normF{\Wt}^2$, at the time budget
$T=50\,t_{\rm esc}+10^4/\max(\bar\lambda,1)$, with $t_{\rm esc}=(0.3\isc)^{-(\Kc-2)}/((\Kc-2)\tw{\min})$ the
aligned escape time of the weakest component and $\bar\lambda=\tr\TK(0)/d^\Ns$, or at a step limit ($10^5$
steps by default; $3\cdot10^4$ or $2\cdot10^4$ in the $\Ns=8$ and $\isc\times\Kc$ sweeps, $10^4$ for $\Kc\ge10$).
Each final rise is re-integrated from the saved parameters on a 200-point linear grid
(\path{run_regime.refine_events}); events and widths follow the final-rise rule (\cref{tab:exp-common}). Runs written
before this rule are relabelled from their recorded log and fine rows (\path{analysis.relabel_last_crossing}). Deep in the
saddle-to-saddle regime at $\Kc\ge7$ the widths are not resolved; separation ratios there are lower bounds, and
no label depends on them.

\paragraph{Problems.}
Exact population loss, double precision, fan-in initialization \eqref{eq:init}; teacher seeds from a
stream independent of the initialization seeds. Site covariances \path{ttn.losses.power_law_cov}$(d,\beta)$
(eigenvalues $\propto n^{-\beta}$, $n=1,\dots,d$, trace $d$), rotated by an angle in the $(e_1,e_2)$ plane where misaligned.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Aligned covariance} ($\Ns=4$, $d=\chi=4$): odeco teachers on covariance eigenvectors, T1 $(1,0.8)$
    and T2 $(1,0.6)$ with the smaller component on $e_1$, $\beta=0.25$; one-component teachers on $e_1$ or $e_4$
    with $\beta=0$--$1.5$ ($\Lambda=0.008$--$33$); three components $(1,0.7,0.5)$ for the exact-clock check. Variants
    from the same initialization: $(\Sig,\tw{})$, $(\Id,\tw{})$, $(\Id,\Lambda\tw{})$, $(\Id,\Lambda^{1/2}\tw{})$, and $(\Id,\Lambda^{1/2}\tw{})$
    from the mapped initialization (physical legs multiplied by $\Sigi i^{1/2}$).
  \item \emph{Round 1, generic teachers} ($\Ns=4$, $d=\chi=4$): misaligned covariance with an odeco teacher on
    $e_1$, $e_2$ (weights $(1,0.8)$), $\beta\in\{0.5,1\}$, angles $0$, $10$, $20$, $30$, $45^\circ$, also as the absorbed
    white problem from the mapped initialization; non-orthogonal CP teachers $\fac1i=e_1$,
    $\fac2i=\cos\phi\,e_1+\sin\phi\,e_2$ (overlap $\cos\phi$), weights $(1,\tw2)$; correlated inputs (one-factor model,
    odeco teacher on $e_1$, $e_2$, weights $(1,0.7)$, correlation $c=0$--$0.5$), with
    the dense second-moment matrix from Isserlis' theorem (\path{correlated.py}, checked against Monte
    Carlo and the closed form for $\Ns=2$).
  \item \emph{Random TTN teachers}: MPS4 teachers with $d=3$, bond ranks $(2,3,2)$, the $n$-th index of each bond
    weighted by $0.5^n$, $\normF{\Wt}=1$, teacher seeds 101--105, MPS4 student with $\chi=4$; cross-tree: MPS8 teachers
    ($d=2$, bond ranks 2, index weights $0.6^n$, $\normF{\Wt}=1$) on a binary8 student ($\chi=4$), teacher seeds 201--203,
    then 203, 204, 209, 212, 218, and 491, 548, 643, 694, 846 (the five of seeds 200--999 whose root cut
    exceeds every bottom bond by the largest margins); binary8 teachers (seeds 201--203) on an MPS8 student.
  \item \emph{Round 2, generic teachers} ($d=2$, $\chi=4$, \cref{tab:dt-saddle}): CP overlap $\cos\phi=0.2$, $0.45$
    ($\Ns=4$) and $0.45$, $0.7$ ($\Ns=8$), weights $(1,0.7)$; misaligned by $20^\circ$ ($\beta=1$ at $\Ns=4$, $\beta=2$ at

  \item \emph{Stars} ($d=2$, $\chi=3$): $q=0000$ (weight 2), $r=1010$, $r'=1001$ (weights $\tw r=\tw{r'}$); the binary8

\paragraph{Dynamics.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Round-1 runs at a single $\isc$ (and the aligned-covariance sweeps): SciPy DOP853 on the flat
    parameter vector, tolerances $10^{-9}$ and $10^{-14}$, 2000 logarithmic output times; checked against the

  \item $\isc$ sweeps: \path{ttn.dynamics.integrate_gf} (LSODA, tolerances $10^{-9}$ and $10^{-14}$) in two stages:
    until $\Loss<0.98\Loss(0)$, then on 3000 times geometric in $t-t_A$, with the recorder's fine rows; stopped
    at $\Loss<10^{-6}$--$10^{-4}\Loss(0)$ or at $\tmax$ (up to $10^{10}$).
  \item Saddle start: the lowest-loss bond-rank-one critical point (L-BFGS from 12 starts on the

    per-edge rates $\lambda_e$ (\cref{prop:A-mps-rates}) from the Hessian block of the two cores of the edge.
  \item Correlated inputs: SciPy LSODA with the exact Hessian as Jacobian, on the dense loss, in the two

\paragraph{Events and analysis.}
Onset and half-teacher rules of \cref{tab:exp-common}, as follows.
\begin{itemize}
  \item Random initialization: $t_1$ is the first time $\Loss\le\frac12(\Loss(0)+L_1)$, $L_1$ the level of the
    rank-one critical point visited (matched within $5\%$ at the point of minimum speed; runs with no such
    point are \emph{unresolved} and left out); $\Delta t_2$ is the first onset event after $t_1$ minus $t_1$.
    Onset event: $\bs e2\ge f\,\bst e2$ on some edge, $f\in\{0.03,0.05,0.1\}$ (and, from the saddle, at least 10

    (departure) or $\ov q<0.9$ before any $\ov r>\frac12$; the two rules agree for every run with $\varepsilon\le10^{-3}$.

    changing the window moves slopes by $1$--$3\%$. Increments per halving: per-seed regression of
    $\Delta t_2$ on $\log_2(1/\isc)$ with the actual $\isc$.
  \item Rank-one predictions: \path{constrained.py} (L-BFGS on the bond-rank-constrained loss from many
    starts) and \path{predict_rank1.py}; slopes $h_e$ (\cref{cor:A-second-escape}) from \path{theory_log_slope.py} and

\paragraph{Events and run classes.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Overlap events, in the GD, gradient-flow and SGD sweeps and the route test: persistent rule with
    first-rise widths (\cref{tab:exp-common}; \path{common.record_events}, or the integrator's event finder for the
    DOP853 references); first crossings and the number of crossings are kept to count transients. The
    strict-route scripts (\path{collapse_test.py}, \path{check_k2_saddle.py}) use the first-crossing rule on the logged trajectory. No
    experiment of this section uses spectral events: bond spectra enter only through effective bond ranks
    (singular values above $0.1$ times the smallest teacher weight, at checkpoints and at the end) and, in
    the route test, through the second Schmidt value as the route amplitude.
  \item GD outcome classes, checked in this order (\path{analyze_gd.py}): \emph{diverged}; \emph{GF-like}: final
    loss at most $1.05\Loss_{\rm GF}(T)+10^{-3}\Loss_0$, with $\Loss_{\rm GF}(T)$ the reference at the same time, and no
    step raising the loss by more than $10^{-3}\Loss_0$; \emph{catapult}: the same final loss after such a
    rise; \emph{thrown back to the origin}: not at that level and the loss back above $0.9\Loss_0$ after the
    first event; \emph{bounded oscillation}: over the last $20\%$ of the steps (at most 2000) the total
    variation of the loss exceeds ten times its net change and the tail reaches above gradient flow's
    level, or the end point is linearly unstable ($\lr\lambda_{\max}/2>1.001$); \emph{other}: none of these (it
    did not occur in the runs shown). GF-like includes 39 of the 195 small-initialization runs that end,
    like their references, on the last plateau ($\Loss/\Loss_0=0.071$).
  \item SGD: the first escape is the first time any component reaches $\frac12$, compared with gradient
    flow's; a run \emph{forgot} if its first-learned component later falls below $\frac12$. Runs are
    classified before pooling; means $\pm$ s.e.\ are across initializations. Second plateaus use only
    initializations with a resolved gradient-flow plateau ($\sep1>3$) and no near-tie (the eventually
    second component does not peak between $0.15$ and $0.6$ during the first event), and only runs that
    kept the first component and gradient flow's order. Spread exponents: per initialization, least
    squares of $\ln{\rm sd}(\tev1)$ against $\ln(\lr/\batch)$. Collapse law: weighted least squares (weights
    $1/{\rm s.e.}^2$) of $T_{\rm SGD}/T_{\rm GF}$ over the 19 conditions in which every run escaped, one $c_{\rm col}$
    per law; \emph{stalled}: no persistent event by $4T_{\rm GF}$ (the route amplitude decays, \cref{prop:A-sgd-collapse};
    called trapped in the code).
  \item Finite samples: test error $\normF{\W-\Wt}^2/\normF{\Wt}^2$; \emph{recovered}: test error below
    $10^{-2}$ (thresholds $10^{-3}$ and $10^{-1}$ give the same picture); effective bond rank: singular values
    above $0.2$, a tenth of the smallest teacher weight, averaged over the edges; the lazy limit depends
    only on the initial direction and is counted once per (setting, $\nsamp$, seed).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Problems.}
Whitened Gaussian inputs, exact population loss (empirical loss in the finite-sample sweep), double
precision. Teacher seed 100; initialization seeds 0--5, from a stream independent of the teacher's.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{binary4}: binary tree, $\Ns=4$ ($\Kc=3$), $d=3$, $\chi=4$; odeco teacher with weights
    $(3,2,1)$ and Haar-random site bases.
  \item \emph{binary8}, \emph{MPS8}: $\Ns=8$ ($\Kc=7$ and $8$), the same odeco teacher, $d=3$, $\chi=4$.

\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Initializations.}
Fan-in \eqref{eq:init} with per-core scale $\isc$; \emph{balanced}: the same $\Wz$ in the gauge with
$\Q e=0$ on every edge. All deep-tree baselines use the balanced version, because fan-in gradient flow
converges by $t=2\cdot10^6$ in none of the 12 MPS8 odeco runs (the conservation-law trap of
\cref{prop:A-trap}, verified at the route ends in 11 of the 12 stalls), against 10 of 12 (binary8
odeco), 11 of 12 (binary8 E8) and 11 of 12 (MPS8 E8) fan-in runs. Methods whose iterates depend on the
gauge were run from both versions; re-gauged GD, Riemannian GD, ALS and DMRG depend only on $\Wz$
and were run from the balanced one.

\paragraph{Dynamics.}
All methods stop at $\Loss<10^{-6}\Loss_0$ (finite samples: empirical loss below $10^{-8}$ times its
initial value) or at their budget.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Gradient flow}: SciPy LSODA with relative and absolute tolerances $10^{-9}$ and
    $10^{-14}$ and the exact Hessian as Jacobian (Radau as fallback), instead of the default
    integrator of \cref{sec:setup-dynamics}; the crossings $\ov r\in\{0.1,0.5,0.9\}$ are located by
    root finding, so event times are exact. Budgets: $t_{\max}=8/\isc$ (binary4), $2\cdot10^6$
    (deep trees), $4\cdot10^6$ (binary8 scan), $10^6$ (finite samples).
  \item \emph{GD and heavy ball} (PyTorch): $\lr=0.02$, and $0.002$ with momentum $0.9$, on binary4
    ($0.13\,\lr_c$ for both, counting heavy ball's effective step $\lr/(1-0.9)$); $\lr=0.01$ and
    $0.001$ on the deep trees ($0.23$--$0.27\,\lr_c$), at $\isc=0.8$ only, because gradient-flow
    plateaus at $\isc=0.3$ last $10^4$--$10^6$ time units. Budgets $8/\isc$ (binary4) and $2\cdot10^4$
    (deep) gradient-flow time units, at most $5\cdot10^5$ steps.
  \item \emph{Adam} (PyTorch, bias-corrected): moment decay rates $0.9$ and $0.999$ (the second also $0.99$ and
    $0.9$ at $\lr=10^{-3}$ on binary4), $\epsilon=10^{-8}$ ($10^{-30}$ in the $\epsilon$ sweep),
    $\lr\in\{10^{-4},3\cdot10^{-4},10^{-3},10^{-2}\}$ on binary4 and $\{10^{-3},10^{-2}\}$ on the deep
    trees. \emph{RMSProp}: decay rate $0.99$, $\epsilon=10^{-8}$, $\lr\in\{10^{-4},10^{-3}\}$. Budgets
    $10^5$--$3\cdot10^5$ (binary4), $3\cdot10^5$ (deep), $5\cdot10^5$ (binary8 scan) and $2\cdot10^5$
    ($\epsilon$ sweep) steps.
  \item \emph{Re-gauged GD}: $\lr=0.02$ (binary4) and $0.01$ (deep); QR sweeps from the leaves to
    the root bring the cores to the root-centred canonical form every 1 or 50 steps; $5\cdot10^4$,
    $10^5$ and $2\cdot10^5$ (binary8 scan) steps.
  \item \emph{Riemannian GD}: $\lr\in\{0.1,1\}$; tangent projection by least squares with the dense
    Jacobian of $\W(\params)$ (relative cutoff $10^{-12}$), so only small $d^\Ns$ are feasible;
    leaves-to-root HSVD retraction; 2000 steps.
  \item \emph{Gram-preconditioned GD}: $\lr\in\{0.01,0.1,0.5\}$; each Kronecker factor of a core's
    Hessian block is damped by $10^{-3}$ times its mean eigenvalue (scale-free) or, at $\lr=0.1$, by
    an absolute floor $10^{-4}$ or $10^{-2}$; no divergence guard, because the first step overshoots the
    loss before it returns (medians up to $4\cdot10^{34}\Loss_0$ on binary4, single runs up to $5\cdot10^{36}\Loss_0$,
    deep trees $10^{52}\Loss_0$); $5\cdot10^4$ (binary4; $2\cdot10^5$ with a floor) and $10^5$ (deep) steps.
  \item \emph{ALS} (one site) and \emph{DMRG} (two sites, then SVD truncation to at most $\chi$
    values with relative cutoff $10^{-12}$): exact least squares; the orthogonality centre moves by QR
    along a depth-first tour of the tree; at most 50 sweeps.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Events and run classes.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Overlap events by the persistent rule, with first-rise widths on sampled traces and final-rise widths
    for gradient flow; bond events by the half-teacher rule, the first crossing of half the teacher's value, per
    edge, without merging (\cref{tab:exp-common}); transient crossings are not events. Saddle-to-saddle: every
    separation ratio $\sep i>3$ (\cref{tab:regimes}).
  \item Discrete methods record loss and overlaps at every step for the first 2000 steps, then every
    $\max(1,n_{\rm steps}/(5\cdot10^4))$ steps. A first escape or gap shorter than two recording
    intervals is unresolved and excluded from fits (and counted in the result tables). Two components
    that cross $\frac12$ in the same step or micro-step have no resolved order. For Gram-preconditioned
    GD, events are defined only after the loss has returned below $\Loss_0$; a component already above
    $\frac12$ at that point has no resolved event.
  \item Exponents: per seed, a least-squares fit of $\ln T$ against $\ln\isc$ over the $\isc$ at which
    the seed follows its most common learning order (for plateau durations, not for times to reach a
    loss level); mean $\pm$ standard error over seeds. A logarithmic law fitted in the same space
    serves as comparison (gradient flow on binary4: root-mean-square residual of $\ln T$ $0.005$ for
    the power law, $0.62$ for the logarithmic law).
  \item Orders are compared with gradient flow only where both are resolved, for the same problem,
    $\isc$ and seed. Re-gauged GD, Riemannian GD, ALS and DMRG give one trajectory per seed at every
    $\isc$, so their orders are counted over the six independent seeds (at the smallest $\isc$).
  \item \emph{$\epsilon$ test} \eqref{eq:adam-eps}: $T_{\rm GF}(\isc)$ is the seed's exact
    gradient-flow first-escape time at $\isc=0.3$ (\texttt{deep} sweep, same balanced initial point
    up to scale) times $(0.3/\isc)^6$, and $n_{\rm sign}$ the median over $\isc$ of the same seed's
    escape with $\epsilon=10^{-30}$ at the same $\lr$. Runs without an escape within $2\cdot10^5$ steps
    enter only through the check that the prediction exceeds the budget.
  \item Convergence for cost and time-to-loss tables: $\Loss<10^{-3}\Loss_0$. Runs that hit their budget
    are reported as budget-limited (stalled, slow), not as dead routes. RMSProp hovers above $10^{-6}\Loss_0$ on
    the deep trees within $3\cdot10^5$ steps; its events are used, its convergence flags are not.
\end{itemize}

boot (9), chosen before any run for a visible shape difference at $4\times4$. Balanced subsamples of 4000
training and 1600 test images, data seed 0. Site features $\feat{}(x)=(\cos\frac{\pi x}2,\sin\frac{\pi x}2)$
(\emph{raw}), or per site $A_i\feat{}$ with $A_i\E[\feat{}\feat{}\T]A_i\T=\Id$ (\emph{white}; uncentred second moment,
eigenvalues floored at $10^{-3}$ of the largest), which keeps the function class. Model
$f(x)=\ip{\W}{\bigotimes_i\feat{}(x_i)}$ and loss $\frac1{2\nsamp}\sum_j(f(x_j)-y_j)^2$; in the regime sweep the
centred model trains $f-f_0$. Trees from \path{data.make_tree}: binary16 ($\Kc=15$) and MPS16 ($\Kc=16$),
binary64 ($\Kc=63$) and MPS64 ($\Kc=64$) for $8\times8$ images; $\chi=4$ ($8$ in one sweep, where the bottom bonds
are capped at 4 by their two-pixel cut). Pixel orders (\path{data.pixel_order}; site $s$ carries pixel
$\mathrm{order}[s]$, pixels numbered row by row): quadtree (Morton key, column bit lowest), snake
(boustrophedon rows), scrambled$k$ (random permutation from \path{numpy.random.default_rng}$(k+12345)$),
and for the order test quadtreeT (Morton order of the transposed image), rowmajor, colmajor, hilbert
(Hilbert curve) and mortonrev (site $s$ carries the pixel whose Morton key is $s$ with its bits
reversed). Fan-in initialization \eqref{eq:init} at per-core scale $\isc$ with initialization seeds
0--4, independent of the data seed. Double precision, one thread per process.

\paragraph{Dynamics} (\path{run.py}).
Phase 1: Dormand--Prince 5(4) with relative and absolute tolerances $10^{-8}$ and $10^{-14}$, the clock
re-anchored ($t\to0$, offset kept) whenever an accepted step is shorter than $10^{-9}t$, so that escapes at
$t\sim10^{15}$--$10^{20}$ are resolved; it stops at the first accepted step at which the loss has fallen
by $1\%$ of $\Loss(0)$ (time $t_a$). Phase 2: LSODA with the same tolerances on the clock $\tau=t-t_a$, with the
Gauss--Newton part of the Hessian (closed-form per-sample Jacobians) as Newton matrix, up to
$\tau=10^4$ ($3000$ for $8\times8$ images; $\min(3000,10^4$ kernel units$)$ in the regime sweep, a kernel unit
being $128/\tr\TK$ at $t_a$). Records: 30 logarithmic times per decade, 1000 linear times (600 for $8\times8$)
and the library's fine rows, merged for every event time. Conservation drift (cumulative, relative to
the largest squared core norm) at most $4.6\cdot10^{-8}$, median $1.8\cdot10^{-9}$ over the 234 main runs.
Two cases rerun with tolerances $10^{-10}$ and $10^{-16}$ (\texttt{tol\_check}) agree in $t_a$ to 7 digits, in
the final loss to 6, and in later drop times to $0.1\%$; the first drop ($\tau\approx10$--$15$) moves by
$0.2$--$0.8$ time units, below every gap reported.

\paragraph{Events and analysis.}
There are no teacher components; events are loss drops and rank events (\cref{tab:exp-common}).
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Loss drops} (\path{analysis.py}): peaks of $-\dd\Loss/\dd t$ that stand out by a factor 3
    against the minima separating them from higher peaks and lower the loss by at least $1\%$ of
    $\Loss(0)$; time: the peak; width: its full width at half maximum. Widths by progress (from $10\%$ to
    $90\%$ of a drop) are not used, since the loss creeps between drops and such widths measure the
    creep. Separation ratios and the S2S signature as in \cref{sec:setup-diagnostics}, with these drops.
  \item \emph{Data-weighted bond spectra} (\path{data.function_spectra}): across edge $e$ the network
    computes $f(x)=\sum_au_a(x)v_a(x)$, with messages $u_a$ from below the edge and $v_a$ from above it; the
    Schmidt values are the square roots of the eigenvalues of $G_u^{1/2}G_vG_u^{1/2}$, where $G_u$ and $G_v$
    are the Gram matrices of the two messages on the training set. They are gauge invariant and equal
    the Frobenius spectra under a product law with identity second moments (checked to
    $2\cdot10^{-12}$). Frobenius spectra are not used: after the escape $\normF{\W}$ grows to
    $10^6$--$10^7$ along directions the data barely see.
  \item \emph{Rank events}: $\bs en/\bs e1$ crossing $0.05$ (log-interpolated); an edge belongs to a drop if
    it crosses inside the drop's half-maximum window widened by one width on each side. Crossings already
    met at $\tau=0$ are flagged as unresolved.
  \item \emph{First-route gaps} (\path{scaling.py}): gap $=$ drop 1 $-$ drop 0 on the $\tau$ clock; a route's
    drop is the one whose widened window contains its rank crossing, and points where it rose between
    drops, merged with drop 0, or where another route rose first are excluded with their reason. Clean
    series: one (order, seed) whose first route, one sibling pair, rises inside drop 1 at every $\isc$, with at
    least four $\isc$ over at least half a decade (11 series); full sweep: 91 of the 124 binary points
    kept (16 between drops, 2 merged, 15 another route first), 10 seed series. Fits per seed: power law
    by least squares in $\ln g$, log law and $A/\isc+B$ by weighted least squares with weights $1/g$; all
    compared by the relative rms residual.
  \item \emph{MPS edges}: time of each edge's first rise after the first drop, per seed over the seven
    $\isc$, both laws fitted with relative residuals (\path{scaling.py}, \texttt{edge\_scaling}).
  \item \emph{Route couplings} (\path{route_amplitudes.py}; the coupling of a route, not the size of its dormant
    entries). A binary TTN of bond rank one computes
    $f=\bar s\prod_vq_v$, $q_v=g_v\cdot\Feat_v$, over its bottom cores $v$, $\Feat_v$ the features of the two pixels
    of $v$. For a pair $(u,w)$ of bottom cores, with the residual $r_j=f(x_j)-y_j$ and $h_{uw}=\bar s\prod_{v\ne u,w}q_v$,
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:real-route-amplitude}
      \mu_{uw}=\norm{P_uM_{uw}P_w}_2,\qquad
      M_{uw}=\frac1\nsamp\sum_jr_j\,h_{uw}(x_j)\,\Feat_u(x_j)\Feat_w(x_j)\T,
    \end{equation}
    with $P_u=\Id-g_ug_u\T/\norm{g_u}^2$; $k_{uw}$ is the number of cores of the tree path from $u$ to $w$.
    Evaluated for all 28 pairs at the plateau midpoint (minimum of $\norm{\dot\params}$ between the first
    two drops, reached by re-integrating from the end of phase 1). States farther than $0.05$ (relative) from a rank-one product are
    dropped (28 of 124). Units (\path{analysis.route_units}): rank-one states of the same (order, seed)
    whose normalized patterns $\mu_{uw}^2/\sum\mu^2$ differ by less than $0.2$ in total variation and whose
    plateau losses differ by less than $0.01$; 96 states form 26 units in the 20 (order, seed) pairs.
  \item \emph{Hessians} (\path{plateau_hessians.py}): exact Hessian (dense residual tensor) at the
    plateau midpoint (minimum $\norm{\dot\params}$ between the first two drops), raw and gauge-projected
    (\cref{sec:setup-diagnostics}); exponents per seed over the runs at the common plateau level
    ($\Loss\in[0.265,0.275]$ binary, $[0.225,0.235]$ MPS; $[0.31,0.345]$ on Fashion-MNIST, pooled).
  \item \emph{Regime sweep} (\path{regime_analysis.py}): kernel on the first 128 test images; labels by
    \cref{tab:regimes} without the convergence rule, which no $\chi=4$ network meets on 4000 labels (lazy if
    $\dTK(T)<0.1$; S2S if at least two drops, all $\sep{}>3$; rich otherwise); the linearized model at
    $\params(t_a)$ in closed form, with its floor $\Loss_{\rm lin}(\infty)$.
  \item \emph{Order test} (\path{order_predictor.py}, \path{order_test.py}; registered analysis): the
    best rank-one fit $f=\prod_vg_v\cdot\Feat_v$ of the labels from 32 starts (start $i$: unit $g_v$ from
    \path{default_rng}$(1000+i)$, 50 steps of higher-order power iteration on $\frac1\nsamp\sum_jy_j\bigotimes_v\Feat_v(x_j)$,
    rescaling to the least-squares amplitude, then alternating least squares until the relative loss
    change is below $10^{-12}$ or 2000 sweeps), the lowest-loss fit balanced, $S$ from
    \eqref{eq:real-route-amplitude} at that state; outcomes O1 (mean over seeds of $\Loss(10^4)$) and O2
    (mean of $\log_{10}\tau(\Loss\le0.25)$ on the running minimum, censored at $10^4$); Spearman correlation $r_S$
    over orders, percentile bootstrap intervals (10,000 resamples of orders) and two-sided permutation
    p-values (10,000 permutations of $S$).
\end{itemize}
