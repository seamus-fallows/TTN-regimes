\subsection{Phenomenology}\label{sec:s2s-phenomenology}

% saddles with odeco teachers"; gap exponents from pooled run sets (binary4 close gap 2 = 0.92 +- 0.11);
% "binary4 plateau error 2.0e-2 for both teachers at sigma = 0.1", "K = 7 <= 2.1e-5" without "chi = 6",
% "falls to 6e-7 at sigma = 0.003" for the close teacher (E10); per-seed R10 magnitudes (E11); K = 7, 8
% gap exponents as meeting the one-decade rule, except binary8 chi = 3 gap 1 (E21); "-0.118..-0.146" as
% the range of all wrong-order plateaus (that is seed 8 only, E6); the binary4 sequence, its sigma^-1

Gradient flow from a small fan-in initialization learns an odeco teacher one component at a time. This
subsection shows such runs, where complete sequences were observed, and which saddles the plateaus are;
\cref{sec:s2s-escape} treats the first escape, and \cref{sec:s2s-locality} teachers whose components
differ on a few sites only.

\paragraph{Setup} (details in \cref{app:exp-s2s}).
Odeco teachers (\cref{sec:setup-loss}) with $\ncomp=3$, $d=4$, Haar-random site bases and weights $(3,2,1)$
(\emph{separated}) or $(1,0.85,0.7)$ (\emph{close}); trees binary4 ($\Kc=3$), MPS4 ($\Kc=4$), binary8 and HT4
($\Kc=7$), MPS8 ($\Kc=8$); $\chi=3=\ncomp$ or $\chi=6$, capped by \eqref{eq:bond-cap} (at $d=4$ on MPS end
bonds and HT leaf bonds). Fan-in initialization with seeds $0$--$9$ at eight values of $\isc\in[0.003,0.1]$
($\Kc=3,4$) or five or six in $[0.07,0.4]$ ($\Kc=7,8$; smaller $\isc$ is out of reach, as the later plateaus
grow like $\isc^{-5}$ and $\isc^{-6}$); also the same $\Wz$ re-gauged to $\Q e=0$ (\emph{balanced}). Gradient flow by LSODA with the
exact Hessian as Jacobian; every event is re-integrated on a fine grid. A run is \emph{complete} if it
learns all three components; \cref{sec:s2s-failure} treats the others.

\paragraph{Prediction.}
\derived{} Two odeco components differ at every site, so the \emph{route} to a new component, the set of
cores that must move together (\cref{sec:s2s-locality}), is the whole tree at every saddle: $k=\Kc$ in
\eqref{eq:locality-k} (\cref{prop:A-steiner}). Hence (i)~the plateaus sit at the
partial fits $\W_S=\sum_{r\in S}\tw r\U r$, $S$ the components learned so far, at the loss
\begin{equation}\label{eq:s2s-levels}
  \Loss_S=\tfrac12\textstyle\sum_{r\notin S}\tw r^2
\end{equation}
($\Lpart{k}$ of \eqref{eq:partial-fit} in the greedy order), with bond spectrum $(\tw r)_{r\in S}$ on every
edge (\cref{prop:teacher-spectra}(a)); (ii)~each event raises the rank of every edge by one; (iii)~every
plateau is the first-escape problem of a $\Kc$-core route (\cref{prop:A-escape-degree}) and lasts
$\propto\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}$ for a fixed initialization direction; (iv)~the plateaus are higher-order saddles,
$\lambda_{\min}(\Hperp)\propto\isc^{\Kc-2}$ (\cref{prop:A-signatures}). The exception to (iv) is binary4, where
only two cores carry sites: a learned set that misses a larger component is a strict saddle,
$\lambda_{\min}=-(\tw r-\tw j)\,\tw j^{(\Kc-2)/\Kc}$ ($j$ learned, $r$ larger and not learned;
\cref{prop:A-piggyback-graph}), reached only after a wrong first escape. Binary4 is a depth-3 deep linear
network (\cref{rem:A-binary4-dln}), for which all of this is known
\knownresult[\citealp{saxe2014exact,jacot2022saddletosaddle,achour2024loss}]; the other four trees are
genuine tensor networks.

\paragraph{Scope.}
\empirical{} \newresult[MPS4, binary8, HT4; known for binary4] Complete runs: binary4 320 of 320 (fan-in, both teachers, $\chi=3,6$) and 60 of 60 (balanced);
MPS4 160/160 at $\chi=6$, 106/160 at $\chi=3$ and 60/60 balanced; binary8 50/50 and HT4 45/50 at $\chi=6$
($\isc=0.07$--$0.3$). At $\chi=3$ few runs with $\Kc=7$ complete (binary8 14 of 180, HT4 12 of 150), and no
MPS8 run learns its third component (0 of 284; 132 learn two; \cref{sec:s2s-failure}). Complete sequences were
thus measured on binary4 and MPS4 over $1.5$ decades of $\isc$, on binary8 and HT4 at $\chi=6$ over $0.6$
decade, and on MPS8 up to the second component.

\paragraph{Plateaus at the rank-constrained partial fits, one rank step per event.}
\empirical{} \newresult[MPS4, binary8, HT4; known for binary4] At every plateau between resolved events
($\sep i>3$) the loss equals \eqref{eq:s2s-levels} for the components learned so far, in whatever order.
The largest relative deviation is $8.5\cdot10^{-3}$ (binary4, separated teacher, $\isc=0.1$), $2.0\cdot10^{-2}$
(binary4, close teacher, at $\isc=0.003$ on a plateau with $\sep{}=5.1$), $2.8\cdot10^{-3}$ (MPS4;
$8.1\cdot10^{-3}$ balanced), and $2.1\cdot10^{-5}$ and $1.5\cdot10^{-4}$ at $\Kc=7,8$ ($\chi=6$, $3$); at
$\isc=0.003$ it is at most $7.4\cdot10^{-7}$, except on binary4 with the close teacher. At these plateaus, in
runs with the S2S signature, the top $\abs S$ singular values of every edge equal the learned weights to a
relative $7.7\cdot10^{-3}$ (binary4, close), $9.0\cdot10^{-4}$ (binary4, separated; $1.7\cdot10^{-9}$ at
$\isc=0.003$) and $3.5\cdot10^{-4}$ (other trees), and the next one is below $0.07$ times the smallest weight:
the plateaus are the critical points $\W_S$ of the problem with all bond ranks fixed at $\abs S$. In 497 of
the 531 complete runs that recorded spectra in their event windows, every event raised by exactly one, on
every edge, the number of singular values above $f\tw r$ ($r$ the component being learned; $f=\frac12$ or a
nearby value that separates $\tw r$ from the learned weights); the exceptions are 28 binary4 runs with the
close teacher at $\isc\ge0.05$, whose events overlap, and 6 HT4 runs.

\paragraph{Plateaus grow as $\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}$.}
\empirical{} \newresult[MPS4, binary8, HT4, MPS8; known for binary4] Every separation ratio exceeds $3$ in
all complete runs on MPS4 (326), binary8 (83) and HT4 (74), and in 218 of 220 on binary4 with the separated
teacher; with the close teacher on binary4 in 5 of 20 runs at $\isc=0.1$, rising to 20 of 20 at
$\isc=0.003$, as close weights make escapes overlap unless $\isc$ is small. Per-seed fits of
$\ln(\tev{i+1}-\tev i)$ against $\ln\isc$ (\cref{tab:s2s-gaps}; run sets in \cref{app:exp-s2s})
give $\Kc-2$ to within $0.1$ per seed on binary4 and $0.04$ on MPS4, over $1.5$ decades. At $\Kc=7,8$ the
means agree with $\Kc-2$ (binary8 $5.02\pm0.01$ and $4.97\pm0.02$, MPS8 $5.95\pm0.05$ for its only gap) but
over $0.6$ decade, below the reporting rule of \cref{sec:setup-diagnostics}; binary8 at $\chi=3$, over up to
$1.1$ decades, gives $4.93\pm0.05$, and HT4 is not a clean confirmation (per seed $3.93$--$5.78$).

\paragraph{Saddle type.}
\empirical{} \newresult[MPS4, binary8, HT4, MPS8; known for binary4] At the plateau midpoints
$\lambda_{\min}(\Hperp)$ vanishes like $\isc^{\Kc-2}$: the cell means of the per-seed slopes are
$1.02$--$1.18$ (binary4), $1.99$--$2.02$ (MPS4), $5.07$--$5.10$ (binary8), $4.88$--$5.13$ (HT4) and $5.99$ (MPS8),
with $\chi=6$ for $\Kc\ge7$ (\cref{tab:s2s-gaps}; the raw Hessian agrees to $0.004$). In cell medians the
magnitude is $0.87$--$1.15$ times the balanced-route value of \eqref{eq:A-plateau-lambda} at $\Kc=3,4$ and
$1.36$--$3.15$ times it at $\Kc=7,8$, where the routes are not balanced; per seed the ratio reaches $20$. The strict exception appears as predicted \knownresult[deep linear networks:
\citealp{achour2024loss}, Thm~7; \citealp{jacot2022saddletosaddle}, Lemma~15]. With the close teacher on
binary4 ($\chi=3$), seed 8 learns in the order 1-3-2 at seven of eight values of $\isc$ ($0.005$--$0.1$), and
seed 5 at two. At the plateau where $1$ and $0.7$ are learned and $0.85$ is not, $\lambda_{\min}$ is $-0.106$
to $-0.146$ at the nine midpoints (raw: $-0.113$ to $-0.149$) and flat in $\isc$ (seed 8: slope $0.03$),
against $-(0.85-0.7)\,0.7^{1/3}=-0.133$; the next event follows within about one width
($\sep{}=0.76$--$1.25$), and this gap does not grow as $\isc\to0$ (seed 8: $\propto\isc^{0.08}$). At
$\isc=0.003$ seed 8 learns greedily: the wrong order, and with it the strict saddle, becomes rare as
$\isc\to0$ (\cref{prop:A-matrix-like}). Otherwise the full order on binary4 is greedy (160 of 160 complete runs
with the separated teacher; $89\%$ and $95\%$ at $\chi=3$, $6$ with the close one); on the other trees it
varies from seed to seed (\cref{sec:s2s-escape}).

\subsection{An exactly solvable case: aligned initialization}\label{sec:s2s-aligned}
% Sources: notes/s2s_core.md 2.2, experiments/s2s_core/results/aligned.csv (aligned.py, one file per
% integrator since review round 1; the values match round-1 reviewer A's own code, verification S3).
% A demonstration and an integrator check of a known solution, not a new claim.

If every core starts diagonal in the teacher's basis (\cref{prop:aligned}), the components decouple
exactly: component $r$ is a product of $\Kc$ per-core amplitudes obeying \eqref{eq:aligned-ode}, and a
balanced start stays balanced, $\dot q_r=q_r^{\Kc-1}(\tw r-q_r^{\Kc})$, the mode equation of deep linear
networks \knownresult[\citealp{saxe2014exact}, eq.~15], here on any tree \proven. Integrating it gives
\eqref{eq:aligned-escape}, $t_r\simeq q_r(0)^{-(\Kc-2)}/((\Kc-2)\tw r)$ \knownresult[from eq.~15 of
\citealp{saxe2014exact}; symmetric matrix factorization from $\alpha\Id$: \citealp{li2021towards}, Thm~6.2]:
with one initial amplitude for all components they are learned in the order of their weights, at times in
the ratio $1/\tw1:1/\tw2:1/\tw3$, and every gap grows like $\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}$. This is the case $k=\Kc$ of
\cref{prop:A-head-start}, where a route through every core never sees the components already learned.
With unequal amplitudes the escape time is set by the cores that start smallest: $m$ of them at $\gamma$, far
below the others, give a time that stays $O(1)$, grows like $\ln(1/\gamma)$, or grows like $\gamma^{-(m-2)}$
for $m=1$, $2$, $\ge3$ \derived{} \knownresult[the trichotomy: \citealp{rawal2026theory}, Thm~4.2; new: the
TTN reduction, \cref{prop:A-product-mode}]. Random initialization is of this unbalanced kind, which is why its
escape times are not those of a balanced start of the same norm (\cref{sec:s2s-escape}).

\paragraph{Test} (\cref{fig:s2s-aligned}).
\empirical{} Separated teacher, $d=4$, $\chi=3$, balanced aligned initialization with per-core amplitude
$q_0=0.01$ (binary4), $0.07$ (MPS4), $0.29$ (binary8, HT4) and $0.34$ (MPS8), so that the events fall near
$t=33$, $50$, $100$ on every tree. Gradient flow on the full TTN (Dormand--Prince 5(4), tolerances $10^{-10}$
and $10^{-14}$) follows the exact solutions: the largest deviation of any overlap is $2.0\cdot10^{-10}$
(binary4), $3.2\cdot10^{-10}$ (MPS4), $2.3\cdot10^{-9}$ (binary8), $2.6\cdot10^{-9}$ (HT4) and $2.7\cdot10^{-9}$
(MPS8); event times agree on the same output grid to $1.3\cdot10^{-11}$ relative; the conservation drift
\eqref{eq:drift} stays below $3\cdot10^{-14}$. LSODA, the integrator of all other runs of this section,
deviates by at most $7.1\cdot10^{-7}$. The test checks contractions, gradients, integrators and event
detection on all five trees.

\subsection{Escape from the origin}\label{sec:s2s-escape}
% Sources: notes/s2s_core.md 1.1-1.5, 2.2b, 2.2c; theory notes/theory_escape.md v2 (E1, E2, E5-E8,
% recipe 2.5, section 9) = app:escape; s2s-core round-2 verification (E1-E3, E14, E16, E17; safe-to-cite
% items 2-7, 18, 19). Known: the exponent K - 2; the per-initialization blow-up identity is in substance
% the activation law of AGF (Kunin et al. 2025, Eqs. 2-3), so only its TTN form is new. WITHDRAWN, do not
% reintroduce: "first component = correlation flow's in 100% of runs" or "largest first at every
% sigma" (1847/1848, E1); "T moved by <= 1.7e-3" (E2); "stopped on direction convergence" for the binary4
% close-teacher jobs (they stopped at the amplitude cap, E3); "residual <= 2e-3" (2.2e-3, E16); 0.9996
% without "separated teacher" (E17); the research plan's t_1 ~ rho_0^{-(K-2)}[tau_align + ...] as a
% prediction (without the alignment term it is the lower bound t_KH; there is no separate alignment time).

How long does the first plateau last, and which component ends it? The research plan's first guess used
only the size of the initialization, $\tev1\propto\norm{\params_0}^{-(\Kc-2)}$. The answer is a blow-up time
computed for each initialization, with no free parameter; the norm gives only a lower bound, low by
factors from $3$ to $3\cdot10^7$.

\paragraph{Prediction: one blow-up time per initialization.}
On the first plateau $\normF{\W}$ is far below the teacher's weights, and gradient flow is to leading order
the flow $\dot\params=\nabla g$ of the correlation $g=\ip{\W}{\Wt}$ (\cref{app:escape}): its direction follows
the spherical gradient flow of $g$, independent of $\isc$, and its norm blows up at the time
\eqref{eq:A-blowup}. As $g$ is homogeneous of degree $\Kc$, the initialization $\params_0=\isc Z$ ($Z$ the
initialization at $\isc=1$) blows up at $\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}T(Z)$, with $T(Z)=\hat t_1(Z)/\tw1$ of
\cref{cor:A-blowup}. The direction tends to the balanced rank-one configuration of one component $r_1$,
which then escapes as a balanced mode (\cref{prop:A-first-event}):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:s2s-first}
  \tev1=\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}\,T(Z)+\delta_\Kc\,\tw{r_1}^{-(2-2/\Kc)}+o(1),
\end{equation}
with $\delta_\Kc$ from \eqref{eq:A-first-event} ($\delta_3=-0.850$, $\delta_4=-0.266$, $\delta_7=0.148$,
$\delta_8=0.201$). $T(Z)$ and $r_1$ come from integrating the leading-order flow from $Z$ (\cref{rem:A-recipe}),
so \eqref{eq:s2s-first} predicts the exponent, the prefactor and the first component of every run. Status:
the scaling and \eqref{eq:A-blowup} are \proven, the offset \derived{} (the direction must have converged to
$r_1$ by the time $\normF{\W}$ is comparable to $\tw{r_1}$). Known: the exponent
\knownresult[deep linear networks: \citealp{jacot2022saddletosaddle}; homogeneous models, from directional
convergence: \citealp{kumar2025early}, Thm~1], and \eqref{eq:A-blowup} is in substance the activation law of
alternating gradient flows \knownresult[\citealp{kunin2025alternating}, Eqs.~2--3]; new \newresult[TTN form]:
its evaluation on trees and the tests below. The escape direction is exactly balanced, with core norms
$1/\Kc$ (\cref{prop:A-kkt}) \proven{} \knownresult[deep linear networks: \citealp{kumar2025early}, Lemma~5;
also from the proof of \citealp{furman2026benign}, Prop.~F.10]; at the end of the leading-order flow every core
holds $1/\Kc$ of $\norm{\params}^2$ to within $1.3\%$ relative in all 1,200 initializations of the $d$ sweep
below \empirical.

\paragraph{Test, seed by seed} (\cref{fig:s2s-first}).
\empirical{} For 231 pairs of initialization and teacher we computed $T(Z)$ and $r_1$ and ran gradient flow
to the first event at eight values of $\isc$ (1,848 runs): $0.002$--$0.1$ on binary4 and MPS4 ($\chi=3,6$,
both teachers, fan-in and balanced) and $0.01$--$0.4$ at $\Kc=7,8$ (mostly separated teacher, fan-in;
\cref{tab:exp-s2s}). At the smallest $\isc$ the prediction \eqref{eq:s2s-first} matches every run to a relative
$8.5\cdot10^{-6}$ (binary4, separated teacher), $2.2\cdot10^{-3}$ (binary4, close teacher, whose direction has
not converged at $\isc=0.002$), $6\cdot10^{-5}$ (MPS4) and $4\cdot10^{-8}$ (binary8, HT4, MPS8 at
$\isc=0.01$). With the offset removed the per-seed exponents are $0.9996$--$0.9997$ ($\pm0.0001$; binary4,
separated), $0.990$--$0.996$ (binary4, close: $2.6$--$3.8$ s.e.\ below $1$, as an unconverged offset makes
them), $1.9999$--$2.0000$ (MPS4), $4.996$--$4.999$ (binary8, HT4) and $5.9995\pm0.0002$ (MPS8). An
independent integration of the spherical flow reproduces $T(Z)$ for the 160 binary4 and MPS4 jobs to
$2\cdot10^{-10}$ (verification review).

\paragraph{The size of the initialization gives only a lower bound.}
From an initialization along a maximizer of $g$ the escape takes
$t_{\rm KH}=\Kc^{\Kc/2-1}\norm{\params_0}^{-(\Kc-2)}/((\Kc-2)\tw1)$ \knownresult[\citealp{kumar2025towards},
text before Thm~11], and every initialization of the same norm takes longer (\cref{cor:A-kh}) \proven. The
median $T/t_{\rm KH}$ is $3.1$--$4.8$ (binary4), $7.9$--$13.7$ (MPS4), $95$--$210$ (HT4), $184$--$2650$ (binary8)

\subsection{Saddle sequence and locality}\label{sec:s2s-locality}
% Sources: notes/locality.md (final; sections 7, 8, 10), agent-runs/locality/REPORT.md, reviews
% notes/reviews/locality_round1.md and locality_round2_verification.md (caveats a-g applied),
% notes/theory_saddles.md v2 (section 9: known vs new), experiments/pilot_h2_locality/README.md,
% notes/literature.md (H2, H3). Settings: app:exp-locality. WITHDRAWN or overstated, do not
% reintroduce: "H2 holds for k = 1..8" or "+-0.05 for k <= 4"; k = 6-7 = k - 2 without "balanced
% initialization only"; ht8 D = {3,4} "measures k = 7"; "rank rises on the Steiner edges in 98.7% /
% 99.75% of events" as a prediction success rate; "spare bond channels remove most dead routes";
% "a stalling seed stalls at every sigma"; "steep MPS exponents come from shrinking route ends";
% "raw and projected Hessian exponents agree to 0.01"; "2 of 1008 stalls with balanced
% initialization"; "log" labels for k* = 2 levels without the fair comparison (TA on MPS4, first level).

After the first escape, gradient flow rests near a saddle that represents what has been learned.
Which component comes next, and how long is the next plateau? In a TTN the answer depends on where
the next component sits in the tree: one that differs from what is learned only on sites carried by
two adjacent cores needs those two cores to move, one spread over the tree needs many. We state the
resulting rule, derived in \cref{app:saddles}, and test it.

\paragraph{Prediction: the route rule.}
Take a two-component pattern teacher (\cref{sec:setup-loss}), $\Wt=\tw1\U1+\tw2\U2$ with
$\tw1>\tw2$, whose components differ exactly on the site set $D$ (sites numbered from $0$, as in
\cref{tab:A-route-degree}). Let $N(D)$ be the set of cores that carry a site of $D$,
$\mathrm{St}(N(D))$ the smallest subtree containing them (their Steiner subtree), and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:locality-k}
  k=\abs{\mathrm{St}(N(D))}.
\end{equation}
The bonds whose teacher rank is two are exactly the bonds of $\mathrm{St}(N(D))$
(\cref{prop:teacher-spectra}(b)). Once $\U1$ is learned, the flow sits near the balanced saddle
$\params_1$ that represents $\tw1\U1$, and new parameters can lower the loss only through
\emph{routes}, connected sets of cores whose new entries combine into a term proportional to the
residual at no Gauss--Newton cost (\cref{lem:A-routes}). The cheapest route to $\U2$ is
$\mathrm{St}(N(D))$, with coupling $c_2=\tw2\tw1^{(\Kc-k)/\Kc}$ (\cref{prop:A-steiner}), and the
escape is the first-escape problem of a $k$-core TTN (\cref{prop:A-escape-degree}). With
$\Delta t_2=\tev2-\tev1$ the time between the two learning events,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:locality-rule}
  \Delta t_2\simeq
  \begin{cases}
    O(1)\ \text{(no plateau)}, & k=1,\\[0.5ex]
    \dfrac{1-\tw2/\tw1}{\lambda_2}\,\ln\dfrac1\isc,\qquad \lambda_2=\tw2\,\tw1^{(\Kc-2)/\Kc}, & k=2,\\[1.5ex]
    A\,\isc^{-(k-2)}, & k\ge3,
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
(\cref{prop:A-head-start}). For $k=2$ the saddle is strict, with $\lambda_{\min}(\Hperp)=-\lambda_2$
(\cref{prop:A-hessian}); for $k\ge3$ its Hessian has no negative eigenvalue (a higher-order saddle),
and on the plateau $\lambda_{\min}\propto\isc^{k-2}\to0$ (\cref{prop:A-signatures}). The factor
$1-\tw2/\tw1$ is a \emph{head start}: the route grows during the first plateau, so from the saddle
itself the $k=2$ slope is $1/\lambda_2$. The prefactor $A$ is set by the route amplitudes when the
plateau starts and is not predicted from $\isc$ alone for random initialization. Status: the Steiner
identity and the upper bound $k$ on the saddle order are \proven; that the order equals $k$, and
\eqref{eq:locality-rule}, are \derived{} (leading order, small $\isc$).

With several learned components ``the sites where the next component differs'' is ambiguous, and
saddles need not be partial sums of components. The \emph{route rule} (\cref{rem:A-sequence}) works at
the saddle actually reached, the best approximation of $\Wt$ with the current bond-rank profile: if
the learned structure is stable (no wrong order, no star, \cref{prop:A-piggyback-graph}), the next
escape takes the smallest route, of size $k^*$, with a nonzero local residual
(\cref{def:A-local-residual}), raises the bond rank on its internal edges, and the flow moves to the
best approximation with the new profile. We evaluate it numerically (rank-constrained optima by
L-BFGS with restarts); it predicts every plateau level and its $k^*$, and $k^*=k$ for two components.
Status: \derived{} at leading order for each step, \conjecture{} as an algorithm.

\paragraph{What is known and what is new.}
Known in general: dormant weights of lowest homogeneity degree $L$ stay $O(\delta)$ for at least a
time $\propto\delta^{-(L-2)}$, $\delta$ their initial size, if the active weights start within $\delta$
of a local minimum of the loss restricted to them ({\L}ojasiewicz) \knownresult[\citealp{kumar2026learning},
Thm~8]; escapes that need $1$, $2$ or $r\ge3$ small layers take a time of order $1$, $\ln(1/\epsilon)$
or $\epsilon^{-(r-2)}$ \knownresult[\citealp{rawal2026theory}, Thm~4.2] (first escape; the first $r$
layers at scale $\epsilon$, the others $O(1)$); in our model a min-norm critical point has a descent
curve of order $N$, the number of cores touching a connected component of rank-deficient bonds, and
with several such components the smallest gives the least suppressed escape
\knownresult[\citealp{furman2026benign}, Lemmas~D.8--D.9, Remark~D.10 and Fig.~4]; for $N=2$ the curve
is a direction of negative curvature, so the saddle is strict (a one-line consequence, not stated
there); in deep linear networks with at least three weight matrices, which include the binary tree
with $\Ns=4$ (\cref{rem:A-binary4-dln}), every saddle visited from an exactly balanced initialization
that selects the top singular values is non-strict \knownresult[\citealp{achour2024loss}, Thm~7 and the
remark after Prop.~8; full-rank targets with distinct singular values]; and the $k=2$ head start
\knownresult[weaker directions are $O(\epsilon^{1-\tw{r+1}/\tw1})$ when the top ones are learned,
two-layer linear networks: \citealp{zhang2026saddletosaddle}, Thm~4; dormant norms carried across
phases: \citealp{kunin2025alternating}, Sec.~2.2]. New for TTNs \newresult: (i)~the dormant block is the
Steiner route of the residual, which can be a proper subtree of a connected rank-deficient component,
so $k^*=\abs{\mathrm{St}(N(D))}$ can be far below Furman et al.'s $N$ ($N=\Kc$ at a rank-one saddle with
$\chi\ge2$); (ii)~the coupling $c_2$ and the rate $\lambda_2$ (\cref{eq:A-coupling,eq:A-lambda2});
(iii)~the head start for $k\ge3$ in closed form, an effective initial route amplitude
$\isc(1-\tw2/\tw1)^{-1/(k-2)}$ (the carry-over of dormant amplitudes is that of
\citealp{kunin2025alternating}); (iv)~the piggyback-graph criterion for learned saddles
that are strict although $k^*\ge3$ (\cref{prop:A-piggyback-graph}); (v)~the conservation-law trap
(\cref{prop:A-trap}); (vi)~the measured dependence on the topology, below.

\paragraph{Setup} (details in \cref{app:exp-locality}).
Scope of every statement below: orthogonal pattern teachers with $d=2$ and weights $(1,0.5)$ unless
stated, $\chi=2$ ($3$ for the three-component teachers B4, N4, E8), whitened inputs, gradient flow on
the exact population loss. Nine trees: MPS with $\Ns=4,6,8$
(MPS4, MPS6, MPS8), binary4, binary8, HT4, HT8, and two custom trees with $\Ns=6$, \emph{star3} (a root
without sites, three children with two sites each) and \emph{midroot6} (the MPS6 chain rooted at a
middle core). Protocols: \emph{small} (fan-in initialization \eqref{eq:init} at scale $\isc$, first
plateau included), \emph{saddle} (the exact balanced saddle $\tw1\U1$ plus a fan-in perturbation of
scale $\isc$; $\Delta t_2=\tev2$ times the escape alone) and \emph{balanced} (the fan-in $\Wz$ re-gauged
to $\Q e=0$). Event $r$ is the last upward crossing of $\ov r=\frac12$. Per seed (seeds $0$--$7$), over
the (at least three) $\isc$ at which it escapes, we fit $\ln\Delta t_2$ against $\ln(1/\isc)$
(exponent $p$), or $\Delta t_2$ against $\ln(1/\isc)$ (the $k=2$ slope), and report mean $\pm$ s.e.\ over
seeds (median); log and power laws are compared by the residual of $\ln\Delta t_2$ on the same data
(``fair comparison''). Fits use the runs that learn $\U1$ first, without rotations (an overlap crossing
$\frac12$ more than once). About $11{,}600$ runs in all.

\paragraph{Saddle type: strict for $k=2$, higher order for $k\ge3$.}
\empirical{} \newresult[for TTNs; deep linear networks: \citealp{achour2024loss}] At the plateau midpoint
(minimum speed between the events) we compute $\lambda_{\min}$ of $\Hperp$ and of the raw Hessian;
near rank-deficient states the gauge projection removes part of the escape direction
(\cref{app:exp-locality}), so raw values are quoted where the two differ. The type is read from the
scaling of $\lambda_{\min}$ with $\isc$ (\cref{prop:A-signatures}); the fixed threshold of
\cref{sec:setup-diagnostics} would call $k=3$ plateaus strict for $\isc\gtrsim0.01$--$0.04$. For $k=2$,
$\lambda_{\min}=-0.5000=-\lambda_2$ from the saddle at every $\isc$, projected and raw ($-0.2500$ and
$-0.7500$ for $\tw2=0.25$, $0.75$), with one negative eigenvalue per active route, also at $\chi=4$, $8$.
From small initialization the raw value is $-0.498$ to $-0.499$ for $\isc\le10^{-3}$ and departs from
$-\lambda_2$ where the plateau is too short to reach the saddle ($-0.437$ for MPS8 $\{3,4\}$ at
$\isc=0.008$; $-0.581$ against $-0.75$ for $\tw2=0.75$). For $k\ge3$, $\lambda_{\min}\propto\isc^{x}\to0$
with $x$ growing with $k$ (\cref{fig:locality-hessian}); full ranges over rows: $x=0.81$--$1.06$ for $k=3$
(raw $0.92$--$1.06$), $1.64$--$2.16$ for $k=4$, $2.82$--$3.76$ for $k=5$, $4.00$ and $4.04$ for $k=6$
(balanced binary8 and HT4, one decade; $4.27$ on MPS8; $3.19$--$5.23$ in the short rows), and $5.01$
(balanced HT4) up to $6.5$ (short rows) for $k=7$. $x$ is within $0.2$ of $k-2$ in 126 of 135 rows with $k=3$--$5$, and the raw $x$ is
within $0.2$ of the row's duration exponent $p$ in $95\%$ of 169 rows with $k\ge3$ (median
$\abs{x-p}=0.02$): where the plateau law deviates, the curvature deviates the same way. Raw and
projected exponents differ by up to $0.11$. No plateau with $k\ge3$ has $\isc$-independent negative
curvature, as predicted for saddles visited in the greedy order; wrong-order and star saddles, strict
with $k^*\ge3$ by \cref{prop:A-piggyback-graph}, were not tested.

\paragraph{Bond spectra: the new component enters through the Steiner edges only.}
\empirical{} \newresult{} The second component raises the teacher's bond rank exactly on the edges of
$\mathrm{St}(N(D))$ (\cref{prop:teacher-spectra}(b)), so the bond spectra show which cores the escape
uses (\cref{fig:locality-bonds}). Through all 5,131 two-component events the second singular value of
every edge outside the Steiner subtree stays at the initialization-noise level, from the plateau
midpoint to the end of the run: $\bs e2\le4.3\,\isc^2\tw2$, scaling as $\isc^{2.07}$ (below
$3\cdot10^{-3}\tw2$ for $\isc\le0.03$). This \emph{leakage bound} is the substantive result: the new
component enters through the Steiner edges only, up to $O(\isc^2)$. The rest is \emph{single-route
consistency}: the edges whose effective rank (number of $\bs ek$ above $0.05$ times the teacher's value)
is higher at $\ov2=0.9$ than at the plateau midpoint are the Steiner edges in $99.75\%$ of the 4,448
events with a resolved plateau ($99.6\%$, $99.9\%$ with thresholds $0.03$, $0.1$). Given the leakage
bound a non-Steiner edge cannot cross the threshold, so this number checks only that every Steiner
edge rises; it is not a prediction success rate. The Steiner edges also cross together (median spread
of crossing times $5\cdot10^{-5}$ event widths, 95th percentile $0.25$, growing as $\isc^{1.94}$), which
tests that they rise as one route, not the timing of the event.

\paragraph{Reading $k$ from a single run.}
On a balanced route the new overlap grows as $\dot{\ov2}\propto\ov2^{\,2-2/k}$ at small $\ov2$, so the
local growth exponent $\gamma=\dd\ln\dot{\ov2}/\dd\ln\ov2$ gives $k=2/(2-\gamma)$
(\cref{eq:A-growth-exponent}) \derived{} \knownresult[the exponent $2-2/k$ of a balanced product of
$k$ factors: \citealp{arora2019implicit,razin2022implicit}]. Fitted over $\ov2\in[10^{-4},10^{-2}]$, the
medians per row give $k=2.00$, $3.0$, $4.0$, $5.1$--$5.3$, $6.0$, $7.0$--$7.7$ for $k=2$ (saddle start) to
$7$ \empirical{} \newresult[as a meter of $k$]. Validity range: from the saddle, or from small
initialization if the plateau is long enough for the window to lie after the escape transient. The
estimate fails for small-initialization $k=2$ on MPS6 and MPS8 ($2.4$--$3.3$; plateau shorter than the
transient), is biased high for $k\ge5$ (HT8: $5.3$ for $k=5$, $7.7$ for $k=7$), and cannot separate $k=7$
from $8$, since $\dd k/\dd\gamma=k^2/2$ (per-run 5--95\% ranges $\pm1$ at $k=7$; MPS8 $\{0,7\}$, $k=8$: $7.9$
from small initialization, 3 runs, $6.1$ from the saddle, 2 runs).

Two things end a saddle-to-saddle sequence early: a route dies, its end cores decaying to roundoff under
the conservation laws (the trap of \cref{prop:A-trap}), or a plateau outlasts the budget. Both appear in
the odeco runs of \cref{sec:s2s-phenomenology}, whose routes span the tree, and in the pattern-teacher runs
of \cref{sec:s2s-locality}. An odeco run is \emph{trapped} if a component not yet learned has a route-end
slice below $10^{-8}\isc$ and \emph{stalled} if its budget ended with every such slice above it
(\cref{app:exp-s2s}); a pattern-teacher run is flagged \emph{stalled} when its gradient stays below
$10^{-12}$, and \emph{dead} when a longer run confirms route ends at roundoff.

\paragraph{Odeco teachers: the trap grows with $\Kc$} (\cref{fig:s2s-dormant}).
\empirical{} \newresult{} No run traps at $\Kc=3$ (0 of 380). On MPS4 at $\chi=3$ the separated teacher's outcome
is fixed by the initialization direction: the same 4 of 10 seeds trap at all eight values of
$\isc\in[0.003,0.1]$ (32 of 80 runs); with the close teacher 14 of 80 runs trap and 8 stall, not fixed by the
direction (one seed traps at 6 of 8 values). The dormant slices at both route ends decay at a rate
$\propto\isc^2$, constant per seed to a few percent (at most $2.4\%$ in the committed fits, $5.3\%$ in an
independent refit), equal at the two ends and seed-dependent ($0.26$ to $1.06\,\isc^2$): the signature of the
mechanism, whose rate is $\propto\abs{Q_{\nu\nu}}=O(\isc^2)$ (its magnitude is not tested). Decay time
$\propto\isc^{-2}$ against escape time $\propto\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}$ explains the pattern \derived: at $\Kc=3$ the route
escapes first, at $\Kc=4$ both are of the same order, at $\Kc\ge5$ the ends are decided long before the route
could escape. At $\Kc=7,8$ with $\chi=3$ (fan-in, $\isc=0.15$--$0.3$) at least 9 of 30 runs trap on binary8,
24/30 on HT4 and 14/30 on MPS8 (lower bounds: some stalled runs end with slices still decaying). With $\chi=6$
none traps on binary8 (0 of 50), but 5 of 50 do on HT4 (one seed, at every $\isc$) and 7 of 50 on MPS8 (34 more
stall), whose route ends sit on bonds capped at $d=4$, one channel above the teacher rank. The classes are
unchanged at a 100 times smaller absolute tolerance (9 of 9 runs). The optimizer experiment met the same trap
on its odeco MPS8: fan-in gradient flow converged in none of 12 runs, with dead route ends in 11
(\cref{app:exp-optimizers}).

\paragraph{Relation to the conservation-law trap.}
In the reduced model of \cref{prop:A-trap} a route end decays iff $c=Q_{\nu\nu}+\norm{y_{\rm route}}^2<0$
(an \emph{unfavourable} end), and a route dies for good only if all its ends are unfavourable. The odeco
runs show the mechanism's signature where it was measured ($\Kc=4$): decay rates $\propto\isc^2$, constant
per seed and equal at both route ends, and no trap from balanced initialization. The pattern-teacher runs
test the criterion itself. Evaluated once, at the start of the plateau ($\ov1=0.99$), in the 122 fan-in and
saddle-start runs of the stall diagnosis, this joint criterion is strongly associated with death (30 of 55 runs with both
ends unfavourable died, 7 of 67 with a favourable end) but is neither sufficient (25 predicted-dead
runs escaped) nor necessary: seven dead runs had a live end at the plateau start, which by the
theory's own refutation criterion (``a dead stall with a live route end'') counts against the
mechanism as stated, unless $c$ changes sign later in the plateau ($y_{\rm route}$ is not conserved; not
checked). Further conflicts with the theory remain open: (i)~exactly balanced initialization is
expected to remove the trap, its route ends not decaying; it leaves no dead route in either experiment
(consistent), but on the pattern-teacher MPS8 routes the ends shrink to $10^{-10}$--$10^{-8}$ and escapes
are delayed by $10^5$ or more (inconsistent; the balanced odeco MPS8 runs stall instead with route slices
near $0.03\,\isc$); (ii)~the heuristic frequencies (about half of the two-ended HT routes dead under
fan-in initialization, \cref{prop:A-trap}; fewer stalls on MPS with $\chi=d$) are contradicted: 0 of
144 small-initialization HT4 runs with $k=5$ stall, against 30 of 48 on MPS4 $\{0,3\}$ and 17 of 48 on
MPS6 $\{0,3\}$ (the odeco HT4 routes, with four leaf ends, trap in at least 24 of 30 runs at $\chi=3$);
(iii)~spare channels avoided stalls in the runs of \cref{app:boundaries}, here only on routes with an
uncapped end, and the rise on capped routes has no established mechanism.

\paragraph{Steep MPS routes.}
\empirical{} (mechanism open) One row stays steep at small $\isc$: MPS8 $\{1,5\}$ ($k=5$) from the
saddle, $p=3.67\pm0.20$ at $\isc\in[0.012,0.03]$ (median $3.83$; 4 fitted seeds, $0.4$ decades, 10 of 24
runs stalled), with a matching Hessian exponent ($3.74$ projected, $3.75$ raw). Its fitted seeds escape
at every $\isc$, so it meets a refutation criterion of the theory (a per-seed exponent off by more than
$0.2$ at $\isc\le0.05$ in runs without stall or sign correction), on thin evidence. In the steep MPS rows
the route-end amplitudes at the plateau midpoint scale as $\isc^{\alpha}$ with $\alpha=1.24$--$1.91$ and
shrink during the plateau (median ratio of midpoint to first event $0.53$--$0.78$, against $0.85$--$1.03$
in on-target rows); but $\alpha>1$ also occurs in on-target rows (binary8 $\{0,7\}$, balanced: $\alpha=1.34$,
$p=2.99$), so the steep exponents are associated with shrinking route ends, not shown to be caused by
