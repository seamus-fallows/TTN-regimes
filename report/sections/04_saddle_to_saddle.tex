\section{The saddle-to-saddle regime}\label{sec:s2s}

This section treats gradient flow from small initialization, where a run passes from plateau to plateau
(\cref{tab:regimes}). It answers questions Q1--Q4 of \cref{sec:intro-question}: which saddles are
visited, how long the flow stays near each, which component comes next, and how all of this depends on
the tree. The organizing idea is the \emph{route} (\cref{sec:setup-routes}; box below): near a saddle,
new parameters lower the loss at no Gauss--Newton cost only through routes, and, when the spectral
condition of \cref{app:saddle-order} holds, the number of cores of the cheapest route sets the type of the
saddle and the length of the plateau \derived{} (\cref{app:saddles}). Unless stated otherwise, inputs are whitened, the loss is the exact population
loss, the initialization is fan-in (\cref{sec:setup-init}), and events are dated as in
\cref{sec:setup-diagnostics}, by the rule of each experiment (\cref{app:experiments}).

\Cref{sec:s2s-phenomenology} shows complete sequences for odeco teachers, \cref{sec:s2s-aligned} the
exactly solvable aligned case, and \cref{sec:s2s-escape} predicts the first escape of each run from its
initialization. \Cref{sec:s2s-locality} tests the route rule for the later escapes on two-component
teachers, \cref{sec:s2s-saddle-order} says when the route gives the saddle order, and
\cref{sec:s2s-several} treats teachers with more components. \Cref{sec:s2s-order} asks which component is
learned first, and \cref{sec:s2s-failure} how sequences end early. Supplementary tables, secondary checks
and the provenance of the numbers are in \cref{app:supp-s2s}.

\textbf{How to read every plateau law.}
\emph{Toy model.} A product of $k$ scalars, each started at $\isc$, with $\dot a=a^{k-1}(s-a^k)$ (the
balanced mode of \eqref{eq:aligned-ode}), reaches $O(1)$ in a time of order one for $k=1$, in
$\ln(1/\isc)/s$ for $k=2$, and in $\isc^{-(k-2)}/((k-2)s)$ for $k\ge3$. If the factors start unequal, those
that start smallest set the time: $k$ of them at scale $\isc$, far below the others, give the same three laws
\derived{} (\cref{prop:A-product-mode}) \knownresult[\citealp{rawal2026theory}, Thm~4.2]. Every plateau
law of this section has this form, with $k$ the number of cores that must move together; prefactors and the
head start of $k=2$ need the full analysis.

\emph{Why routes.} A route (\cref{sec:setup-routes}) moves $\W$ only through all of its cores at once, by
the product of their small new entries, so its gain comes at no Gauss--Newton cost of the same order
(\cref{lem:A-routes}): near the saddle it fits the residual like a fresh $k$-core TTN started near zero, and
its escape is the first escape (\cref{sec:s2s-escape}) with $\Kc$ replaced by $k$, the toy model with $k$
factors (\cref{prop:A-escape-degree}). The next plateau is therefore set by $k^*$: it is absent ($k^*=1$),
lasts $\propto\ln(1/\isc)$ ($k^*=2$), or lasts $\propto\isc^{-(k^*-2)}$ ($k^*\ge3$). For a component that
differs from the learned one on the sites $D$, the route is the Steiner subtree of the $m=\abs{V_D}$ cores
that carry $D$, so it includes the cores on the paths between them \proven{} (\cref{prop:A-steiner}).

\emph{Example} (teacher B4, as in the introduction: patterns $0000$, $0110$, $1001$ with weights $1$, $0.3$, $0.6$;
$0000$ is learned first on both trees). On MPS4 the $0.3$ component ($0110$) differs on sites 1 and 2, on adjacent
cores: $k=2$, a logarithmic plateau. The $0.6$ component ($1001$) differs on sites 0 and 3, at the two ends of the
chain: $k=4$, a $\isc^{-2}$ plateau. So the smaller component is learned first (\emph{locality beats weight}):
$0110$ before $1001$ in all 60 runs. On binary4 both components need the two leaf cores and the root: $k=3$ for
both, each comes after a $\isc^{-1}$ plateau, and they are learned in the order of their weights, $1001$ before
$0110$, in all 48 runs (\cref{sec:s2s-several}).

\subsection{Phenomenology}\label{sec:s2s-phenomenology}

% ("safe to cite" list and errata E1-E21 override the note). Numbers from experiments/s2s_core/results/*.csv.

Gradient flow from a small fan-in initialization learns an odeco teacher one component at a time. This
subsection shows such runs and which saddles the plateaus are.

\paragraph{Setup} (details in \cref{app:exp-s2s}).
Odeco teachers (\cref{sec:setup-loss}) with $\ncomp=3$, $d=4$, Haar-random site bases and weights $(3,2,1)$
(\emph{separated}) or $(1,0.85,0.7)$ (\emph{close}); trees binary4 ($\Kc=3$), MPS4 ($\Kc=4$), binary8 and HT4
($\Kc=7$), MPS8 ($\Kc=8$); $\chi=3=\ncomp$ or $\chi=6$, capped by \eqref{eq:bond-cap}. Fan-in initialization
with seeds $0$--$9$ at eight values of $\isc\in[0.003,0.1]$ ($\Kc=3,4$) or five or six in $[0.07,0.4]$
($\Kc=7,8$, whose later plateaus grow like $\isc^{-5}$ and $\isc^{-6}$); also the same $\Wz$ re-gauged to
$\Q e=0$ (\emph{balanced}). A run is \emph{complete} if it learns all three components; \cref{sec:s2s-failure}
treats the others.

\paragraph{Prediction.}
\derived{} Two odeco components differ at every site, so the route to a new component is the whole tree at
every saddle, $k=\Kc$ (\cref{prop:A-steiner}). Hence (i)~the plateaus sit at the partial fits
$\W_S=\sum_{r\in S}\tw r\U r$, $S$ the components learned so far, at the loss
\begin{equation}\label{eq:s2s-levels}
  \Loss_S=\tfrac12\textstyle\sum_{r\notin S}\tw r^2,
\end{equation}
with bond spectrum $(\tw r)_{r\in S}$ on every edge (\cref{prop:teacher-spectra}(a)); (ii)~each event raises the
rank of every edge by one; (iii)~every plateau is the first-escape problem of a $\Kc$-core route
(\cref{prop:A-escape-degree}) and lasts $\propto\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}$ for a fixed initialization direction; (iv)~the
plateaus are higher-order saddles, $\lambda_{\min}(\Hperp)\propto\isc^{\Kc-2}$ (\cref{prop:A-signatures}). The
exception to (iv) is binary4, where only two cores carry sites: a learned set that misses a larger component
is a strict saddle, $\lambda_{\min}=-(\tw r-\tw j)\,\tw j^{(\Kc-2)/\Kc}$ ($j$ learned, $r$ larger and not
learned; \cref{prop:A-piggyback-graph}), reached only after an escape out of weight order (in our runs the
second escape: order 1-3-2). Binary4 is a depth-3 deep linear network (\cref{rem:A-binary4-dln}), for which all
of this is known \knownresult[\citealp{saxe2014exact,jacot2022saddletosaddle,achour2024loss}]; the other four
trees are genuine tensor networks.

\paragraph{Plateaus at the rank-constrained partial fits, one rank step per event.}
\empirical{} Complete sequences were measured on binary4 and MPS4 over $1.5$ decades of $\isc$, on binary8 and
HT4 at $\chi=6$ over $0.6$ decade, and on MPS8 up to its second component (run counts in
\cref{app:supp-s2s-phen}). At every plateau between resolved events ($\sep i>3$) the loss equals
\eqref{eq:s2s-levels} for the components learned so far, in whatever order: to a relative $2\cdot10^{-2}$ at
worst (binary4, close teacher), and to $7.4\cdot10^{-7}$ in the other cells at $\isc=0.003$. The top $\abs S$
singular values of every edge equal the learned weights (to a relative $7.7\cdot10^{-3}$ at worst), and the
next one is below $0.07$ times the smallest weight: the plateaus are the critical points $\W_S$ of the problem
with all bond ranks fixed at $\abs S$. In 497
of the 531 complete runs that recorded spectra in their event windows, every event raised by exactly one, on
every edge, the number of singular values above about half the weight being learned (the exceptions:
overlapping events on binary4 with the close teacher at $\isc\ge0.05$, and 6 HT4 runs).
\newresult[MPS4, binary8, HT4; known for binary4]

\paragraph{Plateaus grow as $\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}$.}
\empirical{} Per-seed fits of $\ln(\tev{i+1}-\tev i)$ against $\ln\isc$ give $\Kc-2$ to within $0.1$ per seed on
binary4 and $0.04$ on MPS4, over $1.5$ decades (limited range for some binary4 close-teacher seeds: after the cut
$\sep i>3$ they keep four or five values of $\isc$, about $0.8$ decade). At $\Kc=7,8$ the means agree with
$\Kc-2$ (binary8 $5.02\pm0.01$ and $4.97\pm0.02$, MPS8 $5.95\pm0.05$ for its only gap), but over $0.6$ decade
(limited range); HT4 is not a clean confirmation (per seed $3.93$--$5.78$). All fits: \cref{tab:s2s-gaps}.
\newresult[MPS4, binary8, HT4, MPS8; known for binary4]

\paragraph{Saddle type.}
\empirical{} At the plateau midpoints $\lambda_{\min}(\Hperp)$ vanishes like $\isc^{\Kc-2}$: the cell means of
the per-seed slopes are $1.02$--$1.18$ (binary4), $1.99$--$2.02$ (MPS4), $4.88$--$5.13$ ($\Kc=7$, $\chi=6$) and
$5.99$ (MPS8). In the medians of the fan-in cells the magnitude is $0.87$--$1.15$ times the balanced-route value
of \eqref{eq:A-plateau-lambda} at $\Kc=3,4$ ($1.19$ for balanced binary4) and $1.36$--$3.9$ times it at
$\Kc=7,8$, where the routes are not balanced. The strict exception appears as predicted: on binary4 with the
close teacher, the runs that learn in the order 1-3-2 rest at $\lambda_{\min}$ between $-0.11$ and $-0.15$, flat
in $\isc$, against $-(0.85-0.7)\,0.7^{1/3}=-0.133$, and leave within about one event width; the wrong order, and
with it the strict saddle, becomes rare as $\isc\to0$ (\cref{prop:A-matrix-like}; seeds in
\cref{app:supp-s2s-phen}). Otherwise the full order on binary4 is greedy (all 160 complete runs with the
separated teacher; $89\%$ and $95\%$ at $\chi=3$, $6$ with the close one); on the other trees it varies from seed
to seed (\cref{sec:s2s-order}). \newresult[MPS4, binary8, HT4, MPS8] \knownresult[binary4 and its strict saddle, deep
linear networks: \citealp{achour2024loss}, Thm~7; \citealp{jacot2022saddletosaddle}, Lemma~15]

\subsection{An exactly solvable case: aligned initialization}\label{sec:s2s-aligned}
% Sources: notes/s2s_core.md 2.2, experiments/s2s_core/results/aligned.csv. A demonstration and an
% integrator check of a known solution (Prop. 2.4 is stated once, in Sec. 2); the test is in app:supp-s2s-aligned.

If every core starts diagonal in the teacher's basis (\cref{prop:aligned}), the components decouple exactly, a
balanced start stays balanced, and each component follows the mode equation of deep linear networks, here on any
tree \proven{} \knownresult[\citealp{saxe2014exact}, eq.~15]. By \eqref{eq:aligned-escape}, with one initial
amplitude for all components they are learned in the order of their weights, at times in the ratio
$1/\tw1:1/\tw2:1/\tw3$, and every gap grows like $\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}$ \knownresult[from eq.~15 of
\citealp{saxe2014exact}; symmetric matrix factorization from a multiple of the identity: \citealp{li2021towards},
Thm~6.2]. This
is the case $k=\Kc$ of \cref{prop:A-head-start}: a route through every core never sees the components already
learned. Random initialization is unbalanced, so its escape times are not those of a balanced start of the same
norm (the toy model of the box; \cref{sec:s2s-escape}). The exact solution also checks the integrators: gradient
flow on the full TTN follows it on all five trees, every overlap to within $3\cdot10^{-9}$ \empirical{}
(\cref{fig:s2s-aligned} in \cref{app:supp-s2s-aligned}).

\subsection{Escape from the origin}\label{sec:s2s-escape}

How long does the first plateau last? The answer is a blow-up time computed for each initialization, with no
free parameter; the norm of the initialization gives only a lower bound, low by factors from $3$ to $3\cdot10^7$.

\paragraph{Prediction: one blow-up time per initialization.}
On the first plateau $\normF{\W}$ is far below the teacher's weights, and gradient flow is to leading order (LO)
the flow $\dot\params=\nabla g$ of the correlation $g=\ip{\W}{\Wt}$ (\cref{app:escape}). In the time
$\dd\tau=\norm{\params}^{\Kc-2}\dd t$ the direction $\hat\params$ follows the spherical gradient flow of $g$,
independent of $\isc$, and $\dd\ln\norm{\params}/\dd\tau=\Kc\,g(\hat\params)$ (\cref{prop:A-spherical}). Hence
$\norm{\params}$ blows up at
\[
  t^*=\norm{\params_0}^{-(\Kc-2)}\int_0^\infty e^{-(\Kc-2)\Kc G(\tau)}\,\dd\tau,\qquad
  G(\tau)=\int_0^\tau g(\hat\params)\,\dd\tau',
\]
which for $\params_0=\isc Z$ ($Z$ the initialization at $\isc=1$) is $\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}\mathcal{T}(Z)$, with $\mathcal{T}(Z)$
independent of $\isc$ (\cref{cor:A-blowup}). The direction tends to the balanced rank-one configuration of one
component $r_1$, which then escapes as a balanced mode (\cref{prop:A-first-event}):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:s2s-first}
  \tev1=\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}\,\mathcal{T}(Z)+\delta_\Kc\,\tw{r_1}^{-(2-2/\Kc)}+o(1),
\end{equation}
with $\delta_\Kc$ from \eqref{eq:A-first-event} ($\delta_3=-0.850$, $\delta_4=-0.266$, $\delta_7=0.148$,
$\delta_8=0.201$). $\mathcal{T}(Z)$ and $r_1$ come from integrating the LO flow from $Z$ (\cref{rem:A-recipe}), so
\eqref{eq:s2s-first} predicts the exponent, the prefactor and the first component of every run. Status: the
scaling and the blow-up identity are \proven{} (at LO); the offset is \derived{} (the direction must have
converged to $r_1$ by the time $\normF{\W}$ is comparable to $\tw{r_1}$), and so is the first component, which
needs the KKT point reached to be a component configuration (\cref{prop:A-component-hessian}; not for stars,
\cref{rem:A-sequence}). The escape direction is exactly balanced, with core norms
$1/\Kc$ (\cref{prop:A-kkt}) \proven; at the end of the LO flow every core holds $1/\Kc$ of $\norm{\params}^2$ to
within $1.3\%$ in all 1,200 initializations of the $d$ sweep \empirical. \knownresult[the exponent: deep linear
networks, \citealp{jacot2022saddletosaddle}; homogeneous models, \citealp{kumar2025early}, Thm~1; the identity is
the activation law of \citealp{kunin2025alternating}, Eqs.~2--3; balance: \citealp{kumar2025early}, Lemma~5, and
the proof of \citealp{furman2026benign}, Prop.~F.10] \newresult[TTN form: its evaluation on trees and the tests]

\paragraph{Test, seed by seed} (\cref{fig:s2s-first}).
\empirical{} For 231 pairs of initialization and teacher on the five trees (1,848 runs, eight values of $\isc$
each), the prediction matches every run at the smallest $\isc$ to a relative $4\cdot10^{-8}$ to $2.2\cdot10^{-3}$
(the largest for binary4 with the close teacher, whose direction has not converged at $\isc=0.002$). With the
offset removed, the per-seed exponents are $\Kc-2$ to within $0.004$, except in that cell ($0.990$--$0.996$).
Details: \cref{app:supp-s2s-escape}.

\paragraph{The size of the initialization gives only a lower bound.}
From an initialization along a maximizer of $g$ the escape takes
$t_{\rm KH}=\Kc^{\Kc/2-1}\norm{\params_0}^{-(\Kc-2)}/((\Kc-2)\tw1)$ \knownresult[\citealp{kumar2025towards}, text
before Thm~11], and every initialization of the same norm takes longer (\cref{cor:A-kh}) \proven. The median
$\mathcal{T}/t_{\rm KH}$ over the cells of the first-escape sweep is $3.1$--$4.8$ on binary4, $7.9$--$13.7$ on MPS4, $95$--$210$
on HT4, $184$--$2650$ on binary8 and $1460$ on MPS8, and it reaches $2.9\cdot10^7$ on binary16 \empirical{}
\newresult[the bound on trees and the ratios]. Most of $\norm{\params_0}$ sits in core slices orthogonal to the
teacher's factors, which do not move at leading order (\cref{prop:A-reduced}), and the moving slices form an
unbalanced product path (\cref{prop:A-product-mode}; the toy model of the box): the norm law is a bound, not a
prediction.

\paragraph{Dependence on $d$, $\chi$ and $\Kc$.}
\empirical{} The prefactor $\mathcal{T}=\lim_{\isc\to0}\isc^{\Kc-2}\tev1$ falls slowly with $\chi$ (log-log slopes $-0.26$
to $-0.76$, against $-0.47$ to $-2.5$ for $t_{\rm KH}$): $\chi$ acts only through the moving slices. At full bond
$\chi=d^2$ it does not depend on $d$ (binary4 medians $\approx\pi/2$ for $d=2,3,4$, the closed form of
\cref{prop:A-product-mode}, \derived{} given end directions that do not rotate). Its median ranges from $0.42$
(HT3, $\Kc=5$) to $814$ (binary16, $\Kc=15$), growing with $\Kc$ within each tree family, and a heuristic with no
free parameter tracks it within a factor $0.87$--$2.13$ (\conjecture, heuristic). Re-gauging to $\Q e=0$ changes
$\mathcal{T}$ per seed by a factor $0.88$--$1.92$ and leaves the exponent unchanged. Details and
\cref{fig:s2s-prefactor}: \cref{app:supp-s2s-escape}. \newresult

\subsection{Locality of later escapes: two components}\label{sec:s2s-locality}
% Sources: notes/locality.md (final; sections 7, 8, 10); reviews locality_round1.md and
% locality_round2_verification.md (caveats a-g applied); notes/theory_saddles.md v3; theory_saddle_order.md v2;
% pilot experiments/pilot_h2_locality (in app:supp-s2s-locality). Settings: app:exp-locality. WITHDRAWN or
% overstated, do not reintroduce: "H2 holds for k = 1..8" or "+-0.05 for k <= 4"; k = 6-7 = k - 2 without
% "balanced initialization only"; ht8 D = {3,4} "measures k = 7"; "rank rises on the Steiner edges in 98.7% /
% 99.75% of events" as a prediction success rate; "spare bond channels remove most dead routes"; "a stalling seed
% stalls at every sigma"; "steep MPS exponents come from shrinking route ends"; "raw and projected Hessian
% exponents agree to 0.01"; "2 of 1008 stalls with balanced initialization"; "log" labels for k* = 2 levels
% without the fair comparison (TA on MPS4, first level).

After the first escape, gradient flow rests near a saddle that represents what has been learned. Which
component comes next, and how long is the next plateau? In a TTN the answer depends on where the next component
sits in the tree (box at the start of this section). We state the route rule, derived in \cref{app:saddles},
and test it on two-component teachers.

\paragraph{Prediction: the route rule.}
Take a two-component pattern teacher (\cref{sec:setup-loss}), $\Wt=\tw1\U1+\tw2\U2$ with $\tw1>\tw2$, whose
components differ exactly on the site set $D$ (sites numbered from $0$, as in \cref{tab:A-route-degree}). As in
\cref{sec:setup-routes}, the route size is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:locality-k}
  k=\abs{\mathrm{St}(V_D)},
\end{equation}
the number of cores of the Steiner subtree of the cores $V_D$ that carry $D$, and the bonds whose teacher rank
is two are exactly the bonds of $\mathrm{St}(V_D)$ (\cref{prop:teacher-spectra}(b)). Once $\U1$ is learned, the
flow sits near the balanced saddle $\params_1$ that represents $\tw1\U1$, and new parameters can lower the loss
at no Gauss--Newton cost only through routes (\cref{lem:A-routes}); for two-component teachers in the correct
order no other curve descends faster (\cref{cor:A-order}). The cheapest route to $\U2$ is
$\mathrm{St}(V_D)$, with coupling $c_2=\tw2\tw1^{(\Kc-k)/\Kc}$ (\cref{prop:A-steiner}), and the escape is the
first-escape problem of a $k$-core TTN (\cref{prop:A-escape-degree}). With $\Delta t_2=\tev2-\tev1$ the time
between the two learning events,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:locality-rule}
  \Delta t_2\simeq
  \begin{cases}
    O(1)\ \text{(no plateau)}, & k=1,\\[0.5ex]
    \dfrac{1-\tw2/\tw1}{\lambda_2}\,\ln\dfrac1\isc,\qquad \lambda_2=\tw2\,\tw1^{(\Kc-2)/\Kc}, & k=2,\\[1.5ex]
    A\,\isc^{-(k-2)}, & k\ge3,
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
(\cref{prop:A-head-start}). For $k=2$ the saddle is strict, with $\lambda_{\min}(\Hperp)=-\lambda_2$
(\cref{prop:A-hessian}); for $k\ge3$ its Hessian has no negative eigenvalue (a higher-order saddle), and on the
plateau $\lambda_{\min}\propto\isc^{k-2}\to0$ (\cref{prop:A-signatures}). The factor $1-\tw2/\tw1$ is a
\emph{head start}: the route grows during the first plateau, so from the saddle itself the $k=2$ slope is
$1/\lambda_2$. The prefactor $A$ is set by the route amplitudes when the plateau starts and is not predicted
from $\isc$ alone for random initialization. Status: the Steiner identity, the upper bound $k$ on the saddle order
and, for two-component teachers learned in this (greedy) order, the matching lower bound, so that the order is
exactly $k$ on every tree (\cref{cor:A-order}), are \proven; \eqref{eq:locality-rule} is \derived{} (leading
order, small $\isc$). \knownresult[mechanism and trichotomy: \citealp{kumar2026learning}, Thm~8;
\citealp{rawal2026theory}, Thm~4.2; descent along a whole connected set of rank-deficient bonds:
\citealp{furman2026benign}, Lemma~D.8; head start for $k=2$: \citealp{zhang2026saddletosaddle}, Thm~4,
\citealp{kunin2025alternating}, Sec.~2.2] \newresult[the route is the Steiner subtree, which can be a proper
subtree of such a set; the coupling and rate; the head start for $k\ge3$]

\paragraph{Setup} (details in \cref{app:exp-locality}).
Orthogonal pattern teachers with $d=2$ and weights $(1,0.5)$ unless stated, $\chi=2$, whitened inputs, gradient
flow on the exact population loss. Nine trees: MPS with $\Ns=4,6,8$, binary4, binary8, HT4, HT8, and two trees
with $\Ns=6$, \emph{star3} (a root without sites, three children with two sites each) and \emph{midroot6} (the
MPS6 chain rooted at a middle core). Protocols: \emph{small} (fan-in initialization at scale $\isc$, first
plateau included), \emph{saddle} (the exact balanced saddle $\tw1\U1$ plus a fan-in perturbation of scale
$\isc$; $\Delta t_2=\tev2$ times the escape alone) and \emph{balanced} (the fan-in $\Wz$ re-gauged to $\Q e=0$).
Per seed (seeds $0$--$7$) we fit $\ln\Delta t_2$ against $\ln(1/\isc)$ (exponent $p$), or $\Delta t_2$ against
$\ln(1/\isc)$ (the $k=2$ slope), over the values of $\isc$ at which the seed escapes (at least three; fewer than
the row's six or seven for seeds that stall at some $\isc$, \cref{sec:s2s-failure}), and report mean $\pm$ s.e.\
over seeds (median). Log and power laws are compared by the residual of $\ln\Delta t_2$ on the same data
(\emph{fair comparison}). Fits use the runs that learn $\U1$ first, without rotations (an overlap crossing
$\frac12$ more than once). About $11{,}600$ runs in all.

\empirical{} (\cref{fig:locality-exponents}; every row in \cref{tab:locality-exponents}) There is no plateau for

one for $k=2$, and the exponent is $k-2$ for $k=3$ (all nine trees), $4$ and $5$, on trees with $\Kc$ from $3$ to

\paragraph{$k=2$: a logarithmic plateau with a head start.}

\empirical{} Since $k$ depends on the tree, one teacher has different plateau laws on different trees. For

\paragraph{Bond spectra: the new component enters through the Steiner edges only.}
\empirical{} The second component raises the teacher's bond rank exactly on the edges of $\mathrm{St}(V_D)$
(\cref{prop:teacher-spectra}(b)), so the bond spectra show which cores the escape uses (\cref{fig:locality-bonds}).

the initialization-noise level, from the plateau midpoint to the end of the run: $\bs e2\le4.3\,\isc^2\tw2$,

Steiner edge rises, and that they rise together: the edges whose effective rank is higher at $\ov2=0.9$ than at the
plateau midpoint are the Steiner edges in $99.75\%$ of the 4,448 events with a resolved plateau, and their crossing
times differ by a median $5\cdot10^{-5}$ event widths. \newresult

\subsection{When is \texorpdfstring{$k^*$}{k*} the saddle order?}\label{sec:s2s-saddle-order}
% Sources: notes/theory_saddle_order.md v2; saddle_order_verification_proof.md and
% saddle_order_verification_numerics.md. Formal statements and the counterexample: app:saddle-order (A.4).
% Claims review A: M3 ("can fail", not "fails"), M4 (escape law derived, forgetting empirical, seen from small
% initialization only), m24 (the control teacher's exponents). Pending: saddle-dyn (dynamics of the theorem).

The route gives a descent of order $k^*$ at every learned saddle, so the saddle order (\cref{sec:setup-routes}) is
at most $k^*$. Can anything descend faster?

\paragraph{The theorem, informally.}
\proven{} (\cref{thm:A-order-lower,thm:A-order-sharp,cor:A-order}) At a saddle with one learned component $j$,
and with a spare bond channel along the route, the saddle order is exactly $k^*$ unless a piggyback coupling of
the residual on small sets of cores exceeds the learned weight $\tw j$. This never happens for $k^*=2$, nor for
two-component teachers learned in weight order, on any tree. For $k^*=3$ or $4$ it happens exactly when the saddle
is strict ($\lambda_{\max}(\Gamma)>\tw j$ below; equality at $k^*=4$ is open). From $k^*=5$ on it can happen while
the Hessian is positive semidefinite, and the order then drops below $k^*$. Saddles with two or more learned
components are not covered. \newresult

\paragraph{The piggyback idea.}
Besides opening new channels, the flow can rotate the site vectors of single learned cores, piggybacking on the
learned channel. Two such rotations together produce a component that differs from the learned one on exactly
those two cores, at second order and without new channels. The \emph{piggyback graph}
(\cref{prop:A-piggyback-graph}) has one node per pair (core, rotated pattern) and one edge of weight $\tw r$ per
residual component $r$ that differs from the learned one on exactly two cores; if its largest eigenvalue
$\lambda_{\max}(\Gamma)$ exceeds $\tw j$, the learned saddle is strict. This happens when a larger component is
still waiting (a wrong order, \cref{sec:s2s-order}) or when several components share a core (a \emph{star},
\cref{sec:teacher-star}). From $k^*=5$ on, small clusters of cores can piggyback in the same way: new entries on a
cluster whose Steiner subtree has fewer than $k^*/2$ cores, together with rotations of other cores, couple through
the residual, and a generalized piggyback operator (\cref{def:A-gen-piggyback}) decides whether they descend
before the route.

\paragraph{A counterexample.}
On MPS6 the teacher $000000$ (weight $1$), $110001$ and $110010$ ($0.8$ each) has $k^*=5$ and a positive
semidefinite Hessian at the saddle of its first component, yet new entries on cores 0 and 1 aimed at the
non-residual pattern $110000$, together with rotations of cores 4 and 5, descend at order 4 \proven{}
(\cref{app:saddle-order}). Gradient flow from this saddle escapes in $\propto\isc^{-2}$ instead of $\isc^{-3}$
\derived{} (checked on this teacher: 5 seeds from the saddle, and the seeds that visit it from small
initialization), and then forgets the first component \empirical{} (seen from small initialization). For the
matched control with weights $0.6$, which meets the condition and has order $5$, the small-initialization exponent
is about $3$ in some seeds and about $4$ in others, unexplained (\cref{sec:s2s-failure}).

\paragraph{Which experiments satisfy the condition.}
Every two-component fit of \cref{sec:s2s-locality} (the runs that learn the larger component first), and every
saddle with one learned component of the multi-component teachers of \cref{sec:s2s-several} (there

\subsection{Several components}\label{sec:s2s-several}
% Sources: notes/locality.md sections 5 and 7.4; experiments/locality/results/levels_summary.csv,
% route_sequences.json; teachers as in app:exp-locality. Claims review A: m5 (levels found, one exception),
% m26 (error bars for B4 and TA).

\paragraph{Prediction: the route rule at rank-constrained optima.}
With several learned components ``the sites where the next component differs'' is ambiguous, and saddles need not
be partial sums of components. The \emph{route rule} (\cref{rem:A-sequence}) works at the saddle actually
reached, the best approximation of $\Wt$ with the current bond-rank profile: if the learned structure is stable
(no wrong order, no star, and from $k^*=5$ on no generalized star; \cref{sec:s2s-saddle-order}), the next escape
takes the smallest route, of size $k^*$, with a nonzero local residual (\cref{def:A-local-residual}), raises the
bond rank on its internal edges, and the flow moves to the best approximation with the new profile. We evaluate
it numerically (rank-constrained optima by L-BFGS with restarts); for two components $k^*=k$. Status: \derived{}
at leading order for each step, \conjecture{} as an algorithm.

\paragraph{Test.}
\empirical{} (predictions \derived{} at leading order) Seven teachers (patterns as site values, weights in
brackets): TA $0000$ ($1$), $1100$ ($0.6$), $1110$ ($0.35$); TB as TA plus $1111$ ($0.2$); N4 $0000$ ($1$),
$1100$ ($0.6$), $1111$ ($0.4$); B4 $0000$ ($1$), $0110$ ($0.3$), $1001$ ($0.6$); E8 $0^8$ ($1$), $0^31^20^3$
($0.4$), $010^41\,0$ ($0.7$); TC $0^8$ ($1$), $1^20^6$ ($0.6$), $1^40^4$ ($0.35$), $1^8$ ($0.2$); TD $0^8$ ($1$),
$0^31^20^3$ ($0.6$), $1^20^6$ ($0.35$); $\chi=2$ ($3$ for B4, N4, E8; \cref{app:exp-locality}). We detect the
plateaus of $\Loss(t)$ (separation ratio above $3$, linear time) and match them to the levels predicted by the
route rule (within $5\%$). Every predicted level with $k^*\ge3$ is found as a plateau ($88$--$100\%$ of the runs;
$35$--$65\%$ for a few $k^*=3$ levels within $10\%$ of the next one), with one exception below, and its duration
exponent is within $0.2$ of $k^*-2$ ($1.02$--$1.19$, $2.02$--$2.08$, $2.97$--$3.17$, $4.05$ for $k^*=3,4,5,6$).
The exception is B4's second level on HT4 with fan-in initialization, $4.73\pm0.90$ (limited range: 4 fitted
seeds, 23 of 48 runs stalled; $3.17\pm0.10$ with balanced initialization). The $k^*=2$ levels are logarithmic
where measured over three decades from the saddle (four levels of TC, the first of TD); elsewhere (TD's second
level, the first levels on MPS8 from small initialization) a power law below $1$ fits better and the law is not
determined. At the predicted saddles the measured bond-rank profile equals the predicted one in $93.9\%$ of 3,114
resolved (run, level) pairs. \newresult

\paragraph{Locality beats weight.}
\empirical{} On MPS4 all 60 runs learn B4's $0.3$ component before its $0.6$ component, after a $k^*=2$ level too
short to be detected as a plateau; the $0.6$ component follows after a $\isc^{-2}$ plateau ($p=2.015\pm0.013$, 5
seeds). On binary4 all 48 runs learn them in weight order, after two $\isc^{-1}$
plateaus ($1.051\pm0.008$ and $1.028\pm0.005$, 8 seeds). As $\isc\to0$ a logarithmic route beats a power-law
route whatever the weights (\cref{rem:A-sequence}) \derived; at finite $\isc$ weights and prefactors compete.
\newresult

\paragraph{The route rule, not the literal rule.}
\empirical{} Three teachers discriminate between rules. The literal rule (the Steiner count of the sites where
the next component differs from the closest learned one) predicts no plateau before the third component of TA on
any tree; there is a $\isc^{-1}$ plateau on binary4 ($1.023\pm0.005$, 8 seeds) and a $\isc^{-2}$ one on HT4
($2.084\pm0.040$, 7 seeds), as the route rule predicts (\cref{fig:locality-trees}). For TD on MPS8 (from the
saddle) the last plateau has $k=2$ by the literal rule, $k=4$ by the exact bond-rank rule (cores whose bond rank
must grow to add the component exactly, \cref{lem:A-general-S}) and $k^*=3$ by the route rule, which first
learns part of the component through a two-core route: measured $1.012\pm0.010$ (8 seeds). For TC on MPS8 (from
the saddle) the route rule predicts a $k^*=4$ plateau where the bond-rank rule predicts $k=5$: measured
$2.018\pm0.017$. \newresult

\paragraph{Whole sequences: a saddle hopper.}\label{sec:s2s-hopper}
\empirical{} (bookkeeping of known theory) The sequence rules of this section (first escape, route rule, branches
after a wrong first escape, race of logarithmic routes) were implemented as a predictor that hops from saddle to
saddle, and scored on all committed runs without tuning (\cref{app:supp-s2s-hopper}). Where its prediction is
informative (one predicted order, run not stalled) it matches $97.0\%$ of the event sequences, against $95.8\%$ for
the baseline ``learn the components in weight order''; it beats the baseline only on the 107 runs where the route
rule overrides the weight order (B4 on MPS4, E8 on MPS8: $88.8\%$ against $0\%$). Its race across several saddles of
the nested teacher TC (MPS8, from the saddle) predicts five plateau slopes within $3.5\%$ \empirical{} \newresult.

587 rank-one saddles that the scored runs can reach, the condition of \cref{sec:s2s-saddle-order} fails at 30, where

Which component is learned first, and how random is the answer? With aligned initialization every tree learns the
components in the order of their weights (\cref{sec:s2s-aligned}), and a deep linear network of depth three leaves
the origin along its top singular pair as $\isc\to0$ \knownresult[\citealp{jacot2022saddletosaddle}, Thm~2; with
probability one if the gradient at the origin has full rank]. In a TTN with random initialization the answer
depends on $m=\abs{V_D}$, the number of cores that carry a site on which the two components differ (for odeco
teachers $m=\Ns$ on an MPS, $4$ on binary8 and $2$ on binary4): the order is a race set by the initialization when
$m\ge3$ and greedy as $\isc\to0$ when $m=2$.

\paragraph{Prediction.}
As $\isc\to0$ the first component learned is the local maximum of $g=\ip{\W}{\Wt}$ on the sphere whose basin
contains the initial direction, since the spherical flow does not depend on $\isc$ (\cref{sec:s2s-escape})
\derived{} \knownresult[convergence to a KKT point selected by the initialization: \citealp{kumar2025early},
Thm~1]. For $m\ge3$ every component is such a maximum (\emph{tensor-like} pairs); for $m=2$ only the larger one,
the smaller having $n$ unstable directions, with $n=\bd e$ if its two cores share the bond $e$ and $n=1$ otherwise
(\emph{matrix-like} pairs; \cref{prop:A-component-hessian}) \derived. With weights $(1,w)$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item \emph{Race.} On an MPS the components of an odeco teacher evolve independently at leading order
    (\cref{prop:A-reduced}); component $r$ blows up at $\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}\hat t_{1,r}/\tw r$ with $\hat t_{1,r}$ \iid,
    so
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:order-race}
      P(\text{larger first})=P\big(\hat t_{1,2}/\hat t_{1,1}>w\big),
    \end{equation}
    independent of $\isc$ (\cref{prop:A-race}) \proven{} (at LO). Cores without sites (binary, HT trees) couple
    the components and make the race approximate.
  \item \emph{Matrix-like pairs.} $P(\text{smaller first})\to0$ \derived. To end near the smaller component, all
    $n$ unstable deviations must start below $\isc^{(1-w)/w}$ (\cref{prop:A-matrix-like}(b)), which gives
    $P(\text{smaller first})\propto\isc^{\gamma}$ with $\gamma=n(1-w)/w$: for $n=1$ \empirical{} (heuristic
    mechanism; the data below support it), for $n=\bd e$ a \conjecture. When a wrong order does happen, the
    deviation is therefore of order one at the start of the wrong plateau, and the correction in (iii) takes a time
    $O(1/\abs{\lambda_{\min}})$, not $\ln(1/\isc)/\abs{\lambda_{\min}}$ \empirical{} (heuristic mechanism).
  \item \emph{After a wrong first escape} (\cref{rem:A-wrong-order}) \derived{}, with the Hessian at the saddle
    \proven{} (\cref{prop:A-hessian,prop:A-piggyback-graph}) and $k$ the Steiner count \eqref{eq:locality-k}:
    \emph{rotation} for $m=2$, $k\ge3$: the saddle is strict along the piggyback direction,
    $\lambda_{\min}=-(1-w)w^{(\Kc-2)/\Kc}$; the learned bond index turns to $\U1$, and $\U2$ is forgotten (the loss
    rests at $\frac12w^2$) and relearned after a plateau $\propto\isc^{-(k-2)}$. \emph{Adding} for $m=2$, $k=2$: a
    two-core route with $\lambda_{\min}=-w^{(\Kc-2)/\Kc}$ (multiplicity $\bd e-1$) puts $\U1$ on a spare channel and
    nothing is forgotten. \emph{Kept} for $m\ge3$: no negative Hessian eigenvalue; $\U1$ is added after a plateau
    $\propto\isc^{-(k-2)}$ and the wrong order stays.
\end{enumerate}
A correction through a strict saddle thus exists only where the wrong order becomes rare as $\isc\to0$.

\paragraph{Setup} (\cref{app:exp-order}).
Two-component teachers $\Wt=\U1+w\U2$: odeco on five trees, or pattern teachers on MPS4 and MPS6 whose components
differ on the sites $D$ (numbered from $0$; on an MPS core $i$ carries site $i$), in the standard site basis, which
the rotation invariance of the initialization makes general; $d=2$ unless stated, fan-in initialization, exact
population loss. The component learned first is the one whose overlap $\ov r$ first crosses $\frac12$; probabilities
are frequencies over seeds $0$--$399$ ($0$--$999$ in the $\isc$ scans of binary4 and MPS4), the same seeds for

\paragraph{Status.}
The order is set by the basin of the initial direction for tensor-like pairs (\proven{} at LO on MPS, \derived{}
elsewhere; \empirical{} on four trees), not for matrix-like pairs, which become greedy as $\isc\to0$. A wrong order
is corrected through a strict saddle in a time $\propto\ln(1/\isc)$ only from the saddle and only for $m=2$ pairs on
non-adjacent cores; natural runs correct in $O(1/\abs{\lambda_{\min}})$, adjacent pairs add the larger component,
and tensor-like pairs keep the wrong order. Open: the prefactor of the $m=2$ law, the exponent of adjacent pairs,
and a closed-form race when cores without sites couple the components.

A saddle-to-saddle run can end \emph{stalled} (\cref{sec:setup-diagnostics}): with a dead route (trap), whose
route ends have decayed to roundoff; on a slow route, live but with a plateau longer than the budget; or at the
floor of an unrealizable centred target (\cref{sec:other-lazy}). This subsection treats dead and slow routes in the
odeco runs of \cref{sec:s2s-phenomenology}, whose routes span the tree, in the pattern-teacher runs of
\cref{sec:s2s-locality}, and in the regime maps of \cref{sec:regime-map} (ends and slices of a route:
\cref{sec:setup-routes}).

\paragraph{Mechanism of dead routes.}
\derived{} (reduced model, \cref{prop:A-trap}) The conservation laws (\cref{prop:conservation}) tie the new
entries of each route end to the imbalance of its bond, of order $\isc^2$ under fan-in initialization. On a plateau
the Gauss--Newton term makes the new entries of an end that the imbalance does not favour decay at a rate of order
$\isc^2$. Decay in a time $\propto\isc^{-2}$ competes with an escape in $\propto\isc^{-(k-2)}$: for $k=3$ the route
escapes first, for $k=4$ both are of the same order, and for $k\ge5$ the ends are decided long before the route
could escape. Exactly balanced initialization ($\Q e=0$) is expected to protect the ends \conjecture{} (for ends
that are tree leaves see below; in four-layer matrix factorization exactly balanced initialization can fail,
\citealp{luo2025global}).
\knownresult[related shrinkage of dormant weights: \citealp{jacot2022saddletosaddle}, App.~C.1;
\citealp{kumar2025towards}; \citealp{kunin2025alternating}, Lemma~B.1]

\paragraph{A criterion from the initialization (pending verification).}
\derived{} (one learned component, leading order in the small entries, $\isc\to0$) On the plateau only the new
entries normal to the learned manifold pay a Gauss--Newton cost. For a route end $u$ with route neighbour $w$ this
conserves $C_u=Q_{\nu\nu}+\norm{Y_{\rm nv}}^2-\norm{X_{\rm nv}}^2$, the Gram of the end's new entries minus its
partner's ($Q_{\nu\nu}$ the imbalance of their bond on the route's channel; $X_{\rm nv}$, $Y_{\rm nv}$ the new entries of
$u$ and $w$ that pair with nothing at second order), and the end dies iff $C_u<0$ and the route is long enough
($k\ge4$ if $w$ carries sites, $k\ge6$ at an HT or binary leaf). A route is dead iff two or more of its ends die;
with one dying end it is pinned and escapes after a time $\propto\isc^{-(2k-6)}$; routes with $k\le3$ never trap.
The state at the start of the plateau is $\isc$ times a function of the initialization direction (through the
$\isc$-free correlation flow of \cref{sec:s2s-escape}), so the criterion is computed from the initialization
alone, and whether a run traps depends on its direction, not on $\isc$. Under fan-in initialization (saddle start,
partner with sites) an end dies with probability $I_{F_u/(F_u+F_w)}(n_u/2,n_w/2)$ ($I$ the regularized incomplete
beta function, $F$ the fan-ins, $n$ the numbers of physical directions normal to the learned pattern): $0.392$ at an
MPS end that is a tree leaf and $0.5$ at the root end ($d=\chi=2$). With balanced initialization ends that are tree
leaves cannot die, and spare bond dimension protects an end only when it reaches that end's own route edge, which
reconciles the MPS8 stalls at $\chi=2\ncomp$ with the binary trees. \empirical{} Without any gradient flow the
criterion separates the 3,492 fan-in locality runs ($89\%$ of the predicted-dead runs stall, against $1.8\%$ of the
predicted-alive ones); in the pilot 102 of 109 predicted-dead runs die; on MPS4 at $\chi=2$ all 150 predicted-dead
runs stall or die, and none of the other 344. For the odeco teachers the trap is predicted only from the state
after the second escape (20 of 20 MPS4 and 6 of 6 HT4 runs checked), not from the initialization. Known failures:
$k=4$ with spare inner channels, the marginal case $k=6$ on HT4, $\isc\ge0.2$, and the multi-stage odeco traps.
Details: \cref{app:supp-s2s-trap}. \knownresult[the mechanism overlaps \citealp{furman2026benign}, Thm~G.1, on
imbalance, and \citealp{kunin2025alternating}, Lemma~B.1]

\paragraph{Odeco teachers: dead routes grow with $\Kc$} (\cref{fig:s2s-dormant}).

Spare bond channels remove the stalls only on routes with an uncapped end; on routes with both ends capped they rise
from 90 to 106 at $\chi=4$. Counts and the effect on the exponents: \cref{app:supp-s2s-failure}. \newresult

\paragraph{The regime maps meet the same failures.}\label{sec:s2s-stalls-maps}
\empirical{} (confirming the prediction of \cref{app:boundaries}; mechanism \conjecture, heuristic) With $\chi$
equal to the teacher rank $\ncomp$ the last component needs a route through all $\Kc$ cores, which can die for
$\Kc\ge5$ (already at $\Kc=4$ for some initializations, as above). In a stall test at $\chi=\ncomp=3$
(\cref{tab:stall-test}) most runs on binary8 and every fan-in run on MPS8 do not learn the last component within
the budget (9 of 18 fan-in MPS8 runs learn only the first), in the plain as in the centred model, so the initial
function is not the cause; from fan-in initialization the route is dead (28 of 32 stalled plain runs), and balanced
initialization removes the dead routes but not the stalls. In the plain-model $\isc\times\Kc$ sweeps 546 of the 795
stalled runs end with the $\ncomp$-th bond singular value at roundoff, a dead route (182 of the 795 stopped at the
step limit). Spare channels remove the
stalls only where no route end is capped: on binary8 at $d=3$, $\chi=6$ no run stalls at $\isc\le0.63$ (30 runs),
but on MPS8 every run stalls (50 of 50), its end bonds being capped at $d=3$, the teacher rank; one spare channel per
end is not enough either (MPS8 and HT4 at $d=4$, above). With the centred model at $\chi=4$ the stalls below the
saddle-to-saddle boundary sit at a partial fit, fixed by the initialization (\cref{sec:regime-K}). At practical
budgets, stalls thus occupy part of the saddle-to-saddle side of every deep tree whose route ends are capped.

\paragraph{Long plateaus are not barren plateaus.}\label{sec:s2s-barren}
\derived{} From small initialization the gradient is $O(\isc^{\Kc-1})$ and the first plateau lasts
$\propto\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}$: deep trees at small $\isc$ stall as if on a barren plateau. The cause differs: the gradient
is small because the initialization is, not because it concentrates around zero over random initializations of
fixed norm; with the fan-in normalization at $\isc=1$ the initial function has unit norm on every tree
(\cref{prop:init-norm}). \knownresult[barren plateaus of tensor networks: global but not local losses of
unitary-embedded MPS, \citealp{liu2022presence}; absent for isometric MPS, TTNS and MERA,
\citealp{miao2024isometric,barthel2025absence}]

\paragraph{What the mechanism explains, and what it does not.}
\empirical{} The odeco runs show the signature of the mechanism where it was measured ($\Kc=4$, separated
teacher), and balanced initialization leaves no dead route in either experiment. The first criterion of the reduced
model (\cref{prop:A-trap}) was only weakly associated with death in the pattern-teacher runs (30 of 55 runs with both
ends unfavourable died, 7 of 67 with a favourable end); the criterion above replaces it, pending its verification.
Open: the very late escapes of balanced MPS8 routes (delays of $10^5$ and more), the mechanism of the rise of the
stalls on capped routes with spare channels, and the steep exponents of some MPS routes with $k=5$ (MPS8 $\{1,5\}$
from the saddle, $3.67\pm0.20$, limited range), which go with shrinking route ends. Details:
\cref{app:supp-s2s-failure}.
