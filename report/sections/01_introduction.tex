\section{Introduction and summary of findings}\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{Question and motivation}\label{sec:intro-question}

\input{tikz/teaser}
Tensor networks whose contraction pattern is a tree (\emph{tree tensor networks}, TTNs), among them
matrix product states (MPS, or tensor trains), hierarchical Tucker (HT) formats and binary trees, are
used as machine-learning models for classification and generative modelling
\citep{stoudenmire2016supervised,novikov2018exponential,han2018unsupervised,stoudenmire2018learning,%
cheng2019tree,liu2019machine,glasser2019expressive}, and their expressive power has been related to
that of deep networks \citep{cohen2016expressive,levine2018deep}. How they train is less understood.
Work on their trainability has focused on barren plateaus, which occur for global but not for local
losses of unitary-embedded MPS \citep{liu2022presence} and are absent from the energy optimization of
isometric MPS, TTNS and MERA \citep{miao2024isometric,barthel2025absence}. Which dynamics gradient
methods produce on a TTN, from which initializations, and why, has not been characterized
(\cref{sec:intro-prior}).

A TTN regression model, $f(x)=\ip{\W}{\feat{0}(x)\otimes\dots\otimes\feat{\Ns-1}(x)}$ with
$\W$ contracted from $\Kc$ cores (\cref{sec:setup-model}), is multilinear in the cores and homogeneous
of degree $\Kc$ in them, like a deep linear network. There, small initialization gives saddle-to-saddle
(incremental) learning, in which the components of the target are learned one at a time after plateaus
that lengthen as the initialization shrinks \citep{saxe2014exact,jacot2022saddletosaddle}, and large
initialization gives lazy training, in which the model stays close to its linearization
\citep{jacot2018neural,chizat2019lazy}. What does the tree add? Four things that a product of matrices
lacks. (i)~A component of the target can be \emph{local} on the tree: it can differ from what has been
learned on a few input positions (\emph{sites}) only, and then only the cores that hold those sites, and
the cores between them, need to change (\cref{fig:teaser}a). (ii)~The number of cores $\Kc$ and the
depth of the tree are different quantities: an MPS with $\Ns$ sites has depth $\Ns-1$, a binary tree
$\log_2\Ns-1$, and the binary tree with four sites (binary4) has depth one but is a product of three
matrices, a depth-3 linear network. (iii)~The bond dimension $\chi$ plays the role of width, but what a
bond can carry is capped by its \emph{cut dimension}, the largest rank that the matricization of $\W$
across that bond can have. (iv)~Teachers can be genuinely tensorial, so that the correlation
$\ip{\W}{\Wt}$ has many local maxima on the sphere.

We ask, for gradient flow on the exact population loss of a teacher (\cref{sec:setup}), which saddles
are visited from small initialization (Q1); how the plateaus scale with the initialization scale $\isc$,
the number of cores $\Kc$ and the bond dimension $\chi$ (Q2); how the position of a component on the
tree sets its escape (Q3, \emph{locality}); which component is learned first (Q4); which regimes occur
and where their boundaries lie (Q5); and what an input covariance (Q6), the structure of the teacher
(Q7), discrete steps, noise and finite samples (Q8), the optimizers used for tensor networks (Q9: Adam,
the sweeps of alternating least squares (ALS) and of the density-matrix renormalization group (DMRG),
Riemannian methods) and real data (Q10) change. The model is kept minimal (whitened Gaussian inputs
unless stated, realizable teachers, double precision; \cref{sec:setup-model} says why), so that theory
can be compared with experiment number by number. The main focus is the saddle-to-saddle regime
(\cref{sec:s2s}).

\subsection{Summary of findings}\label{sec:intro-summary}

\paragraph{The answer in six points.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item From small initialization, gradient flow learns the teacher one component at a time. Each
    plateau sits near a critical point with all bond ranks fixed, and each escape raises the bond ranks
    along a \emph{route}, a connected set of cores whose new entries must grow together
    (\cref{fig:teaser}; definitions in \cref{sec:setup-routes}).
  \item The first plateau lasts $\propto\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}$, with a prefactor that one integration computes
    for each initialization before training. The estimate from the norm of the initialization alone is
    only a lower bound, off by factors of up to $3\cdot10^7$.
  \item The tree sets the later plateaus. The next component needs the $k^*$ cores of the smallest
    subtree that contains the cores carrying the sites where it differs (a \emph{Steiner subtree}), and
    the next plateau is absent ($k^*=1$), $\propto\ln(1/\isc)$ ($k^*=2$) or $\propto\isc^{-(k^*-2)}$
    ($k^*\ge3$). Hence, as $\isc\to0$, locality beats weight, and the same teacher has different plateau
    laws on an MPS and on a binary tree. At a saddle with one learned component and a spare bond channel
    along the route, $k^*$ is exactly the order of the saddle under a spectral condition; from $k^*=5$ on
    the order can be lower even when the saddle is not strict.
  \item Which component comes first is a race set by the initialization when two components differ on
    sites carried by three or more cores; when they differ on two cores, learning becomes greedy as
    $\isc\to0$. In every case tested, teachers whose components are not orthogonal make the saddle after
    the first escape strict on an MPS, but not on binary and HT trees.
  \item At larger $\isc$, with the initial function removed, training becomes rich and then lazy. A lazy
    regime exists only when every bond reaches its cut dimension; below it training stalls on a loss
    floor.
  \item Gradient descent (GD) with steps below a sharp stability limit, $2/(\Kc\tw1^{2-2/\Kc})$ with $\tw1$
    the largest teacher weight, keeps the saddle sequence on a shifted clock; gradient noise (SGD) does
    not shorten the first plateau on average, lengthens later ones and can stall a run on one (stochastic
    collapse); Adam keeps the first component but leaves the origin in a number of steps that does not
    depend on $\isc$, until its regularizer $\epsilon$ brings the gradient-flow law back at small $\isc$;
    ALS, DMRG and Riemannian gradient descent remove the plateaus. What decides is invariance: steps proportional to the gradient vanish near the
    origin, steps invariant under rescaling the gradient do not, and methods equivariant under the gauge
    group of the network see the initialization only through its frames. In short, the tree, the teacher
    and the input covariance shape the landscape; $\isc$ and the step size mostly set the clock.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Main findings.}
\Cref{tab:intro-summary} sorts the findings by research question. Each item below states a claim, the scale of its evidence and its status; numbers and scope are in the
sections cited, and novelty is discussed in \cref{sec:intro-prior}.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.,ref=\arabic*,leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item\label{it:intro-s2s} \textbf{Saddle-to-saddle learning on tensor networks}
    (\cref{sec:s2s-phenomenology}). From small initialization, gradient flow learns an orthogonal teacher
    one component at a time on binary4, MPS4, binary8 and HT4. Each plateau is the critical point with all
    bond ranks fixed, each escape raises every bond rank by one (497 of 531 runs), and the plateaus grow as
    $\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}$ at higher-order saddles (over less than a decade of $\isc$ at $\Kc=7,8$; not cleanly
    on HT4) \empirical{}
    (predicted \derived).
  \item\label{it:intro-first} \textbf{The first escape: a prefactor for each initialization}
    (\cref{sec:s2s-escape}). Near the origin the norm of the parameters blows up at
    $\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}\mathcal{T}(Z)$, an integral along the spherical flow of the correlation $g=\ip{\W}{\Wt}$ from
    the initialization direction $Z$ \proven, plus an $O(1)$ offset \derived. Computed before training, it
    predicts the first escape (231 pairs) and its component (1,847 of 1,848 runs) \empirical; the
    norm-based time is a lower bound \proven, low by up to $3\cdot10^7$ \empirical.
  \item\label{it:intro-route} \textbf{The route rule}
    (\cref{sec:s2s-locality,sec:s2s-several}). With one learned component, the next one's cheapest route
    is the Steiner subtree of the cores carrying its differing sites \proven{}
    (orthogonal pattern teachers): its plateau is absent, logarithmic or $\propto\isc^{-(k^*-2)}$ for
    $k^*=1$, $2$, $\ge3$ \derived. In about 11,600 runs on nine trees the exponent is $k-2$ for $k\le5$
    (named exceptions), and the rule predicts three- or four-component levels (one exception)
    \empirical{} (as an algorithm: \conjecture).
  \item\label{it:intro-order-thm} \textbf{When $k^*$ is the saddle order} (\cref{sec:s2s-saddle-order}). With
    one learned component $j$ and a spare bond channel along the route, the saddle order is exactly $k^*$
    when the residual's ``piggyback'' coupling on small sets of cores stays below the learned weight
    $\tw j$, as for two-component teachers learned in weight order \proven; several learned components are
    not covered. From $k^*=5$ on it can be lower at a non-strict saddle: a three-component MPS6 teacher
    with $k^*=5$ has order $4$ \proven{} (gradient flow escapes in $\propto\isc^{-2}$, not $\isc^{-3}$, \derived).
    \placeholder{Pending (saddle-dyn): one sentence on the pre-registered test of the theorem's dynamics.}
  \item\label{it:intro-strict} \textbf{Strict or higher-order}
    (\cref{sec:s2s-locality,sec:teacher-generic}). With one learned component of a pattern teacher the
    saddle is strict iff a two-core route exists or the piggyback coupling exceeds $\tw j$ \proven.
    Non-orthogonal teachers open two-core routes where adjacent cores carry sites: on an MPS the
    rank-one saddle is strict, its escape logarithmic at the predicted rate, while binary and HT trees (no
    sites on internal cores) stay higher-order (13 teachers, $\Ns=4,8$) \derived{} \empirical.
  \item\label{it:intro-order} \textbf{Order of learning} (\cref{sec:s2s-order}). For
    two components differing on three or more cores, a $\isc$-independent race set by the initialization
    decides which comes first (exact at leading order on an MPS \proven; 67,200 runs
    \empirical); on two cores, learning becomes greedy as $\isc\to0$ \derived, the wrong order dying out like
    a power of $\isc$ \empirical{} (heuristic mechanism; adjacent cores: \conjecture). As $\isc\to0$ a smaller
    component with a shorter route wins (locality beats weight) \derived{} \empirical.
  \item\label{it:intro-failure} \textbf{Dead routes} (\cref{sec:s2s-failure}). Runs stall,
    often with a dead route (an end core decayed to roundoff): 398 of 5,682 pattern-teacher runs, all with
    $k\ge4$, and most runs with $\Kc=7,8$ and $\chi$ at the teacher rank \empirical. A conserved quantity of
    each route end decides which ends die \derived; for pattern teachers it is computed from the
    initialization alone ($93\%$ of predicted-dead runs stall, $15\%$ of predicted-pinned ones, with one
    dying end, and $1.8\%$ of predicted-alive ones; gaps from $k=6$ on) \empirical.
  \item\label{it:intro-regimes} \textbf{The map of regimes} (\cref{sec:regime-map,sec:other-lazy,%
    sec:other-rich,sec:other-large-chi}). With the initial function removed, raising $\isc$ leads from
    saddle-to-saddle through a rich window to lazy training \empirical, which needs full bonds \derived{}
    \empirical; on binary4 both boundaries scale as $\chi^{-1/2}$, the lazy one as predicted \derived{}
    \empirical. The saddle-to-saddle boundary rises with $\Kc$; with the initial function kept and
    spare channels, a limit of $1$ is rejected at $\Kc\le15$ (binary trees, post hoc) \empirical. At large $\chi$ a
    $\mu$P-type parameterization keeps the kernel moving \derived{} \empirical.
  \item\label{it:intro-optim} \textbf{Steps, noise and optimizers}
    (\cref{sec:other-optim,sec:other-optimizers}). GD is stable at the balanced global minimum only below
    $\lr_c=2/(\Kc\tw1^{2-2/\Kc})$ \proven, and below it keeps gradient flow's sequence on an $O(\lr)$-shifted
    clock \derived{} \empirical. SGD keeps the first-plateau law \derived, lengthens later plateaus on average
    and can stall a run \empirical{} (law: \conjecture). Adam keeps the first component, with a
    $\isc$-independent first escape until $\epsilon$ restores the law \derived{} \empirical; ALS, DMRG and
    Riemannian GD have no plateaus \derived, and ALS and DMRG do not see $\isc$ \proven.
  \item\label{it:intro-cov} \textbf{Covariance and teachers} (\cref{sec:covariance-teacher}). A
    product covariance preconditions a whitened problem \proven; an aligned one reweights race and
    landscape \derived{} \empirical. Rank-one plateaus of random MPS4 teachers sit at the
    bond-rank-one critical point, separated from every SVD-type truncation for 3 of 5 teachers (not at
    higher ranks); binary8 students learn bottom-up; stars (smaller components sharing a core) make a flow
    started at a larger learned one forget it \empirical.
  \item\label{it:intro-real} \textbf{Real data} (\cref{sec:real-data}). On MNIST a data-only share of
    route couplings correlates with the final loss across pixel orders (pre-registered) \empirical; why is a
    \conjecture.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What is known and what is new}\label{sec:intro-prior}
% Sources: notes/literature.md v1.2 and notes/reviews/citation_check.md (part b, rows B1-B27); the
% novelty paragraphs of Secs. 4.4 and 4.5 and the novelty notes of the old findings list are merged here.
A systematic literature search (about 270 queries, arXiv listings up to October 2026, and a check of
every citation and of prior work for every new result; \cref{app:reproducibility}) found no paper on the
saddle-to-saddle dynamics of gradient flow on TTNs with dense cores and $\Kc\ge3$; it is weakest for
2025--2026 work on training MPS and tensor trains. This is the only place where novelty is discussed;
elsewhere the markers \knownresult{} and \newresult{} carry it. Findings are numbered as in
\cref{sec:intro-summary}.

\paragraph{Deep linear networks.} They have exact solutions from decoupled initialization
\citep{saxe2014exact,saxe2019mathematical}, incremental learning
\citep{gidel2019implicit,gissin2020implicit,arora2019implicit,li2021towards}, and saddle-to-saddle
dynamics from small initialization, with the first escape proven and the later saddles conjectured
\citep{jacot2022saddletosaddle}; the full sequence is proven at depth two \citep{jiang2023algorithmic},
the TTN with two cores, and for decoupled or identity-type deeper factorizations
\citep{chou2024gradient}. With three or more matrices every saddle that selects the top singular values,
visited from an exactly balanced initialization, is non-strict (full-rank targets with distinct singular
values; \citealp[Thm~7 and the remark after Prop.~8]{achour2024loss}); balance is
conserved by gradient flow \citep{arora2018optimization,du2018algorithmic,kunin2021neural,marcotte2023abide}.
Binary4 is such a network (\cref{rem:A-binary4-dln}), so its saddle sequence (finding~\ref{it:intro-s2s}),
greedy limit \citep[Thm~2]{jacot2022saddletosaddle} and strict wrong-order saddles
\citep[Thm~7]{achour2024loss}, \citep[Lemma~15]{jacot2022saddletosaddle} (finding~\ref{it:intro-order}) are
known, as are the decoupled modes of aligned initialization, our case $k=\Kc$, and, for two layers, the
reweighting by a commuting input covariance \citep[Thm~2]{gidel2019implicit} and early alignment
\citep{atanasov2022neural} (finding~\ref{it:intro-cov}). New: the sequence on genuine tensor networks
(MPS4, binary8, HT4) and the preconditioned form of a product covariance on trees.

\paragraph{Homogeneous models near the origin.} Their weights align with a KKT point of the correlation with the
target, selected by the initialization \citep[Thm~1]{kumar2025early}, and escape after a time that grows
as a negative power of the initialization \citep{kumar2025early,kumar2025towards}; dormant weights of the
lowest homogeneity degree stay small for a time of the same kind \citep[Thm~8]{kumar2026learning};
escapes that need $1$, $2$ or $k\ge3$ small layers take a time of order one, logarithmic, or
$\propto\isc^{-(k-2)}$, with $\isc$ their scale \citep[Thm~4.2]{rawal2026theory}; alternating
gradient flows carry dormant amplitudes across phases \citep{kunin2025alternating}; weaker directions get
a head start in two-layer linear networks \citep[Thm~4]{zhang2026saddletosaddle}; and the leap of
\citet{abbe2023sgd} counts the coordinates that must be learned at once. Hence known in our setting: the
exponent $\Kc-2$ of the first escape \citep{jacot2022saddletosaddle,kumar2025early}, its per-initialization
blow-up identity (the activation law of \citealp[Eqs.~2--3]{kunin2025alternating}), the norm-based time
as the escape time from a maximizer \citep{kumar2025towards}, the balance of the escape direction (linear
chains: \citealp[Lemma~5]{kumar2025early}; it also follows from the proof of
\citealp[Prop.~F.10]{furman2026benign}) and the head start of two-core routes. New: the evaluation on
trees, the per-run tests and the gap to the norm-based time (finding~\ref{it:intro-first}); and which cores
are dormant later: the Steiner route of the residual's sites, its coupling and rate, and the closed-form
head start for $3\le k<\Kc$ (finding~\ref{it:intro-route}).

\paragraph{The landscape of our model.} \Citet{furman2026benign} study it for exactly our model and
loss: minimum-norm local minima are global; at a minimum-norm critical point every connected component of
rank-deficient bonds gives a descent curve whose order is its number of cores, and with several
components ``the smallest core delivers the least suppressed escape'' \citep[Lemmas~D.8--D.9,
Remark~D.10 and Fig.~4]{furman2026benign}; one parity saddle has a lower bound on its order; points off the
minimum-norm set can be spurious minima \citep[Thm~G.1]{furman2026benign}. For two cores their curve has
negative curvature, so the saddle is strict (a one-line consequence, not stated there). They do not treat
dynamics. New relative to this: a route can be a proper subtree of one rank-deficient component, so $k^*$
can lie far below its number of cores ($\Kc$ at a rank-one saddle with $\chi\ge2$;
finding~\ref{it:intro-route}); the matching lower bound on the saddle order with one learned component,
and that it can fail from $k^*=5$ on even when the saddle is not strict (finding~\ref{it:intro-order-thm}; lower bounds were known for deep linear
networks, order two or more, \citealp[Thm~7]{achour2024loss}, and for one parity saddle,
\citealp[Prop.~5.3]{furman2026benign}); the piggyback criterion for strict learned saddles with
$k^*\ge3$, and the forgetting by stars (findings~\ref{it:intro-strict},~\ref{it:intro-cov}; in deep linear
networks the piggyback graph is one edge, \citealp[Thm~7]{achour2024loss}); and the tree contrast for
generic teachers, whose rank-one saddle on an MPS has order $2$ where the landscape bound allows $\Kc$
(finding~\ref{it:intro-strict}). Locality beats weight has only analogues: the smallest connected set of
rank-deficient cores escaping first \citep[Fig.~4, Remark~D.10]{furman2026benign}, and the lowest leap
first \citep{abbe2023sgd}. Dead routes (finding~\ref{it:intro-failure})
relate to known shrinkage of dormant weights \citep[App.~C.1]{jacot2022saddletosaddle},
\citep{kumar2026learning}, \citep[Lemma~B.1]{kunin2025alternating} and to the failure of exactly
balanced initialization in four-layer matrix factorization \citep{luo2025global}; new are the route-end
condition and the measurements.

\paragraph{Tensor factorization.} Small initialization biases CP factorization towards low rank
\citep{razin2021implicit} and hierarchical tensor factorization, whose internal nodes are local CP
factorizations rather than dense cores, towards low hierarchical rank \citep{razin2022implicit};
over-parameterized orthogonal tensor decomposition learns the largest component first
\citep{ge2021understanding}; lazy training of a tensor decomposition needs many components
\citep{wang2020beyond}; growth exponents depend on depth in deep CP factorization \citep{hariz2022implicit};
the Tucker landscape has descents of order 2, 3 and 4 \citep{frandsen2022optimization}; and tensor-train
completion shows a plateau, then abrupt rank growth \citep{milanesi2021implicit}. In multi-spike tensor
PCA the recovery order is a greedy selection on (weight)$\times$(initial correlation)$^{\Kc-2}$ for tensors of
order $\Kc\ge3$ and follows the signal-to-noise ratios for matrices \citep[Thm~1.5]{benarous2025stochastic},
\citep[gradient flow, Thm~1.3]{benarous2025permutation}, the dichotomy we find between pairs that differ
on three or more cores and on two; related orderings hold for the tensor power method
\citep[Lemma~5.1]{anandkumar2014tensor} and for units quadratic in their weights
\citep[Prop.~5]{zhang2026saddletosaddle}. New for TTNs (finding~\ref{it:intro-order}): the classification by
the number of cores, the exact $\isc$-independent race on MPS, the TTN form of the selection rule (which
names $76$--$92\%$ of the winners), the order statistics, the finite-$\isc$ law of the greedy case, the branches after a wrong first escape beyond
binary4, and three components. The critical points of the rank-constrained problem are known in substance
\citep{elden2011perturbation,holtz2012alternating}; new are that the hierarchical SVD truncation is
generically not critical (a loss gap of fourth order in a perturbation of the teacher), that the rank-one plateaus of random MPS4 teachers sit at
the bond-rank-one critical point (separated from every SVD-type truncation for 3 of 5 teachers; in CP
factorization a rank-one trajectory is known, \citealp{razin2021implicit}), the tree contrast and the
bottom-up order (findings~\ref{it:intro-strict},~\ref{it:intro-cov}).

\paragraph{Lazy and rich training.} Lazy training and the tangent kernel
\citep{jacot2018neural,chizat2019lazy,lee2019wide}, its failure when the tangent space misses the target
\citep{ghorbani2019limitations}, the crossover read from the kernel \citep{geiger2020disentangling,
bordelon2022selfconsistent}, the kernel-to-rich transitions of diagonal networks
\citep{woodworth2020kernel,pesme2023saddletosaddle,berthier2023incremental}, imbalance
\citep{kunin2024get,domine2025lazy}, mixed dynamics \citep{tu2024mixed,lyu2024dichotomy} and
parameterizations of wide networks \citep{yang2021tensor,yang2024spectral} are known; for tensor networks,
kernel limits of MPS and tensor trains \citep{guo2021neural,wesel2025tensor} and factor-wise scaling
rules \citep{qiu2024compute,potapczynski2024searching}. Known in our setting: maps of regimes as such, the
effect of a light layer in two-layer networks \citep{kunin2024get,domine2025lazy}, and the floor below full
bonds as a linearized model that fits only its tangent space \citep{chizat2019lazy,ghorbani2019limitations}.
New for TTNs (finding~\ref{it:intro-regimes}): the map, measured on one tree and as lower bounds beyond seven
cores, the full-bond condition, the lazy constants, the root-imbalance map (a light root makes training
lazy, a heavy one does not), and a feature-learning
($\mu$P-type) parameterization.

\paragraph{Tensor networks, steps and optimizers.} TTNs rest on the numerical analysis of hierarchical
formats and their sweeping algorithms
\citep{hackbusch2009new,grasedyck2010hierarchical,oseledets2011tensor,holtz2012manifolds,%
uschmajew2013geometry,holtz2012alternating,white1992density}, which also underlie Riemannian training
\citep{novikov2018exponential,steinlechner2016riemannian}; their barren plateaus
\citep{liu2022presence,miao2024isometric,barthel2025absence} are of another kind: there the gradient
concentrates over random initializations, here it is small because the initialization is. Known for
steps and noise: the edge of stability and catapults \citep{cohen2021gradient,lewkowycz2020large},
stability limits and two-cycles in deep linear and scalar networks
\citep{mulayoff2020unique,kreisler2023gradient,ghosh2025learning,macdonald2025convergence}, the modified
flow behind our event shift \citep{barrett2021implicit}, SGD stability and stochastic collapse
\citep{li2017stochastic,mulayoff2024exact,chen2023stochastic}, SGD experiments in deep linear networks
\citep{corlouer2026stochastic}, and plateaus at singular points, which the natural gradient avoids
\citep{amari2006singularities}; concurrently with this work, Adam's bounded first escape and its $\epsilon$
law in deep matrix factorization \citep{sabanayagam2026optimizer}. New for TTNs
(finding~\ref{it:intro-optim}): the stability limit on general trees, the event shift, the delay law of
SGD and its collapse condition, the first component kept by Adam, and the $\isc$-independence of sweeps.
