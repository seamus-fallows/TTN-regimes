\subsection{Steps, gradient noise, finite samples and optimizers}\label{app:D-optim}

Details behind \cref{sec:other-optim,sec:other-optimizers}; settings in \cref{app:exp-optim,app:exp-optimizers}.

\paragraph{Gradient descent below and at the stability limit.}
\empirical{} An independent code placed the edge of binary $\Ns=4$ between $0.99\lr_c$ and $1.01\lr_c$. Below $\lr_c$ a
larger step only enlarges transient excursions of near-tied components, which cross $\frac12$ and fall back (at
$\isc=0.1$ in $0.09\pm0.04$ of 45 initializations at $0.1\lr_c$ and $0.18\pm0.06$ at $0.9\lr_c$). Of the 526 event-shift
comparisons at $\lr\le0.1\lr_c$, the 15 misses beyond $5\%$ are later events, worst where $c_e\approx0$ after a near-tied
race and the $O(\lr^2)$ term dominates; Richardson extrapolation to $\lr\to0$ recovers $c_e$ to a median relative
$1.2\cdot10^{-5}$ (57 events). At $0.9\lr_c$ the relative error of $\tev1$ is $0.111$, $0.056$, $0.023$, $0.0098$ and
$0.0036$ for $\isc=0.3$, $0.1$, $0.03$, $0.01$, $0.003$ (five seeds).

\paragraph{Above the limit: edge of stability.}
\derived{} \empirical{} For $\lr_c<\lr<2/\lambda_2(\Hess)$, with $\lambda_2(\Hess)$ the second-largest Hessian eigenvalue at
the minimum, only the radial mode of component 1 is unstable, and GD on
it is the balanced scalar map of \cref{fig:optim-gd}a (\cref{app:optimizer}): a two-cycle from $\lr_c$, period four
from $1.245\lr_c$, chaos from $1.32\lr_c$, then returns to the origin interleaved with divergence ($\Kc=3$). On binary
$\Ns=4$ component 1 oscillates while components 2 and 3 are still learned, and the time-averaged excess loss equals
$\tw1^2$ times the map's mean loss within $0.9\%$ at $1.05$--$1.3\lr_c$ (for example $0.4496$ against $0.4496$ at
$1.2\lr_c$, 25 runs; \eqref{eq:A-eos-loss} is its small-excess limit). The map's bounded chaotic window does not
survive: the network diverges at $1.5$--$1.75\lr_c$ (38 of 40 runs), is thrown back to the origin saddle and relearns
its components again and again at $2\lr_c$ (25 of 25), and diverges from $2.5\lr_c$. The deep trees, started at
$\isc=0.9$ where the gradient-flow states are $10$--$35\%$ sharper than $\lambda_{\rm bal}$, already oscillate or
catapult at $0.9\lr_c$ in some runs (2 of 5 on binary $\Ns=8$, 3 of 5 on the MPS).

\paragraph{Larger initialization: catapults.}
\empirical{} \knownresult[catapults: \citealp{lewkowycz2020large}; flatter, more balanced minima in scalar networks:
\citealp{kreisler2023gradient}, Thms~3.2--3.3; limit sharpness at most $2/\lr$ in two-factor factorization:
\citealp{wang2022large}, Thm~3.2; balancing while oscillating beyond the edge: \citealp{ghosh2025learning},
Prop.~2] Centred model on binary $\Ns=4$ at $\chi=9$, the cut dimension, where all 45 gradient-flow references
converge: $\lambda_{\rm GF}\propto\isc^{3.965\pm0.014}$ over $\isc=1.41$--$16$ (five seeds), so $2/\lambda_{\rm GF}$ equals
$\lr_c$ for $\isc\le0.3$, $0.65\lr_c$ at $\isc=1$ and $2.7\cdot10^{-4}\lr_c$ at $\isc=8$ (\cref{fig:optim-gd}c). With steps
of $0.5$--$4$ times each run's own $2/\lambda_{\rm GF}$, all runs up to $0.95$ times are GF-like at every $\isc$, and
monotone GD ends between $0.95$ and $1.2$ times. Beyond, small initialization ($\isc=0.1$, $0.3$) oscillates and then
diverges, since the balanced minimum is already the flattest; from $\isc=1$ on GD catapults: the loss spikes (by up
to $2\cdot10^5\Loss_0$ at $\isc=8$) and then reaches gradient flow's final loss (86 of 90 runs below $10^{-6}\Loss_0$)
at a flatter minimum, of sharpness $0.58$--$1.00$ times $2/\lr$ (per run), for steps up to $1.5$ ($\isc=1$) and $3$
($\isc=2$--$8$) times $2/\lambda_{\rm GF}$; at $4$ times all runs diverge. For $\isc\ge2$ the window thus ends between
$6/\lambda_{\rm GF}$ and $8/\lambda_{\rm GF}$, $1.5$--$2$ times the end of the catapult window of the two-layer model
of \citet{lewkowycz2020large} (four times the inverse curvature, $4/\lambda_{\rm GF}$ here). Imbalanced small initializations ($\isc=0.1$ with the root core at scale 2, 4 or 6)
reproduce the known selection: the 104 of 150 runs that converge end $0.05$--$3\%$ below
$\min(\lambda_{\rm GF},2/\lr)$.

\paragraph{SGD: the first plateau.}
\derived{} \empirical{} On an aligned mode \eqref{eq:A-sgd-first} gives a mean delay of order
$\lr(1+3^\Ns/\batch)\ln(1/\isc)$ and a standard deviation of about $(3^\Ns\tev1^{\rm GF}\lr/\batch)^{1/2}$, $3^\Ns$ being
the kurtosis of the product features (the exact factor is in \cref{app:optimizer}). With online SGD on binary
$\Ns=4$ (1,780 runs; $\batch=1$--$256$; five initializations with ten noise seeds each), at $\batch=1$ the mean delays
are $+0.24\pm0.10$, $-0.01\pm0.12$ and $+0.13\pm0.07$ at $\isc=0.03$, $0.1$, $0.3$ (s.e.\ across initializations;
escape times $24.2$, $7.2$, $2.3$), against $+0.17$, $+0.12$, $+0.08$ predicted: consistent, but resolved only at
$\isc=0.03$. The spread scales as a power $0.43$--$0.52$ of $\lr/\batch$ (five sweeps, s.e.\ $0.01$--$0.05$; three s.e.\
below $\frac12$ at $\isc=0.1$), against $\frac12$. In the sweep over $\batch$ at $\lr=5\cdot10^{-4}$
(\cref{fig:optim-sgd}a), 11 of the 23 cell means of the relative change of the first escape are negative, the
largest $-1.0\%\pm0.9\%$ ($\isc=0.1$, $\batch=4$) and the most significant $-0.32\%\pm0.13\%$ ($\isc=0.1$, $\batch=128$);
the positive ones reach $+5\%$ at $\batch=1$. Run by run, the first escape comes earlier than gradient flow's in 787
of the 1,600 runs from random initialization (by more than $5\%$ in 141), and the second plateau is shorter in 657
of the 1,529 runs that kept the first component; on the aligned route no escaped run was faster (114 of 114).

\paragraph{SGD: the later-plateau law.}
\empirical{} The aligned-route test starts with component 1 learned and the route at per-core amplitude
$b_0=0.002$--$0.01$, so that $T_{\rm GF}=50$--$250$ is exact; $\lr/\batch=3.1\cdot10^{-5}$--$2.5\cdot10^{-4}$, six noise
seeds per point. One fitted $c_{\rm col}=32.6$ (the commuting-pair estimate of \path{notes/theory_optimizer.md} gives
about 26 for this teacher) yields a chi-square statistic of 379 for 18 degrees of freedom, against 535 for a pole
$1/(1-x)$ and 929 for $1+x/2$; but the effective $c_{\rm col}$ grows from 30 at small $x$ to 45 near the stall at $x=1$. $\batch=16$ and $64$ agree
within $1.8$ combined s.e.\ at eight matched points, although $\batch=64$ is higher at all three escaped points with
$b_0=0.003$. At $\lr/\batch=1.25\cdot10^{-4}$, $T_{\rm SGD}/T_{\rm GF}=1.13$, $1.37$ and $2.5$--$2.7$ for $b_0=0.01$, $0.005$,
$0.003$, and all runs stall at $0.002$ (no persistent event by $4T_{\rm GF}$; the route amplitude decays). From random initialization the second plateau is lengthened by
$19.8\pm5.1\%$ at $\isc=0.03$, $\batch=1$ (the 16 of 30 runs that kept component 1), against $17.7\%$ from
\eqref{eq:optim-collapse} with no further fit, but by $10.8\pm0.6\%$ against $4.4\%$ at $\isc=0.1$.

\paragraph{SGD: stability and forgetting.}
\empirical{} Started $10^{-5}$ from the minimum, SGD converged in 5 of 5 runs up to $1.5\lr^*_{\rm ms}$ and diverged in
9 of 10 at $2.5\lr^*_{\rm ms}$ ($\batch=1$, $16$), as the computed almost-sure threshold, about $1.9\lr^*_{\rm ms}$,
predicts; $\lr^*_{\rm ms}/\batch$ is $1.07\cdot10^{-3}$ and $1.0\cdot10^{-3}$ at $\batch=1$, $16$ but $0.82\cdot10^{-3}$ and
$0.44\cdot10^{-3}$ at $\batch=64$, $256$. In the random-initialization runs the first learned component fell back below
$\frac12$ in $20$--$66\%$ of the runs at $0.47\lr^*_{\rm ms}$ (most in the longest runs), $4\%$ at $0.38\lr^*_{\rm ms}$,
1 of 50 at $0.24\lr^*_{\rm ms}$ and none below; these are also the only runs whose order differs from gradient flow's
(10) or is unresolved (19 at $\batch=1$). On the aligned-route plateaus (\cref{fig:optim-sgd}c), $\ov1$ fell below
$\frac12$ in 0 of 72 runs at $\lr\le0.076\lr^*_{\rm ms}$ (all escaped by $t=405$), 1 of 36 at $0.12$--$0.15\lr^*_{\rm ms}$
(runs of up to 1000 time units) and 20 of 36 at $0.25$--$0.30\lr^*_{\rm ms}$, so the safe fraction $0.25\lr^*_{\rm ms}$
seen with another teacher (\cref{app:optimizer}) does not carry over.

\paragraph{Finite samples.}
\empirical{} \derived{} Binary tree with $\Ns=8$, $d=2$ ($\W\in\R^{256}$), odeco teacher $(3,2)$ of bond rank 2 (its rank-2
TTNs form a manifold of dimension 28), $\nsamp$ Gaussian inputs with noise-free labels, five seeds; GD on the empirical
loss (step $1/\lambda_{\max}$, $6\cdot10^4$ steps); students with $\chi=4$, whose tangent space at a random point has
dimension 112 (\cref{prop:A-kernel}), and $\chi=16$, where all bonds are full; test error
$\normF{\W-\Wt}^2/\normF{\Wt}^2$ (\cref{fig:optim-samples}). Small initialization ($\isc=0.3$, $0.5$) recovers the teacher
with its bond ranks: test error below $10^{-2}$ in 5 of 5 seeds from $\nsamp=256$ at $\chi=4$ (about $2\cdot10^{-3}$ at
256 and $2\cdot10^{-4}$ at 384, limited by unfinished training) and from $\nsamp=384$ at $\chi=16$ ($\isc=0.5$ only), with
mean effective bond rank $2.0$--$2.1$. For $\nsamp\le96$ it fits the training data (to $1.3\%$ of the initial loss)
with a wrong tensor in 89 of 90 runs (mean test error $1.0$--$3.8$). The exact lazy limit fails as dimension counting
predicts \derived: at $\chi=4$ the teacher lies outside the tangent space, and the error is $1.7$--$32$ at every
$\nsamp$, worse than predicting zero; at $\chi=16$ it is exact only from $\nsamp=d^\Ns=256$. The centred model at
$\isc=1$, which is not lazy (its parameters move by $12$--$69\%$), reaches $0.01$--$0.04$ at $\nsamp=384$--$512$ with bond
rank $2.4$--$2.9$; at $\isc=1.5$ and $2$ it had not converged after $3\cdot10^5$ steps and is not ranked. With $\Ns=6$,
$d=3$ ($\W\in\R^{729}$, manifold dimension 48), small initialization recovers the teacher in 5 of 5 seeds at
$\nsamp=384$, while the $\chi=4$ lazy limit is worse than predicting zero at every $\nsamp\le384$. The learned bond
ranks are ordered small initialization $<$ centred $<$ lazy limit at every $\nsamp$, except at $\chi=16$ for
$\nsamp\ge256$, where the lazy limit is exact. A linearized model can only fit the projection of the target on its
tangent space \knownresult[\citealp{chizat2019lazy}, Thm~2.5; \citealp{ghorbani2019limitations}, Thm~2; lazy training
of a tensor decomposition needs $\Omega(d^{\Ns-1})$ components for a rank-one target: \citealp{wang2020beyond},
Thm~1]. Scope: one teacher per setting, noise-free labels, GD with capped steps, and no monotone trend in $\isc$ within
small initialization. The threshold of small initialization, 200--250 samples or 4--9 times the teacher-manifold
dimension with $\chi=4$ students, is unexplained.

\paragraph{Adam's first escape and its $\epsilon$.}
\empirical{} \Cref{fig:optimizers-adam-escape} shows both regimes. On binary $\Ns=4$, \eqref{eq:adam-eps} puts the
crossover $\isc_\times$ near $10^{-8}$ (\derived, not tested), far below the $2\cdot10^{-4}$ at which the initial
gradient falls under $\epsilon$. On the MPS with $\Ns=8$ the prediction for each seed combines that seed's exact
gradient-flow escape at $\isc=0.3$, scaled by $(0.3/\isc)^6$, with its own escape at $\epsilon=10^{-30}$. Every run that
escaped within $2\cdot10^5$ steps is counted; at $\lr=10^{-2}$, $\isc=0.03$ measured over predicted is $1.010$--$1.035$,
and near the crossover $\isc_\times\approx0.05$--$0.1$ the deviations run from $-5\%$ to $+17\%$.

\paragraph{Later plateaus and minima under Adam.}
\empirical{} Later gaps depend weakly on $\isc$ on binary $\Ns=4$ (exponents $0.0$--$0.4$). On binary $\Ns=8$ they grow
as $\isc$ decreases, to a median $8\cdot10^4$ (up to $1.3\cdot10^5$) steps at $\isc=0.25$, but per-seed local slopes
range from $-0.4$ to $5.5$, so over half a decade no law is established (gradient flow: $4.72$ and $4.85$, prediction
5); with $\isc\le0.4$, 83--100\% of these runs are saddle-to-saddle. Adam and RMSProp break the conservation of
$\Q e$ and end at imbalanced minima (balance index
$\normF{\Q e}/(\normF{\Gm{v}{e}\Gm{v}{e}\T}+\normF{\Gm{p}{e}\Gm{p}{e}\T})=0.21$--$0.96$), $1.01$--$6.1$ times sharper than the
balanced value \eqref{eq:A-sharpness}, the more so the larger the step. From balanced initializations (and on
binary $\Ns=4$) gradient flow, GD and heavy ball end within $0.2\%$ of it; from fan-in initialization on binary $\Ns=8$
they keep the initial imbalance (balance index up to $0.35$) and end up to $1.07$, $1.11$ and $1.13$ times sharper.

\paragraph{Re-gauged and Gram-preconditioned GD.}
\derived{} \empirical{} Re-gauging moves all of $\normF{\W}\sim\isc^\Kc$ into the root, whose environment becomes an
isometry, so the root fits the teacher's projection onto the current frames in a time $O(1)$ for any $\isc$; random
frames keep only a fraction $\prod_e\chi/d^{n_e}$ of each component over the root's edges ($2.4\cdot10^{-3}$ on binary
$\Ns=8$, $0.2$ on $\Ns=4$), so on the deep tree they must rotate and learning is sequential again, with $\isc$-free event
times (exponents at most $0.02$ in size; on the MPS with $\Ns=8$ the times at $\isc=0.3$ and $0.8$ differ by
$27$--$44\%$). Gram preconditioning moves each core towards its own least-squares solution, of size
$\sim\isc^{-(\Kc-1)}$ ($\isc^{-\Kc}$ times the core), so its first step overshoots the loss by $\sim\isc^{-2\Kc(\Kc-1)}$
(\derived{} in the aligned scalar model; medians up to $4\cdot10^{34}\Loss_0$ on binary $\Ns=4$, single runs up to
$5\cdot10^{36}$, deep trees $10^{52}$); it has no flat plateau, but its time depends on $\isc$ (\cref{tab:optimizers}).
In aligned deep matrix factorization, undamped Gauss--Newton blocks likewise remove the escape barrier and damping
restores the gradient-descent law \knownresult[\citealp{sabanayagam2026optimizer}, Prop.~9].

\paragraph{Finite samples under Adam.}
\empirical{} With $\nsamp=45$ and $60$ samples on binary $\Ns=4$ (four data draws, six initializations,
$\isc\in\{0.01,0.1\}$, paired comparisons), gradient flow and Adam show no consistent difference in population loss:
at $\nsamp=60$ every run that met the training tolerance recovered the teacher; at $\nsamp=45$ none did, and the paired
differences range from $-5.1$ to $+4.0$ ($\Loss_0=7$; Adam better in 8--13 of 21--23 pairs per setting). Only a
threshold count differs (a fourth bond singular value above $0.1\,\bst{e}{\min}$: Adam with $\lr=10^{-2}$ in 35 of 44
runs, gradient flow in 5); the entropy rank does not.

\paragraph{Which invariance removes which feature.}
The reading of \cref{tab:optimizers} by two properties of an update, how its step scales with the gradient and the
metric in which it moves $\W$, is given once, in \cref{sec:summary-parameters} (``Landscape and clock''), with the
conservation drifts measured under gradient flow, GD and heavy ball. A law for Adam's later plateaus is open
(\cref{sec:open}).
