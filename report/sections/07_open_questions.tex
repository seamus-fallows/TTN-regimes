\paragraph{Landscape and clock.}
Two kinds of effect run through the table. Some parameters change the landscape: which saddles exist,
at which levels, and of which type. These are the tree, the teacher and the covariance and, through the
bond caps, $\chi$ and $d$. Others change only the clock, the time spent near each saddle: above all
$\isc$, which at leading order changes neither the direction of the first escape nor the order of pairs
that differ on three or more cores, and the step size below its stability limit. The balance of the
initialization leaves the landscape and the exponents unchanged, shifts the prefactors and occasionally
the first component, and decides whether a route survives its plateau. Optimizers can change both, and
two properties of an update decide what survives (\cref{tab:optimizers}). The first is how its step
scales with the gradient, which near the origin is $O(\isc^{\Kc-1})$ while the parameters must move by
their own size: a step proportional to the gradient needs a time $\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}$, a step invariant
under its rescaling (Adam) does not, and an absolute scale in the preconditioner, such as Adam's
$\epsilon$, brings the law back \derived; Adam still follows the sign of the gradient, which one
component dominates, so learning stays sequential and its first component is gradient flow's (\conjecture). The second is the metric. Under
gradient flow $\W$ moves through $\Jac\Jac\T$ ($\Jac=\partial\W/\partial\params$), which vanishes like
$\isc^{2(\Kc-1)}$ at the origin, whereas Riemannian gradient descent (in continuous time the
Gauss--Newton flow $\dot\params=\Jac^+(\Wt-\W)$) and the exact block minimizations of ALS and DMRG move
$\W$ at a rate of order one. These methods are equivariant under the whole gauge group
\eqref{eq:gauge}, including the rescaling of a core against its neighbour, and see $\Wz$ only through
its frames, so they keep neither plateaus \derived{} nor $\isc$ \proven; plateaus at singular points of
a parametrization, and the better behaviour of the natural gradient there, are known for multilayer
perceptrons \knownresult[\citealp{amari2006singularities}]. Equivariance alone is not enough: Gram
preconditioning is gauge-equivariant up to damping \derived{} but moves all cores at once from their
current values. Balance and the flattest minimum (\cref{prop:A-pattern-rule}) rest on the conserved
$\Q e$, the charges of the gauge symmetry under Euclidean gradient flow (\cref{prop:conservation}); on
binary4 their measured relative drift is about $10^{-11}$ under gradient flow and, growing with $\isc$,
$4\cdot10^{-5}$--$2\cdot10^{-3}$ under GD and $10^{-3}$--$5\cdot10^{-2}$ under heavy ball (momentum flows conserve fewer quantities, with time- and velocity-dependent laws,
\citealp{marcotte2024keep}), while Adam, equivariant only under coordinate permutations and sign flips,
breaks them at order one \empirical. Where an entry of the table rests on one tree, one teacher or a
single $\isc$, the sections cited give its scope.

\subsection{Open questions}\label{sec:open}

The questions are ranked by their importance to the main question, the saddle-to-saddle dynamics of
TTNs, and then by how directly they can be settled. For each we say what is known and, in one sentence,
what would settle it.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.,leftmargin=*,itemsep=4pt]
  \item \textbf{The whole saddle sequence.} Is the sequence of saddles fixed by an algorithm on the
    teacher and the tree, as conjectured for deep linear networks \citep{jacot2022saddletosaddle} and
    proven for diagonal networks \citep{pesme2023saddletosaddle}? The route rule (\cref{rem:A-sequence}) is
    derived at leading order for each step and predicts the plateau levels measured with three and four
    components and, where they are long enough to measure, their laws, with one exception
    (\cref{sec:s2s-several}); as an algorithm it is a \conjecture: the saddle-order theorem covers one
    learned component only; with several, the fixed-rank block (couplings through learned superpositions)
    is not worked out; ties between routes are decided by random route amplitudes; and cheaper routes can
    insert intermediate plateaus by learning part of a component. What exists is bookkeeping: an
    implementation of the known rules that hops from saddle to saddle (\cref{sec:s2s-hopper}) agrees
    with the measured sequences, but its prediction differs from the weight order without rotations in
    only 107 runs, and on the informative locality runs it scores $97.0\%$ against that baseline's $95.8\%$
    (both $96.6\%$ on the odeco binary4 runs) \empirical. What is missing is a predictive version. \emph{What would settle it:} a rule that runs the
    per-initialization race (\cref{rem:A-recipe}) at every saddle and includes the lower orders of
    \cref{thm:A-order-sharp}, compared run by run with gradient flow, together with a version of
    \cref{thm:A-order-lower} at saddles of higher bond rank (its proof needs the learned part to be a
    product across a core).
  \item \textbf{Saddle orders below the route's, and long routes.} Where the order is $2\ell<k^*$
    (\cref{thm:A-order-sharp}), the escape should take a time $\propto\isc^{-(2\ell-2)}$ instead of
    $\isc^{-(k^*-2)}$ \derived, and the learned component is then forgotten and relearned \empirical. The
    escape law was checked on one teacher only (MPS6), from the saddle and from small initialization; the
    forgetting was seen there from small initialization, and in 6 of the 7 HT4 runs that learn the smaller
    component first (order $6$, not $k=7$). Also open: the borderline case in which the largest eigenvalue of the
    generalized piggyback operator equals $\tw j$ ($k^*\ge4$); \cref{thm:A-order-sharp} without its index
    condition, or for signed and non-pattern residuals; the length of the plateau after the forgetting; and
    an exponent near $4$ in half of the control seeds of the same check, where the route predicts $3$.
    Long routes are only partly measured: $k=6,7$ over one decade, with balanced initialization only, on
    binary8 and HT4; MPS8 with $k=6$ gives $4.51\pm0.19$ against $4$; $k=8$ was not measured; and at $k=4$
    a second negative Hessian eigenvalue of the route's order appears on the plateau, so for $k\ge5$ such
    directions might change how $\lambda_{\min}$ scales with $\isc$ (\cref{prop:A-signatures}).
    \emph{What would settle it:} pre-registered per-seed fits of the escape time from the saddle plus a
    perturbation of size $\varepsilon$ (predicted $\propto\varepsilon^{-(\text{order}-2)}$) on every case
    with order below $k^*$ and on controls, and balanced runs over more than a decade for $k\ge6$, with
    plateau Hessians.
  \item \textbf{Prefactors from random initialization.} The exponents are predicted, the prefactors are
    not, except that of the first escape, which is exact at leading order but has no closed form on trees
    with internal path amplitudes (binary8, HT, MPS with $\Ns\ge6$; the path-amplitude mode of
    \cref{prop:A-product-mode} is good to a factor $1.2$--$1.6$ there). Later prefactors are set by the
    route amplitudes at the start of each plateau, which vary between seeds by factors above $20$ at
    $k\ge4$; for generic teachers, offsets of up to about $50$ time units would decide the order of
    near-tied escapes at accessible $\isc$ \conjecture{} (heuristic, \cref{prop:A-agf-race}). \emph{What
    would settle it:} a law for how route amplitudes are carried through each escape, tested against the
    per-seed prefactors already measured (\cref{sec:s2s-escape,sec:s2s-locality}).
  \item \textbf{Why routes die, and how often.} The conserved quantity $C_u$ of each route end
    (\cref{prop:A-trap}) predicts from the initialization which pattern-teacher runs trap ($93\%$ of the
    predicted-dead runs stall, $15\%$ of the predicted-pinned ones (one dying end), $1.8\%$ of the predicted-alive
    ones), why HT routes with $k=5$ do not stall from small initialization (0 of 144 HT4 runs), and why
    spare channels protect only ends whose own bond has them (\cref{sec:s2s-failure}). Open: from $k=6$ on,
    three-core clusters of inner route cores are a further death channel that the criterion omits, and
    whether a route with one dying end escapes (after $\propto\isc^{-(2k-6)}$) is a \conjecture, contradicted at
    $k=6$ by one gradient-flow run; the odeco traps are predicted only from the state after the second
    escape; spare
    channels raise the stalls on routes whose ends are both capped, reproduced per run but without an
    isolated mechanism; balanced initialization removes dead routes yet delays some escapes by $10^5$ or
    more; and whether the odeco runs at $\Kc=7,8$ that end with live route ends face long plateaus or slowly
    dying routes is a \conjecture{} (from fan-in initialization most stalls of the regime map are dead
    routes). One route (MPS8, $D=\{1,5\}$, from the saddle) is steeper than predicted ($3.67\pm0.20$ against
    $3$), with no established mechanism. \emph{What would settle it:} the cluster channel added to the reduced
    flow and tested at $k=6$--$8$, a $\isc$-free model of the second odeco plateau, and long runs continued from pinned states.
  \item \textbf{The learning order in closed form.} The race of independent components is exact only on
    MPS; where cores without sites couple the components it still names the first component in
    $95$--$100\%$ of the runs on binary8 and HT4 but in only 537 of 640 on binary4, and it has no closed
    form there. For pairs whose differing sites sit on two cores, the prefactor of $P(\text{smaller
    first})$ is not derived, the exponent $n(1-w)/w$ (weight ratio $w$) with $n=\bd e$ for adjacent cores
    is a \conjecture{} supported only at the smallest $\isc$, and whether gradient flow has the
    leading-order exponent is open. That $P(\text{larger first})\to\frac12$ as $\Kc\to\infty$, and that
    $P\to1$ needs both $d$ and $\chi$ to grow, are untested conjectures (\cref{sec:s2s-order},
    \cref{prop:A-race}). \emph{What would settle it:} a leading-order emulation of the coupled race on
    binary and HT trees, and $\isc$ scans down to $10^{-6}$ with $1{,}000$ seeds for adjacent pairs.
  \item \textbf{Regime boundaries at large $\Kc$ and large $\chi$.} With the initial function kept and
    spare channels, a saddle-to-saddle boundary tending to $1$, the limit of aligned initialization, is
    rejected at $\Kc\le15$ (binary trees, $\chi=4$, teacher $(3,2)$, $d=2$, $3$): at $\Kc=8$--$15$ the lower
    bounds of the 85 censored seeds ($1.07$--$2.03$) all exceed 1 and both candidate laws, $\isc_c^{\Kc}=\text{const}$ and $\isc_c^{\Kc}/\sqrt{d^\Ns}=\text{const}$, and 76 of
    the 96 seeds stay saddle-to-saddle up to the scale at which the initial function overlaps the teacher
    (about $\sqrt d$ at these $\Kc$; about $1.15\sqrt d$ in the limit) \empirical; this comparison of the
    bounds with the laws, and the domain censoring it relies on, were added after the pre-registration. The
    proposed mechanism, the model unlearning its initial function on
    the plateau, is consistent with the data but not confirmed (\conjecture). With the initial function
    removed and full bonds the two laws are not resolved, so whether $\isc_c(\Kc)$ tends to $\sqrt d$, a
    fixed amplitude per component, remains a \conjecture{} (\cref{sec:regime-K}). Its $\chi^{-1/2}$ scaling is measured on one tree over less than a decade and is
    not derived, and that the rich window shrinks to a band of width $O(1/\Kc)$ in $\ln\isc$ is a
    \conjecture{} (\cref{sec:regime-sigma-chi,sec:other-rich}). Under the $\mu$P-type parameterization the
    kernel keeps moving as $\chi$ grows on binary4, but the limit is not derived (a two-population mean
    field coupled through the root is a \conjecture), the runs on binary8 are incomplete, and whether
    saddle-to-saddle dynamics survives $\chi\to\infty$ there is unknown (\cref{sec:other-large-chi}).
    \emph{What would settle it:} the large-$\Kc$ limit of the centred, realizable problem, with budgets
    long enough at $\Kc\ge8$, a causal test of the unlearning mechanism, and the limiting $\mu$P dynamics on
    binary4 compared with the runs at $\chi=16$--$128$.
  \item \textbf{Adam and gradient noise on later plateaus.} Adam keeps gradient flow's first component,
    and mostly its order, and makes the first escape independent of $\isc$, but no law is established for
    its later plateaus (per-seed local slopes from $-0.4$ to $5.5$ on binary8, over half a decade), its
    $\epsilon$ law was tested only at $\Kc=8$, and that it stays sequential because one component dominates
    the sign of the gradient is a \conjecture, as is the $(1+\beta)/(1-\beta)$ delay of heavy ball
    (\cref{sec:other-optimizers}). For SGD the law $T_{\rm SGD}/T_{\rm GF}=-\ln(1-x)/x$ is heuristic, holds
    to about $10\%$ only for $T_{\rm SGD}/T_{\rm GF}\lesssim1.5$, and its one coefficient is fitted ($32.6$,
    against $26$ from a commuting-pair estimate); the rate at which noise erases learned components near
    the stability limit, and whether noise can revive a dead route, are open (\cref{sec:other-optim}).
    \emph{What would settle it:} a sign-flow analysis of the route equations with and without $\epsilon$,
    and the top Lyapunov exponent of the linearized route block under noise, tested on SGD runs started
    from stalled states.
  \item \textbf{Covariance, teachers, real data and samples beyond the tested range.} Under a misaligned
    covariance the slope of the escape from random initialization is only bounded
    (\cref{sec:cov-misaligned}); correlated inputs were run neither on HT trees nor on MPS with $\Ns>4$,
    where a theory check on MPS6 found a slow second stage that is not a single strict saddle; later
    saddles of generic teachers on binary and HT students were not timed (\cref{sec:teacher-generic}); and
    that the local maxima of $g$ have bond rank one is a conjecture (\cref{app:teachers}). On MNIST, the
    share of the data's route couplings on short routes correlates with the final loss across pixel orders
    in a pre-registered test (Spearman correlation $r_S=-0.60$ over 27 orders), but not with the early
    speed; that it acts through which label-relevant interactions lie on routes of five rather than seven
    cores is a \conjecture, and on the MPS tree shape and pixel order are confounded (\cref{sec:real-data}).
    Small initialization recovers the teacher with its bond ranks from about $200$--$250$ samples, $4$--$9$
    times the dimension of the teacher manifold (students with $\chi=4$), an unexplained threshold resting on one teacher per
    setting (\cref{sec:other-optim}). \emph{What would settle it:} $\isc$ sweeps on HT4 and MPS6 under
    correlated inputs, timing every later route on binary16 in several pixel orders, and sample-size sweeps
    over teachers with manifolds of different dimension.
  \item \textbf{Exact theory and scope.} The theorems of \citet{kumar2025towards} on the dynamics after an
    escape need non-degenerate critical points, which the gauge orbits exclude for $\chi\ge2$; a quotient
    (Morse--Bott) version would make the escape path rigorous. Whether the per-bond conservation laws are
    all the conservation laws (the algorithm of \citealp{marcotte2023abide}, run on small trees) and the
    explicit metric $(\Jac\Jac\T)^{-1}$ of balanced TTNs are open. Beyond the model: input laws that are not
    products, classification and several outputs, pointwise nonlinearities inside the network, and what all
    of this implies for the choice of initialization and of tree in practice. \emph{What would settle it:}
    the quotient version of those theorems, first for chains, and the route rule tested with a label leg on
    one core, as in MPS classifiers \citep{stoudenmire2016supervised}.
\end{enumerate}
