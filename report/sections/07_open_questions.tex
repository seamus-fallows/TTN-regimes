\section{Open questions}\label{sec:open}

The questions below are ranked by their importance to the main question, the saddle-to-saddle dynamics
of TTNs, and then by how directly they can be settled. For each we say what is known and what would
settle it; the sections and notes named give the details.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.,leftmargin=*,itemsep=4pt]
  \item \textbf{The whole saddle sequence.} Is the sequence of saddles fixed by an algorithm on the
    teacher and the tree, as conjectured for deep linear networks \citep{jacot2022saddletosaddle} and
    proven for diagonal networks \citep{pesme2023saddletosaddle}? The route rule (\cref{rem:A-sequence})
    is derived at leading order for each step and predicts the plateau levels and laws measured with three
    and four components, with few exceptions (\cref{sec:s2s-locality}), but as an algorithm it is a
    \conjecture: the saddle-order theorem covers one learned component only; with several, the
    fixed-rank block (couplings through learned superpositions) is not worked out; ties between routes are
    decided by random route amplitudes; and cheaper routes can insert intermediate plateaus by learning
    part of a component. \emph{What would settle it:} a version of \cref{thm:A-order-lower} at saddles of
    higher bond rank, with sectors built from the learned column and row spaces of every bond (its proof
    needs the learned part to be a product across a core); and an implementation of the rule that hops
    from saddle to saddle (at each plateau, compute the fixed-rank block and the local residuals of all
    small connected sets of cores, follow the most unstable direction, re-optimize at the new rank
    profile), compared run by run with gradient flow on the teachers of
    \cref{sec:s2s-locality,sec:teacher-ttn}.
  \item \textbf{Dynamics when the saddle order is below the route's.} Where the order is $2\ell<k^*$
    (\cref{thm:A-order-sharp}), the escape should take a time $\propto\isc^{-(2\ell-2)}$ instead of
    $\isc^{-(k^*-2)}$ \derived, and the learned component is then forgotten and relearned \empirical; both
    were checked on one teacher, from the saddle and on the few seeds that visit it from small
    initialization.
    Also open: the borderline case in which the largest eigenvalue of the generalized piggyback operator
    equals $\tw j$ ($k^*\ge4$); \cref{thm:A-order-sharp} without its
    index condition, or for signed and non-pattern residuals; the length of the plateau after the
    forgetting; and an exponent near $4$ in half of the control seeds of the same check, where the route
    predicts $3$ (\path{notes/theory_saddle_order.md}, section 6). \emph{What would settle it:}
    pre-registered fits, per seed and over at least $1.5$ decades, of the escape time from the saddle plus
    a perturbation of size $\varepsilon$ (predicted $\propto\varepsilon^{-(\text{order}-2)}$) on every case
    with order below $k^*$ (MPS6 with two residuals or with one wrong-order residual; HT4 and binary8 with
    $D$ all sites) and on controls of order $k^*$; and, from small initialization, the fraction of runs that
    visit the saddle and the $\isc$ below which the reduced exponent holds.
  \item \textbf{Prefactors from random initialization.} The exponents are predicted, the prefactors are
    not, except that of the first escape, which is exact at leading order but has no closed form on trees
    with internal path amplitudes (binary8, HT, MPS with $\Ns\ge6$; the path-amplitude mode of
    \cref{prop:A-product-mode} is good to a factor $1.2$--$1.6$ there, \path{notes/theory_escape.md},
    section 7). Later prefactors are set by the route amplitudes at the start of each plateau, which vary
    between seeds by factors above $20$ at $k\ge4$; for generic teachers, offsets of up to about $50$ time
    units decide the order of near-tied escapes at accessible $\isc$ (\cref{prop:A-agf-race}).
    \emph{What would settle it:} a law for how route amplitudes are carried through each escape, tested
    against the per-seed prefactors already measured (\cref{sec:s2s-escape,sec:s2s-locality}); the
    distribution of the offsets over initializations would give the probability of each order at finite
    $\isc$.
  \item \textbf{Why routes die, and how often.} The reduced model of \cref{prop:A-trap} explains the decay
    of route ends at a rate $\propto\isc^2$ and the role of balance, but its criterion, evaluated at the
    start of the plateau, is neither sufficient nor necessary (seven dead runs had a live end); balanced
    initialization removes dead routes yet delays some escapes by $10^5$ or more; HT routes stall far less
    often than the heuristic predicts; spare channels raise the stalls on routes whose ends are capped, for
    no known reason; and whether most stalled odeco runs at $\Kc=7,8$ are long plateaus or dead is a
    \conjecture{} (\cref{sec:s2s-failure}). One route (MPS8, $D=\{1,5\}$, from the saddle) is steeper than
    predicted ($p=3.67\pm0.20$ against $3$), with no mechanism. \emph{What would settle it:} following
    $Q_{\nu\nu}+\norm{y_{\rm route}}^2$ through the whole plateau, not only at its start, in the runs of the
    stall diagnosis; a reduced model with capped end bonds; continuing every stalled run to a fixed
    multiple of its estimated plateau; and the trapped fraction against $\isc$ at $k\ge5$ for fan-in and
    balanced initialization (predicted to tend to the probability that all route ends are unfavourable,
    \conjecture).
  \item \textbf{Long routes.} Exponents for $k=6,7$ were measured over one decade, with balanced
    initialization only, on binary8 and HT4; MPS8 with $k=6$ gives $4.51\pm0.19$ against $4$; $k=8$ was
    not measured, and the growth exponent of a single run cannot separate $k=7$ from $8$
    (\cref{sec:s2s-locality}). At $k=4$ a second negative Hessian eigenvalue of the same order as the
    route's appears on the plateau, and for $k\ge5$ such directions might change how $\lambda_{\min}$ scales
    with $\isc$ (\cref{prop:A-signatures}). \emph{What would settle it:} balanced runs over more than a
    decade on MPS8 ($k=6$, $8$) and HT8 ($k=7$) with more seeds, and plateau Hessians for $k\ge5$ whose
    negative directions are identified as route or new-index pairs.
  \item \textbf{The learning order in closed form.} The race of independent components is exact only on
    MPS; where cores without sites couple the components (binary, HT) it still names the first component
    in $94$--$100\%$ of runs but has no closed form. For matrix-like pairs the prefactor of
    $P(\text{smaller first})$ is not derived, the exponent $n(1-\varrho)/\varrho$ with $n=\bd e$ for adjacent
    cores is a \conjecture{} supported only at the smallest $\isc$, and whether gradient flow has the
    leading-order exponent is open. The conjectures that $P(\text{larger first})\to\frac12$ as $\Kc\to\infty$
    and that $P\to1$ needs both $d$ and $\chi$ to grow are untested (\cref{sec:s2s-order},
    \cref{prop:A-race}). \emph{What would settle it:} a leading-order emulation of the coupled race on
    binary8 and HT trees; $\isc$ scans down to $10^{-6}$ for adjacent pairs on MPS4 and MPS6 with
    $1{,}000$ seeds; race statistics on MPS with $\Kc$ up to $16$, and on uncapped bonds with growing $d$.
  \item \textbf{The regime boundary at large $\Kc$, and in $\chi$.} Beyond seven cores the
    saddle-to-saddle boundary is only bracketed, on a target that is unrealizable at the bond dimensions
    used, and all large-$\Kc$ runs have $d=2$; so whether $\isc_c(\Kc)$ tends to $1$, the limit of aligned
    initialization, or to $\sqrt d$, a fixed amplitude per component, is a \conjecture{}
    (\cref{sec:regime-K}). Its $\chi^{-1/2}$ scaling is measured on one tree over less than a decade and is
    not derived, and the claim that the rich window shrinks to a band of width $O(1/\Kc)$ in $\ln\isc$ if
    both boundaries have finite limits in $\fsc$ is a \conjecture{}
    (\cref{sec:regime-sigma-chi,sec:other-rich}). \emph{What would settle it:} a realizable $\Kc$ sweep
    (spare channels or full bonds) at $d=2$ and $d=3$, with budgets long enough at $\Kc\ge8$ and a
    pre-registered comparison of $\isc_c^{\Kc}=\text{const}$ with $\isc_c^{\Kc}/\sqrt{d^\Ns}=\text{const}$;
    a derivation of the effective escape amplitude $\propto\isc\sqrt\chi$; maps on a second tree over more
    than a decade of $\chi$.
  \item \textbf{Large bond dimension.} Under the $\mu$P-type parameterization the kernel keeps moving as
    $\chi$ grows on binary4, but the limit itself is not derived (a two-population mean field with a
    pairwise coupling through the root is a \conjecture), the runs on the binary tree with $\Ns=8$ are
    incomplete, and whether saddle-to-saddle dynamics survives $\chi\to\infty$ in this parameterization is
    unknown (\cref{sec:other-large-chi}). \emph{What would settle it:} the limiting dynamics on binary4,
    compared with the runs at $\chi=16$--$128$; complete runs on deeper trees; the transfer of learning
    rates across $\chi$.
  \item \textbf{Adam's later plateaus.} Adam keeps the order and makes the first escape independent of
    $\isc$, but no law is established for its later plateaus (per-seed local slopes from $-0.4$ to $5.5$ on
    the binary tree with $\Ns=8$, over half a decade), its $\epsilon$ law was tested only at $\Kc=8$, and
    that it stays sequential because one component dominates the sign of the gradient is a
    \conjecture, as is the $(1+\beta)/(1-\beta)$ delay of heavy ball (\cref{sec:other-optimizers}).
    \emph{What would settle it:} a sign-flow analysis of the route equations with and without $\epsilon$;
    later plateaus over at least a decade of $\isc$ with every step recorded; the $\epsilon$ test on trees
    with $\Kc=3$--$7$.
  \item \textbf{Gradient noise on later plateaus.} The law $T_{\rm SGD}/T_{\rm GF}=-\ln(1-x)/x$ is
    heuristic and holds to about $10\%$ only for $T_{\rm SGD}/T_{\rm GF}\lesssim1.5$; its coefficient
    $c_\gamma$ is fitted ($32.6$, against $26$ from a commuting-pair estimate) and grows near the trap.
    The rate at which noise erases learned components near the stability limit, a formula for the
    almost-sure stability threshold, and whether noise can revive a dead route are open
    (\cref{sec:other-optim}; \path{notes/theory_optimizer.md}, section 6). \emph{What would settle it:} the
    top Lyapunov exponent of the linearized route block (route entries, mixed learned entries,
    noise-driven couplings) for general trees and route degrees; SGD runs started from the stalled states
    of \cref{sec:s2s-failure}.
  \item \textbf{Covariance and teachers beyond the tested range.} Under a misaligned covariance the slope
    of the escape from random initialization is only bounded, since the head start under the
    preconditioned flow is not derived (\cref{sec:cov-misaligned}). Correlated inputs were run neither on
    HT trees (predicted crossover $c^*\sim\isc^2$, heuristic) nor on MPS with $\Ns>4$, where a theory check
    on MPS6 found a slow second stage that is not a single strict saddle
    (\path{notes/theory_covariance.md}, section 9). Later saddles of generic teachers on binary and HT
    students were not timed (\cref{sec:teacher-generic}), and that the local maxima of $g$ have bond rank
    one, and that random initialization reaches one, are conjectures (\cref{app:teachers}). A natural case
    is untried: correlations that decay with distance on the tree, the question of whether the data
    should match the tree. \emph{What would settle it:} the preconditioned head start; $\isc$ sweeps with
    onset events on HT4 and MPS6 under correlated inputs; the timing of later rank increases on binary8
    and HT8 with generic teachers; a search for local maxima of $g$ of higher bond rank on small trees.
  \item \textbf{Real data: why the pixel order matters.} A share of route amplitude computed from the data
    before training ranks pixel orders by their final loss in a pre-registered test, but not by their
    early speed; that it acts through which label-relevant interactions lie on routes of five rather than
    seven cores is a \conjecture, and the data-only rank-one search is a noisy proxy for the state that
    gradient flow reaches (\cref{sec:real-data}; \path{notes/real_data.md}, sections 8.4 and 9.6). On the
    MPS, tree shape and pixel order are confounded (snake order only) and two edges rise like power laws
    where rank-one counting predicts logarithmic routes; on binary16 the curvature exponent at the first
    plateau ($0.87$--$0.89$ on three seeds) lies below the $1$ of $k=3$. \emph{What would settle it:}
    timing the rank crossing of every later route on binary16 and checking that pixel orders differ in
    when their routes with $k=5$ escape; longer horizons or larger $\isc$, at which routes with $k=7$
    escape too and the effect should fade; MPS chains in several pixel orders; larger images.
  \item \textbf{Finite samples.} Small initialization recovers the teacher with its bond ranks from about
    $200$--$250$ samples, $5$--$9$ times the dimension of the teacher manifold, in both settings tested; this
    threshold is unexplained, and it rests on one teacher per setting, noise-free labels and gradient
    descent with capped steps (\cref{sec:other-optim}). \emph{What would settle it:} sample-size sweeps
    over teachers and trees with manifolds of different dimension, with label noise, compared with
    dimension counts.
  \item \textbf{Exact theory and scope.} The theorems of \citet{kumar2025towards} on the dynamics after an
    escape need non-degenerate critical points, which the gauge orbits exclude for $\chi\ge2$; a quotient
    (Morse--Bott) version would make the escape path rigorous. Whether the per-bond conservation laws are
    all the conservation laws (the algorithm of \citealp{marcotte2023abide}, run on small trees) and the
    explicit metric $(\Jac\Jac\T)^{-1}$ of balanced TTNs are open. Beyond the model: input laws that are
    not products (beyond the one-factor model), classification and several outputs, nonlinear local
    features, and what all of this implies for the choice of initialization and of tree in practice.
    \emph{What would settle it:} the quotient version of those theorems, first for chains; the
    completeness algorithm run on trees with three or four cores; and, for the scope, the route rule tested
    with a label leg on one core, as in MPS classifiers \citep{stoudenmire2016supervised}, and with
    correlated, non-Gaussian features beyond \cref{sec:real-data}.
\end{enumerate}
