\section{Other regimes}\label{sec:other-regimes}

This section looks at the regimes of \cref{sec:regime-map} other than saddle-to-saddle: lazy training and its
failure below the cut dimension, the rich window between the two boundaries, runs that stall, and the limit of
large bond dimension under three parameterizations; then at how discrete steps, gradient noise and finite
samples (\cref{sec:other-optim}) and the optimizers used in practice (\cref{sec:other-optimizers}) modify the
gradient-flow picture. Settings of the first four subsections: \cref{sec:regime-protocol,app:exp-regimes}.

\subsection{Lazy regime}\label{sec:other-lazy}

At large $\isc$ the centred model trains like its linearization at $\params_0$: the function moves along the range
of the tangent kernel $\TK=\Jac\Pre\Jac\T$, which hardly changes. Lazy training is known in general
\knownresult[\citealp{jacot2018neural,chizat2019lazy,lee2019wide}]; what the tree adds is the kernel. At
initialization $\E\TK$ is a multiple of the identity at every bond dimension, but the rank of $\TK$ is the dimension
of the manifold of tensors with the given bond ranks, which reaches $d^\Ns$ only when every bond is full
(\cref{prop:A-kernel}) \proven{} \newresult[on every tree].

\paragraph{Lazy only with full bonds.}
\derived{} \empirical{} \knownresult[that a linearized model fits only the projection of the target on its
tangent space: \citealp{chizat2019lazy}, Thm~2.5; \citealp{ghorbani2019limitations}, Thm~2] With a bond below its
cut dimension the linearized flow fits only the projection of $\Wt$ on the range of $\TK$ and stops at
$\frac12\normF{P_\perp\Wt}^2$, on binary4 a fraction $(1-\chi/d^2)^2$ of $\Loss(0)$ on average ($0.44$, $0.31$,
$0.11$ at $\chi=3$, $4$, $6$; \cref{app:boundaries}). In addition the centred target $\Wt+\Wz$ is unrealizable at
every $\isc$, and gradient flow ends on its best approximation (\cref{fig:other-regimes}(a)): at small $\isc$ this
floor rises like $\isc^{2\Kc}$ (mean $(1-\dim\mathcal M_\chi/d^\Ns)\,\isc^{2\Kc}/\normF{\Wt}^2$, the seed median about
$0.7$ times it, \eqref{eq:A-centred-floor}), and at large $\isc$ it saturates at the lazy residual: at $\isc=40$ the
final loss of each of the 18 centred runs at $\chi=3$, $4$, $6$ equals its residual computed at initialization to
$0.13\%$, with $\dTK^{\rm probe}<10^{-3}$. Once the floor exceeds the convergence threshold (for every seed from
$\isc=1.0$, $1.0$, $1.6$) the runs are stalled by the rule of \cref{tab:regimes}, though their dynamics are
kernel-like at large $\isc$.
A lazy regime therefore needs every bond at its cut dimension (binary4: $\chi\ge9$), which grows like $d^{\Ns/2}$
for the middle bonds.

\paragraph{Anisotropic, ill-conditioned kernels.}
\derived{} \empirical{} \newresult[for TTNs; the mean kernels of MPS and TT models:
\citealp{guo2021neural,wesel2025tensor}] A single initialization has an isotropic kernel only if every bond is far
above its cut dimension; at a capped full bond the cores next to it are square Gaussian matrices and
$\lambda_{\min}(\TK)/\bar\lambda\sim10^{-4}$ (\cref{app:boundaries}). The lazy fit then converges in a kernel time of
order $(\bar\lambda/\lambda_{\min})\ln(1/{\rm tol})$. On the binary tree with $\Ns=8$, $d=2$, the square full bonds
($\chi=16$) are not lazy within the step limit: every run from $\isc=2.3$ stops there at the same seed-dependent
loss, $\Loss/\Loss(0)=0.05$--$0.44$, for every $\isc$. Over-parameterized twice (bonds $8$ above two sites, $32$
above four), the runs are lazy in 2 of 5 at $\isc=1.42$ and in 4 of 5 or 6 at each $\isc$ from $2.47$ to $7.6$ (the
others stopped at the step limit at $\Loss/\Loss(0)=0.0014$--$0.0054$ with $\dTK^{\rm probe}<0.02$); the kernel
boundary is $1.45$ (probe kernel, median of four seeds). At practical $\chi$ the lazy regime is thus kernel
regression with a random, tree-dependent and anisotropic kernel, not with its mean.

\paragraph{Lazy by the kernel is not small motion.}
\derived{} Under the standard parameterization the root of binary4 carries a share $\chi^2/(2d^2+\chi^2)$ of the
kernel, and its own kernel block does not depend on it: a lazy run can be a trained readout over fixed random
features, with the root moving by more than $100\%$ while $\dTK<0.1$ (\cref{app:boundaries}). Per-core changes
should be reported next to $\dTK$ (\cref{sec:other-large-chi}).

\subsection{Rich, non-incremental regime}\label{sec:other-rich}

Between the two boundaries the runs converge and move their kernel ($\dTK^{\rm probe}$ of the binary4 rich runs:
$0.10$ to $10^7$, median 68), but their events overlap: some $\sep i\le3$.

\paragraph{What sets the crossover.}
\derived{} (aligned initialization) \empirical{} (random) The plateaus between events grow like
$\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}$ as $\isc\to0$ while the event widths stay $O(1)$, so the separation ratios fall through $3$ as
$\isc$ grows; for aligned initialization \cref{prop:A-s2s-aligned} gives the boundary exactly, with the threshold
exponent of \citet{gissin2020implicit} \knownresult. From random initialization the crossover is a seed property:
on binary4 the per-seed boundaries at one $\chi$ span a factor $1.9$--$16$ for $\chi\le12$ and $1.4$--$2.7$ for
$\chi\ge16$ (six seeds). In $\isc$ the rich window spans a factor $18$--$23$ on binary4 at large $\chi$ and $2.7$ on
the binary tree with $\Ns=8$ (\cref{sec:regime-sigma-chi}). If both boundaries have finite limits in the
function-space scale $\fsc=\isc^\Kc$, the window shrinks to a band of width $O(1/\Kc)$ in $\ln\isc$
\conjecture.

\paragraph{Transients.}
\empirical{} During an escape, components not yet learned rise transiently (for matrix-like pairs the peak falls
like $\isc^{2(1-\varrho)}$, \conjecture, \cref{app:boundaries}). In 116 of the $9{,}165$ runs such a transient
crossed $\frac12$ before the component was learned (14 more cross $\frac12$ repeatedly only in a component never
learned); with events dated at the first crossing, 15 runs would be rich instead of saddle-to-saddle. Events are
therefore dated by the final rise (\cref{sec:setup-diagnostics}).

\paragraph{Lazy, then rich.}
\derived{} ($\Ns=4$) \empirical{} \knownresult[analogues: mixed lazy and active dynamics,
\citealp{tu2024mixed}; lazy-then-rich transitions, \citealp{lyu2024dichotomy}] The two-copy model is realizable at
a deficient bond when $2\chi$ reaches the teacher's bond rank. At large $\isc$ it first fits the tangent part lazily
and sits at the lazy residual, then escapes by rotating its bond subspaces, after a gradient-flow time
$\propto\isc^2$, a kernel time $\propto\isc^{2\Kc}$ (\cref{app:boundaries}). On binary4 at $\chi=3$ (six seeds) the
runs converge (rich) for $\isc=1.0$--$4.0$, end at $\Loss/\Loss(0)=0.005$--$0.05$ at $\isc=6.3$, at $0.13$--$0.44$ at
$\isc=10$, and at the lazy residual from $\isc=16$ on: the escape leaves any fixed budget as $\isc$ grows. A light
root with a deficient bond behaves the same way, lazy and then rich (\cref{sec:regime-imbalance,app:boundaries}).

\paragraph{Merged first escapes.}
\empirical{} \newresult{} In the two-copy model at small $\isc$ the two copies start identical, so their
near-origin escapes coincide: each copy learns part of both of the first two components at once, and the first
two events merge. On binary4 at $\chi=18$, $24$, $36$ the smallest $\isc$ ($0.010$) is labelled saddle-to-saddle in
5, 4 and 5 of six seeds and the next ($0.016$) in 4, 3 and 2, but the crossing $\min_i\sep i=5$ exists in only 2,
3 and 0 seeds (the largest $\min_i\sep i$ at these $\isc$ is $7.1$, $14.4$, $4.8$), so the sweep fails condition
(iii) of the saddle-to-saddle signature. Adam's runs from small initialization are rich,
non-incremental in the same sense (\cref{sec:other-optimizers}).

\subsection{Stalled runs}\label{sec:other-stalled}

A run is stalled when its loss is still above $10^{-3}\Loss(0)$ at the end of its budget (\cref{tab:regimes}). In
the maps of \cref{sec:regime-map} this has four causes: the floor of an unrealizable centred target
(\cref{sec:other-lazy}); a plateau longer than the budget, since a route through $k$ cores takes a time
$\propto\isc^{-(k-2)}$ (\cref{sec:s2s-locality}); a dead route, whose end cores have decayed to roundoff under the
conservation laws (the trap of \cref{prop:A-trap}); and the step limit, met by unfinished lazy fits at square
bonds and by some deep-tree runs (for example on MPS with $\Ns=8$ at $\isc=0.1$ after $2\cdot10^4$ steps, at
$t=1.4\cdot10^9$ of a budget of $5.7\cdot10^9$). Only the first is specific to the centred model.

\paragraph{Bond dimension equal to the teacher rank.}
\empirical{} confirming the prediction of \cref{app:boundaries} (mechanism: \conjecture, heuristic) With
$\chi=\ncomp$ the last component must use the last free channel of every bond, through a route of all $\Kc$ cores,
and fan-in initialization makes the root bonds child-heavy ($\E\Q e=\isc^2(1-1/\chi)\Id\succ0$), so the route ends
decay at a rate $\propto\isc^2$ while the escape needs a time $\propto\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}$ (\cref{sec:s2s-failure}); for
$\Kc\ge5$ the route can die first.
The stall test (\cref{tab:stall-test}; $d=3$, $\chi=3$, odeco teacher $(3,2,1)$, $\isc=0.3$, $0.45$, $0.6$, six
seeds, budgets $6\cdot10^4$--$1.6\cdot10^7$ time units) confirms it: most runs on the binary tree with $\Ns=8$
($\Kc=7$) and on the MPS with $\Ns=8$ ($\Kc=8$; every fan-in run) never learn the last component, in the plain as
in the centred model, so $\Wz$ is not the cause. From fan-in initialization the stalled route is dead: its third
bond singular value ends at roundoff (below $10^{-12}$; in 28 of 32 stalled plain runs). Gauge-balanced
initialization (the same
$\Wz$ re-gauged to $\Q e=0$) removes the dead routes but not the stall: the route still decays, its third singular
value is $1.4\cdot10^{-5}$ (median) at the end, and 12 of 18 runs do not escape. This is the trap of
\cref{sec:s2s-failure} in the regime maps; that section gives the odeco and pattern-teacher evidence, the
criterion for a dead route and the conflicts that remain open.

\paragraph{Stalls across the number of cores.}
\empirical{} In the plain-model $\isc\times\Kc$ sweeps (\cref{app:exp-regimes}), 546 of the 795 stalled runs end
with a dead route, 212 with a route still decaying and 37 on a long plateau (by the $\ncomp$-th bond singular value
at the end, with the thresholds of \cref{tab:stall-test} and $10^{-4}$). Spare channels help where every route end has one: on the binary tree with $\Ns=8$ at $d=3$, $\chi=6$
no run stalls at $\isc\le0.63$ (30 runs), but on the MPS with $\Ns=8$ every run stalls, its end bonds being capped
at $d=3$, the teacher rank; at $d=2$ the MPS with full bonds still stall in up to 3 of 6 seeds. With the centred
model at $\chi=4$ the stalls below the boundary sit at a partial fit and are fixed by the initialization
(\cref{sec:regime-K}). At practical budgets stalls thus occupy part of the saddle-to-saddle side of every deep
tree whose route ends lack spare channels, as on the pattern-teacher routes of \cref{sec:s2s-failure}.

\paragraph{Long plateaus are not barren plateaus.}
\derived{} From small initialization the gradient is $O(\isc^{\Kc-1})$ and the first plateau lasts
$\propto\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}$ (\cref{sec:s2s-escape}): deep trees at small $\isc$ stall as if on a barren plateau. The
cause differs: the gradient is small because the initialization is, not because it concentrates around zero over
random initializations of fixed norm, and with the fan-in normalization at $\isc=1$ the initial function has unit
norm on every tree (\cref{prop:init-norm}). For tensor networks, barren plateaus are known for global but not local
losses of unitary-embedded MPS \knownresult[\citealp{liu2022presence}] and are absent from the energy optimization of
isometric MPS, TTNS and MERA \knownresult[\citealp{miao2024isometric,barthel2025absence}].

\subsection{Large bond dimension: NTK versus \texorpdfstring{$\mu$P}{muP}}\label{sec:other-large-chi}

As $\chi$ grows at fixed $\isc$, the scales of the cores and their learning-rate multipliers decide whether the
features still move. We compare three parameterizations, written as initial scales and multipliers $\lrm v$
(\cref{sec:setup-init}, variant (e)): the \emph{standard} one (SP: fan-in initialization, $\lrm v=1$); \emph{NTK}
($\lrm v=1/\fanin v$, so that every core contributes equally to the expected kernel); and \emph{$\mu$P-TTN}, the
spectral condition of \citet{yang2024spectral} applied with the injective norm of each core, since a core only
ever acts on product vectors: fan-in initialization except at the root, which is scaled by
$(\sum_\ell m_\ell)^{-1/2}=(2\chi)^{-1/2}$ on binary4 ($m_\ell$ its child-leg dimensions), and $\lrm v=\bd v/\fanin v$
(\cref{app:boundaries}) \derived{} \newresult[the spectral condition is known; factor-wise rules for structured
layers: \citealp{qiu2024compute,potapczynski2024searching}].

\paragraph{Predictions.}
\derived{} (binary4, centred, isotropic limit $\chi\gg d^2$, fixed $\xi=\normF{\Wt}/\fsc$) In each parameterization
the first- and second-order constants $c_1$, $c_2$ of the law \eqref{eq:A-lazy-dk} carry the same power of $\chi$, so
the kernel change scales as $\chi^{-3}$ (SP), $\chi^{-1}$ (NTK) and $\chi^0$ ($\mu$P-TTN); the parameters move as in
\cref{tab:large-chi} (\cref{app:boundaries}; constants in \path{notes/theory_regimes.md}, R4 and R12). Under SP the
root, with fan-in $\chi^2$, carries almost all of the kernel and the model becomes linear in the root over fixed
random features: lazy at any fixed $\isc$ as $\chi$ grows, centred or not. Under NTK the cores move by
$O(\chi^{-1/2})$ or less, also lazy in the limit, as for the NTK of MPS \knownresult[\citealp{guo2021neural}].
Under $\mu$P-TTN every core's update stays of order one.

\paragraph{Measurements.}
\empirical{} confirming the predictions, except for the SP leaf cores ($6.5$ standard errors steeper) and, at $3.3$
standard errors, the SP parameter change. Binary4, $d=3$, teacher $(3,2,1)$, centred,
$\isc=1.5$ ($\xi=1.11$), $\chi=16$--$128$ (four values, $0.9$ decade), six seeds; tensor kernel
(\cref{fig:other-regimes}(b), (c); \cref{tab:large-chi}). Under $\mu$P-TTN the kernel change approaches the
predicted level, $5.1$, $5.7$, $6.1$, $6.2$ at $\chi=16$, $32$, $64$, $128$ against $6.2$--$6.3$: feature learning
survives. Under NTK the fit over $\chi=16$--$128$ is pulled by $\chi=16$--$32$, where $\dTK$ is still $0.4$--$0.6$;
between $\chi=64$ and $128$ the slope is $-1.01$, with levels $0.250$ and $0.124$ against $0.240$ and $0.120$ from
the second-order law with the NTK constants. Under SP the kernel change falls like $\chi^{-3.02\pm0.06}$, while at
$\chi=128$ the root still moves by $9\%$ and the leaf cores by $2\cdot10^{-4}$ (medians; \cref{fig:other-regimes}(c)). At $\isc=1$ ($\xi=3.74$, further from the
lazy limit) the slopes are shallower (SP $-2.58\pm0.06$, NTK $-0.62\pm0.02$, $\mu$P-TTN $+0.089\pm0.024$): the
exponents hold as $\xi\to0$. The plain model gives the same exponents within the errors ($\isc=1.5$: $-3.06\pm0.07$,
$-0.83\pm0.04$, $+0.095\pm0.024$): with full, over-parameterized bonds, centring does not change the scaling. Scope:
binary4 only; runs on the binary tree with $\Ns=8$ are incomplete and not interpreted. The factor-wise rule of
structured layers, applied to a TTN, makes the initial kernel of that tree vanish like $\chi^{-2}$ and slows the
initial fit about linearly in $\chi$ (\cref{app:boundaries}). The $\mu$P-TTN limit itself is not derived; on binary4
it may be a two-population mean field with a pairwise coupling through the root \conjecture.

\subsection{Discrete steps, gradient noise and finite samples}\label{sec:other-optim}

% errata 1-7 override the note and are applied below), theory in notes/theory_optimizer.md v2 and
% app:optimizer; settings in app:exp-optim; every number from experiments/optim_samples/results/*.csv.
% WITHDRAWN, do not reintroduce: "an O(eta) step flips near-tied races and can remove a plateau" (and
% the conjecture that flips vanish as sigma -> 0); the chi = 3 centred-model claims ("the GD limit is
% 2/lambda_GF ~ sigma^{-2(K-1)}", "no bounded oscillation on the lazy side"); "event shifts match to
% 3 digits for every event"; "P7 confirmed"; "B = 16 and 64 agree to 0.5%"; "no forgetting at
% <= 0.24 eta*_ms"; "persistent order changes at B = 1" (all are forget-and-relearn runs);
% "generalization degrades monotonically with the init scale"; from the theory note v1:
% noise-driven escapes faster than gradient flow, eta_ms as the divergence threshold, an unbiased
% SGD escape time, O(sigma^2) excess sharpness.

Gradient flow is the limit of gradient descent (GD) as the step $\lr\to0$ and of online SGD as
$\lr/\batch\to0$, and the population loss is the limit of the empirical loss as $\nsamp\to\infty$. How
much of \cref{sec:s2s} survives finite values? GD keeps gradient flow's saddle sequence up to a sharp
stability limit, with event times shifted by a computable $O(\lr)$; gradient noise never shortens a
plateau, but it lengthens later ones and can trap the dynamics at a saddle; and from a finite training
set small initialization recovers the teacher with its bond ranks, while the lazy limit fails as
dimension counting predicts.

\paragraph{Setup} (details in \cref{app:exp-optim}).
Whitened Gaussian inputs, odeco teacher with weights $(3,2,1)$, $d=\chi=3$, fan-in initialization (seeds
0--4); binary trees with $\Ns=4$ ($\Kc=3$) and $\Ns=8$ ($\Kc=7$), MPS with $\Ns=8$ ($\Kc=8$). GD and online SGD
(fresh inputs at every step) run on the clock $t=\lr\times(\text{steps})$ and are compared with gradient
flow from the same initial point (DOP853, tolerances $10^{-10}$ and $10^{-14}$). Events are persistent,
as in \cref{sec:other-optimizers}: the last upward crossing of $\ov r=\frac12$.

\paragraph{The stability limit.}
\proven{} \knownresult[deep linear networks: \citealp{mulayoff2020unique,ghosh2025learning}]
\newresult[general trees] At the balanced global minimum the Hessian $J\T J$ has largest eigenvalue
$\lambda_{\rm bal}=\Kc\tw1^{2-2/\Kc}$, and every other aligned minimum is sharper (\cref{prop:A-pattern-rule};
with $\bd e=\ncomp$ every other minimum, \derived), so GD can converge to a global minimum only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:optim-etac}
  \lr<\lr_c=\frac{2}{\Kc\,\tw1^{2-2/\Kc}}
\end{equation}
($0.1541$, $0.0435$ and $0.0366$ for the three trees). Along the saddle-to-saddle path the sharpness is
$O(\isc^{\Kc-2})$ on the first plateau and $\Kc\max_r\tw r^{2-2/\Kc}$ over the components learned so far
\derived{} (\cref{app:optimizer}): a step above $\lr_c$ is felt only once component 1 is learned, and there
is no catapult at the start (the condition $\lr\lambda_{\max}(\Hess)>2$ of \citet{lewkowycz2020large} fails
near the origin). Measured \empirical{} on binary $\Ns=4$ ($\isc=0.003$--$0.3$, five seeds, up to 18 steps
from $0.01\lr_c$ to $3\lr_c$; \cref{fig:optim-gd}a): all 195 runs with $\lr\le0.95\lr_c$ are GF-like and all
80 at $1.05$--$1.3\lr_c$ oscillate (an independent code placed the edge between $0.99\lr_c$ and $1.01\lr_c$).
Below $\lr_c$, GD learned the components in gradient flow's order in all 765 runs, including 40 more
seeds on binary $\Ns=4$ and both deep trees at $\isc=0.9$; a larger step only enlarges transient excursions of
near-tied components, which cross $\frac12$ and fall back (at $\isc=0.1$ in $0.09\pm0.04$ of 45
initializations at $0.1\lr_c$ and $0.18\pm0.06$ at $0.9\lr_c$).

\paragraph{Below the limit, GD is gradient flow on a shifted clock.}
\derived{} \newresult[for TTNs] \knownresult[the modified flow: \citealp{barrett2021implicit}] To first
order in $\lr$, GD follows the modified flow $\dot\params=-(\Id+\frac\lr2\Hess)\nabla\Loss$, whose critical
points are those of $\Loss$: the saddles are gradient flow's, and each event moves by $\lr c_e+O(\lr^2)$,
with $c_e$ computed without free parameter from the variational equation along the gradient-flow path
(\cref{prop:A-gd-shift,app:exp-optim}). Measured \empirical: over 526 comparisons at $\lr\le0.1\lr_c$ the
shift agrees with $\lr c_e$ within $0.3\%$, $1\%$ and $5\%$ in $66\%$, $90\%$ and $97\%$ of the cases, all 202
first events within $1\%$; the 15 misses beyond $5\%$ are later events, worst where $c_e\approx0$ after a
near-tied race and the $O(\lr^2)$ term dominates. Richardson extrapolation to $\lr\to0$ recovers $c_e$ to a median relative
$1.2\cdot10^{-5}$ (57 events). Since $c_1$ grows like $\ln(1/\isc)$ while $\tev1\propto\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}$, the
relative error of $\tev1$ vanishes as $\isc\to0$: at $0.9\lr_c$ it is $0.111$, $0.056$, $0.023$, $0.0099$ and
$0.0036$ for $\isc=0.3$, $0.1$, $0.03$, $0.01$, $0.003$ (five seeds; \cref{fig:optim-gd}b). At small
initialization every stable step thus reproduces gradient flow, the more closely the smaller $\isc$, as
GD does in \cref{sec:other-optimizers}.

\paragraph{Above the limit: edge of stability.}
\derived{} \knownresult[the scalar map and its two-cycle: \citealp{ghosh2025learning}, Thm~1,
\citealp{macdonald2025convergence}, Thm~5.3; edge of stability: \citealp{cohen2021gradient}] For
$\lr_c<\lr<2/\lambda_2(\Hess)$ only the radial mode of component 1 is
unstable, and GD on it is the balanced scalar map of \cref{fig:optim-gd}a (\cref{app:optimizer}): a
two-cycle from $\lr_c$, period four from $1.245\lr_c$, chaos from $1.32\lr_c$, then returns to the origin
interleaved with divergence ($\Kc=3$). Measured \empirical{} on binary $\Ns=4$: component 1 oscillates while
components 2 and 3 are still learned, and the time-averaged excess loss equals $\tw1^2$ times the map's
mean loss within $0.9\%$ at $1.05$--$1.3\lr_c$ (for example $0.4496$ against $0.4496$ at $1.2\lr_c$, 25 runs;
\eqref{eq:A-eos-loss} is its small-excess limit). The map's bounded chaotic window does not survive:
the network diverges at $1.5$--$1.75\lr_c$ (38 of 40 runs), is thrown back to the origin saddle and
relearns its components again and again at $2\lr_c$ (25 of 25), and diverges from $2.5\lr_c$. The deep
trees, started at $\isc=0.9$ where the gradient-flow states are $10$--$35\%$ sharper than
$\lambda_{\rm bal}$, already oscillate or catapult at $0.9\lr_c$.

\paragraph{Larger initialization: catapults.}
\empirical{} \knownresult[catapults: \citealp{lewkowycz2020large}; flatter, more balanced minima in scalar
networks: \citealp{kreisler2023gradient}, Thms~3.2--3.3; limit sharpness at most $2/\lr$ in two-factor
factorization: \citealp{wang2022large}, Thm~3.2; balancing while oscillating beyond the edge:
\citealp{ghosh2025learning}, Prop.~2] At larger $\isc$ the minimum that gradient
flow reaches is sharper, and $2/\lambda_{\rm GF}$, with $\lambda_{\rm GF}$ the largest Hessian eigenvalue there,
replaces $\lr_c$. We follow it into the lazy regime with the centred model (\cref{sec:setup-init}) on
binary $\Ns=4$ at $\chi=9$, the cut dimension, where all 45 gradient-flow references converge:
$\lambda_{\rm GF}\propto\isc^{3.965\pm0.014}$ over $\isc=1.41$--$16$ (five seeds), close to the scale
$\isc^{2(\Kc-1)}=\isc^4$ of the tangent kernel at initialization (\cref{prop:A-kernel}), so $2/\lambda_{\rm GF}$
equals $\lr_c$ for $\isc\le0.3$, $0.65\lr_c$ at $\isc=1$ and $2.7\cdot10^{-4}\lr_c$ at $\isc=8$
(\cref{fig:optim-gd}c). With steps of $0.5$--$4$ times each run's own $2/\lambda_{\rm GF}$, all runs up to $0.95$
times are GF-like at every $\isc$, and monotone GD ends between $0.95$ and $1.2$ times. Beyond, small
initialization ($\isc=0.1$, $0.3$) oscillates and then diverges, since the balanced minimum is already the
flattest; from $\isc=1$ on GD catapults: the loss spikes (by up to $2\cdot10^5\Loss_0$ at $\isc=8$) and then
reaches gradient flow's final loss (86 of 90 runs below $10^{-6}\Loss_0$) at a flatter minimum, of
sharpness $0.58$--$1.00$ times $2/\lr$ (per run), for steps up to $1.5$ ($\isc=1$) and $3$ ($\isc=2$--$8$) times
$2/\lambda_{\rm GF}$; at $4$ times all runs diverge. For $\isc\ge2$ the window thus ends between
$6/\lambda_{\rm GF}$ and $8/\lambda_{\rm GF}$, $1.5$--$2$ times the $4/\lambda_0$ of the two-layer model of
\citet{lewkowycz2020large}. Imbalanced small initializations ($\isc=0.1$ with the root core at scale 2, 4
or 6) reproduce the known selection: the 104 of 150 runs that converge end $0.05$--$3\%$ below
$\min(\lambda_{\rm GF},2/\lr)$.

\paragraph{SGD: the first plateau keeps its law.}
\derived{} \newresult[for TTNs; framework: \citealp{li2017stochastic,ziyin2024typeii}] The per-sample
gradient carries the Jacobian $J=\partial\W/\partial\params$, which vanishes at the origin like the drift,
so noise cannot drive the first escape and its exponent $\Kc-2$ is unchanged. On an aligned mode it only
delays and spreads the escape, by \eqref{eq:A-sgd-first}: the mean delay is
$O(\lr(1+\kappa_{\rm n}/\batch)\ln(1/\isc))$ and the standard deviation $(\kappa_{\rm n}\tev1^{\rm GF}\lr/\batch)^{1/2}$,
with $\kappa_{\rm n}\approx3^\Ns$ set by the kurtosis of the product features. This is consistent with
the experiments of \citet{corlouer2026stochastic} on deep linear networks (their Fig.~2: order kept, SGD
slower).
Measured \empirical{} with online SGD on binary $\Ns=4$ (1780 runs; $\batch=1$--$256$; five initializations
with ten noise seeds each): no setting shortened the first plateau (\cref{fig:optim-sgd}a). At $\batch=1$
the mean delays are $+0.24\pm0.10$, $-0.01\pm0.12$ and $+0.13\pm0.07$ at $\isc=0.03$, $0.1$, $0.3$ (s.e.\ across
initializations; escape times $24.2$, $7.2$, $2.3$), against $+0.17$, $+0.12$, $+0.08$ predicted: consistent,
but resolved only at $\isc=0.03$. The spread scales as $(\lr/\batch)^p$ with $p=0.43$--$0.52$ (five sweeps, s.e.\ $0.01$--$0.05$),
against $\frac12$.

\paragraph{SGD: later plateaus are lengthened, and can trap.}
\conjecture{} (heuristic law) \knownresult[mechanism: stochastic collapse, \citealp{chen2023stochastic}]
Once a component is learned, a route of $k\ge3$ cores (\cref{sec:s2s-locality}) has no curvature, but its
entries are coupled to the learned cores by zero-mean multiplicative noise of order one, which drives
their log-amplitude down at a rate $c_\gamma\lr/\batch$. \Cref{prop:A-sgd-collapse} turns this into
\begin{equation}\label{eq:optim-collapse}
  \frac{T_{\rm SGD}}{T_{\rm GF}}=\frac{-\ln(1-x)}{x},\qquad x=c_\gamma\frac{\lr}{\batch}T_{\rm GF},
\end{equation}
for a $k=3$ plateau of gradient-flow length $T_{\rm GF}$, and trapping for $x\ge1$. Test \empirical{} on binary
$\Ns=4$ from the aligned route (component 1 learned, components 2 and 3 at per-core amplitude
$b_0=0.002$--$0.01$, so that $T_{\rm GF}=50$--$250$ is exact; $\lr/\batch=3.1\cdot10^{-5}$--$2.5\cdot10^{-4}$; 144 runs,
six noise seeds per point; \cref{fig:optim-sgd}b): every escaped run was slower than gradient flow, and
all 30 runs with $z=(\lr/\batch)T_{\rm GF}\ge0.025$ were trapped. One fitted $c_\gamma=32.6$ (the commuting-pair
estimate of \cref{app:optimizer} gives 26 for this teacher) yields $\chi^2=379$ for 18 degrees of freedom, against 535 for a pole $1/(1-x)$ and 929 for
$1+x/2$, and $x=1$ separates escaped from trapped conditions in 23 of 24 cases; but the effective $c_\gamma$
grows from 30 at small $x$ to 45 near the trap, so the law holds to about $10\%$ only for
$T_{\rm SGD}/T_{\rm GF}\lesssim1.5$. Only $\lr/\batch$ matters: $\batch=16$ and $64$ agree within $1.8$ combined s.e.\
at eight matched points, although $\batch=64$ is higher at all three escaped points with $b_0=0.003$. From
random initialization the second plateau is lengthened by
$19.8\pm5.1\%$ at $\isc=0.03$, $\batch=1$ (the 16 of 30 runs that kept component 1) against $17.7\%$ from
\eqref{eq:optim-collapse} with no further fit, but by $10.8\pm0.6\%$ against $4.4\%$ at $\isc=0.1$
(\cref{fig:optim-sgd}a). Consequence \derived: at fixed $\lr/\batch$ a later plateau grows faster than
$\isc^{-(k-2)}$ and becomes infinite at a finite $\isc$; on the aligned route at $\lr/\batch=1.25\cdot10^{-4}$,
$T_{\rm SGD}/T_{\rm GF}=1.13$, $1.37$ and $2.5$--$2.7$ for $b_0=0.01$, $0.005$, $0.003$, and all runs are trapped at
$0.002$: SGD stalls where gradient flow is only slow. A strict ($k=2$) route still escapes if
$\abs{\lambda_-}>c_\gamma\lr/\batch$ \derived: on the MPS with $\Ns=4$ and a pattern teacher $(3,0.3)$ whose
second component needs $k=2$, SGD at $\batch=1$ learned it in 5 of 5 runs, $8\pm5\%$ later than gradient
flow \empirical. No SGD run escaped a plateau faster than gradient flow.

\paragraph{SGD: stability limit and forgetting.}
\derived{} \newresult[the reduction to the pattern basis] \knownresult[the criteria:
\citealp{wu2018how,chemnitz2025characterizing}; exact mean square: \citealp{mulayoff2024exact}, Thm~5] At
the minimum the noise vanishes, and
the exact mean-square limit \eqref{eq:A-ms-threshold} on binary $\Ns=4$ is $\lr^*_{\rm ms}=0.00107$, $0.0161$
and $0.112$ for $\batch=1$, $16$, $256$, against $\lr_c=0.154$: per sample, small-batch SGD is about 150 times
less stable than GD, because Gaussian product features have kurtosis $3^\Ns$. Started $10^{-5}$ from the
minimum, SGD converged in 5 of 5 runs up to $1.5\lr^*_{\rm ms}$ and diverged in 9 of 10 at $2.5\lr^*_{\rm ms}$
($\batch=1$, $16$) \empirical, as the computed almost-sure threshold, about $1.9\lr^*_{\rm ms}$, predicts. Well
below it, noise on long plateaus erases learned components, which are then relearned \empirical: in the
random-initialization runs the first learned component fell back below $\frac12$ in $20$--$66\%$ of the runs at
$0.47\lr^*_{\rm ms}$ (most in the longest runs), $4\%$ at $0.38\lr^*_{\rm ms}$, 1 of 50 at $0.24\lr^*_{\rm ms}$ and
none below; these are also the only runs whose order differs from gradient flow's (10) or is unresolved
(19). On the aligned-route plateaus (up to 1000 time units; \cref{fig:optim-sgd}c), $\ov1$ fell below
$\frac12$ in 0 of 72 runs at $\lr\le0.076\lr^*_{\rm ms}$, 1 of 36 at $0.12$--$0.15\lr^*_{\rm ms}$ and 20 of 36 at
$0.25$--$0.30\lr^*_{\rm ms}$, so the safe fraction $0.25\lr^*_{\rm ms}$ seen with another teacher
(\cref{app:optimizer}) does not carry over. Near its limit SGD breaks the saddle sequence rather than
compressing it.

\paragraph{Finite samples: which interpolant?}
\empirical{} \newresult[for TTN regression] Binary tree with $\Ns=8$, $d=2$ ($\W\in\R^{256}$), odeco teacher
$(3,2)$ of bond rank 2 (its rank-2 TTNs form a manifold of dimension 28), $\nsamp$ Gaussian inputs with
noise-free labels, five seeds; GD on the empirical loss (step $1/\lambda_{\max}$, $6\cdot10^4$ steps); students with
$\chi=4$, whose tangent space at a random point has dimension 112 (\cref{prop:A-kernel}), and $\chi=16$, where
all bonds are full; test error $\normF{\W-\Wt}^2/\normF{\Wt}^2$ (\cref{fig:optim-samples}). Small
initialization ($\isc=0.3$, $0.5$) recovers the teacher with its bond ranks: test error below $10^{-2}$ in 5 of
5 seeds from $\nsamp=256$ at $\chi=4$ (about $2\cdot10^{-3}$ at 256 and $2\cdot10^{-4}$ at 384, limited by unfinished
training) and from $\nsamp=384$ at $\chi=16$, with mean effective bond rank $2.0$--$2.1$. For $\nsamp\le96$ it
fits the training data (to $1.3\%$ of the initial loss) with a wrong tensor in 89 of 90 runs (mean test
error $1.0$--$3.8$). The exact lazy limit, the minimum-norm solution
of the model linearized at initialization, fails as dimension counting predicts \derived: at $\chi=4$ the
teacher lies outside the tangent space, and the error is $1.7$--$32$ at every $\nsamp$, worse than predicting
zero; at $\chi=16$ it is exact only from $\nsamp=d^\Ns=256$. The centred model at $\isc=1$, which is not lazy (its
parameters move by $12$--$69\%$), reaches $0.01$--$0.04$ at $\nsamp=384$--$512$ with bond rank $2.4$--$2.9$; at
$\isc=1.5$ and $2$ it had not converged after $3\cdot10^5$ steps and is not ranked. With $\Ns=6$, $d=3$
($\W\in\R^{729}$, manifold dimension 48), small initialization recovers the teacher in 5 of 5 seeds at
$\nsamp=384$, while the $\chi=4$ lazy limit is worse than predicting zero at every $\nsamp\le384$. The learned bond
ranks are ordered small initialization $<$ centred $<$ lazy limit at every $\nsamp$, except at $\chi=16$ for
$\nsamp\ge256$, where the lazy limit is exact. A bias of small initialization towards low (hierarchical)
rank is known for tensor factorizations \knownresult[\citealp{razin2021implicit,razin2022implicit};
tensor-train completion, empirically: \citealp{milanesi2021implicit}; matrices: \citealp{arora2019implicit}],
and a linearized model can only fit the projection of the target on its tangent space
\knownresult[\citealp{chizat2019lazy}, Thm~2.5; \citealp{ghorbani2019limitations}, Thm~2; lazy training of
a tensor decomposition needs $\Omega(d^{\Ns-1})$ components for a rank-one target: \citealp{wang2020beyond},
Thm~1].
Scope: one teacher per
setting, noise-free labels, GD with capped steps, and no monotone trend in $\isc$ within small
initialization. The threshold of small initialization, 200--250 samples or 5--9 times the
teacher-manifold dimension in both settings, is unexplained.

\paragraph{When does gradient flow describe practical training?}
\derived{} (from the results above; one teacher, trees with $\Ns\le8$) GD follows gradient flow whenever
$\lr<2/\lambda_{\rm GF}(\isc)$, with event times off by $\lr c_e$ and, from small initialization, the same saddle
sequence; this limit is $\lr_c$ at small initialization and falls like $\isc^{-2(\Kc-1)}$ on the lazy side, where
steps up to $1.5$--$3$ times larger still reach gradient flow's loss through a catapult. SGD needs in
addition $\lr<\lr^*_{\rm ms}(\batch)$ (about $10^{-3}\batch$ here), $c_\gamma(\lr/\batch)T\ll1$
for every later plateau of length $T$, and $\lr$ a small fraction of $\lr^*_{\rm ms}$ when learned
components must survive long plateaus ($0.1\lr^*_{\rm ms}$ was safe over 1000 time units). Because plateaus
grow like $\isc^{-(k-2)}$, at fixed $\lr/\batch$ SGD eventually stalls or forgets where gradient flow is only
slow.

\subsection{Optimizers used in practice}\label{sec:other-optimizers}
% Sources: notes/optimizers.md v2 (its section 5 lists the claims withdrawn after review round 1),
% agent-runs/optimizers/REPORT.md, notes/reviews/optimizers_round1.md; settings in
% app:exp-optimizers. Withdrawn, do not reintroduce: Gram preconditioning is sigma-free; orders of
% ALS/DMRG/Riemannian GD "unrelated to the weights"; Adam's later plateaus at "half GF's rate" or set
% by the adaptive edge of stability; Adam generalizes worse than gradient flow.

Tensor-network models are trained with Adam \citep{kingma2015adam}, with ALS and DMRG sweeps
\citep{white1992density,holtz2012alternating} (in machine learning often with a gradient step on the
merged two-site core instead of an exact local solve, \citealp{stoudenmire2016supervised}) or with
Riemannian methods \citep{novikov2018exponential,steinlechner2016riemannian}, which use the geometry of
fixed-rank tensor manifolds \citep{holtz2012manifolds,uschmajew2013geometry}. Which features of the
gradient-flow picture of
\cref{sec:s2s} survive them? \Cref{tab:optimizers} collects the answers, \cref{fig:optimizers-side}
shows them from one initial point, and the evidence follows.

\paragraph{Setup} (details in \cref{app:exp-optimizers}).
Exact population loss, whitened inputs, double precision. Binary tree, $\Ns=4$ ($\Kc=3$), $d=3$,
$\chi=4$, odeco teacher $(3,2,1)$: all methods start from the same 42 fan-in initializations (seven
$\isc\in[0.002,0.128]$, six seeds). Deep trees, $\Ns=8$: binary ($\Kc=7$) and MPS ($\Kc=8$), with the
same teacher and with a pattern teacher E8 whose weaker components need $k=6$ and $k=2$ cores on the
MPS (\cref{prop:teacher-spectra}); $\isc\in\{0.3,0.8\}$, plus seven $\isc\in[0.25,0.8]$ (binary) and
eight $\isc\in[0.01,0.3]$ (MPS); six seeds; gauge-balanced initializations, and fan-in ones for the
gauge-dependent methods (fan-in gradient flow stalls on the odeco MPS, \cref{prop:A-trap}). Methods as
listed in \cref{tab:optimizers}, with GD and heavy ball ($\beta=0.9$) at effective steps
$\lr/(1-\beta)\le0.27\lr_c$ (\cref{app:optimizer}) and Adam's $\epsilon=10^{-8}$ unless stated. Clocks: $t$ for gradient flow (LSODA, events by root finding), $\lr n$ for GD,
$\lr n/(1-\beta)$ for heavy ball, steps for the other gradient methods, sweeps for ALS and DMRG;
exponents $p$ ($T\propto\isc^{-p}$) are per-seed fits, mean $\pm$ s.e.

\paragraph{GD and heavy ball reproduce gradient flow.}
\empirical{} \newresult[for TTNs] GD with $\lr=0.13\lr_c$ on binary $\Ns=4$ has gradient flow's
exponents to within $0.003$ and its order from all 42 initial points; its first-escape delay grows by
$0.0199\pm0.0000$ per e-fold of $1/\isc$, the asymptotic slope $\lr(\Kc-1)/2=0.020$ that follows from
\cref{prop:A-gd-shift}. Heavy ball's delay grows $19.0=(1+\beta)/(1-\beta)$ times faster
(\conjecture: the momentum analogue), hence $p=0.970\pm0.002$. On the deep trees ($\isc=0.8$) GD and
heavy ball escape after $1.001$--$1.006$ and $1.01$--$1.11$ times gradient flow's time.

\paragraph{Adam: same order, but the first escape forgets $\isc$.}
\derived{} \newresult[for TTNs; in aligned deep matrix factorization the sign-descent escape time is
likewise bounded as the initialization vanishes: \citealp{sabanayagam2026optimizer}, Thm~2 and Prop.~6]
With
$\epsilon=0$ Adam's step $-\lr\hat m_n/\sqrt{\hat v_n}$ is
invariant under a common rescaling of past gradients \knownresult[\citealp{kingma2015adam}], and near
the origin $\nabla\Loss$ is homogeneous of degree $\Kc-1$; so the iterates from $c\params_0$ with step
$c\lr$ are $c$ times those from $\params_0$ with step $\lr$, Adam sees $\isc$ only through $\isc/\lr$,
and the number $n_1$ of steps to the first escape stays bounded as $\isc\to0$ (in the sign-flow limit
$\lr\to0$ at fixed $\beta_1,\beta_2$ \knownresult[\citealp{ma2022qualitative}; Adam as sign descent:
\citealp{balles2018dissecting}], $\lr n_1$ tends to a constant). Measured
\empirical: $p=0.009$--$0.013$ on binary $\Ns=4$ for four learning rates, RMSProp alike
(\cref{fig:optimizers-adam-escape}); $p=0.049\pm0.022$ and $0.145\pm0.033$ ($\lr=10^{-3}$, $10^{-2}$;
small but not zero) on binary $\Ns=8$, where gradient flow has $5.00\pm0.02$ ($\isc\le0.4$, prediction
5; fit computed from the committed runs in the review of this experiment). The selection survives \empirical: Adam and RMSProp pick gradient flow's first component in
94--100\% of the resolved deep-tree runs and keep E8's locality order on the MPS (the weaker $k=2$
component before the stronger $k=6$ one, in 4--5 of 6 seeds, as gradient flow). On binary $\Ns=4$ the
first escape is as wide as its own time, so 79--100\% of the runs fail $\sep i>3$ and are rich,
non-incremental by \cref{tab:regimes}.

\paragraph{Adam's $\epsilon$ brings the gradient-flow law back.}
\derived{} \newresult[for TTNs; concurrently, the same law in aligned deep matrix factorization:
\citealp{sabanayagam2026optimizer}, Prop.~7] Where $\abs{\partial_j\Loss}\ll\epsilon$ Adam is gradient flow with
$t=\lr n/\epsilon$: from a small $\isc$ it crawls along the gradient-flow path until the gradient
reaches $\epsilon$, then escapes as in the sign regime,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:adam-eps}
  n_1\simeq\frac{\epsilon}{\lr}\,T_{\rm GF}(\isc)+n_{\rm sign}(\lr),
\end{equation}
so the law $T_{\rm GF}\propto\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}$ returns, with prefactor $\epsilon/\lr$, below the
crossover $\isc_\times$ where $\epsilon T_{\rm GF}=\lr n_{\rm sign}$. Test \empirical{} on the odeco MPS
with $\Ns=8$ (eight $\isc\in[0.01,0.3]$, six seeds, $\lr=10^{-3}$, $10^{-2}$): per seed, from that
seed's exact gradient-flow escape at $\isc=0.3$ times $(0.3/\isc)^6$ and its own escape with

The crossover depends on $\lr$ and on the tree: on binary $\Ns=4$, \eqref{eq:adam-eps} puts it near

\paragraph{Later plateaus and minima under Adam.}
\empirical{} Later gaps depend weakly on $\isc$ on binary $\Ns=4$ ($p=0.0$--$0.4$). On binary $\Ns=8$
they grow as $\isc$ decreases, up to $8\cdot10^4$ steps at $\isc=0.25$, but per-seed local slopes range
from $-0.4$ to $5.5$, so over half a decade no law is established (gradient flow: $4.72$ and $4.85$,
prediction 5); with $\isc\le0.4$, 83--100\% of these runs are S2S. Adam and RMSProp break the
conservation of $\Q e$ and end at imbalanced minima (balance index
$\normF{\Q e}/(\normF{\Gm{v}{e}\Gm{v}{e}\T}+\normF{\Gm{p}{e}\Gm{p}{e}\T})=0.21$--$0.96$),
$1.01$--$6.1$ times sharper than the balanced value \eqref{eq:A-sharpness}, the more so the larger the
step; gradient flow, GD and heavy ball end within $0.2\%$ of it.

\paragraph{No plateaus: sweeps and Riemannian GD.}
\proven{} \newresult[elementary] In the canonical form centred at core $v$ the environment of $v$ is an
isometry, so the least-squares core is $\nabla_{\G v}\ip{\W}{\Wt}$, a function of the other cores'
frames, which are $Q$ factors of QR decompositions and do not change when a core is multiplied by a
positive number: every ALS and DMRG iterate is independent of $\isc$. Riemannian GD,
$\W\leftarrow\mathrm{HSVD}_\chi(\W-\lr P_{T(\W)}(\W-\Wt))$ with $P_{T(\W)}$ the projector onto the
tangent space of the fixed-rank manifold, maps $\W_0$ at $\lr=1$ to
$\mathrm{HSVD}_\chi(P_{T(\W_0)}\Wt)$, and that tangent space is the same at $c\W_0$: it is exactly
$\isc$-free too. Measured \empirical: ALS and DMRG fit the teacher within one sweep, with two
components crossing $\ov r=\frac12$ in the same micro-step in 59 of 60 runs: no resolved order.
Riemannian GD needs 2--7 steps; it fits all components in one step on binary $\Ns=4$ but stages them on
binary $\Ns=8$, the weakest strictly last in 5 of 6 seeds for both teachers (3 and 2 of 6 on the MPS).

\paragraph{Re-gauged and Gram-preconditioned GD.}
Re-gauging moves all of $\normF{\W}\sim\isc^\Kc$ into the root, whose environment becomes an isometry,
so the root fits the teacher's projection onto the current frames in a time $O(1)$ for any $\isc$
\derived; on deep trees random frames keep only a fraction $\prod_e\chi/d^{n_e}$ of each component
over the root's edges ($2.4\cdot10^{-3}$ on binary $\Ns=8$, $0.2$ on $\Ns=4$), so they must rotate and
learning is sequential again, with $\isc$-free event times (\empirical, $\abs{p}\le0.02$). Gram
preconditioning moves each core towards its own least-squares solution, $\sim\isc^{-(\Kc-1)}$ times
larger than the core, so its first step overshoots the loss by $\sim\isc^{-2\Kc(\Kc-1)}$ (\derived; up
to $4\cdot10^{34}$); it has no flat plateau, but its time depends on $\isc$ (\empirical,
\cref{tab:optimizers}).

\paragraph{Finite samples.}
\empirical{} With $\nsamp=45$ and $60$ samples on binary $\Ns=4$ (four data draws, six
initializations, $\isc\in\{0.01,0.1\}$, paired comparisons), gradient flow and Adam reach the same
population loss: at $\nsamp=60$ every run that met the training tolerance recovered the teacher; at
$\nsamp=45$ none did, and Adam was better in 8--13 of 21--23 pairs per setting. Only a threshold count
differs (a fourth bond singular value above $0.1\,\bst{e}{\min}$: Adam with $\lr=10^{-2}$ in 35 of 44
runs, gradient flow in 5); the entropy rank does not.

\paragraph{Interpretation: which invariance removes which feature.}
Two properties of an update decide what survives (\cref{tab:optimizers}, second column). The first is
how its step scales with the gradient, which near the origin is $O(\isc^{\Kc-1})$ while the parameters
must move by their own size: a step proportional to the gradient needs a time $\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}$, a
step invariant under its rescaling (Adam) does not, and an absolute scale in the preconditioner, such
as Adam's $\epsilon$, brings the law back \derived; Adam still follows the gradient's sign, which one
component dominates, so learning stays sequential and ordered (\conjecture). The second is the
metric. Under gradient flow $\W$ moves through $\Jac\Jac\T$ ($\Jac=\partial\W/\partial\params$), which
vanishes like $\isc^{2(\Kc-1)}$ at the origin, whereas Riemannian GD (in continuous time the
Gauss--Newton flow $\dot\params=\Jac^+(\Wt-\W)$) and the exact block minimizations of ALS and DMRG
move $\W$ at a rate of order one. These methods are equivariant under the whole gauge group
\eqref{eq:gauge}, including the rescaling of a core against its neighbour, and see $\W_0$ only through
its frames, so they keep neither plateaus (\derived) nor $\isc$ (\proven); plateaus at singular points
of a parametrization, and the better behaviour of the natural gradient there, are known for multilayer
perceptrons \knownresult[\citealp{amari2006singularities}]. Equivariance alone is not enough: Gram
preconditioning is gauge-equivariant up to damping \derived{} but moves all cores at once from their
current values. Balance and the flattest minimum (\cref{prop:A-pattern-rule}) rest on the conserved
$\Q e$, the charges of the gauge symmetry under Euclidean gradient flow (\cref{prop:conservation});
their measured relative drift is $10^{-11}$, $10^{-5}$ and $3\cdot10^{-4}$ under gradient flow, GD and
heavy ball (momentum flows conserve fewer quantities than gradient flow, with time- and velocity-dependent
laws, \citealp{marcotte2024keep}), while Adam,
equivariant only under coordinate permutations and sign flips, breaks them at order one \empirical. A
law for Adam's later plateaus is open (\cref{sec:open}).
