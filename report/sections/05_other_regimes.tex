\section{Other regimes}\label{sec:other-regimes}

This section gives the detail of the regimes other than saddle-to-saddle that \cref{sec:regime-map} maps: lazy
training and its failure below the cut dimension, the rich window between the two boundaries, and the limit of
large bond dimension under three parameterizations. Runs that stall are treated with the failure modes of
saddle-to-saddle training in \cref{sec:s2s-failure}.\label{sec:other-stalled} The section then asks how
discrete steps, gradient noise and finite samples (\cref{sec:other-optim}) and the optimizers used in practice
(\cref{sec:other-optimizers}) change the gradient-flow picture. Settings of the first three subsections:
\cref{sec:regime-protocol,app:exp-regimes}; supplementary results: \cref{app:D-regimes,app:D-optim}.

\subsection{Lazy regime}\label{sec:other-lazy}

At large $\isc$ the centred model trains like its linearization at $\params_0$: the function moves along the range
of the tangent kernel $\TK$, which hardly changes. Lazy training is known in general
\knownresult[\citealp{jacot2018neural,chizat2019lazy,lee2019wide}]; what the tree adds is the kernel. At
initialization $\E\TK$ is a multiple of the identity at every bond dimension, but the rank of $\TK$ is the dimension
of the manifold of tensors with the given bond ranks, which reaches $d^\Ns$ only when every bond is full
(\cref{prop:A-kernel}) \proven{} \newresult[the mean kernel on every tree and the full-bond condition]
\knownresult[the rank as the dimension of the fixed-rank manifold:
\citealp{holtz2012manifolds,uschmajew2013geometry}].

\paragraph{What sets the lazy boundary.}
\derived{} \empirical{} On binary4 the kernel change has a zero-mean first-order part, linear in $\Wz$, and a
coherent second-order part (\cref{prop:A-lazy}). Near the boundary the measured $\dTK$ is $0.96$ times the law
$\sqrt{(c_1\xi)^2+(c_2\xi^2)^2}$ of \eqref{eq:A-lazy-dk} (median; $0.69$--$1.82$), and at large $\chi$ the
second-order part sets the boundary: the first-order estimate alone matches only up to $\chi=12$ (at $\chi=36$ it
gives $0.59$ against the measured $1.09$). Hence $\isc_{\rm lazy}\propto\chi^{-1/2}$ rather than $\chi^{-1}$
(\cref{sec:regime-sigma-chi}).

\paragraph{Lazy only with full bonds.}
\derived{} \empirical{} \knownresult[that a linearized model fits only the projection of the target on its
tangent space: \citealp{chizat2019lazy}, Thm~2.5; \citealp{ghorbani2019limitations}, Thm~2] With a bond below its
cut dimension the linearized flow fits only the projection of $\Wt$ on the range of $\TK$ and stops at
$\frac12\normF{P_\perp\Wt}^2$, on binary4 a fraction $(1-\chi/d^2)^2$ of $\Loss(0)$ on average ($0.44$, $0.31$,
$0.11$ at $\chi=3$, $4$, $6$; \cref{app:boundaries}). In addition the centred target $\Wt+\Wz$ is unrealizable at
every $\isc$, and gradient flow ends on its best approximation (\cref{fig:other-regimes}(a)): all 159 stalled
centred runs at $\chi=3$, $4$, $6$ end on the Eckart--Young minimum of $\Wt+\Wz$ across the cut $01|23$ (to
$0.04\%$). At small $\isc$ this floor rises like $\isc^{2\Kc}$. With $\chi$ equal to the teacher's bond rank
($\chi=3$) its mean is $(1-\dim\mathcal M_\chi/d^\Ns)\,\isc^{2\Kc}/\normF{\Wt}^2$ \eqref{eq:A-centred-floor}, and the
median of the six seeds is $0.48$ times it; at $\chi=4$, $6$, where the spare bond rank absorbs part of $\Wz$, the
median floor is $0.18$ and $0.044$ times that expression, which applies only at $\chi=3$. At large $\isc$ the floor
saturates at the lazy residual: at $\isc=40$ the final loss of each of the 18 centred runs at $\chi=3$, $4$, $6$
equals its residual computed at initialization to $0.13\%$, with $\dTK^{\rm probe}<10^{-3}$. Once the floor exceeds
the convergence threshold (for every seed from $\isc=1.0$, $1.0$, $1.6$ at $\chi=3$, $4$, $6$) the runs are stalled
on a floor by the rule of \cref{tab:regimes}, though their dynamics are kernel-like at large $\isc$. A lazy regime therefore needs every bond at its cut dimension (binary4: $\chi\ge9$), which grows
like $d^{\Ns/2}$ for the middle bonds.

\paragraph{Anisotropic, ill-conditioned kernels.}
\derived{} \empirical{} \newresult[for TTNs; the mean kernels of MPS and TT models:
\citealp{guo2021neural,wesel2025tensor}] A single initialization has an isotropic kernel only if every bond is far
above its cut dimension; at a capped full bond the cores next to it are square Gaussian matrices and
$\lambda_{\min}(\TK)/\bar\lambda\sim10^{-4}$ (\cref{app:boundaries}). The lazy fit then converges in a kernel time of
order $(\bar\lambda/\lambda_{\min})\ln(1/{\rm tol})$. On the binary tree with $\Ns=8$, $d=2$, the square full bonds
($\chi=16$) are not lazy within the step limit: every run from $\isc=2.3$ stops there at the same seed-dependent
loss, $\Loss/\Loss(0)=0.05$--$0.44$, for every $\isc$. Over-parameterized twice (bonds $8$ above two sites, $32$
above four), the runs are lazy in 2 of 5 at $\isc=1.42$ and in 4 of 5 or 6 at each $\isc$ from $2.47$ to $7.6$ (the
others stopped at the step limit at $\Loss/\Loss(0)=0.0014$--$0.0054$ with $\dTK^{\rm probe}<0.02$); the kernel
boundary is $1.45$ (probe kernel, median of four seeds). At practical $\chi$ the lazy regime is thus kernel
regression with a random, tree-dependent and anisotropic kernel, not with its mean.

\paragraph{Lazy by the kernel is not small motion.}
\derived{} Under the standard parameterization the root of binary4 carries a share $\chi^2/(2d^2+\chi^2)$ of the
kernel, and its own kernel block does not depend on it: a lazy run can be a trained readout over fixed random
features, with the root moving by more than $100\%$ while $\dTK<0.1$ (\cref{app:boundaries}). Per-core changes
should be reported next to $\dTK$ (\cref{sec:other-large-chi}).

\subsection{Rich, non-incremental regime}\label{sec:other-rich}

Between the two boundaries the runs converge and move their kernel ($\dTK^{\rm probe}$ of the binary4 rich runs:
$0.10$ to $10^7$, median 68), but their events overlap: some $\sep i\le3$.

\paragraph{What sets the crossover.}
\derived{} (aligned initialization) \empirical{} (random) The plateaus between events grow like
$\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}$ as $\isc\to0$ while the event widths stay $O(1)$, so the separation ratios fall through $3$ as
$\isc$ grows; for aligned initialization \cref{prop:A-s2s-aligned} gives the boundary exactly, with the threshold
exponent of \citet{gissin2020implicit} \knownresult. From random initialization the crossover is a seed property:
on binary4 the per-seed boundaries at one $\chi$ span a factor $1.9$--$16$ for $\chi\le12$ and $1.4$--$2.7$ for
$\chi\ge16$ (six seeds). If both boundaries have finite limits in the function-space scale $\fsc=\isc^\Kc$, the
rich window of \cref{sec:regime-sigma-chi} shrinks to a band of width $O(1/\Kc)$ in $\ln\isc$ \conjecture. During
an escape, components not yet learned rise transiently, in 116 of the $9{,}165$ runs above $\frac12$; events are
therefore dated by their final rise (\cref{app:D-regimes}).

\paragraph{Lazy, then rich.}
\derived{} ($\Ns=4$) \empirical{} \knownresult[analogues: mixed lazy and active dynamics,
\citealp{tu2024mixed}; lazy-then-rich transitions, \citealp{lyu2024dichotomy}] The two-copy model is realizable at
a deficient bond when $2\chi$ reaches the teacher's bond rank. At large $\isc$ it first fits the tangent part lazily
and sits at the lazy residual, then escapes by rotating its bond subspaces, after a gradient-flow time
$\propto\isc^2$, a kernel time $\propto\isc^{2\Kc}$ (\cref{app:boundaries}). On binary4 at $\chi=3$ (six seeds) the
runs converge (rich) for $\isc=1.0$--$4.0$, end at $\Loss/\Loss(0)=0.005$--$0.05$ at $\isc=6.3$, at $0.13$--$0.44$ at
$\isc=10$, and at the lazy residual from $\isc=16$ on: the escape leaves any fixed budget as $\isc$ grows. A light
root with a deficient bond behaves the same way, lazy and then rich (\cref{sec:regime-imbalance,app:boundaries}).

\paragraph{Merged first escapes.}
\empirical{} \newresult{} In the two-copy model at small $\isc$ the two copies start identical, so their
near-origin escapes coincide: each copy learns part of both of the first two components at once, and the first
two events merge. On binary4 at $\chi=18$, $24$, $36$ the smallest $\isc$ ($0.010$) is labelled saddle-to-saddle in
5, 4 and 5 of six seeds and the next ($0.016$) in 4, 3 and 2, but the crossing $\min_i\sep i=5$ exists in only 2,
3 and 0 seeds (the largest $\min_i\sep i$ at these $\isc$ is $7.1$, $14.4$, $4.8$), so the sweep fails condition
(iii) of the saddle-to-saddle signature. Adam's runs from small initialization are rich,
non-incremental in the same sense (\cref{sec:other-optimizers}).

\subsection{Large bond dimension: NTK versus \texorpdfstring{$\mu$P}{muP}}\label{sec:other-large-chi}

As $\chi$ grows at fixed $\isc$, the scales of the cores and their learning-rate multipliers decide whether the
features still move. We compare three parameterizations, written as initial scales and multipliers $\lrm v$
(\cref{sec:setup-init}, variant (e)): the \emph{standard} one (SP: fan-in initialization, $\lrm v=1$); \emph{NTK}
($\lrm v=1/\fanin v$, so that every core contributes equally to the expected kernel); and \emph{$\mu$P-TTN}, the
spectral condition of \citet{yang2024spectral} applied with the injective norm of each core, since a core only
ever acts on product vectors: fan-in initialization except at the root, which is scaled by the inverse square root
of the sum of its child-leg dimensions, $(2\chi)^{-1/2}$ on binary4, and $\lrm v=\bd v/\fanin v$
(\cref{app:boundaries}) \derived{} \newresult[the spectral condition is known; factor-wise rules for structured
layers: \citealp{qiu2024compute,potapczynski2024searching}].

\paragraph{Predictions.}
\derived{} (binary4, centred, isotropic limit $\chi\gg d^2$, fixed $\xi=\normF{\Wt}/\fsc$) In each parameterization
the first- and second-order constants $c_1$, $c_2$ of the law \eqref{eq:A-lazy-dk} carry the same power of $\chi$, so
the kernel change scales as $\chi^{-3}$ (SP), $\chi^{-1}$ (NTK) and $\chi^0$ ($\mu$P-TTN); the parameters move as in
\cref{tab:large-chi} (constants in \cref{app:boundaries}). Under SP the root, with fan-in $\chi^2$, carries almost all
of the kernel and the model becomes linear in the root over fixed random features: lazy at any fixed $\isc$ as
$\chi$ grows, centred or not. Under NTK the cores move by $O(\chi^{-1/2})$ or less, also lazy in the limit, as for
the NTK of MPS \knownresult[\citealp{guo2021neural}]. Under $\mu$P-TTN every core's update stays of order one.

\paragraph{Measurements.}
\empirical{} Binary4, $d=3$, teacher $(3,2,1)$, centred, $\isc=1.5$ ($\xi=1.11$), $\chi=16$--$128$ (four values,
$0.9$ decade), six seeds; tensor kernel (\cref{fig:other-regimes}(b), (c); fitted exponents with standard errors in
\cref{tab:large-chi}). The ordering is as predicted. Under SP the kernel change falls like $\chi^{-3.02\pm0.06}$,
while at $\chi=128$ the root still moves by $9\%$ and the leaf cores by $2\cdot10^{-4}$ (medians). Under $\mu$P-TTN
the kernel change approaches the predicted level, $5.1$, $5.7$, $6.1$, $6.2$ at $\chi=16$, $32$, $64$, $128$ against
$6.2$--$6.3$: feature learning survives. Under NTK the fit over $\chi=16$--$128$ ($-0.79\pm0.04$) is pulled by
$\chi=16$--$32$, where $\dTK$ is still $0.4$--$0.6$; between $\chi=64$ and $128$ the local slope is $-1.01$, with
levels $0.250$ and $0.124$ against $0.240$ and $0.120$ from the second-order law with the NTK constants. Several
fitted exponents miss their predictions by more than three standard errors: the NTK kernel ($5.5$), the SP leaf
cores ($6.5$) and parameters ($3.3$), and the small $\mu$P-TTN exponents of the kernel, the parameters and the leaf
cores ($+0.085$, $-0.064$, $-0.065$; $3.5$--$3.6$ from $0$). At $\isc=1$ ($\xi=3.74$, further from the lazy limit)
the slopes are shallower (SP $-2.58\pm0.06$, NTK $-0.62\pm0.02$, $\mu$P-TTN $+0.089\pm0.024$): the predicted
exponents are limits as $\xi\to0$. The plain model gives the same exponents within the errors ($\isc=1.5$:
$-3.06\pm0.07$, $-0.83\pm0.04$, $+0.095\pm0.024$): with full, over-parameterized bonds, centring does not change the
scaling. Scope: binary4 only; runs on the binary tree with $\Ns=8$ are incomplete and not interpreted. The
factor-wise rule of structured layers, applied to a TTN, makes the initial kernel of that tree vanish like
$\chi^{-2}$ and slows the initial fit about linearly in $\chi$ (\cref{app:boundaries}). The $\mu$P-TTN limit itself
is not derived; on binary4 it may be a two-population mean field with a pairwise coupling through the root
\conjecture.
