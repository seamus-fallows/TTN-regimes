\section{Other regimes}\label{sec:other-regimes}

This section gives the detail of the regimes other than saddle-to-saddle that \cref{sec:regime-map} maps: lazy
training and its failure below the cut dimension, the rich window between the two boundaries, and the limit of
large bond dimension under three parameterizations. Runs that stall are treated with the failure modes of
saddle-to-saddle training in \cref{sec:s2s-failure}.\label{sec:other-stalled} The section then asks how
discrete steps, gradient noise and finite samples (\cref{sec:other-optim}) and the optimizers used in practice
(\cref{sec:other-optimizers}) change the gradient-flow picture. Settings of the first three subsections:
\cref{sec:regime-protocol,app:exp-regimes}; supplementary results: \cref{app:D-regimes,app:D-optim}.

\subsection{Lazy regime}\label{sec:other-lazy}

At large $\isc$ the centred model trains like its linearization at $\params_0$: the function moves along the range
of the tangent kernel $\TK$, which hardly changes. Lazy training is known in general
\knownresult[\citealp{jacot2018neural,chizat2019lazy,lee2019wide}]; what the tree adds is the kernel. At
initialization $\E\TK$ is a multiple of the identity at every bond dimension, but the rank of $\TK$ is the dimension
of the manifold of tensors with the given bond ranks, which reaches $d^\Ns$ only when every bond is full
(\cref{prop:A-kernel}) \proven{} \newresult[the mean kernel on every tree and the full-bond condition]
\knownresult[the rank as the dimension of the fixed-rank manifold:
\citealp{holtz2012manifolds,uschmajew2013geometry}].

\paragraph{What sets the lazy boundary.}
\derived{} \empirical{} On binary4 the kernel change has a zero-mean first-order part, linear in $\Wz$, and a
coherent second-order part (\cref{prop:A-lazy}). Near the boundary the measured $\dTK$ is $0.96$ times the law
$\sqrt{(c_1\xi)^2+(c_2\xi^2)^2}$ of \eqref{eq:A-lazy-dk} (median; $0.69$--$1.82$), and at large $\chi$ the
second-order part sets the boundary: the first-order estimate alone matches only up to $\chi=12$ (at $\chi=36$ it
gives $0.59$ against the measured $1.09$). Hence $\isc_{\rm lazy}\propto\chi^{-1/2}$ rather than $\chi^{-1}$
(\cref{sec:regime-sigma-chi}).

\paragraph{Lazy only with full bonds.}
\derived{} \empirical{} \knownresult[that a linearized model fits only the projection of the target on its
tangent space: \citealp{chizat2019lazy}, Thm~2.5; \citealp{ghorbani2019limitations}, Thm~2] With a bond below its
cut dimension the linearized flow fits only the projection of $\Wt$ on the range of $\TK$ and stops at
$\frac12\normF{P_\perp\Wt}^2$, on binary4 a fraction $(1-\chi/d^2)^2$ of $\Loss(0)$ on average ($0.44$, $0.31$,
$0.11$ at $\chi=3$, $4$, $6$; \cref{app:boundaries}). In addition the centred target $\Wt+\Wz$ is unrealizable at
every $\isc$, and gradient flow ends on its best approximation (\cref{fig:other-regimes}(a)): all 159 stalled
centred runs at $\chi=3$, $4$, $6$ end on the Eckart--Young minimum of $\Wt+\Wz$ across the cut $01|23$ (to
$0.04\%$). This floor rises like $\isc^{2\Kc}$ at small $\isc$ (at $\chi=3$ as \eqref{eq:A-centred-floor} predicts,
lower at $\chi=4$, $6$, where the spare bond rank absorbs part of $\Wz$) and saturates at the lazy residual at large
$\isc$ (\cref{app:D-regimes}). Once it exceeds the convergence threshold (for every seed from $\isc=1.0$, $1.0$, $1.6$
at $\chi=3$, $4$, $6$) the runs are stalled on a floor by the rule of \cref{tab:regimes}, though their dynamics are
kernel-like at large $\isc$. A lazy regime therefore needs every bond at its cut dimension (binary4: $\chi\ge9$),
which grows like $d^{\Ns/2}$ for the middle bonds.

\paragraph{Anisotropic, ill-conditioned kernels.}
\derived{} \empirical{} \newresult[for TTNs; the mean kernels of MPS and TT models:
\citealp{guo2021neural,wesel2025tensor}] A single initialization has an isotropic kernel only if every bond is far
above its cut dimension; at a capped full bond the cores next to it are square Gaussian matrices and
$\lambda_{\min}(\TK)/\bar\lambda\sim10^{-4}$ (\cref{app:boundaries}). The lazy fit then converges in a kernel time of
order $(\bar\lambda/\lambda_{\min})\ln(1/{\rm tol})$. On the binary tree with $\Ns=8$, $d=2$, the square full bonds
($\chi=16$) are not lazy within the step limit: every run from $\isc=2.3$ stops there at the same seed-dependent
loss, $\Loss/\Loss(0)=0.05$--$0.44$, for every $\isc$. With bonds over-parameterized twice most runs from
$\isc\approx1.45$ on are lazy, though they too reach the step limit (\cref{app:D-regimes}). At practical $\chi$ the
lazy regime is thus kernel regression with a random, tree-dependent and anisotropic kernel, not with its mean.

\paragraph{Lazy by the kernel is not small motion.}
\derived{} Under the standard parameterization the root of binary4 carries a share $\chi^2/(2d^2+\chi^2)$ of the
kernel, and its own kernel block does not depend on it: a lazy run can be a trained readout over fixed random
features. In one checked run the root moved by more than $100\%$ while $\dTK<0.1$ \empirical{}
(\cref{app:boundaries}). Per-core changes should be reported next to $\dTK$ (\cref{sec:other-large-chi}).

\subsection{Rich, non-incremental regime}\label{sec:other-rich}

Between the two boundaries the runs converge and move their kernel ($\dTK$ of the centred binary4 rich runs:
$0.10$ to $10^7$, median 55), but their events overlap: some $\sep i\le3$.

\paragraph{What sets the crossover.}
\derived{} (aligned initialization) \empirical{} (random) The plateaus between events grow like
$\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}$ as $\isc\to0$ while the event widths stay $O(1)$, so the separation ratios fall through $3$ as
$\isc$ grows; for aligned initialization \cref{prop:A-s2s-aligned} gives the boundary exactly, with the threshold
exponent of \citet{gissin2020implicit} \knownresult. From random initialization the crossover is a seed property:
on binary4 the per-seed boundaries at one $\chi$ span a factor $1.9$--$16$ for $\chi\le12$ and $1.4$--$2.7$ for
$\chi\ge16$ (six seeds). If both boundaries have finite limits in the function-space scale $\fsc=\isc^\Kc$, the
rich window of \cref{sec:regime-sigma-chi} shrinks to a band of width $O(1/\Kc)$ in $\ln\isc$ \conjecture. During
an escape, components not yet learned rise transiently, in 116 of the $9{,}165$ runs above $\frac12$; events are
therefore dated by their final rise (\cref{app:D-regimes}).

\paragraph{Lazy, then rich.}
\derived{} ($\Ns=4$) \empirical{} \knownresult[analogues: mixed lazy and active dynamics,
\citealp{tu2024mixed}; lazy-then-rich transitions, \citealp{lyu2024dichotomy}] The two-copy model is realizable at
a deficient bond when $2\chi$ reaches the teacher's bond rank. At large $\isc$ it first fits the tangent part lazily
and sits at the lazy residual, then escapes by rotating its bond subspaces, after a gradient-flow time
$\propto\isc^2$, a kernel time $\propto\isc^{2\Kc}$ (\cref{app:boundaries}). On binary4 at $\chi=3$ (six seeds) the
runs converge (rich) for $\isc=1.0$--$4.0$, end at $\Loss/\Loss(0)=0.005$--$0.05$ at $\isc=6.3$, at $0.13$--$0.44$ at
$\isc=10$, and at the lazy residual from $\isc=25$ on (within $3.4\%$ at $\isc=16$): the escape leaves any fixed
budget as $\isc$ grows. A light root with a deficient bond behaved the same way, lazy and then rich, in the one
seed checked (\cref{app:boundaries}) \empirical.

\paragraph{Crowded first escapes.}
\empirical{} In the two-copy model at small $\isc$ the two copies start identical, so their near-origin escapes
start together and the first two events crowd: on binary4 at $\chi=18$--$36$, $\min_i\sep i$ at $\isc=0.010$ is only
$2.7$--$14$ (centred: $12$--$29$) and grows far more slowly than $\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}$, so the two-copy sweep is no
quantitative check of the boundary (\cref{app:D-regimes}). On binary4 Adam's runs are rich, non-incremental for
another reason: the first escape is as wide as its own time (\cref{sec:other-optimizers}).

\subsection{Large bond dimension: NTK versus \texorpdfstring{$\mu$P}{muP}}\label{sec:other-large-chi}

As $\chi$ grows at fixed $\isc$, the scales of the cores and their learning-rate multipliers decide whether the
features still move. We compare three parameterizations, written as initial scales and multipliers $\lrm v$
(\cref{sec:setup-init}, variant (e)): the \emph{standard} one (SP: fan-in initialization, $\lrm v=1$); \emph{NTK}
($\lrm v=1/\fanin v$, so that every core contributes equally to the expected kernel); and \emph{$\mu$P-TTN}, the
spectral condition of \citet{yang2024spectral} applied with the injective norm of each core, since a core only
ever acts on product vectors: fan-in initialization except at the root, which is scaled by the inverse square root
of the sum of its child-leg dimensions, $(2\chi)^{-1/2}$ on binary4, and $\lrm v=\bd v/\fanin v$
(\cref{app:boundaries}) \derived{} \newresult[the spectral condition is known; factor-wise rules for structured
layers: \citealp{qiu2024compute,potapczynski2024searching}].

\paragraph{Predictions.}
\derived{} (binary4, centred, isotropic limit $\chi\gg d^2$, fixed $\isc$; fixed $\xi=\normF{\Wt}/\fsc$ under SP and NTK)
In each parameterization
the first- and second-order constants $c_1$, $c_2$ of the law \eqref{eq:A-lazy-dk} carry the same power of $\chi$, so
the kernel change scales as $\chi^{-3}$ (SP), $\chi^{-1}$ (NTK) and $\chi^0$ ($\mu$P-TTN); the parameters move as in
\cref{tab:large-chi} (constants in \cref{app:boundaries}). Under SP the root, with fan-in $\chi^2$, carries almost all
of the kernel and the model becomes linear in the root over fixed random features: lazy at any fixed $\isc$ as
$\chi$ grows, centred or not. Under NTK the cores move by $O(\chi^{-1/2})$ or less, also lazy in the limit, as for
the NTK of MPS \knownresult[\citealp{guo2021neural}]. Under $\mu$P-TTN every core's update stays of order one.

\paragraph{Measurements.}
\empirical{} Binary4, $d=3$, teacher $(3,2,1)$, centred, $\isc=1.5$ ($\xi=1.11$), $\chi=16$--$128$ (four values,
$0.9$ decade), six seeds; tensor kernel (\cref{fig:other-regimes}(b), (c); fitted exponents with standard errors in
\cref{tab:large-chi}), confirming the predicted exponents in sign and size. Under SP the kernel change falls like
$\chi^{-3.02\pm0.06}$, while at $\chi=128$ the root still moves by $9\%$ and the leaf cores by $2\cdot10^{-4}$
(medians). Under $\mu$P-TTN the kernel change approaches the predicted level, $5.1$, $5.7$, $6.1$, $6.2$ at $\chi=16$,
$32$, $64$, $128$ against $6.2$--$6.3$: feature learning survives. Under NTK the fit over $\chi=16$--$128$
($-0.79\pm0.04$, $5.5$ standard errors from $-1$) is pulled by the pre-asymptotic $\chi=16$--$32$, where $\dTK$ is still
$0.4$--$0.6$; between $\chi=64$ and $128$ the local slope is $-1.01$, with levels $0.250$ and $0.124$ against $0.240$ and
$0.120$ from the second-order law with the NTK constants. The SP kernel and root and the NTK parameters and leaf
cores match within $1.1$ standard errors (the NTK root within $2.9$); the $\mu$P-TTN kernel, parameters and leaf cores
drift slightly ($+0.085$, $-0.064$, $-0.065$; $3.5$--$3.6$ standard errors from $0$, set by $\chi=16$); the SP leaf
cores ($6.5$) and parameter change ($3.3$ standard errors) are steeper than predicted. Further from the lazy limit
($\isc=1$) the slopes are shallower, so the predicted exponents are limits as $\xi\to0$; the plain model gives the same
exponents within the errors (\cref{app:D-regimes}). Scope: binary4 only; runs on the binary tree with $\Ns=8$ are
incomplete and not interpreted. The $\mu$P-TTN limit itself
is not derived; on binary4 it may be a two-population mean field with a pairwise coupling through the root
\conjecture.

Gradient flow is the limit of gradient descent (GD) as the step $\lr\to0$ and of online SGD as
$\lr/\batch\to0$, and the population loss is the limit of the empirical loss as $\nsamp\to\infty$. How much of
\cref{sec:s2s} survives finite values? GD keeps gradient flow's saddle sequence up to a sharp stability limit,
with event times shifted by a computable $O(\lr)$; gradient noise does not shorten plateaus on average, but it
lengthens later ones and can trap the dynamics at a saddle; and from a finite training set small initialization
recovers the teacher with its bond ranks, while the lazy limit fails as dimension counting predicts. Setup
(\cref{app:exp-optim}): odeco teacher $(3,2,1)$, $d=\chi=3$, fan-in initialization (five seeds) on the binary trees
with $\Ns=4$ ($\Kc=3$) and $\Ns=8$ ($\Kc=7$) and the MPS with $\Ns=8$ ($\Kc=8$); GD and online SGD (fresh inputs at
every step) run on the clock $t=\lr\times(\text{steps})$ and are compared with gradient flow from the same initial
point. Details: \cref{app:D-optim}.

\paragraph{The stability limit.}
\proven{} \knownresult[deep linear networks: \citealp{mulayoff2020unique,ghosh2025learning}]
\newresult[general trees] At the balanced global minimum the Hessian $J\T J$ has largest eigenvalue
$\lambda_{\rm bal}=\Kc\tw1^{2-2/\Kc}$, and every other aligned minimum is sharper (\cref{prop:A-pattern-rule};
with $\bd e=\ncomp$ every other minimum, \derived), so GD can converge to a global minimum only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:optim-etac}
  \lr<\lr_c=\frac{2}{\Kc\,\tw1^{2-2/\Kc}}
\end{equation}
($0.1541$, $0.0435$ and $0.0366$ for the three trees). Along the saddle-to-saddle path the sharpness is
$O(\isc^{\Kc-2})$ on the first plateau and $\Kc\max_r\tw r^{2-2/\Kc}$ over the components learned so far
\derived{} (\cref{app:optimizer}): a step above $\lr_c$ is felt only once component 1 is learned, and there is no
catapult at the start (the condition $\lr\lambda_{\max}(\Hess)>2$ of \citet{lewkowycz2020large} fails near the
origin). Measured \empirical{} on binary $\Ns=4$ ($\isc=0.003$--$0.3$, five seeds, 18 steps from $0.01\lr_c$ to
$3\lr_c$; \cref{fig:optim-gd}a): all 195 runs with $\lr\le0.95\lr_c$ are GF-like and all 80 at $1.05$--$1.3\lr_c$
oscillate. Below $\lr_c$, GD learned the components in gradient flow's order in all 765 runs, including 40 more
seeds on binary $\Ns=4$ and both deep trees at $\isc=0.9$ (on the deep trees within a budget in which gradient flow
learns one or two of the three components: on the MPS only the first component is compared in 36 of 45 runs).

\paragraph{Below the limit, GD is gradient flow on a shifted clock.}
\derived{} \newresult[for TTNs] \knownresult[the modified flow: \citealp{barrett2021implicit}] To first order in
$\lr$, GD follows the modified flow $\dot\params=-(\Id+\frac\lr2\Hess)\nabla\Loss$, whose critical points are those of
$\Loss$: the saddles are gradient flow's, and each event moves by $\lr c_e+O(\lr^2)$, with $c_e$ computed without
free parameter from the variational equation along the gradient-flow path (\cref{prop:A-gd-shift,app:exp-optim}).
Measured \empirical: over 526 comparisons at $\lr\le0.1\lr_c$ the shift agrees with $\lr c_e$ within $0.3\%$, $1\%$
and $5\%$ in $66\%$, $90\%$ and $97\%$ of the cases, all 202 first events within $1\%$. Since the shift constant of the first event grows like
$\ln(1/\isc)$ (asymptotically, \conjecture{} in \cref{app:optimizer}; confirmed here) while $\tev1\propto\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}$,
the relative shift of $\tev1$ vanishes as $\isc\to0$: at $0.9\lr_c$ it falls from $0.11$ at $\isc=0.3$ to $0.0036$ at
$\isc=0.003$ (\cref{fig:optim-gd}b). At small initialization every
stable step thus reproduces gradient flow, the more closely the smaller $\isc$.

\paragraph{Above the limit, and at larger initialization.}
\derived{} \empirical{} \knownresult[the scalar map and its two-cycle: \citealp{ghosh2025learning}, Thm~1,
\citealp{macdonald2025convergence}, Thm~5.3; edge of stability: \citealp{cohen2021gradient}; catapults:
\citealp{lewkowycz2020large}] Just above $\lr_c$ only the radial mode of component 1 is unstable, and GD on it is the
balanced scalar map of \cref{fig:optim-gd}a (\cref{app:optimizer}): a two-cycle, then chaos, whose time-averaged
excess loss the network matches to $0.9\%$ while it learns the other components; from $1.5\lr_c$ on it diverges or
is thrown back to the origin saddle (binary $\Ns=4$). At larger $\isc$ the minimum that gradient flow reaches is
sharper and $2/\lambda_{\rm GF}$ replaces $\lr_c$, with $\lambda_{\rm GF}\propto\isc^{3.965\pm0.014}$ on the lazy side
(centred binary $\Ns=4$, $\chi=9$), close to the scale $\isc^{2(\Kc-1)}$ of the tangent kernel; from $\isc=1$ on, steps up
to $1.5$--$3$ times $2/\lambda_{\rm GF}$ catapult to a flatter minimum and still reach gradient flow's loss
(\cref{fig:optim-gd}c; \cref{app:D-optim}).

\paragraph{SGD: the first plateau keeps its law.}
\derived{} \newresult[for TTNs; framework: \citealp{li2017stochastic,ziyin2024typeii}] The per-sample gradient
carries the Jacobian $J=\partial\W/\partial\params$, which vanishes at the origin like the drift, so noise cannot drive
the first escape and its exponent $\Kc-2$ is unchanged; on an aligned mode it only delays and spreads the escape
\eqref{eq:A-sgd-first}, consistent with the experiments of \citet{corlouer2026stochastic} on deep linear networks
(their Fig.~2: order kept, SGD slower). Measured \empirical{} with online SGD on binary $\Ns=4$ (1,780 runs,
$\batch=1$--$256$): on average no setting shortened the first plateau beyond its noise (11 of the 23 cell means of
\cref{fig:optim-sgd}a are negative, all within $2.5$ standard errors of zero and none below $-1.0\%$), while single
runs scatter to both sides of gradient flow (earlier in 787 of the 1,600 runs from random initialization, by more
than $5\%$ in 141).

\paragraph{SGD: later plateaus are lengthened, and can trap.}
\conjecture{} (heuristic law) \knownresult[mechanism: stochastic collapse, \citealp{chen2023stochastic}]
Once a component is learned, a route of $k\ge3$ cores (\cref{sec:s2s-locality}) has no curvature, but its entries are
coupled to the learned cores by zero-mean multiplicative noise of order one, which drives their log-amplitude down
at a rate $c_{\rm col}\lr/\batch$, with a collapse constant $c_{\rm col}$ that depends on teacher and tree.
\Cref{prop:A-sgd-collapse} turns this into
\begin{equation}\label{eq:optim-collapse}
  \frac{T_{\rm SGD}}{T_{\rm GF}}=\frac{-\ln(1-x)}{x},\qquad x=c_{\rm col}\frac{\lr}{\batch}T_{\rm GF},
\end{equation}
for a $k=3$ plateau of gradient-flow length $T_{\rm GF}$, and trapping for $x\ge1$. Test \empirical{} on binary
$\Ns=4$ from the aligned route (component 1 learned, the route started at an amplitude that makes $T_{\rm GF}=50$--$250$
exact; 144 runs; \cref{fig:optim-sgd}b): every escaped run was slower than gradient flow (114 of 114), none of the
30 runs at $(\lr/\batch)T_{\rm GF}\gtrsim0.025$ escaped (29 trapped, one diverged), and with one fitted
$c_{\rm col}=32.6$ the condition $x=1$ separates escaped from trapped settings in 23 of 24 cases; the law holds to
about $10\%$ for $T_{\rm SGD}/T_{\rm GF}\lesssim1.5$, and within the errors only $\lr/\batch$ matters. At the largest
$\lr/\batch$ the learned component also fell below $\frac12$ in 19 of 24 runs, so the clean trap is the longest
plateau ($T_{\rm GF}=250$) at half that $\lr/\batch$ (6 of 6 runs, one dip). From random initialization the second
plateau is lengthened as \eqref{eq:optim-collapse} predicts at $\isc=0.03$ but more than predicted at $\isc=0.1$
(\cref{app:D-optim}). Consequence: at fixed $\lr/\batch$ a later plateau grows faster than
$\isc^{-(k-2)}$ and, by the law, becomes infinite at a finite $\isc$ \conjecture; on the aligned route the ratio
$T_{\rm SGD}/T_{\rm GF}$ rises from $1.13$ to $2.7$ and then every run traps as $T_{\rm GF}$ grows \empirical: SGD stalls
where gradient flow is only slow. By the same drift model a strict ($k=2$) route still escapes if
$\abs{\lambda_-}>c_{\rm col}\lr/\batch$ \conjecture: on the MPS with $\Ns=4$ and a pattern teacher $(3,0.3)$ whose second
component needs $k=2$, SGD at $\batch=1$ learned it in 5 of 5 runs, on average $8\pm5\%$ later than gradient flow
\empirical.

\paragraph{SGD: stability limit and forgetting.}
\derived{} \empirical{} \newresult[the reduction to the pattern basis] \knownresult[the criteria:
\citealp{wu2018how,chemnitz2025characterizing}; exact mean square: \citealp{mulayoff2024exact}, Thm~5] At the minimum
the noise vanishes, and the exact mean-square limit \eqref{eq:A-ms-threshold} on binary $\Ns=4$ is
$\lr^*_{\rm ms}=0.00107$, $0.0161$ and $0.112$ for $\batch=1$, $16$, $256$, against $\lr_c=0.154$: per sample,
small-batch SGD is about 150 times less stable than GD, because Gaussian product features have kurtosis $3^\Ns$ and
the coupling to the other patterns adds a factor $1.8$. Well below this limit, noise on long plateaus erases learned
components, which are then relearned: near its limit SGD breaks the saddle sequence rather than compressing it
(\cref{app:D-optim}).

\paragraph{Finite samples: which interpolant?}
\empirical{} \newresult[for TTN regression] On the binary tree with $\Ns=8$, $d=2$ ($\W\in\R^{256}$) and an odeco
teacher $(3,2)$ with noise-free labels, GD on the empirical loss from small initialization recovers the teacher with
its bond ranks from $\nsamp=256$ samples at $\chi=4$ (test error below $10^{-2}$ in 5 of 5 seeds), while the exact
lazy limit, the minimum-norm solution of the model linearized at initialization, fails as dimension counting
predicts \derived: at $\chi=4$ the teacher lies outside the tangent space and the error is worse than predicting zero
at every $\nsamp$ (\cref{app:D-optim}, \cref{fig:optim-samples}). A bias of small initialization towards low
(hierarchical) rank is known for tensor factorizations \knownresult[\citealp{razin2021implicit,razin2022implicit};
tensor-train completion, empirically: \citealp{milanesi2021implicit}; matrices: \citealp{arora2019implicit}].

\paragraph{When does gradient flow describe practical training?}
\empirical{} \derived{} (a summary of the results above; one teacher; GD on trees with $\Ns\le8$, SGD on binary
$\Ns=4$) GD follows gradient flow whenever $\lr<2/\lambda_{\rm GF}(\isc)$, with event times off by $\lr c_e$ and, from
small initialization, the same saddle sequence; this limit is $\lr_c$ at small initialization and falls like
$\isc^{-2(\Kc-1)}$ on the lazy side (measured at $\Kc=3$: $\isc^{-3.97}$), where steps up to $1.5$--$3$ times larger still
reach gradient flow's loss through a catapult. SGD needs in addition $\lr<\lr^*_{\rm ms}(\batch)$ (about
$10^{-3}\batch$ here for $\batch\le16$), $c_{\rm col}(\lr/\batch)T_{\rm GF}\ll1$ for every later plateau of gradient-flow length $T_{\rm GF}$, and $\lr$
a small fraction of $\lr^*_{\rm ms}$ when learned components must survive long plateaus (no erasure in 72 runs of up
to 400 time units at $\lr\le0.076\lr^*_{\rm ms}$; one in six runs of 1000 time units at $0.15\lr^*_{\rm ms}$). Because
plateaus grow like $\isc^{-(k-2)}$, at fixed $\lr/\batch$ SGD eventually stalls or forgets where gradient flow is
only slow.

Tensor-network models are trained with Adam \citep{kingma2015adam}, with ALS and DMRG sweeps

$k=2$ and $k=6$ cores on the MPS). GD and heavy ball ($\beta=0.9$) use effective steps $\lr/(1-\beta)\le0.27\lr_c$,
Adam $\epsilon=10^{-8}$ unless stated; each method runs on its own clock. Details: \cref{app:D-optim}.

\paragraph{GD and heavy ball reproduce gradient flow.}
\empirical{} \newresult[for TTNs] GD with $\lr=0.13\lr_c$ on binary4 has gradient flow's exponents to within $0.003$
and its order from all 42 initial points; its first-escape delay grows by $0.0199\pm0.0000$ per e-fold of $1/\isc$,
the asymptotic slope $\lr(\Kc-1)/2=0.020$ that follows from \cref{prop:A-gd-shift} (asymptotically, \conjecture{} in
\cref{app:optimizer}; confirmed here). Heavy ball's delay grows $18.9\approx(1+\beta)/(1-\beta)=19$ times faster
(\conjecture: the momentum analogue). On the deep trees ($\isc=0.8$) GD and heavy ball escape after $1.001$--$1.006$
and $1.01$--$1.11$ times gradient flow's time.

\paragraph{Adam: same first component, but the first escape forgets $\isc$.}
\derived{} \newresult[for TTNs; in aligned deep matrix factorization the sign-descent escape time is likewise bounded
as the initialization vanishes: \citealp{sabanayagam2026optimizer}, Thm~2 and Prop.~6] With $\epsilon=0$ Adam's step
$-\lr\hat m_n/\sqrt{\hat v_n}$ is invariant under a common rescaling of past gradients
\knownresult[\citealp{kingma2015adam}], and near the origin $\nabla\Loss$ is homogeneous of degree $\Kc-1$; so the
iterates from $\kappa\params_0$ with step $\kappa\lr$ are $\kappa$ times those from $\params_0$ with step $\lr$ ($\kappa>0$), and Adam sees $\isc$
only through $\isc/\lr$. Its first step is $-\lr\,\mathrm{sign}\,\nabla\Loss(\params_0)$, after which $\abs{\params}\sim\lr$
and the initial gradient weighs $\sim(\isc/\lr)^{\Kc-1}$ in the averages, so the trajectory has a limit as
$\isc/\lr\to0$ and the number $n_1$ of steps to the first escape stays bounded as $\isc\to0$ (in the sign-flow limit
$\lr\to0$ at fixed $\beta_1,\beta_2$ \knownresult[\citealp{ma2022qualitative}; Adam as sign descent:
\citealp{balles2018dissecting}], $\lr n_1$ tends to a constant). Measured \empirical: first-escape exponents
$0.009$--$0.013$ on binary4 for four learning rates of Adam, and $0.012\pm0.004$ and $0.001\pm0.019$ for RMSProp; on
binary $\Ns=8$, $0.049\pm0.022$ and $0.145\pm0.033$ ($\lr=10^{-3}$, $10^{-2}$; fitted over $\isc\in[0.25,0.8]$, $0.09$ and
$0.17$ over $\isc\le0.4$: small but not zero), where gradient flow has $4.991\pm0.002$ ($\isc\le0.4$, prediction 5). The
first component survives \empirical: Adam and RMSProp pick gradient flow's first component in 94--100\% of the
resolved deep-tree runs (87--98\% on binary4) and keep E8's locality order on the MPS (the weaker $k=2$ component
before the stronger $k=6$ one, in 4--5 of 6 seeds, as gradient flow); the full order of gradient flow holds in
87--98\% of the binary4 runs but only 67--83\% of the deep-tree runs. On binary4 the first escape is as wide as its
own time, so 79--100\% of the runs fail $\sep i>3$ and are rich, non-incremental by \cref{tab:regimes}; on binary
$\Ns=8$ most are saddle-to-saddle.

\paragraph{Adam's $\epsilon$ brings the gradient-flow law back.}
\derived{} \newresult[for TTNs; concurrently, the same law in aligned deep matrix factorization:
\citealp{sabanayagam2026optimizer}, Prop.~7] Where $\abs{\partial_j\Loss}\ll\epsilon$ Adam is gradient flow with
$t=\lr n/\epsilon$: from a small $\isc$ it crawls along the gradient-flow path until the gradient reaches $\epsilon$,
then escapes as in the sign regime,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:adam-eps}
  n_1\simeq\frac{\epsilon}{\lr}\,T_{\rm GF}(\isc)+n_{\rm sign}(\lr),
\end{equation}
so the law $T_{\rm GF}\propto\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}$ returns, with prefactor $\epsilon/\lr$, below the crossover $\isc_\times$
where $\epsilon T_{\rm GF}=\lr n_{\rm sign}$. Test \empirical{} on the odeco MPS with $\Ns=8$ (eight
$\isc\in[0.01,0.3]$, six seeds, $\lr=10^{-3}$, $10^{-2}$; no fitted parameter): below the crossover measured over
predicted $n_1$ is $0.9989$--$1.0003$ at $\lr=10^{-3}$ ($\isc=0.02$--$0.03$) and $1.0009$--$1.0062$ at $\lr=10^{-2}$
($\isc=0.015$--$0.02$), and \eqref{eq:adam-eps} predicts which runs escape in all 60 cases with $\isc\le0.05$; near
$\isc_\times\approx0.05$--$0.1$ the deviations are systematic (\cref{app:D-optim}). There the crawl term is more than
$98\%$ of $n_1$, so the test checks that Adam is gradient flow on the clock $\lr n/\epsilon$ rather than the additive
form. Only $\Kc=8$ was tested.


\paragraph{No plateaus: sweeps and Riemannian GD.}
\proven{} \newresult[elementary] In the canonical form centred at core $v$ the environment of $v$ is an isometry, so
the least-squares core is $\nabla_{\G v}\ip{\W}{\Wt}$, a function of the other cores' frames, which are the orthonormal factors of
QR decompositions and do not change when a core is multiplied by a positive number: every ALS and DMRG iterate is
independent of $\isc$. Riemannian GD, $\W\leftarrow\mathrm{HSVD}_\chi(\W-\lr P_{\W}(\W-\Wt))$ with $P_{\W}$ the
projector onto the tangent space at $\W$ of the fixed-rank manifold and $\mathrm{HSVD}_\chi$ the truncation of the
hierarchical singular value decomposition to bond dimension $\chi$, maps $\W_0$ at $\lr=1$ to
$\mathrm{HSVD}_\chi(P_{\W_0}\Wt)$, and that tangent space is the same at every positive multiple of $\W_0$: it is exactly
$\isc$-free too. Measured \empirical: ALS and DMRG fit the
teacher within one sweep, with two components crossing $\ov r=\frac12$ in the same micro-step in 59 of 60 runs: no
resolved order. Riemannian GD needs 2--7 steps; on binary $\Ns=4$ it fits all components within two steps (all three
cross $\frac12$ at step 1 in 5 of 6 seeds), but it stages them on binary $\Ns=8$, the weakest strictly last in 5 of 6
seeds for both teachers (3 and 2 of 6 on the MPS).

\paragraph{Re-gauged and Gram-preconditioned GD.}
\derived{} \empirical{} Re-gauging moves all of $\normF{\W}\sim\isc^\Kc$ into the root, whose environment becomes an
isometry: the root fits the teacher's projection onto the current frames in a time $O(1)$ for any $\isc$, and on the
binary tree with $\Ns=8$, whose random frames keep only a small part of each component, learning is sequential
again with $\isc$-free event times. Gram preconditioning has no flat plateau but overshoots on its first step, and its time
depends on $\isc$ (\cref{tab:optimizers}; details in \cref{app:D-optim}).

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
Two properties of an update decide what survives (\cref{tab:optimizers}, second column): how its step scales with
the gradient, which near the origin is $O(\isc^{\Kc-1})$ (a step proportional to the gradient needs a time
$\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}$, a step invariant under its rescaling does not, and an absolute scale such as Adam's $\epsilon$ brings
the law back, \derived), and the metric: ALS, DMRG and Riemannian GD see $\W_0$ only through its frames, so they keep
no $\isc$ (\proven{} for ALS and DMRG, exact for Riemannian GD at $\lr=1$), and because they move $\W$ at a rate of order
one they keep no plateaus \derived; re-gauged GD shares the equivariance and the $\isc$-independence but not the rate
(details in \cref{app:D-optim}).
