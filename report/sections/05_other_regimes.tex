\subsection{Optimizers used in practice}\label{sec:other-optimizers}
% Sources: notes/optimizers.md v2 (its section 5 lists the claims withdrawn after review round 1),
% agent-runs/optimizers/REPORT.md, notes/reviews/optimizers_round1.md; settings in
% app:exp-optimizers. Withdrawn, do not reintroduce: Gram preconditioning is sigma-free; orders of
% ALS/DMRG/Riemannian GD "unrelated to the weights"; Adam's later plateaus at "half GF's rate" or set
% by the adaptive edge of stability; Adam generalizes worse than gradient flow.

Tensor-network models are mostly trained with Adam \citep{kingma2015adam}, with ALS and DMRG sweeps
\citep{white1992density,holtz2012alternating,stoudenmire2016supervised} or with Riemannian methods
\citep{uschmajew2013geometry,novikov2018exponential}. Which features of the gradient-flow picture of
\cref{sec:s2s} survive them? \Cref{tab:optimizers} collects the answers, \cref{fig:optimizers-side}
shows them from one initial point, and the evidence follows.

\paragraph{Setup} (details in \cref{app:exp-optimizers}).
Exact population loss, whitened inputs, double precision. Binary tree, $\Ns=4$ ($\Kc=3$), $d=3$,
$\chi=4$, odeco teacher $(3,2,1)$: all methods start from the same 42 fan-in initializations (seven
$\isc\in[0.002,0.128]$, six seeds). Deep trees, $\Ns=8$: binary ($\Kc=7$) and MPS ($\Kc=8$), with the
same teacher and with a pattern teacher E8 whose weaker components need $k=6$ and $k=2$ cores on the
MPS (\cref{prop:teacher-spectra}); $\isc\in\{0.3,0.8\}$, plus seven $\isc\in[0.25,0.8]$ (binary) and
eight $\isc\in[0.01,0.3]$ (MPS); six seeds; gauge-balanced initializations, and fan-in ones for the
gauge-dependent methods (fan-in gradient flow stalls on the odeco MPS, \cref{prop:A-trap}). Methods as
listed in \cref{tab:optimizers}, with GD and heavy ball ($\beta=0.9$) at effective steps
$\lr/(1-\beta)\le0.27\lr_c$ (\cref{app:optimizer}) and Adam's $\epsilon=10^{-8}$ unless stated. Clocks: $t$ for gradient flow (LSODA, events by root finding), $\lr n$ for GD,
$\lr n/(1-\beta)$ for heavy ball, steps for the other gradient methods, sweeps for ALS and DMRG;
exponents $p$ ($T\propto\isc^{-p}$) are per-seed fits, mean $\pm$ s.e.

\paragraph{GD and heavy ball reproduce gradient flow.}
\empirical{} \newresult[for TTNs] GD with $\lr=0.13\lr_c$ on binary $\Ns=4$ has gradient flow's
exponents to within $0.003$ and its order from all 42 initial points; its first-escape delay grows by
$0.0199\pm0.0000$ per e-fold of $1/\isc$, the asymptotic slope $\lr(\Kc-1)/2=0.020$ that follows from
\cref{prop:A-gd-shift}. Heavy ball's delay grows $19.0=(1+\beta)/(1-\beta)$ times faster
(\conjecture: the momentum analogue), hence $p=0.970\pm0.002$. On the deep trees ($\isc=0.8$) GD and
heavy ball escape after $1.001$--$1.006$ and $1.01$--$1.11$ times gradient flow's time.

\paragraph{Adam: same order, but the first escape forgets $\isc$.}
\derived{} \newresult[for TTNs] With $\epsilon=0$ Adam's step $-\lr\hat m_n/\sqrt{\hat v_n}$ is
invariant under a common rescaling of past gradients \knownresult[\citealp{kingma2015adam}], and near
the origin $\nabla\Loss$ is homogeneous of degree $\Kc-1$; so the iterates from $c\params_0$ with step
$c\lr$ are $c$ times those from $\params_0$ with step $\lr$, Adam sees $\isc$ only through $\isc/\lr$,
and the number $n_1$ of steps to the first escape stays bounded as $\isc\to0$ (in the sign-flow limit
$\lr\to0$ \knownresult[\citealp{balles2018dissecting}], $\lr n_1$ tends to a constant). Measured
\empirical: $p=0.009$--$0.013$ on binary $\Ns=4$ for four learning rates, RMSProp alike
(\cref{fig:optimizers-adam-escape}); $p=0.049\pm0.022$ and $0.145\pm0.033$ ($\lr=10^{-3}$, $10^{-2}$;
small but not zero) on binary $\Ns=8$, where gradient flow has $5.00\pm0.02$ ($\isc\le0.4$, prediction
5; fit computed from the committed runs in the review of this experiment). The selection survives \empirical: Adam and RMSProp pick gradient flow's first component in
94--100\% of the resolved deep-tree runs and keep E8's locality order on the MPS (the weaker $k=2$
component before the stronger $k=6$ one, in 4--5 of 6 seeds, as gradient flow). On binary $\Ns=4$ the
first escape is as wide as its own time, so 79--100\% of the runs fail $\sep i>3$ and are rich,
non-incremental by \cref{tab:regimes}.

\paragraph{Adam's $\epsilon$ brings the gradient-flow law back.}
\derived{} \newresult{} Where $\abs{\partial_j\Loss}\ll\epsilon$ Adam is gradient flow with
$t=\lr n/\epsilon$: from a small $\isc$ it crawls along the gradient-flow path until the gradient
reaches $\epsilon$, then escapes as in the sign regime,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:adam-eps}
  n_1\simeq\frac{\epsilon}{\lr}\,T_{\rm GF}(\isc)+n_{\rm sign}(\lr),
\end{equation}
so the law $T_{\rm GF}\propto\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}$ returns, with prefactor $\epsilon/\lr$, below the
crossover $\isc_\times$ where $\epsilon T_{\rm GF}=\lr n_{\rm sign}$. Test \empirical{} on the odeco MPS
with $\Ns=8$ (eight $\isc\in[0.01,0.3]$, six seeds, $\lr=10^{-3}$, $10^{-2}$): per seed, from that
seed's exact gradient-flow escape at $\isc=0.3$ times $(0.3/\isc)^6$ and its own escape with
$\epsilon=10^{-30}$, with no fitted parameter, measured/predicted is $0.9989$--$1.0003$ ($\lr=10^{-3}$,
$\isc=0.02$--$0.03$) and $1.0009$--$1.0062$ ($\lr=10^{-2}$, $\isc=0.015$--$0.02$) for every run that
escaped within $2\cdot10^5$ steps, and \eqref{eq:adam-eps} predicts which runs do in all 60 cases with
$\isc\le0.05$; near $\isc_\times\approx0.05$--$0.1$ the deviations are systematic ($-5\%$ to $+17\%$).
The crossover depends on $\lr$ and on the tree: on binary $\Ns=4$, \eqref{eq:adam-eps} puts it near
$10^{-8}$ (\derived, not tested), far below the $2\cdot10^{-4}$ at which the initial gradient falls
under $\epsilon$. Only $\Kc=8$ was tested.

\paragraph{Later plateaus and minima under Adam.}
\empirical{} Later gaps depend weakly on $\isc$ on binary $\Ns=4$ ($p=0.0$--$0.4$). On binary $\Ns=8$
they grow as $\isc$ decreases, up to $8\cdot10^4$ steps at $\isc=0.25$, but per-seed local slopes range
from $-0.4$ to $5.5$, so over half a decade no law is established (gradient flow: $4.72$ and $4.85$,
prediction 5); with $\isc\le0.4$, 83--100\% of these runs are S2S. Adam and RMSProp break the
conservation of $\Q e$ and end at imbalanced minima (balance index
$\normF{\Q e}/(\normF{\Gm{v}{e}\Gm{v}{e}\T}+\normF{\Gm{p}{e}\Gm{p}{e}\T})=0.21$--$0.96$),
$1.01$--$6.1$ times sharper than the balanced value \eqref{eq:A-sharpness}, the more so the larger the
step; gradient flow, GD and heavy ball end within $0.2\%$ of it.

\paragraph{No plateaus: sweeps and Riemannian GD.}
\proven{} \newresult[elementary] In the canonical form centred at core $v$ the environment of $v$ is an
isometry, so the least-squares core is $\nabla_{\G v}\ip{\W}{\Wt}$, a function of the other cores'
frames, which are $Q$ factors of QR decompositions and do not change when a core is multiplied by a
positive number: every ALS and DMRG iterate is independent of $\isc$. Riemannian GD,
$\W\leftarrow\mathrm{HSVD}_\chi(\W-\lr P_{T(\W)}(\W-\Wt))$ with $P_{T(\W)}$ the projector onto the
tangent space of the fixed-rank manifold, maps $\W_0$ at $\lr=1$ to
$\mathrm{HSVD}_\chi(P_{T(\W_0)}\Wt)$, and that tangent space is the same at $c\W_0$: it is exactly
$\isc$-free too. Measured \empirical: ALS and DMRG fit the teacher within one sweep, with two
components crossing $\ov r=\frac12$ in the same micro-step in 59 of 60 runs: no resolved order.
Riemannian GD needs 2--7 steps; it fits all components in one step on binary $\Ns=4$ but stages them on
binary $\Ns=8$, the weakest strictly last in 5 of 6 seeds for both teachers (3 and 2 of 6 on the MPS).

\paragraph{Re-gauged and Gram-preconditioned GD.}
Re-gauging moves all of $\normF{\W}\sim\isc^\Kc$ into the root, whose environment becomes an isometry,
so the root fits the teacher's projection onto the current frames in a time $O(1)$ for any $\isc$
\derived; on deep trees random frames keep only a fraction $\prod_e\chi/d^{n_e}$ of each component
over the root's edges ($2.4\cdot10^{-3}$ on binary $\Ns=8$, $0.2$ on $\Ns=4$), so they must rotate and
learning is sequential again, with $\isc$-free event times (\empirical, $\abs{p}\le0.02$). Gram
preconditioning moves each core towards its own least-squares solution, $\sim\isc^{-(\Kc-1)}$ times
larger than the core, so its first step overshoots the loss by $\sim\isc^{-2\Kc(\Kc-1)}$ (\derived; up
to $4\cdot10^{34}$); it has no flat plateau, but its time depends on $\isc$ (\empirical,
\cref{tab:optimizers}).

\paragraph{Finite samples.}
\empirical{} With $\nsamp=45$ and $60$ samples on binary $\Ns=4$ (four data draws, six
initializations, $\isc\in\{0.01,0.1\}$, paired comparisons), gradient flow and Adam reach the same
population loss: at $\nsamp=60$ every run that met the training tolerance recovered the teacher; at
$\nsamp=45$ none did, and Adam was better in 8--13 of 21--23 pairs per setting. Only a threshold count
differs (a fourth bond singular value above $0.1\,\bst{e}{\min}$: Adam with $\lr=10^{-2}$ in 35 of 44
runs, gradient flow in 5); the entropy rank does not.

\paragraph{Interpretation: which invariance removes which feature.}
Two properties of an update decide what survives (\cref{tab:optimizers}, second column). The first is
how its step scales with the gradient, which near the origin is $O(\isc^{\Kc-1})$ while the parameters
must move by their own size: a step proportional to the gradient needs a time $\isc^{-(\Kc-2)}$, a
step invariant under its rescaling (Adam) does not, and an absolute scale in the preconditioner, such
as Adam's $\epsilon$, brings the law back \derived; Adam still follows the gradient's sign, which one
component dominates, so learning stays sequential and ordered (\conjecture). The second is the
metric. Under gradient flow $\W$ moves through $\Jac\Jac\T$ ($\Jac=\partial\W/\partial\params$), which
vanishes like $\isc^{2(\Kc-1)}$ at the origin, whereas Riemannian GD (in continuous time the
Gauss--Newton flow $\dot\params=\Jac^+(\Wt-\W)$) and the exact block minimizations of ALS and DMRG
move $\W$ at a rate of order one. These methods are equivariant under the whole gauge group
\eqref{eq:gauge}, including the rescaling of a core against its neighbour, and see $\W_0$ only through
its frames, so they keep neither plateaus (\derived) nor $\isc$ (\proven); plateaus at singular points
of a parametrization, and the better behaviour of the natural gradient there, are known for multilayer
perceptrons \knownresult[\citealp{amari2006singularities}]. Equivariance alone is not enough: Gram
preconditioning is gauge-equivariant up to damping \derived{} but moves all cores at once from their
current values. Balance and the flattest minimum (\cref{prop:A-pattern-rule}) rest on the conserved
$\Q e$, the charges of the gauge symmetry under Euclidean gradient flow (\cref{prop:conservation});
their measured relative drift is $10^{-11}$, $10^{-5}$ and $3\cdot10^{-4}$ under gradient flow, GD and
heavy ball (momentum flows lose most conservation laws, \citealp{marcotte2024keep}), while Adam,
equivariant only under coordinate permutations and sign flips, breaks them at order one \empirical. A
law for Adam's later plateaus is open (\cref{sec:open}).
