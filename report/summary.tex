\begin{abstract}
We study gradient flow on tree tensor networks (TTNs) trained to fit a low-rank teacher tensor, with most
of the effort on the saddle-to-saddle regime reached from small initialization. The main result is that
after the first component is learned, the length of each plateau is set by a graph quantity, the number $k$
of cores in the smallest subtree that must change to add the next component: no plateau for $k=1$, a
plateau $\propto\ln(1/\sigma)$ for $k=2$, and $\propto\sigma^{-(k-2)}$ for $k\ge3$. We prove that $k$ is
exactly the order of the saddle under a spectral condition, and that the order can be lower when the
condition fails. We also give a criterion, computed from the initialization, for which runs stall
permanently, and we map the lazy and rich regimes.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting}

\paragraph{Model.} A TTN represents a tensor $W\in(\mathbb R^d)^{\otimes N}$ by $K$ cores placed on the nodes of
a tree. Each of the $N$ \emph{sites} (tensor modes) is an open leg of one core. Each tree edge is a bond of
dimension $\chi$ shared by two cores, and $W$ is the contraction over all bonds. Cutting a bond $e$ splits
the sites into two groups; the matricization of $W$ along that split has rank at most $\chi_e$. We use three
trees: the MPS (a chain, one site per core, $K=N$), the binary tree (sites in pairs on the bottom cores,
$K=N-1$) and the hierarchical Tucker (HT) tree (one leaf core per site with a binary tree above, $K=2N-1$;
internal cores carry no sites). The model is
\begin{equation}
f(x)=\langle W,\,x_0\otimes\dots\otimes x_{N-1}\rangle,\qquad
L=\tfrac12\lVert W-W^*\rVert_F^2,
\end{equation}
where $L$ is the population loss for whitened Gaussian inputs (up to a constant). $L$ is a polynomial of
degree $2K$ in the cores, so this is a deep linear network (DLN) whose factors are arranged on a tree rather
than a chain. The teacher is
\begin{equation}
W^*=\sum_{r=1}^R s_r\,U_r,\qquad U_r=u^{(r)}_0\otimes\dots\otimes u^{(r)}_{N-1},\quad s_1>s_2>\dots>0.
\end{equation}
In a \emph{pattern teacher} every $u^{(r)}_n$ is a standard basis vector, so $U_r$ is labelled by a string
such as $00011000$. We train by gradient flow from fan-in Gaussian cores of scale $\sigma$
($G_v=\sigma Z_v/\sqrt{F_v}$, $F_v$ the fan-in of core $v$), so $\lVert W\rVert\sim\sigma^K$ at initialization.
As in DLNs, gradient flow conserves on every bond $e$ between a core $v$ and its parent $p$ the imbalance
$Q_e=G_vG_v^\top-G_pG_p^\top$ (both cores matricized with the $e$-leg as rows).

\paragraph{Routes.} Let component $j$ be learned and $r$ not. Let $D$ be the set of sites where the patterns of
$r$ and $j$ differ, $V_D$ the set of cores carrying them, and $\St(V_D)$ the Steiner subtree of $V_D$ (the
smallest subtree containing it). We call $\St(V_D)$ the \emph{route} of $r$ and write
\begin{equation}
k=\lvert\St(V_D)\rvert,\qquad k^*=\min_{r\ \text{not learned}}k .
\end{equation}
Figure~\ref{fig:route} gives an example. The \emph{order} of a saddle $\theta_S$ is the largest $p$ with
$L(\theta)-L(\theta_S)\ge-C\lVert\theta-\theta_S\rVert^p$ near $\theta_S$; order 2 is a strict saddle.

\paragraph{Status labels.} \ev{proven}: theorem with proof; \ev{derived}: calculation under stated
approximations (typically leading order in $\sigma$); \ev{empirical}: measured; \ev{conjecture}: not
established. Novelty labels: \NEW{} (no prior statement found in a literature search of about 270 queries),
\TTN, \KNOWN. Each numerical result was re-checked by an independent re-analysis, except the test marked in
Result~3.

\section{Results}

\subsection*{1. First escape \hfill\TTN}
Near the origin $L\approx\tfrac12\lVert W^*\rVert^2-g(\theta)$ with $g=\langle W,W^*\rangle$, homogeneous of degree
$K$. Writing $\theta=\rho\hat\theta$ and $d\tau=\rho^{K-2}dt$, the direction $\hat\theta$ follows a
$\sigma$-independent spherical flow and $d\ln\rho/d\tau=K\,g(\hat\theta)$. Hence, for $\theta_0=\sigma Z$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:first}
t_1=\sigma^{-(K-2)}\,\mathcal T(Z)+O(1),\qquad
\mathcal T(Z)=\lVert Z\rVert^{-(K-2)}\int_0^\infty e^{-K(K-2)G(\tau)}d\tau,\quad G(\tau)=\int_0^\tau g(\hat\theta)\,d\tau'
\end{equation}
\ev{proven at leading order; offset derived}. $\mathcal T(Z)$ is computed from the initialization by one
integration before training; it predicts $t_1$ in 231 initialization--teacher pairs (1,848 runs, five trees)
to a relative $4\cdot10^{-8}$--$2\cdot10^{-3}$ at the smallest $\sigma$, and the first component learned in
1,847 of 1,848 runs \ev{empirical}. The norm-based time
$t_{\rm KH}=K^{K/2-1}\lVert\theta_0\rVert^{-(K-2)}/((K-2)s_1)$ is a lower bound \ev{proven}; median
$\mathcal T/t_{\rm KH}$ ranges from 3 (binary4) to 2650 (binary8) and reaches $2.9\cdot10^7$ on binary16, because
most of $\lVert\theta_0\rVert$ lies in core slices that do not move at leading order.
After the escape each plateau sits at the critical point of the problem with bond ranks fixed, and each
escape raises the rank of every bond on the route by one (497 of 531 runs with recorded spectra)
\ev{empirical}.
\emph{Prior work:} the exponent $K-2$ and the blow-up identity hold for homogeneous models
\citep{kumar2025early,kunin2025alternating}; $t_{\rm KH}$ is from \citet{kumar2025towards}. New: the evaluation
on trees, the run-by-run tests and the size of the gap to $t_{\rm KH}$.

\subsection*{2. Plateau length after the first escape: the route rule \hfill\NEW}
At a saddle with one learned component $j$, perturb the cores by $\delta$. Multilinearity gives
$W(\theta_S+\delta)=\sum_{\mathcal J}W_{\mathcal J}[\delta]$, a sum over sets $\mathcal J$ of perturbed cores.
Entries on unused bond channels move $W$ only when every core of a connected set $\mathcal C$ uses them, and
then by a product of $\lvert\mathcal C\rvert$ small entries with no Gauss--Newton cost at that order. For a
pattern teacher the residual seen by such a set is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:steiner}
\mathcal R_{\mathcal C}=s_j^{(K-\lvert\mathcal C\rvert)/K}\sum_{r:\,V_{D_r}\subseteq\mathcal C}s_r\,U_r\big|_{\mathcal C}
\end{equation}
\ev{proven}, so the cheapest route to component $r$ is $\St(V_{D_r})$, with coupling
$c_r=s_r s_j^{(K-k)/K}$, and the saddle order is at most $k^*$. If the order equals $k^*$ (Result 3), the
route amplitude $b$ obeys $\dot b\approx c_r b^{k-1}$ and, for two components learned in weight order,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:route}
\Delta t_2\simeq
\begin{cases}
O(1), & k=1,\\[2pt]
\dfrac{1-s_2/s_1}{\lambda_2}\ln\dfrac1\sigma,\quad \lambda_2=s_2\,s_1^{(K-2)/K}, & k=2,\\[8pt]
\dfrac{1-s_2/s_1}{(k-2)\,s_2\,s_1^{(K-k)/K}}\;\sigma^{-(k-2)}, & k\ge3
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
\ev{derived; the prefactor for $k\ge3$ assumes aligned initialization}. The factor $1-s_2/s_1$ is a head
start: the route grows during the first plateau. For $k=2$ the saddle is strict with
$\lambda_{\min}=-\lambda_2$; for $k\ge3$ the Hessian has no negative eigenvalue.

\emph{Tests} \ev{empirical}. About 11,600 runs of two-component teachers on nine trees ($K=3$ to 15). The
per-seed exponent of $\Delta t_2$ is $k-2$ for $k=3,4,5$ (named exceptions in the report), and for $k=6,7$ on
binary8 and HT4 with balanced initialization. For $k=2$ a log law fits better than a power law in all 14
rows, with slope $0.98$--$1.01$ against the predicted $1$ (head start included) and $2.00$--$2.07$ against $2$
when started at the saddle. The second bond singular value stays below $4.3\,\sigma^2 s_2$ on every bond off
the route in all 5,131 events. Because $k$ depends on the tree, a smaller component with a shorter route is
learned first as $\sigma\to0$: teacher $0000\,(1)$, $0110\,(0.3)$, $1001\,(0.6)$ on MPS4 learns $0110$
($k=2$) before $1001$ ($k=4$) in every run, while binary4 ($k=3$ for both) learns them in weight order.
With three or four components the rule, applied at the rank-constrained optimum reached at each plateau,
predicts the plateau levels and their exponents within $0.2$ (seven teachers; one exception).

\emph{Prior work:} the trichotomy $O(1)$ / logarithmic / $\sigma^{-(k-2)}$ for $k$ small layers
\citep[Thm~4.2]{rawal2026theory}; the $k=2$ head start in two-layer linear networks
\citep[Thm~4]{zhang2026saddletosaddle}; a descent curve along a whole connected set of rank-deficient bonds
\citep[Lemma~D.8]{furman2026benign}. New: that the relevant set is the Steiner subtree of the differing
sites, which can be much smaller than the rank-deficient set; the coupling $c_r$; the head start for
$k\ge3$; the measurements.

\subsection*{3. When $k^*$ is the saddle order \hfill\NEW}
Result 2 gives an upper bound. For the lower bound define, for a pattern teacher, the \emph{generalized
piggyback graph} at level $\ell$: its nodes are pairs $(B,\pi)$ of a core set $B$ with $\lvert\St(B)\rvert\le\ell$
and a pattern $\pi$ on the sites of $B$ that differs from $j$ on every core of $B$; every unlearned
component $r$ adds an edge of weight $s_r$ between $(B_1,\pi_r|_{B_1})$ and $(B_2,\pi_r|_{B_2})$ for every split
$V_{D_r}=B_1\sqcup B_2$. Let $\lambda_\ell$ be the largest eigenvalue of its adjacency matrix $\Gamma^{(\ell)}$.

\begin{thm*}[\ev{proven}; Appendix~\ref{app:order}]
Let $\theta_S$ represent one learned component $j$, with a spare bond channel on the route.
(a) If $\lambda_{\lceil k^*/2\rceil-1}<s_j$, the saddle order is exactly $k^*$.
(b) If $\lambda_\ell>s_j$ for some $\ell<k^*/2$ (and enough spare channels), the order is at most $2\ell$.
Hence the order is $\min(k^*,2\ell^*)$, with $\ell^*$ the first level at which $\lambda_\ell$ exceeds $s_j$.
\end{thm*}
Consequences: the order is $2$ when $k^*=2$; for $k^*\in\{3,4\}$ it is $k^*$ iff $\lambda_{\max}(\Gamma^{(1)})<s_j$
(otherwise the saddle is strict); for two-component teachers learned in weight order it is $k^*$ on every
tree. It can drop below $k^*$ at a saddle with positive semidefinite Hessian: on MPS6 with
$j=000000\,(1)$ and $110001$, $110010\,(0.8$ each$)$, $k^*=5$ but $\lambda_2=0.8\sqrt2>1$, and the order is $4$
(measured slope $4.000$, constant $-0.0350$ against $-0.0350$). The descent uses new entries on cores 0 and 1
toward the pattern $110000$, which is not a teacher component, combined with rotations of cores 4 and 5.
Two independent checks of the proofs and 44 numerical instances agree.

\emph{Dynamics.} Where the order is $p<k^*$ the escape should take $\propto\sigma^{-(p-2)}$ rather than
$\sigma^{-(k^*-2)}$ \ev{derived}. A pre-registered test (about 2,800 runs) measured $2.001\pm0.001$ for $p=4$
($k^*=5,6$) and $3.996$--$3.998$ for $p=6$ ($k^*=7$) from the saddle, where the scale lies below the predicted
crossover; the route law fits $10^3$--$10^5$ times worse. Above the crossover the results were mixed
\ev{empirical; \textbf{not independently verified}}.

\emph{Prior work:} the upper bound \citep{furman2026benign}; lower bounds only for DLNs (strict versus
non-strict, \citealp[Thm~7]{achour2024loss}) and for one parity saddle \citep[Prop.~5.3]{furman2026benign}.

\subsection*{4. Tree shape and strict saddles \hfill\NEW}
With one learned component of a pattern teacher, $\theta_S$ is strict iff $k^*=2$ or
$\lambda_{\max}(\Gamma^{(1)})>s_j$ \ev{proven}; the rotation block of the Hessian is
$s_j^{(K-2)/K}(s_j I-\Gamma^{(1)})$. For non-orthogonal teachers (overlapping components, misaligned input
covariance, correlated sites, random TTN teachers) the first plateau sits at a rank-one critical point
$\bar s\,y_0\otimes\dots\otimes y_{N-1}$ that is not a teacher component. Criticality removes every single-core
direction, so a two-core route has a nonzero residual only if both cores carry sites. On an MPS every bond
joins two such cores, and bond $e=(i,i+1)$ has Hessian eigenvalue $-\bar s^{(N-2)/N}s_2(M_e)$, where $M_e$ is
$W^*$ contracted with $y$ on all sites except $i,i+1$; the second escape takes
$h_e\ln(1/\sigma)$ with $h_e=(1-s_2(M_e)/\bar s)/(\bar s^{(N-2)/N}s_2(M_e))$ \ev{derived}, which matches the
measurements within $1$--$1.6\%$. On binary trees with $N=2^n$ and on HT trees no two adjacent cores both carry
sites, so $k^*\ge3$ and the escape stays polynomial (13 teachers, $N=4,8$) \ev{derived, empirical}.
\emph{Prior work:} for DLNs the piggyback graph is a single edge \citep[Thm~7]{achour2024loss}.

\subsection*{5. Order of learning \hfill\TTN}
Let two components differ on sites carried by $m$ cores. For $m\ge3$ every component is a local maximum
of $g$ on the sphere; on an MPS with an orthogonal teacher the components evolve independently at leading
order, so component $r$ blows up at $\sigma^{-(K-2)}\hat t_r/s_r$ with i.i.d.\ $\hat t_r$, and
\begin{equation}
P(\text{larger first})=P\big(\hat t_2/\hat t_1>s_2/s_1\big),
\end{equation}
independent of $\sigma$ \ev{proven at leading order}. All 40,800 MPS runs of the main sweep learn the winner of
this race first; overall $P(\text{larger first})=0.60$--$0.88$ at $s_2/s_1=0.5$ (67,200 runs) \ev{empirical}.
For $m=2$ the smaller component is a saddle of the spherical flow with $n$ unstable directions, and
$P(\text{smaller first})\propto\sigma^{\gamma}$, $\gamma=n(1-w)/w$, $w=s_2/s_1$: consistent with the data for
$n=1$ \ev{empirical, heuristic mechanism}, a conjecture for $n=\chi_e$.
\emph{Prior work:} greedy learning in DLNs \citep[Thm~2]{jacot2022saddletosaddle}; the tensor/matrix
dichotomy and the rule $\arg\max_r s_rX_r^{K-2}$ in multi-spike tensor PCA \citep{benarous2025stochastic}.
New: the classification by $m$, the exact race on MPS, the $\sigma^\gamma$ law.

\subsection*{6. Permanent stalls: a criterion from the initialization \hfill\NEW}
Of 5,682 two-component runs, 398 stall permanently, all with $k\ge4$; with $\chi$ at the teacher rank most
runs with $K=7,8$ stall \ev{empirical}. The stalled runs have a route-end core whose route entry has decayed
to roundoff. Let $u$ be an end of the route (it carries a differing site) and $w$ its neighbour on the
route, sharing bond $e$. Let $X_u^\perp$, $X_w^\perp$ be their entries on the unused channels of $e$ with
physical index orthogonal to the learned pattern; the route entry of $u$ is one of them. On the plateau
these blocks produce a two-core term that the residual does not contain, so the loss contains a term
$\tfrac\kappa2\lVert X_u^{\perp\top}X_w^\perp\rVert^2$ (rows of $X$ indexed by the unused channels) and the flow on it is a two-layer linear network with
zero target. It conserves
\begin{equation}
C_u=X_u^\perp X_u^{\perp\top}-X_w^\perp X_w^{\perp\top},
\end{equation}
and $X_u^\perp X_u^{\perp\top}\to[C_u]_+$. With one unused channel, the route entry of $u$ survives iff
$C_u>0$ \ev{proven in the reduced flow}. The decay rate is $\propto\sigma^2$ ($\sigma^4$ at a binary or HT
leaf, whose neighbour carries no site), while the escape takes $\sigma^{-(k-2)}$; so routes with $k\le3$
never die, for $k=4$ decay and escape are of the same order and a reduced model of the route decides, and
for $k=5$ a route dies when two or more of its ends die (one dying end pins it) \ev{derived}. Because the state at
the start of the plateau is $\sigma$ times a function of the initial direction, $C_u$ is computed from
the initialization. At a saddle start with fan-in noise an end dies with probability
$I_{F_u/(F_u+F_w)}(n_u/2,n_w/2)$ ($I$ the regularized incomplete beta function, $n$ the number of orthogonal
physical directions): $0.392$ at an MPS chain end, $0.5$ at a root end ($d=\chi=2$) \ev{proven}. On the 3,492
fan-in runs with $k\ge4$, $93\%$ of runs predicted dead stall, against $1.8\%$ of those predicted alive
\ev{empirical}. Balanced initialization ($Q_e=0$) protects ends that are tree leaves and cuts stalls tenfold.
Derivation: Appendix~\ref{app:trap}.
\emph{Prior work:} a conserved quantity decides which side of a unit can vanish
\citep[Lemma~B.1]{kunin2025alternating}; imbalance can create spurious minima \citep[Thm~G.1]{furman2026benign}.

\subsection*{7. Lazy and rich regimes \hfill\NEW}
The tangent kernel at a generic initialization has rank
$\sum_vF_v\chi_v-\sum_e\chi_e^2$ (capped at $d^N$), the dimension of the fixed-rank manifold; it reaches $d^N$
iff every bond is full, $\chi_e\ge\min(d^{n_e},d^{N-n_e})$ with $n_e$ the number of sites below $e$
\ev{proven}. Lazy training therefore reaches zero loss only with full bonds; otherwise it stops at
$\tfrac12\lVert P_\perp W^*\rVert^2$. We map regimes with the initial function subtracted
($f_\theta-f_{\theta_0}$). On binary4 with full bonds, training is saddle-to-saddle below
$\sigma_c\approx0.3\,\chi^{-1/2}$ \ev{empirical} and lazy above $\sigma_{\rm lazy}\approx6.5\,\chi^{-1/2}$
\ev{derived; measured slope $-0.504\pm0.011$}; the $\chi^{-1/2}$ comes from a coherent second-order kernel
change. The saddle-to-saddle boundary rises with $K$ (above $\sigma=1$ for most seeds at $K\ge8$); its
large-$K$ limit is open. We also give a $\mu$P-type parameterization that keeps the kernel moving as $\chi$
grows \ev{derived}.
\emph{Prior work:} regime maps for linear and diagonal networks \citep{woodworth2020kernel,kunin2024get}.

\subsection*{8. Optimizers}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \TTN{} GD is linearly stable at the balanced global minimum iff
$\eta<\eta_c=2/(K s_1^{2-2/K})$, since $\lambda_{\max}(\nabla^2L)=Ks_1^{2-2/K}$ there \ev{proven}. Below
$\eta_c$ GD follows the saddle sequence of gradient flow (765 of 765 runs) with an $O(\eta)$ shift of the
event times \ev{derived, empirical}. DLN case: \citet{mulayoff2020unique}.
\item \TTN{} SGD does not shorten the first plateau; it lengthens later plateaus and can trap routes
\ev{empirical}.
\item \KNOWN{} Adam's first escape takes a $\sigma$-independent number of steps until $\epsilon$ dominates
\ev{derived, empirical}; concurrent with \citet{sabanayagam2026optimizer}.
\item ALS and DMRG are invariant to $\sigma$ \ev{proven}; they and Riemannian GD have no plateaus \ev{derived}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{9. MNIST \hfill\NEW}
On $4\times4$ MNIST images with a binary tree over the pixels ($N=16$), the first route learned is a pair of
sibling bottom cores ($k=3$). In a pre-registered test over 27 pixel orderings, the share of squared
route couplings on routes with $k\le5$, computed from the data before training, predicted the ranking of
the orderings by training loss at a fixed time (Spearman $-0.60$, 95\% CI $[-0.82,-0.22]$; $-0.55$ within
the 20 random orderings) but not by time to reach loss $0.25$ \ev{empirical}. Orderings that are equally
local in the image but pair pixels vertically rank 13--21 of 27, so spatial locality alone does not explain
the effect. That the ordering acts by placing label-relevant pixel pairs on routes short enough to escape
within the training time is a \ev{conjecture}.

\section{Already known in this setting}
The binary tree with $N=4$ is exactly a depth-3 DLN, so its saddle sequence, greedy limit and strict
wrong-order saddles are known \citep{saxe2014exact,jacot2022saddletosaddle,achour2024loss}. The conservation
of $Q_e=G_vG_v^\top-G_pG_p^\top$ on every bond is the tree form of DLN balancedness; the balanced, minimum-norm
escape direction from the origin is in \citet{kumar2025early}; the two-core TTN is matrix factorization
\citep{jiang2023algorithmic}; low hierarchical-rank bias of hierarchical tensor factorization is in
\citet{razin2022implicit}. We found no prior analysis of saddle-to-saddle dynamics on TTNs with $K\ge3$
dense cores.

\section{Limitations}
Small trees ($N\le16$, mostly $N\le8$), exact population loss with whitened inputs, and mostly synthetic
teachers. The saddle-order theorem covers saddles with one learned component. The large-$K$ boundary of the
saddle-to-saddle regime is open. The dynamics test of Result~3 was not independently re-checked.

\section{Derivations}

Throughout, $\theta_S$ is a balanced critical point representing $s_jU_j$ (one learned component). Each core
then equals $q_j=s_j^{1/K}$ times the pattern of $j$ on its sites and channel 1 on its bonds. $\mathcal R=W^*-s_jU_j$
is the residual, $L_S=\tfrac12\lVert\mathcal R\rVert^2$, and entries on bond channels other than 1 are \emph{new}.

\subsection{Escape from the origin (Result 1)}
Near the origin $\dot\theta=\nabla g(\theta)+O(\lVert\theta\rVert^{2K-1})$. Since $g$ is homogeneous of degree $K$,
$\nabla g(\rho\hat\theta)=\rho^{K-1}\nabla g(\hat\theta)$ and $\langle\hat\theta,\nabla g(\hat\theta)\rangle=Kg(\hat\theta)$.
Hence $\dot\rho=\rho^{K-1}Kg(\hat\theta)$ and $\dot{\hat\theta}=\rho^{K-2}(I-\hat\theta\hat\theta^\top)\nabla g(\hat\theta)$.
In the time $d\tau=\rho^{K-2}dt$ the direction obeys a $\rho$-free equation and $\rho(\tau)=\rho_0e^{KG(\tau)}$, so
$dt=\rho_0^{-(K-2)}e^{-K(K-2)G(\tau)}d\tau$, and $\rho$ blows up at
$t^*=\rho_0^{-(K-2)}\int_0^\infty e^{-K(K-2)G}d\tau$. With $\rho_0=\sigma\lVert Z\rVert$ this is \eqref{eq:first}. The
direction converges to a KKT point of $g$ on the sphere, which is balanced with core norms $1/K$; the $O(1)$
offset in \eqref{eq:first} is the time a balanced rank-one mode takes from there to the event.

\subsection{Routes and the Steiner identity (Result 2)}
For a set $\mathcal J$ of cores, let $W_{\mathcal J}[\delta]$ be $W$ with the cores in $\mathcal J$ replaced by
perturbations $\delta_v$ and the rest at $\theta_S$. Then $W(\theta_S+\delta)=\sum_{\mathcal J}W_{\mathcal J}[\delta]$ and
\begin{equation}
L(\theta_S+\delta)=L_S-\sum_{\mathcal J\ne\emptyset}\langle\mathcal R,W_{\mathcal J}[\delta]\rangle
+\tfrac12\Big\lVert\sum_{\mathcal J\ne\emptyset}W_{\mathcal J}[\delta]\Big\rVert^2 .
\end{equation}
A new index of $\delta_v$ contracts, in a neighbouring unperturbed core, with a zero block. So
$W_{\mathcal J}[\delta]\ne0$ with only new entries requires every new index to be matched inside $\mathcal J$:
each connected part $\mathcal C$ of $\mathcal J$ has at least two cores, and
$W_{\mathcal C}[\delta]=\mathcal W_{\mathcal C}(\delta)\otimes\bigotimes_{f\in\partial\mathcal C}\Sigma_f$, where $\mathcal W_{\mathcal C}$ is the small TTN
formed by the $\delta_v$ and $\Sigma_f$ is the learned network beyond the boundary bond $f$. For a proper subset of
$\mathcal C$ the product vanishes, so the gain $\langle\mathcal R,W_{\mathcal C}\rangle$ is of order $\lvert\mathcal C\rvert$ in $\delta$
while the quadratic term is of order $2\lvert\mathcal C\rvert$.

Beyond $f$ the learned network is $\Sigma_f=q_j^{n_f}U_j|_{H_f}$ ($n_f$ cores, sites $H_f$). Contracting
$\mathcal R=\sum_{r\ne j}s_rU_r$ with it gives zero unless $U_r$ agrees with $U_j$ on $H_f$, i.e.\ unless all sites
where $r$ differs from $j$ lie on cores of $\mathcal C$. This gives \eqref{eq:steiner}, and a connected
$\mathcal C\supseteq V_{D_r}$ contains $\St(V_{D_r})$. Along the curve where each route core gets $t$ times the
pattern of $r$ on a new channel, $L-L_S=-c_rt^k+\tfrac12q_j^{2(K-k)}t^{2k}$.

\emph{Escape time.} With old entries frozen and route amplitudes $b_v$,
$\dot b_v=A\prod_{u\ne v}b_u\,(s_r-A\prod_ub_u)$, $A=q_j^{K-k}$. For a balanced route at small amplitude
$\dot b=c_rb^{k-1}$, so $b^{-(k-2)}$ decreases at rate $(k-2)c_r$ (or $\ln b$ grows at rate $c_r$ for $k=2$).
During the first plateau the route already grows with coupling $s_rq_j(t)^{K-k}$ while $q_j$ rises from
$\sigma$; integrating with $\dot q_j\simeq s_jq_j^{K-1}$ gives
$b_{\rm eff}^{-(k-2)}=\sigma^{-(k-2)}(1-s_r/s_j)+O(1)$ for $k\ge3$ and
$\ln(1/b_{\rm eff})=(1-s_r/s_j)\ln(1/\sigma)+O(1)$ for $k=2$, which gives \eqref{eq:route}.

\subsection{Saddle order (Result 3)}\label{app:order}
Let $P_v$ project the physical space of core $v$ onto the pattern of $j$, $P_v^\perp=I-P_v$, and
$\Pi_B=\bigotimes_{v\in B}P_v^\perp\otimes\bigotimes_{v\notin B}P_v$. The $2^K$ sector projectors are orthogonal and
sum to the identity. With $\deg B=\lvert\St(B)\rvert$, $k^*=\min\{\deg B:\Pi_B\mathcal R\ne0\}$. The general
operator $\Gamma^{(\ell)}$ acts on $\bigoplus_{\deg B\le\ell}\mathrm{range}(\Pi_B)$ with block
$(x,y)\mapsto\langle\Pi_{B_1\sqcup B_2}\mathcal R,x\otimes y\rangle$ for disjoint $B_1,B_2$ and zero otherwise; for a pattern
teacher it is the adjacency matrix of the graph in Result~3.

\begin{thm*}[Lower bound]
If $\lambda_{\ell-1}<s_j$ for some $2\le\ell\le\lceil k^*/2\rceil$, then
$L(\theta)-L_S\ge-C\lVert\theta-\theta_S\rVert^{\min(k^*,2\ell)}$ near $\theta_S$.
\end{thm*}
\emph{Proof sketch.} Let $y=\langle U_j,W\rangle$ and $\mu_B=\Pi_BW/y$. Sector orthogonality gives exactly
\[
L-L_S=\tfrac12(y-s_j)^2+\sum_{B\ne\emptyset}\Big[\tfrac{y^2}{2}\lVert\mu_B\rVert^2-y\langle\Pi_B\mathcal R,\mu_B\rangle\Big].
\]
Families $(f_B)$ form a commutative algebra under $(f*g)_B=\sum_{B_1\sqcup B_2=B}f_{B_1}\otimes g_{B_2}$; let
$\psi=\log\mu$ (set-partition cumulants). If the new entries $\nu_v$ of a core vanish, the contraction factorizes
at $v$ and no cumulant contains a set through $v$; analyticity turns this into
$\lVert\psi_B\rVert\le C\prod_{v\in\St(B)}\lVert\nu_v\rVert$. Split sets into low ($\deg\le\ell-1$) and high. Low sets carry
no residual, the Gauss--Newton term is at least $(1-C\varepsilon)\lVert\psi_{\rm low}\rVert^2$, and the gain terms
with two low cumulants sum to $\tfrac12\langle\psi_{\rm low},\Gamma^{(\ell-1)}\psi_{\rm low}\rangle$. Every other term
is $O(\varepsilon^{k^*})$ or $O(\varepsilon^{2\ell})$. Hence
$L-L_S\ge\tfrac y2(s_j-\lambda_{\ell-1}-O(\varepsilon))\lVert\psi_{\rm low}\rVert^2-O(\varepsilon^{\min(k^*,2\ell)})$. \qed

\begin{thm*}[Sharpness]
For a pattern teacher, if a node set $X$ of the level-$\ell$ graph ($\ell<k^*/2$) has adjacency spectral radius
$r(A_X)>s_j$ and each bond has enough spare channels, there is a curve with
$\lVert\theta(t)-\theta_S\rVert\le t$ and $L-L_S\le-Ct^{2\ell}$.
\end{thm*}
\emph{Proof sketch.} Take the Perron vector $x$ of $A_X$. Rotate each single-core node by $\varepsilon x$ toward its
pattern (old entries) and give each multi-core node $(B,\pi)$ new entries of size
$q_j(\varepsilon x_{B,\pi})^{1/\deg B}$ on $\St(B)$, each on its own spare channel. Then
$L-L_S=-\tfrac{s_j}{2}(r(A_X)-s_j)\varepsilon^2+O(\varepsilon^3)$ while the distance is $O(\varepsilon^{1/\ell})$. \qed

For a two-component teacher in weight order every node has at most one edge, so $\lambda_\ell\in\{0,s_r\}$ with
$s_r<s_j$ and the order is $k^*$. In the MPS6 example the node $(\{0,1\},11)$ is joined to $(\{5\},1)$ by $110001$
and to $(\{4\},1)$ by $110010$: a star with $\lambda_2=0.8\sqrt2$.

\subsection{Route-end trap (Result 6)}\label{app:trap}
Let $u$ be a route end with route neighbour $w$ on bond $e$, and suppose one unused channel of $e$ is in play.
Write $a=\lVert X_u^\perp\rVert^2$, $b=\lVert X_w^\perp\rVert^2$. On the plateau the old entries are at
$\theta_S$ and the new entries are small, so the only term in the loss at second order in these two blocks is
the Gauss--Newton cost $\tfrac\kappa2\lVert X_u^{\perp\top}X_w^\perp\rVert^2$, with $\kappa=q_j^{2(K-2)}$ when $w$
carries sites: their product changes $W$ by a tensor that differs from $U_j$ exactly on the sites of $u$ and
$w$, and the residual has no such component unless some component has the two-core route $\{u,w\}$. Gradient flow on this term gives
\[
\dot a=-2\kappa ab,\qquad \dot b=-2\kappa ab,
\]
so $C_u=a-b$ is conserved and $\dot a=-2\kappa a(a-C_u)$. If $C_u>0$, $a\to C_u$; if $C_u<0$, $a\to0$ at rate
$2\kappa\lvert C_u\rvert$. The route entry of $u$ lies in $X_u^\perp$ (it carries a site where $r$ differs from
$j$), so it survives iff $C_u>0$. With several channels the same computation gives
$X_u^\perp X_u^{\perp\top}\to[C_u]_+$.

\emph{Time scales.} $\lvert C_u\rvert=O(\sigma^2)$, so a dying end decays in time $\propto\sigma^{-2}$, against the
escape time $\sigma^{-(k-2)}$: the end is decided before the escape when $k\ge5$, at the same order when $k=4$,
and the route escapes first when $k\le3$. If $w$ carries no site (binary or HT leaf), the cheapest costly product
passes through three cores, the rate is $\propto\sigma^4$, and the threshold becomes $k\ge6$.

\emph{From the initialization.} The full imbalance $Q_e$ (Section~1) is exactly conserved.
Restricted to the new direction $\nu$ and split into the orthogonal blocks and the entries that pair with
nothing at second order ($X_{\rm nv}$, $Y_{\rm nv}$), it gives
$C_u=Q_{\nu\nu}+\lVert Y_{\rm nv}\rVert^2-\lVert X_{\rm nv}\rVert^2+O(\sigma^3)$, evaluated after the first escape. That
state is $\sigma$ times a function of the initial direction, obtained from the $\sigma$-free flow of A.1.

\emph{Death probability.} At a saddle start the blocks are Gaussian with variances $\sigma^2/F_u$ and
$\sigma^2/F_w$, so $a\sim\sigma^2\chi^2_{n_u}/F_u$ and $b\sim\sigma^2\chi^2_{n_w}/F_w$. With
$Z=\chi^2_{n_u}/(\chi^2_{n_u}+\chi^2_{n_w})\sim\mathrm{Beta}(n_u/2,n_w/2)$, $a<b$ iff $Z<F_u/(F_u+F_w)$, so
$P(\text{end dies})=I_{F_u/(F_u+F_w)}(n_u/2,n_w/2)$.
