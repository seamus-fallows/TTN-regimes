\begin{abstract}
  % About 200 words. Status words (prove, derived, confirmed) follow the tags of Sections 3-6.
  How do gradient methods train tree tensor networks? We study the model
  $f(x)=\langle W,\phi_1(x_1)\otimes\cdots\otimes\phi_N(x_N)\rangle$, with $W$ contracted from $K$ cores on
  a tree, trained on the exact population loss of a teacher. From small initialization (scale $\sigma$),
  gradient flow is saddle-to-saddle: plateaus sit at critical points of the problem with fixed bond ranks,
  and each escape raises the bond ranks along a route, a set of cores that must move together. The first
  plateau lasts $\propto\sigma^{-(K-2)}$, as in other homogeneous models, with a prefactor computed per
  initialization. Later, the tree decides: the smallest route, of $k^*$ cores, gives no plateau
  ($k^*=1$), a logarithmic one ($k^*=2$) or one $\propto\sigma^{-(k^*-2)}$ (derived; confirmed up to
  $k^*=5$). We prove that $k^*$ is the saddle order under a spectral condition, which every
  two-component teacher learned in weight order satisfies, and that the order can be lower from $k^*=5$
  on. With non-orthogonal teachers, the saddle after the first escape is strict on matrix product states,
  not on binary trees. The learning order is a race set by the initialization unless competing components
  differ on only two cores. Larger $\sigma$ leads through a rich regime to a lazy one, which needs every
  bond at its cut dimension.
\end{abstract}
