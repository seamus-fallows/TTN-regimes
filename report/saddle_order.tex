\begin{abstract}
We study gradient flow on tree tensor networks (TTNs) fitted to a target that is a sum of rank-one
tensors. From small initialization the flow learns the rank-one terms one at a time and stays near a
saddle point between consecutive terms. How long it stays is controlled by the order of the saddle, the
exponent of the fastest local decrease of the loss. We compute this order exactly at the critical point that
represents one learned term with one channel per edge. Typically it equals the number of cores in the
smallest subtree that connects the cores on which some unlearned term differs from the learned one. This
number depends on the shape of the tree, so the same target produces plateaus of different lengths on
different trees. The order is lower only when the unlearned terms are, in a precise spectral sense, stronger
than the learned one, and we give its value in that case too. At leading order, a saddle of order $p$ is left
after a time $\propto\ln(1/\sigma)$ if $p=2$ and $\propto\sigma^{-(p-2)}$ if $p\ge3$, where $\sigma$ is the
initialization scale; when two descent mechanisms of different order coexist, the lower order governs the
escape only below a crossover scale. Simulations on nine trees agree with these laws.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

In a deep linear network (DLN) trained from small initialization, gradient flow learns the singular modes
of the target one at a time, with a plateau before each \citep{saxe2014exact,jacot2022saddletosaddle}. Every
plateau lasts $\propto\sigma^{-(K-2)}$, where $K$ is the depth and $\sigma$ the initialization scale. The reason is
that the output is a product of $K$ layers that all start at size $\sigma$: their common size $a$ obeys
$\dot a\approx c\,a^{K-1}$, whose solution from $a(0)=\sigma$ stays small for a time $\propto\sigma^{-(K-2)}$ and then
grows quickly. In a DLN every mode needs every layer, so every plateau has the same exponent.

A TTN is also a product of $K$ factors, its cores, but each core is attached to only some of the inputs. After
the first term is learned, most cores are large. To add the next term, only some cores need to change from
small values, and the length of the next plateau depends on how many. In terms of the loss landscape, it
depends on the order of the saddle at which the flow rests.

This note determines that order (Theorem~\ref{thm:main}) and derives the resulting plateau lengths at leading
order (Section~\ref{sec:plateaus}). Section~\ref{sec:prior} compares the result with prior work.

\section{Setting and definitions}\label{sec:setting}

\paragraph{Tree tensor networks.} Let $T$ be a tree with $K$ nodes. Each node $v$ holds a tensor $G_v$, called a
\emph{core}. A core has one leg for each tree edge at $v$ and, possibly, one \emph{physical} leg of dimension
$D_v$. The legs on a tree edge $e$ have dimension $\chi_e$, the \emph{bond dimension}. Contracting all
tree-edge legs gives a tensor $W(\theta)\in\bigotimes_v\mathbb R^{D_v}$, where $\theta$ denotes all core entries.
The network has $N$ inputs $x_0,\dots,x_{N-1}\in\mathbb R^d$, called \emph{sites}; each site belongs to the physical leg
of one core, so $D_v=d^{n_v}$ if core $v$ carries $n_v$ sites ($D_v=1$ if it carries none). The network computes
$f(x)=\ip{W}{x_0\otimes\dots\otimes x_{N-1}}$.

Three trees serve as examples (Figure~\ref{fig:steiner}): the matrix product state (MPS), a path with one site
per core; the binary tree, with two sites on each bottom core and none on the others; and the hierarchical
Tucker (HT) tree, with one site on each leaf and none on the internal cores.

\paragraph{Target and loss.} Labels are generated by a target tensor $W^*$ as $y=\ip{W^*}{x_0\otimes\dots\otimes x_{N-1}}$.
For whitened Gaussian inputs the population squared loss is, up to a constant,
\begin{equation}
L(\theta)=\tfrac12\lVert W(\theta)-W^*\rVert^2 .
\end{equation}
We take $W^*$ to be a sum of distinct products of standard basis vectors,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target}
W^*=\sum_{r}s_r\,U_r,\qquad U_r=e_{\pi_r(0)}\otimes e_{\pi_r(1)}\otimes\dots\otimes e_{\pi_r(N-1)},\qquad s_r>0,
\end{equation}
where $\pi_r$ is a string of basis indices, one per site (for example $\pi_r=110010$). We call $U_r$ the
\emph{components} of the target. Since the strings are distinct, the tensors $U_r$ are orthonormal.

\paragraph{The saddle after one component is learned.} Fix a component $j$ and let $q_v\in\mathbb R^{D_v}$ be the
basis vector that $U_j$ has on the sites of core $v$, so $U_j=\bigotimes_vq_v$. Let $\theta_S$ have cores
\begin{equation}\label{eq:saddle}
G_v=a_v\;q_v\otimes e_0\otimes\dots\otimes e_0,\qquad a_v>0,\qquad \textstyle\prod_va_v=s_j,
\end{equation}
where $e_0$ is the first basis vector of each tree-edge leg. Then $W(\theta_S)=s_jU_j$: the network represents
component $j$ exactly, using one channel (index $0$) on every edge. The other $\chi_e-1$ indices of each edge are
\emph{unused channels}. The loss at $\theta_S$ is $L_S=\frac12\lVert\mathcal R\rVert^2$, with residual
$\mathcal R=W^*-s_jU_j=\sum_{r\ne j}s_rU_r$; we assume $\mathcal R\ne0$. When some $\chi_e\ge2$ there are other
critical points with $W=s_jU_j$, for example with nonzero entries on an unused channel that meet only zeros in the
neighbouring core; they are not of the form \eqref{eq:saddle}, and the results below concern the points
\eqref{eq:saddle}.

Two points of the form \eqref{eq:saddle} differ only in the scalars $a_v$, and they are related by a diagonal change
of basis on the edges: scaling index $0$ of edge $(u,v)$ by $\lambda$ in $G_u$ and by $1/\lambda$ in $G_v$ leaves $W$
unchanged for every $\theta$ and trades $a_u$ against $a_v$. This map is linear, invertible and preserves $L$, so it
preserves the order defined below. We therefore compute at the balanced point $a_v=a:=s_j^{1/K}$ where convenient.

\paragraph{Order of a saddle.} For a critical point $\theta_S$ let
$\psi(\tau)=\min_{\lVert\theta-\theta_S\rVert\le\tau}\big(L(\theta)-L_S\big)$. Since $L$ is a polynomial, $\psi$ is semialgebraic,
so if $\psi\not\equiv0$ near $0$ then $\psi(\tau)=-c\,\tau^{\alpha}(1+o(1))$ for some $c>0$ and rational $\alpha$. The \emph{order}
of $\theta_S$ is $\ord(\theta_S)=\alpha$: the exponent of the fastest local decrease of the loss. Equivalently, it is the
largest $p$ with $L(\theta)-L_S\ge-C\lVert\theta-\theta_S\rVert^p$ near $\theta_S$. (This differs from the derivative-based
notion of ``higher-order saddle'' used, for example, by Anandkumar and Ge, 2016.) At a critical point, order $2$ is
equivalent to a negative Hessian eigenvalue: if the Hessian is positive semidefinite, Taylor's theorem gives
$L-L_S\ge-C\lVert\theta-\theta_S\rVert^3$. A saddle of order $2$ is called \emph{strict}.

\paragraph{Steiner subtrees.} For a set $B$ of cores, the \emph{Steiner subtree} $\St(B)$ is the smallest subtree of
$T$ containing $B$. We use the symbol both for its set of cores and for the subtree itself: $\lvert\St(B)\rvert$ is its
number of cores, and the edges of $\St(B)$ are the tree edges between its cores. For each component $r\ne j$ let
\begin{equation}
V_r=\{\text{cores carrying a site where }\pi_r\text{ and }\pi_j\text{ differ}\},\qquad k_r=\lvert\St(V_r)\rvert,\qquad k^*=\min_{r\ne j}k_r .
\end{equation}
Figure~\ref{fig:steiner} gives an example. The point $\theta_S$ is critical iff $k^*\ge2$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:decomp}).

\section{Two ways to decrease the loss}\label{sec:mechanisms}

Before stating the theorem we describe the two kinds of perturbation that lower the loss at $\theta_S$. The
theorem says that nothing else does better.

\paragraph{New channel along a subtree.} An entry on an unused channel of edge $e$ is multiplied, in the
contraction, by the neighbouring core's entries on the same channel, which are zero at $\theta_S$. So it changes
$W$ only if the neighbour also uses that channel. A set of cores therefore changes $W$ through unused channels only
if it is connected and all its cores use them together; the change is then a product of one small entry per core.
To produce component $r$ in this way, the set must contain every core on which $\pi_r$ differs from $\pi_j$, hence
all of $\St(V_r)$. With entries of size $\tau$ on the $k_r$ cores of $\St(V_r)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:route}
L-L_S=-c_r\,\tau^{k_r}+O(\tau^{2k_r}),\qquad c_r=s_r\,s_j^{(K-k_r)/K}
\end{equation}
(Proposition~\ref{prop:route}, at the balanced point). So the order is at most $k^*$.

\paragraph{Combining two cheaper perturbations.} A perturbation can also change the indices of $W$ on a set
$B$ of cores at lower cost, in two ways:
\begin{itemize}
\item if $B=\{v\}$ is a single core, by rotating its vector $q_v$ toward another basis vector, keeping the used
channel; the change in $W$ is linear in the rotation angle;
\item if $\lvert B\rvert\ge2$, by a new channel along $\St(B)$ as above; the change in $W$ has size $\eta$ when each
of the $\lvert\St(B)\rvert$ entries has size $\eta^{1/\lvert\St(B)\rvert}$ (in units of $a$).
\end{itemize}
Each such perturbation moves $W$ away from $s_jU_j$, which costs $\frac12s_j^2\eta^2$ at second order. Two of them on
disjoint core sets $B_1,B_2$ also produce, at second order, the product tensor with modified indices on
$B_1\cup B_2$. If that tensor is an unlearned component $r$, this gains $s_rs_j\eta_1\eta_2$. The net change is a
quadratic form in the amplitudes, which is negative in some direction iff the gains beat the costs. The matrix below
records the gains.

\begin{definition}[Coupling matrix]\label{def:coupling}
For $\ell\ge1$, let $\mathcal N_\ell$ be the set of pairs $(B,\beta)$, where $B$ is a nonempty set of cores with
$\lvert\St(B)\rvert\le\ell$ and $\beta$ assigns a basis index to each site of $B$ such that, on every core of $B$, the
indices differ from those of $\pi_j$. The coupling matrix $M_\ell$ is the symmetric matrix indexed by $\mathcal N_\ell$
with entries
\begin{equation}
M_\ell\big[(B_1,\beta_1),(B_2,\beta_2)\big]=\begin{cases}
s_r & \text{if }B_1\cap B_2=\emptyset\text{ and the string equal to }\beta_1\text{ on }B_1,\ \beta_2\text{ on }B_2,\\
 & \text{and }\pi_j\text{ elsewhere is }\pi_r\text{ for some component }r,\\
0 & \text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Let $\mu_\ell$ be its largest eigenvalue, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lstar}
\ell^*=\min\{\ell\ge1:\ \mu_\ell>s_j\}\qquad(\ell^*=\infty\text{ if there is no such }\ell).
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

An unordered pair of elements determines one string, hence at most one component, so each unordered pair contributes
one weight $s_r$ and $\frac12x^\top M_\ell x=\sum_{\{n_1,n_2\}}M_\ell[n_1,n_2]x_{n_1}x_{n_2}$, summed over unordered pairs.
$M_\ell$ is symmetric with nonnegative entries, so $\mu_\ell$ is also its spectral radius (Perron--Frobenius), and $\mu_\ell$ is
nondecreasing in $\ell$ because $M_\ell$ is a principal submatrix of $M_{\ell+1}$. A nonzero entry requires a component $r$
with $V_r=B_1\sqcup B_2$, so if no $V_r$ splits into two sets whose Steiner subtrees have at most $\ell$ cores, then
$M_\ell=0$.

At level $\ell=1$ only single-core rotations occur. Let $z_u$ be the entry of $G_u$ with a physical index other than that
of $q_u$ and all edge indices $0$. The block of the Hessian at $\theta_S$ in these coordinates is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hessblock}
H_{\rm rot}=s_j\,D\big(s_jI-M_1\big)D,\qquad D=\mathrm{diag}(1/a_u),
\end{equation}
which is $s_j^{(K-2)/K}(s_jI-M_1)$ at the balanced point. Since $D$ is invertible, $H_{\rm rot}$ has a negative eigenvalue iff
$\mu_1>s_j$. For $\ell\ge2$ the combined perturbation has $\lVert\theta-\theta_S\rVert\sim\eta^{1/\ell}$ and lowers the loss by
$\sim\eta^2$, which is order $2\ell$ in the distance.

\section{Main result}

\begin{theorem}[Order of the saddle after one learned component]\label{thm:main}
Let $W^*$ be as in \eqref{eq:target} and $\theta_S$ as in \eqref{eq:saddle}, with $\mathcal R\ne0$ and $k^*\ge2$. Assume
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item $\mu_\ell\ne s_j$ for every $\ell<k^*/2$;
\item if $2\ell^*\ge k^*$: $\chi_e\ge2$ on the edges of $\St(V_r)$ for some $r$ with $k_r=k^*$;\\
if $2\ell^*<k^*$: there is a set $X\subseteq\mathcal N_{\ell^*}$ such that the submatrix of $M_{\ell^*}$ on $X$ has largest
eigenvalue above $s_j$ and every edge $e$ has $\chi_e-1\ge\#\{(B,\beta)\in X:\lvert B\rvert\ge2,\ e\text{ is an edge of }\St(B)\}$.
\end{enumerate}
Then
\[
\ord(\theta_S)=\min(k^*,\,2\ell^*).
\]
\end{theorem}

Assumption (a) excludes boundary cases. Assumption (b) provides the unused channels needed by the descent of order
$k^*$ (first case) or $2\ell^*$ (second case). The theorem follows from three bounds, proved in
Section~\ref{sec:proofs}: $\ord\le k^*$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:route}); $\ord\ge\min(k^*,2\ell)$ whenever
$\mu_{\ell-1}<s_j$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:lower}); and $\ord\le2\ell$ whenever $\mu_\ell>s_j$ and $\ell<k^*/2$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:sharp}).

When $2\ell^*<k^*$, the descent of order $2\ell^*$ moves toward index strings that are not components of the target:
each element $(B,\beta)$ it uses with $\lvert B\rvert\ge2$ has $\lvert\St(B)\rvert\le\ell^*<k^*$, while producing a component
alone would require $B=V_r$ and hence $\lvert\St(B)\rvert\ge k^*$. Components appear only through pairs of elements.

\begin{remark}[General targets]\label{rem:general}
The lower bound does not need \eqref{eq:target}. For any $W^*$ and unit vectors $q_v$, let $U_j=\bigotimes_vq_v$,
$s_j=\ip{W^*}{U_j}>0$, $\mathcal R=W^*-s_jU_j$, and $\theta_S$ as in \eqref{eq:saddle}. Then $\theta_S$ is critical iff
$P_{\{v\}}\mathcal R=0$ for every core $v$ (notation of Section~\ref{sec:proofs}), and Proposition~\ref{prop:lower} holds with the
condition $\lambda_{\max}(S_{\ell-1})<s_j$ on the operator defined there, which reduces to $\mu_{\ell-1}<s_j$ for targets
\eqref{eq:target}.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:cases}
Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item If $k^*=2$, the saddle is strict.
\item If $k^*\in\{3,4\}$, the order is $k^*$ if $\mu_1<s_j$ and $2$ if $\mu_1>s_j$. So the order is $k^*$ unless the
Hessian has a negative eigenvalue.
\item If $W^*=s_jU_j+s_rU_r$ with $s_r<s_j$ (the larger component was learned), the order is $k_r$.
\item If $W^*=s_jU_j+s_rU_r$ with $s_r>s_j$, let $\ell_0=\min\max(\lvert\St(B_1)\rvert,\lvert\St(B_2)\rvert)$ over partitions of
$V_r$ into two nonempty sets $B_1,B_2$. The order is $\min(k_r,2\ell_0)$.
\item The order is below $k^*$ while the Hessian is positive semidefinite only if $k^*\ge5$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

Part (iii) covers the typical situation in training, where components are learned in decreasing order of weight:
then the order is $k_r$, the size of a Steiner subtree, which depends on the tree.

\begin{example}[Order below $k^*$ with a positive semidefinite Hessian]\label{ex:pairstar}
MPS with six cores, $d=\chi=2$, learned $\pi_j=000000$ with $s_j=1$, and two unlearned components $\pi_1=110001$ and
$\pi_2=110010$ with weight $0.8$ each. Here $V_1=\{0,1,5\}$, so $k_1=6$, and $V_2=\{0,1,4\}$, so $k_2=5$; hence $k^*=5$.
No component differs from $\pi_j$ on exactly two cores, so $M_1=0$. In $M_2$, the element $(\{0,1\},11)$ is coupled to
$(\{5\},1)$ through component 1 and to $(\{4\},1)$ through component 2, so $\mu_2=0.8\sqrt2\approx1.13>s_j$ and
$\ell^*=2$. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} gives order $\min(5,4)=4$. The Hessian is positive semidefinite: $\mu_1=0<s_j$, so
Proposition~\ref{prop:lower} with $\ell=2$ gives order $\ge4>2$.

The descent uses a new channel on cores 0 and 1 with entries $u,w$ that produce the indices $11$ there (the string
$110000$, not a component), together with rotations $\alpha,\beta$ of cores 4 and 5. With $y=uw$ and in units $s_j=1$,
$L-L_S=\frac12(y^2+\alpha^2+\beta^2)-0.8\,y(\alpha+\beta)$ plus higher-order terms. Minimizing over $\alpha,\beta$ gives
$\alpha=\beta=0.8y$ and $L-L_S=-0.14\,y^2$. On the sphere $u^2+w^2=\tau^2$, $y\le\tau^2/2$, so this family reaches
$-0.035\,\tau^4=-(2\cdot0.8^2-1)\tau^4/8$. Numerically, the minimum of $L-L_S$ over all of the sphere
$\lVert\theta-\theta_S\rVert=\tau$ has slope $4.000$ in $\tau$ and coefficient $-0.0350$, so nothing else contributes at order
$\tau^4$. With weights $0.6$ instead, $\mu_2=0.6\sqrt2\approx0.85<1$ and the order is $5$.
\end{example}

\section{Plateau lengths}\label{sec:plateaus}

The following is a leading-order derivation, not a theorem.

\paragraph{Escape time.} Start gradient flow at the balanced $\theta_S$ plus a perturbation of size $\delta$ along an
order-$p$ descent of Section~\ref{sec:mechanisms}. At leading order in $\delta$, with the other variables relaxed to their
instantaneous minimum, the amplitude $b$ of the descent obeys $\dot b=c\,b^{p-1}$ with $c>0$, so the time to leave the
saddle is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:escape}
t_{\rm esc}\simeq\begin{cases}\ln(1/\delta)/c, & p=2,\\[2pt] \delta^{-(p-2)}/\big((p-2)\,c\big), & p\ge3.\end{cases}
\end{equation}
This is derived (Section~\ref{sec:escape-deriv}) in three cases: a new channel along $\St(V_r)$ with equal entries
($p=k_r$, $c=c_r=s_rs_j^{(K-k_r)/K}$); pure rotations ($\ell^*=1$, $p=2$), where the escape is a linear instability with
rate $c=-\lambda_{\min}(H_{\rm rot})$; and the descent of order $2\ell^*$ when $X$ contains a single element with
$\lvert B\rvert\ge2$. When $2\ell^*<k^*$ both descents are present, and the lower order governs the escape only for $\delta$
below a crossover scale set by their coefficients.

\paragraph{From small initialization.} In a run from initialization scale $\sigma$, the entries that will grow are of order
$\sigma$ when the flow reaches $\theta_S$, so the plateau there lasts $\propto\ln(1/\sigma)$ or $\propto\sigma^{-(p-2)}$. These
entries already grow while component $j$ is being learned. For $W^*=s_jU_j+s_rU_r$ with $s_r<s_j$, accounting for this growth
gives the time $\Delta t$ between learning $j$ and learning $r$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:twocomp}
\Delta t\simeq\frac{1-s_r/s_j}{s_rs_j^{(K-2)/K}}\ln\frac1\sigma\quad(k_r=2),\qquad
\Delta t\simeq\frac{1-s_r/s_j}{(k_r-2)\,s_rs_j^{(K-k_r)/K}}\,\sigma^{-(k_r-2)}\quad(k_r\ge3),
\end{equation}
where the second formula assumes an initialization aligned with the target's basis vectors.

\paragraph{Evidence.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Order.} Minimizing $L-L_S$ numerically over spheres of radius $\tau$ in 32 cases (MPS, binary and HT trees,
$k^*$ from $2$ to $7$, including targets not of the form \eqref{eq:target}) gives slopes within $0.003$ of the order
predicted by Theorem~\ref{thm:main} in all 30 cases it covers; 12 further cases computed with independent code agree to
$5\cdot10^{-4}$.
\item \emph{Two components, $s_r<s_j$.} In about 11,600 gradient-flow runs on nine trees ($K$ from $3$ to $15$), the
fitted exponent of $\Delta t$ in $1/\sigma$ is $k_r-2$ for $k_r=3,4,5$, and for $k_r=6,7$ on two trees with
balanced initialization. For $k_r=2$ a logarithmic law fits better than a power law in all 14 settings, with slope
$0.98$--$1.01$ against the value $1$ predicted by \eqref{eq:twocomp}. On every edge outside $\St(V_r)$ the second
singular value of the corresponding matricization of $W$ stays below $4.3\,\sigma^2s_r$.
\item \emph{Order $2\ell^*<k^*$.} A pre-registered test (about 2,800 runs) started gradient flow at $\theta_S$ plus a
perturbation of size $\delta$ and fitted $t_{\rm esc}\propto\delta^{-\gamma}$. For $\delta$ below the predicted crossover it
found $\gamma=2.001\pm0.001$ for $p=4$ ($k^*=5$ and $6$) and $\gamma=3.996$--$3.998$ for $p=6$ ($k^*=7$); the exponent
$k^*-2$ fits $10^3$--$10^5$ times worse. Above the crossover the results were mixed. This test has not been
independently re-checked.
\end{itemize}

\section{Relation to prior work}\label{sec:prior}

\paragraph{Deep linear networks.} Saddle-to-saddle learning, the exponent $K-2$ and learning in order of decreasing
singular value are established for DLNs \citep{saxe2014exact,jacot2022saddletosaddle}. \citet[Thm~7]{achour2024loss}
show which DLN saddles are strict, a lower bound of the form ``order $\ge3$''. A binary tree with four sites is
exactly a depth-3 DLN; there $k^*=3$ and Theorem~\ref{thm:main} reduces to that classification.

\paragraph{Homogeneous models.} Near the origin the weights of a homogeneous model align with a critical direction of
the correlation with the target and escape after a time $\propto\sigma^{-(K-2)}$ \citep{kumar2025early}. If $1$, $2$ or
$k\ge3$ layers start small while the others are large, the escape takes time $O(1)$, $\propto\ln(1/\sigma)$ or
$\propto\sigma^{-(k-2)}$ \citep[Thm~4.2]{rawal2026theory}. Small weights keep the growth they acquired during earlier
plateaus \citep{kunin2025alternating,zhang2026saddletosaddle}, which is the factor $1-s_r/s_j$ in \eqref{eq:twocomp}.
These results take the number of small factors as given; Theorem~\ref{thm:main} determines it for TTNs.

\paragraph{The landscape of TTNs.} \citet{furman2026benign} analyse the landscape of the same model and loss. They
prove that local minima of minimum norm are global. At a minimum-norm critical point they consider the edges on which
the rank of the model is below that of the target. Each connected set of such edges, touching $n$ cores, gives a
straight-line perturbation along which the loss decreases at order $n$ (their Lemmas~D.8--D.9), an upper bound on the
order. They remark that with several separate such sets the smallest gives the fastest escape. They prove a lower bound
for one saddle (a parity target, their Prop.~5.3) and do not study the dynamics.

\paragraph{What is new.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item A lower bound on the order at the saddles \eqref{eq:saddle}, matching the upper bound, so the order is determined
exactly. Previously lower bounds were known only for DLNs and one parity saddle.
\item The order is set by the smallest single Steiner subtree $\St(V_r)$. At $\theta_S$ an edge has model rank below target
rank exactly when cores of $\bigcup_rV_r$ lie on both sides of it, so the set of such edges is the edge set of
$\St(\bigcup_rV_r)$. For two components this is $\St(V_r)$, the upper bound of \citet{furman2026benign} already equals
$k_r$, and the new part is the lower bound. With more components, $\St(\bigcup_rV_r)$ can be much larger than the smallest
$\St(V_r)$, which sets the order.
\item The case $\mu_\ell>s_j$: the order $2\ell^*$, and descent through index strings that are not components of the target.
\item The escape laws \eqref{eq:escape}--\eqref{eq:twocomp} for TTNs and their numerical tests.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Proofs}\label{sec:proofs}

Throughout, $\varepsilon=\lVert\theta-\theta_S\rVert$, $\nu_v$ is the vector of entries of core $v$ on unused channels (entries with
at least one nonzero edge index), and $C_0,C_1,C_2,C_\gamma,M$ denote constants that do not depend on $\varepsilon$. Sets of
cores in this section are denoted $A,A_1,A_2,B$.

\subsection{Decomposition by the cores on which a tensor differs from $U_j$}

Let $p_v=q_vq_v^\top$ and $p_v^\perp=I-p_v$ on $\mathbb R^{D_v}$, and for a set $B$ of cores
\[
P_B=\bigotimes_{v\in B}p_v^\perp\otimes\bigotimes_{v\notin B}p_v ,
\]
with tensor factors labelled by cores. The $2^K$ projections $P_B$ are orthogonal and sum to the identity. Let
$H_B=\bigotimes_{v\in B}q_v^\perp$ (factors labelled by the cores of $B$); it embeds in $\bigotimes_v\mathbb R^{D_v}$ by tensoring with
$q_v$ for $v\notin B$, and its image is the range of $P_B$, the tensors that differ from $U_j$ exactly on the cores of $B$.
A basis of $H_B$ is given by the products of basis vectors whose indices differ from those of $\pi_j$ on every core of $B$.
For disjoint $B_1,B_2$, $H_{B_1}\otimes H_{B_2}=H_{B_1\sqcup B_2}$, with factors matched by core label. Let $\mathcal R_B=P_B\mathcal R$,
viewed in $H_B$. By \eqref{eq:target},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:RB}
\mathcal R_B=\sum_{r:\,V_r=B}s_rU_r,\qquad\text{so}\qquad k^*=\min\{\lvert\St(B)\rvert:\mathcal R_B\ne0\}.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:decomp}
(i) $\theta_S$ is critical iff $\mathcal R_{\{v\}}=0$ for every core $v$, i.e.\ iff $k^*\ge2$.
(ii) Let $\omega(\theta)=\ip{U_j}{W(\theta)}$ and, for $B\ne\emptyset$, $m_B=P_BW/\omega\in H_B$. Wherever $\omega\ne0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:identity}
L(\theta)-L_S=\tfrac12(\omega-s_j)^2+\sum_{B\ne\emptyset}\Big[\tfrac{\omega^2}{2}\lVert m_B\rVert^2-\omega\ip{\mathcal R_B}{m_B}\Big].
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(i) Entries on unused channels do not change $W$ at first order (Section~\ref{sec:mechanisms}). A change of the entries of
core $v$ on the used channel changes $W$ within $H_\emptyset\oplus H_{\{v\}}$. So the gradient vanishes iff $\mathcal R$ has no
component in any $H_{\{v\}}$; its component in $H_\emptyset$ is zero because $\ip{U_j}{\mathcal R}=0$.
(ii) By orthogonality, $\lVert W-W^*\rVert^2=\sum_B\lVert P_BW-P_BW^*\rVert^2$, with $P_\emptyset(W-W^*)=(\omega-s_j)U_j$, and for
$B\ne\emptyset$, $P_BW^*=\mathcal R_B$ and $P_BW=\omega m_B$. Also $L_S=\frac12\sum_B\lVert\mathcal R_B\rVert^2$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Upper bound $k^*$}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:route}
Let $k_r=k^*$ and $\chi_e\ge2$ on the edges of $\St(V_r)$. At the balanced point, set the entry of each core $v\in\St(V_r)$
with physical index given by $\pi_r$ on the sites of $v$, index $1$ on the edges of $\St(V_r)$ and index $0$ on its other
edges, to $\tau$. Then $\lVert\theta(\tau)-\theta_S\rVert=\sqrt{k^*}\,\tau$ and
\[
L(\theta(\tau))-L_S=-s_r\,s_j^{(K-k^*)/K}\,\tau^{k^*}+\tfrac12s_j^{2(K-k^*)/K}\,\tau^{2k^*}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Expand the contraction by choosing, for each core of $\St(V_r)$, either its original entry or the new one. If a term
uses the new entry on one core of $\St(V_r)$ and the original entry on a neighbouring core of $\St(V_r)$, the shared
edge contracts index $1$ with index $0$ and the term vanishes. Only two terms remain: all original entries, giving
$s_jU_j$, and all new entries on $\St(V_r)$, giving $\tau^{k^*}a^{K-k^*}U$, where $U$ has the indices of $\pi_r$ on the
sites of $\St(V_r)$ and those of $\pi_j$ elsewhere. Since $\pi_r=\pi_j$ outside $V_r$, $U=U_r$. Hence
$W-W^*=-\mathcal R+\tau^{k^*}a^{K-k^*}U_r$, and $\ip{\mathcal R}{U_r}=s_r$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Lower bound}

\paragraph{Cumulants.} Consider families $f=(f_B)_{B\subseteq V}$ with $f_B\in H_B$, with the product
$(f*g)_B=\sum_{B_1\sqcup B_2=B}f_{B_1}\otimes g_{B_2}$. Because tensor factors are labelled by core, this product is commutative
and associative, with unit $(1,0,0,\dots)$, and every family with $f_\emptyset=0$ is nilpotent; so $\exp$ and $\log$ are finite
sums and $\log(f*g)=\log f+\log g$. With $m_\emptyset=1$, define $\kappa=\log m$. Explicitly,
\[
m_B=\sum_{\mathcal P\in\mathrm{Part}(B)}\ \bigotimes_{A\in\mathcal P}\kappa_A,\qquad
\kappa_B=\sum_{\mathcal P\in\mathrm{Part}(B)}(-1)^{\lvert\mathcal P\rvert-1}(\lvert\mathcal P\rvert-1)!\bigotimes_{A\in\mathcal P}m_A ,
\]
sums over set partitions $\mathcal P$ of $B$. Near $\theta_S$, $\omega$ is close to $s_j\ne0$, so the $m_B$ and $\kappa_B$ are
analytic there; they vanish at $\theta_S$ for $B\ne\emptyset$, so $\lVert\kappa_B\rVert\le C_0\varepsilon$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:locality}
For $\lvert B\rvert\ge2$, near $\theta_S$, $\lVert\kappa_B\rVert\le C_0\prod_{x\in\St(B)}\lVert\nu_x\rVert$. In particular
$\lVert\kappa_B\rVert\le C_0\varepsilon^{\lvert\St(B)\rvert}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Vanishing.} Fix $x\in\St(B)$ and suppose $\nu_x=0$. Then all nonzero entries of $G_x$ have edge indices $0$, so $W$
factorizes at $x$: $W=g_x\otimes\bigotimes_bW_b$, where $g_x\in\mathbb R^{D_x}$ is the physical vector of $G_x$, $b$ runs over the
components of $T$ with $x$ removed (node sets $V_b$), and $W_b$ is the contraction of component $b$ with its edge to $x$ set
to index $0$. Correspondingly $\omega=\omega_x\prod_b\omega_b$ with $\omega_x=\ip{q_x}{g_x}$ and $\omega_b=\ip{\bigotimes_{v\in V_b}q_v}{W_b}$, all close
to their nonzero values at $\theta_S$. The projections $P_B$ factorize core by core, so $m=m^{(x)}*\prod_bm^{(b)}$, where
$m^{(x)}_{\{x\}}=p_x^\perp g_x/\omega_x$, $m^{(b)}_A=P^{(b)}_AW_b/\omega_b$ for $A\subseteq V_b$ (with $P^{(b)}_A$ the analogous projection on
the sites of $V_b$), and each has unit component on $\emptyset$. Hence $\kappa=\log m^{(x)}+\sum_b\log m^{(b)}$ is supported on
subsets of $\{x\}$ or of a single $V_b$. $B$ is neither: if $x\in B$, then $B\not\subseteq\{x\}$ and $B\not\subseteq V_b$; if
$x\notin B$ and $B\subseteq V_b$, then the connected set $V_b$ contains $\St(B)$, which contains $x$. So $\kappa_B=0$ whenever
$\nu_x=0$ for some $x\in\St(B)$.

\emph{Bound.} Expand $\kappa_B$ in a power series in $\theta-\theta_S$, absolutely convergent on a polydisc of radius $2r_0$. Setting
$\nu_x=0$ removes exactly the monomials containing a variable of $\nu_x$, and the result is $0$; so every monomial with nonzero
coefficient contains a variable of $\nu_x$, for each $x\in\St(B)$. Fix an ordering of the variables, and from each monomial
factor out, for each $x\in\St(B)$, the first variable of $\nu_x$ that it contains. The remaining series converges on the
polydisc of radius $r_0$ and is bounded there, which gives the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:lower}
Let $\theta_S$ be critical and $2\le\ell\le\lceil k^*/2\rceil$. On $\mathcal H_{\ell-1}=\bigoplus H_A$, summed over nonempty $A$ with
$\lvert\St(A)\rvert\le\ell-1$, define the symmetric bilinear form
\[
Q(x,y)=\sum_{A_1\cap A_2=\emptyset}\ip{\mathcal R_{A_1\sqcup A_2}}{x_{A_1}\otimes y_{A_2}},
\]
and let $S_{\ell-1}$ be its operator. If $\lambda_{\max}(S_{\ell-1})<s_j$, then $L(\theta)-L_S\ge-C_1\varepsilon^{\min(k^*,2\ell)}$ near
$\theta_S$. For targets \eqref{eq:target}, $S_{\ell-1}=M_{\ell-1}$ in the basis of products of basis vectors.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $p=\min(k^*,2\ell)$ and $y_A=\lVert\kappa_A\rVert$. Call $A$ \emph{small} if $\lvert\St(A)\rvert\le\ell-1$ and \emph{large}
otherwise. Then: (F1) a large set has $\lvert\St(A)\rvert\ge\ell\ge2$, so $y_A\le C_0\varepsilon^\ell$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:locality};
(F2) subsets of small sets are small; (F3) $\mathcal R_A=0$ for small $A$, since $\lvert\St(A)\rvert<k^*$; (F4) summing
$\ip{\mathcal R_B}{\kappa_{A_1}\otimes\kappa_{A_2}}$ over all $B$ and all unordered partitions $B=A_1\sqcup A_2$ into small sets gives
$\frac12Q(\kappa_{\rm s},\kappa_{\rm s})\le\frac12\lambda_{\max}(S_{\ell-1})\lVert\kappa_{\rm s}\rVert^2$, where $\kappa_{\rm s}$ collects the
cumulants of small sets.

\emph{Quadratic term of \eqref{eq:identity}.} For small $B$, $m_B-\kappa_B$ is a sum of products of at least two cumulants of
small sets (F2), each at most $C_0\varepsilon\,y_A$ for one factor $A$. So
$\sum_{B\text{ small}}\lVert m_B\rVert^2\ge(1-C_2\varepsilon)\lVert\kappa_{\rm s}\rVert^2$; the terms with large $B$ are nonnegative and dropped.

\emph{Linear term.} Only $B$ with $\mathcal R_B\ne0$ contribute, and they have $\lvert\St(B)\rvert\ge k^*$. Expanding $m_B$ over set
partitions: the single cumulant gives $O(\varepsilon^{k^*})$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:locality}); two small blocks give (F4); one small and
one large block give at most $\frac\gamma2y_{A_1}^2+C_\gamma\varepsilon^{2\ell}$ (Young's inequality and F1); two large blocks give
$O(\varepsilon^{2\ell})$; three or more blocks give $O(\varepsilon)(y_{A_1}^2+y_{A_2}^2)$ if two blocks are small and $O(\varepsilon^{2\ell})$ otherwise.
With $M$ the number of terms in which a given $y_A^2$ appears,
$\sum_B\ip{\mathcal R_B}{m_B}\le\frac12(\lambda_{\max}(S_{\ell-1})+M\gamma+C_0'\varepsilon)\lVert\kappa_{\rm s}\rVert^2+C_\gamma\varepsilon^p$.

\emph{Conclusion.} By \eqref{eq:identity}, dropping $\frac12(\omega-s_j)^2$,
$L-L_S\ge\frac\omega2[\omega(1-C_2\varepsilon)-\lambda_{\max}(S_{\ell-1})-M\gamma-C_0'\varepsilon]\lVert\kappa_{\rm s}\rVert^2-\omega C_\gamma\varepsilon^p$.
With $g=s_j-\lambda_{\max}(S_{\ell-1})>0$, $\gamma=g/(4M)$ and $\omega\ge s_j-C_0''\varepsilon$, the bracket is positive for small $\varepsilon$,
so $L-L_S\ge-C_1\varepsilon^p$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Upper bound $2\ell$}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:sharp}
Let $\ell<k^*/2$ and let $X\subseteq\mathcal N_\ell$ be such that $M_\ell$ restricted to $X$ has largest eigenvalue $\rho>s_j$ and
$X$ satisfies the edge condition of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(b). Then some curve has $\lVert\theta(\tau)-\theta_S\rVert\le\tau$ and
$L(\theta(\tau))-L_S\le-c\,\tau^{2\ell}$ for some $c>0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Work at the balanced point. Let $x\ge0$ be a unit eigenvector of $M_\ell|_X$ for $\rho$; it exists by Perron--Frobenius since the
entries are nonnegative, and $x\ge0$ makes the roots below real. For $0<\eta\le1$:
\begin{itemize}
\item for $(\{v\},\beta)\in X$, set the entry of $G_v$ with physical index $\beta$ and all edge indices $0$ to $a\eta x_{(\{v\},\beta)}$
(a rotation of $q_v$);
\item for $n=(B,\beta)\in X$ with $\lvert B\rvert\ge2$, let $h=\lvert\St(B)\rvert\le\ell$. On every edge of $\St(B)$ choose an unused index
chosen by no other such element of $X$ (possible by the edge condition). Give each core $u\in\St(B)$ the entry
$a(\eta x_n)^{1/h}$ with physical index $\beta$ (if $u\in B$) or that of $\pi_j$ (if $u\notin B$), the chosen indices on the edges
of $\St(B)$, and $0$ on its other edges.
\end{itemize}
Then $\lVert\theta-\theta_S\rVert\le C_0\eta^{1/\ell}$.

\emph{Structure of $W$.} Consider a nonzero term of the contraction and an edge $e$ carrying a nonzero index. That index was
chosen by exactly one element $n$, so both cores at $e$ use entries of $n$. The entries of $n$ carry $n$'s indices on every
edge of $\St(B)$ and $0$ on all other edges, so the term uses $n$'s entries on all of $\St(B)$ and on no core outside it. Hence
each connected set of edges with nonzero indices is the edge set of $\St(B)$ for a single element, used completely. $W$ is
therefore a sum over collections of elements of $X$ on pairwise disjoint cores, in which each element contributes the factor
$\eta x_n$ (the $h$ roots multiply to $\eta x_n$) and changes the indices on its set $B$, and every other core contributes $a$
times its original vector. In particular $W$ is a polynomial in $\eta$ with integer powers, $\omega=s_j$ exactly, and
$m_B=\eta\,m_B^{(1)}+\eta^2m_B^{(2)}+O(\eta^3)$, where $m^{(1)}$ collects single elements and $m^{(2)}$ pairs of elements.

\emph{Order $\eta$.} $\sum_B\lVert m^{(1)}_B\rVert^2=\lVert x\rVert^2=1$. No single element has $B=V_r$, since that would need
$\lvert\St(B)\rvert\ge k^*>\ell$; so $\sum_B\ip{\mathcal R_B}{m^{(1)}_B}=0$ by \eqref{eq:RB}.

\emph{Order $\eta^2$.} If $M_\ell[n_1,n_2]=s_r>0$, then $\St(B_1)$ and $\St(B_2)$ are disjoint: otherwise their union would be a
connected set containing $V_r$ with at most $2\ell-1<k^*$ cores. So every nonzero entry of $M_\ell|_X$ corresponds to a pair of
elements on disjoint cores, and $\sum_B\ip{\mathcal R_B}{m^{(2)}_B}=\frac12x^\top M_\ell|_Xx=\frac\rho2$.

By \eqref{eq:identity} with $\omega=s_j$: $L-L_S=\frac{s_j^2}{2}\eta^2-\frac{s_j\rho}{2}\eta^2+O(\eta^3)=-\frac{s_j}{2}(\rho-s_j)\eta^2+O(\eta^3)$.
Setting $\eta=(\tau/C_0)^\ell$ gives the claim.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and Corollary~\ref{cor:cases}}

If $k^*=2$, the curve of Proposition~\ref{prop:route} has order $2$, every critical point has order at least $2$, and
$\min(2,2\ell^*)=2$. Let $k^*\ge3$.

\emph{Case $2\ell^*\ge k^*$} (including $\ell^*=\infty$). Every $\ell<k^*/2$ has $\ell<\ell^*$, so $\mu_\ell\le s_j$, and $\mu_\ell<s_j$ by
(a). Proposition~\ref{prop:lower} with $\ell=\lceil k^*/2\rceil$ gives $\ord\ge k^*$, and Proposition~\ref{prop:route} gives
$\ord\le k^*$. So $\ord=k^*=\min(k^*,2\ell^*)$.

\emph{Case $2\ell^*<k^*$.} If $\ell^*=1$, Proposition~\ref{prop:sharp} gives $\ord\le2$, so $\ord=2$. If $\ell^*\ge2$, then
$\mu_{\ell^*-1}\le s_j$ by minimality, and $<s_j$ by (a); Proposition~\ref{prop:lower} at $\ell=\ell^*\le\lceil k^*/2\rceil$ gives
$\ord\ge\min(k^*,2\ell^*)=2\ell^*$, and Proposition~\ref{prop:sharp} gives $\ord\le2\ell^*$. \qed

\emph{Corollary.} (i) Shown above. (ii) An order below $k^*\le4$ requires $2\ell^*<4$, i.e.\ $\ell^*=1$, i.e.\ $\mu_1>s_j$. For
\eqref{eq:hessblock}: changing $z_u$ changes $W$ by $z_u\,s_j/a_u$ times an orthonormal tensor, and two such coordinates on cores
$u,v$ couple through any component with $V_r=\{u,v\}$, with coefficient $-s_r\,s_j/(a_ua_v)$. So the block is
$s_jD(s_jI-M_1)D$. (iii) With one unlearned component, each row of $M_\ell$ has at most one nonzero entry, $s_r$, so
$\mu_\ell\le s_r<s_j$ for all $\ell$ and $\ell^*=\infty$. (iv) Each row again has at most one nonzero entry, and some entry is
nonzero iff $\ell\ge\ell_0$; so $\mu_\ell=s_r>s_j$ for $\ell\ge\ell_0$, $\ell^*=\ell_0$, and the order is $\min(k_r,2\ell_0)$. (v) For
$k^*\le4$, by (ii) an order below $k^*$ means order $2$, which is equivalent to a negative Hessian eigenvalue
(Section~\ref{sec:setting}). \qed

\subsection{Derivation of the escape times}\label{sec:escape-deriv}

\emph{New channel along $\St(V_r)$.} With the other entries fixed at the balanced $\theta_S$ and amplitudes $b_v$ on the cores of
$\St(V_r)$, the computation of Proposition~\ref{prop:route} gives
$\dot b_v=a^{K-k_r}\prod_{u\ne v}b_u\,(s_r-a^{K-k_r}\prod_ub_u)$. For equal small amplitudes, $\dot b=c_rb^{k_r-1}$, and
$\frac{d}{dt}b^{-(p-2)}=-(p-2)c$ integrates to \eqref{eq:escape}.

\emph{Rotations ($\ell^*=1$).} The flow is linear at leading order, $\dot z=-H_{\rm rot}z$, and grows along the eigenvector of the
most negative eigenvalue of $H_{\rm rot}$ at rate $c=-\lambda_{\min}(H_{\rm rot})$.

\emph{Order $2\ell^*$ with one multi-core element.} Let $X$ contain one element $(B,\beta)$ with $\lvert B\rvert\ge2$, and $h=\lvert\St(B)\rvert$.
Then $h=\ell^*$: if $h<\ell^*$, all elements of $X$ would lie in $\mathcal N_{\ell^*-1}$, so $\mu_{\ell^*-1}\ge\rho>s_j$, contradicting the
minimality of $\ell^*$. The rotations have curvature of order one and follow the amplitude $y=\prod_{v\in\St(B)}b_v$; minimizing over
them leaves $L-L_S\approx-\frac c2y^2$ with $c>0$ (in Example~\ref{ex:pairstar}, $c=2(0.14)=0.28$). Then
$\dot b_v=c\,b_v\prod_{u\ne v}b_u^2$, and for equal amplitudes $\dot b=c\,b^{2h-1}=c\,b^{2\ell^*-1}$.

\emph{Growth before the plateau.} While $j$ is learned from aligned initialization, its cores grow as $\dot a\simeq s_ja^{K-1}$ from
$a=\sigma$, and the entries on $\St(V_r)$ already grow at rate $s_ra^{K-k_r}b^{k_r-1}$. For $k_r\ge3$, $b^{-(k_r-2)}$ decreases by
$(k_r-2)s_r\int a^{K-k_r}dt=(k_r-2)\frac{s_r}{s_j}\int_\sigma a^{1-k_r}da=\frac{s_r}{s_j}\sigma^{-(k_r-2)}+O(1)$, so at the plateau
$b^{-(k_r-2)}=(1-s_r/s_j)\sigma^{-(k_r-2)}+O(1)$. For $k_r=2$, $\ln b$ grows by $\frac{s_r}{s_j}\ln\frac1\sigma+O(1)$. Inserting into
\eqref{eq:escape} gives \eqref{eq:twocomp}.

\section{Scope}
The theorem covers the saddles \eqref{eq:saddle} with one learned component, targets \eqref{eq:target} for the exact value (the
lower bound holds for any target, Remark~\ref{rem:general}), whitened inputs and the population loss. Saddles with several
learned components and the boundary cases $\mu_\ell=s_j$ are open. Section~\ref{sec:plateaus} is a leading-order derivation, not a
theorem, and covers the three cases listed there.
