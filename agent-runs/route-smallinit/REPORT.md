# route-smallinit: route theory vs gradient-flow training from small random init (star targets)

Status: working - Branch: agent/route-smallinit - Updated: 2026-10-13 20:50 UTC

**Bugs in `ttn/routes.py`: none found.** Two usage notes (not bugs): `RouteTarget.greedy_next` breaks
depth ties by the weight alone, not by s_r A_r(J) as in App. C. My greedy rule uses s_r A_r(J) (A_r at the
balanced partial target). The two differ at one learned set of the 100 (S5A after {00000, 00201}:
s_r A_r picks 11200, weight alone picks 10101); training followed s_r A_r in 27/27 such transitions.
`AlignedManifold.align`'s quality cannot detect a block that the network represents with two redundant
channels (W is then still canonical).

## Summary

Asked: on star-satisfying pattern targets, how well do the paper's predictions describe tree tensor
networks trained by gradient flow from a small random init, and where do they fail? I trained 6000
networks (10 targets on mps/binary/ht trees, K <= 7 cores, 15 init scales sigma from 10^-1.5 to 1e-5,
40 seeds) plus the exact reduced flow on the aligned manifold for every (target, sigma).
**Answer: along the main path the theory is quantitatively right** (plateaus at the partial targets,
next pattern = smallest route depth, durations ~ sigma^-(c-2) per seed, growth with effective depth c,
dynamics on the aligned manifold, and the scalar route equation predicts the plateau length from the
size of the dormant route entries). **It fails in four reproducible ways:** (1) ties in route depth are
decided by the random init, not by the weight; (2) when a lighter pattern is learned before a heavier
one that can reuse it, that plateau is a strict saddle and the heavier pattern takes over by *erasing*
learned patterns (forgetting, 13% of all runs); (3) the dormant entries are not always of order sigma
at plateau entry (B4: sigma^0.70), which changes the log-law slope; (4) in two decoupled-type targets
a fixed set of seeds never learns the last pattern (its route dies: "trapped"). The brief's one-step
predictor from the amplitude P_r fails; the same equation started from the dormant-entry size works.

Plain-word definitions. A *pattern* U_a is a one-hot product tensor; the target is W* = sum_a s_a U_a
with weights s_a. *Amplitude* P_a = <U_a, W>; pattern a is *learned* when P_a > s_a/2; a *learning
event* is the last time that happens. A *plateau* is the time between two learning events. The *route
depth* c of a missing pattern r, given the learned set J, is the number of cores that must grow from
zero to create r (paper eq. 8.4); the *greedy rule* says the next pattern has the smallest c (ties:
larger s_r A_r(J)); the *pairwise rule* measures c from a single learned pattern only (no "splicing" of
several learned patterns); the *aligned manifold* M* is the set of networks whose cores only use the
"block" entries, on which W is exactly a sum of target patterns. sigma is the init scale. Numbers are
at sigma <= 1e-3 (9 grid points) unless stated. Every number: `experiments/route_smallinit/results/headline.md`
(generated by `headline.py` from the CSVs).

Failures of a prediction, each with its evidence:
1. **Tie-break by weight** (App. C): fails for D4, S5A, S7, S5B (see (b)); in D4 the lighter of two
   tied patterns is learned first in 37% of the seeds.
2. **Lighter-first plateaus are strict saddles; takeover with forgetting** (route depth and C.1 item 4):
   S5A history "+10101 +00201 -10101 +00000 -00201 +11200 +00201 +10101" (13/40 seeds, the same at
   every sigma): the plateau at {10101, 00201} ends after ~log(1/sigma) although c*_J = 3, the heavy
   00000 erases 10101 and 00201, which are relearned with the predicted depths. Same in S5B (9 seeds),
   S7 (6 seeds with forgetting), PS (16 seeds: the pair erases 000000). For one learned pattern this is
   Theorem 3.2 (order 2 when the learned pattern is lighter than a missing one); for two learned patterns
   it is a full-space shortcut below c*_J that the paper allows (Remark 8.5) but does not predict.
   All 17 seed fits of one-pattern plateaus where Theorem 3.2 gives order 2 < route depth 3 (S5A
   {00201}, S5B {20200}) are at the log law (exponent 0.107): Theorem 3.2 confirmed, route depth refuted.
3. **Spectral shortcut of the pair-star** (target kind v): the route depth (5) predicts sigma^-3 after
   learning 000000; training gives sigma^-2.000 (15 seeds), Theorem 3.2's order 4. The greedy rule names
   the right next pattern (110010, then 110001 together) but the wrong duration.
4. **Dormant entries not of order sigma** (C.1 item 1) after a first escape that shares cores with the
   missing route: B4 {0000} -> 0110 entries ~ sigma^0.70 and log-law slope 2.33 vs predicted 3.33; the
   exact reduced flow on M* gives the same 2.33, so this is a gap in the formal estimate (eq. C.6), not
   training leaving M*. [conjecture] 0.70 = 1 - s_0110/s_0000.
5. **PS 000000 after the pair**: exponent 3.0 (12 seeds) or 3.9 (11 seeds) at the same route depth 5
   and the same effective depth 5.16; the route is very unbalanced at entry (max/min entry 125x). Not
   explained.
6. **Trapped runs**: D4 13/40 seeds and B4 5/40 seeds, at every sigma: after two patterns, one dormant
   entry of the last route (the smallest at entry, 0.03-0.12 sigma vs 0.2-0.6 sigma in escaping seeds)
   decays to ~1e-14 sigma and the amplitude to ~1e-55; the run stops by the trap rule. Those routes
   span the whole tree including end cores whose bond is capped at d (no spare channel). Not predicted.
   Diagnostic: with the cap removed (2 spare channels on every edge) no run traps (0/80 for D4, 0/80 for
   B4, sigma 1e-3 and 1e-4; `results/diag_trapped.csv`): the traps come from the missing spare channel.
7. **One-step-ahead from P_r** (the brief's form): fails because P_r at entry still holds transients
   (right after the first escape other patterns sit at 1-10% of their weight, then fall by up to 10
   orders of magnitude; `fig_example_run`).
8. **First escape** (not covered by the theory): in 23% of runs another pattern rises with the first
   (H4 62%, PS 89%, B8 29%); in S5A the first plateau is sometimes a mixture (distance 0.21).

## Decisions

- **Targets**: replaced the brief's ht4 {0000,0122,1100,2022}, binary8 {00000000,00001111,01011100,
  10000011} and decoupled mps5 by targets with all later route depths <= 4 (the brief's own limit), found
  by search with the same intent (splice on the greedy path; for binary8 and ht4 the splice changes the
  predicted pattern). Also a first binary8 choice was isomorphic to the ht4 one and was replaced.
- **Bond dimensions**: blocks + 2 per edge, capped at the cut rank (no spare channel next to a single
  site with d = number of blocks). Recorded as `chi` = largest bond in the CSVs.
- **Floor check**: the registered duplicate with the library's arithmetic costs 30-60 min per K = 7 run
  at 1e-5; replaced by a same-cost duplicate with another contraction order for every seed at the 3
  smallest sigma, plus the library duplicate for seeds 0-9 at 1e-5 (deviation 3).
- **Two false starts**: the trap rule fired at the origin for K = 7 at sigma = 1e-5 (fixed: only after a
  first pattern); killing that launch left orphaned workers running old code into the output folder, so
  everything was set aside and the grid rerun from scratch (deviations 1-2).
- **Clean events and passes** (added after seeing forgetting): the brief's event definition (last
  crossing) hides the transient plateaus of runs with forgetting; I kept it as the registered basis and
  added `event_clean` and `pass` bases, reported side by side.
- **One-step predictor B** (dormant-entry size) added next to the brief's predictor A; pre-registered.

## Caveats and next steps

- The theory's tie-break and the lighter-first takeovers suggest testing larger weight gaps and more
  seeds per tie; the trapped routes deserve a direct test with one spare channel added at the capped
  edges (does the trap disappear?).
- The PS "pair first" seeds with exponent ~3.9 are not understood (route depth and effective depth say
  5 -> 3).
- `align` cannot see redundant-channel representations; a direct check of the bond usage would tell
  whether the lighter-first states are off M* in parameter space.
- Exponent fits use 9 sigma values per seed; durations at c = 2 are fitted with the log law.
